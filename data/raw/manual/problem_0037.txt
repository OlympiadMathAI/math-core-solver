ID: 0037

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Sequences, Sliding Sums, Extremal Principle

STATEMENT:
Suppose that $1000$ students are standing in a circle. Prove that there is an integer $k$ with
\[
100\le k\le300
\]
and a contiguous group of $2k$ students such that the first $k$ students in the group contain the same number of girls as the last $k$ students.

SOLUTION 1:
Number the students cyclically and put
\[
a_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if student }i\text{ is a girl},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Extend the sequence periodically by $a_{i+1000}=a_i$ for every integer $i$. Define
\[
S_k(i)=a_i+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+k-1}.
\]
The desired conclusion is equivalent to finding $100\le k\le300$ and $i$ such that
\[
S_k(i)=S_k(i+k).
\tag{1}
\]

Assume for a contradiction that no such pair exists. Choose $i$ for which $S_{100}(i)$ is maximal. Equality in either
\[
S_{100}(i-100)\le S_{100}(i),
\qquad
S_{100}(i+100)\le S_{100}(i)
\]
would already give (1) with $k=100$. Hence
\[
S_{100}(i-100)-S_{100}(i)<0,
\qquad
S_{100}(i)-S_{100}(i+100)>0.
\]
Let
\[
D(j)=S_{100}(j)-S_{100}(j+100).
\]
As $j$ runs from $i-100$ to $i$, the integer $D(j)$ changes from negative to positive, and it is never $0$. Thus there is an index
\[
j\in[i-100,i-1]
\]
such that
\[
S_{100}(j)\le S_{100}(j+100)-1,
\tag{2}
\]
while
\[
S_{100}(j+1)\ge S_{100}(j+101)+1.
\tag{3}
\]
Now
\[
D(j+1)-D(j)
=2a_{j+100}-a_j-a_{j+200}.
\]
From (2)--(3), this difference is at least $2$. Since each $a_r$ is $0$ or $1$, we must have
\[
a_j=0,
\qquad
a_{j+100}=1,
\qquad
a_{j+200}=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Substituting (4) back into (2)--(3) gives
\[
S_{99}(j+1)=S_{99}(j+101).
\tag{5}
\]

Let $p$ and $\ell$ be the least positive integers for which
\[
a_{j-p}=1,
\qquad
a_{j+200+\ell}=1.
\]
By reversing the circular order if necessary, we may assume
\[
p\ge\ell.
\]
If $p\ge200$, then
\[
a_{j-199}=a_{j-198}=\cdots=a_j=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
S_{100}(j-199)=S_{100}(j-99)=0,
\]
contradicting our assumption for $k=100$. Therefore
\[
\ell\le p\le199.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
K=100+\ell.
\]
By (6),
\[
101\le K\le299.
\]
The minimality of $p$ and $\ell$, together with (4), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_K(j-\ell+1)
&=(a_{j-\ell+1}+\cdots+a_j)+S_{99}(j+1)+a_{j+100}\\
&=S_{99}(j+1)+1,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_K(j+101)
&=S_{99}(j+101)
+(a_{j+200}+\cdots+a_{j+200+\ell-1})
+a_{j+200+\ell}\\
&=S_{99}(j+101)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using (5),
\[
S_K(j-\ell+1)=S_K(j+101).
\]
Since
\[
(j-\ell+1)+K=j+101,
\]
this is precisely an equality of the form (1), with $100\le K\le300$. The contradiction proves the result.
