ID: 0381

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Radical Axes, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Power of a Point

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle, and let $F$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$. A point $P$ lies on segment $AF$.

The line through $P$ parallel to $AC$ meets $BC$ at $D$, and the line through $P$ parallel to $AB$ meets $BC$ at $E$.

Let $X\ne A$ lie on the circumcircle of triangle $ABD$ and satisfy
\[
DA=DX.
\]
Let $Y\ne A$ lie on the circumcircle of triangle $ACE$ and satisfy
\[
EA=EY.
\]

Prove that
\[
B,C,X,Y
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
A'=BX\cap CY.
\]

Because $A,B,D,X$ are concyclic and
\[
DA=DX,
\]
the equal chords $DA$ and $DX$ subtend equal angles at $B$. Thus line $BD=BC$ is either the internal or the external angle bisector of $\angle ABX$. Equivalently, line $BX$ is the reflection of line $BA$ in $BC$.

Similarly, line $CY$ is the reflection of line $CA$ in $BC$. Therefore $A'$ is the reflection of $A$ in $BC$. In particular,
\[
A,A',F,P
\]
are collinear.

Let
\[
\omega_B=(ABDX),
\qquad
\omega_C=(ACEY).
\]
Since $PD\parallel AC$ and $PE\parallel AB$, the similar right triangles determined by the altitude give
\[
\frac{FD}{FC}
=
\frac{FP}{FA}
=
\frac{FE}{FB}.
\]
Hence
\[
FD\cdot FB=FE\cdot FC.
\tag{1}
\]
The two sides of (1) are the powers of $F$ with respect to $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$. Thus $F$ has equal powers with respect to the two circles.

The point $A$ belongs to both circles, so their radical axis is line
\[
AF=AA'.
\]
Therefore $A'$ has equal powers:
\[
A'B\cdot A'X=A'C\cdot A'Y.
\]
As $A',B,X$ are collinear and $A',C,Y$ are collinear, the converse of the secant theorem gives
\[
B,C,X,Y
\]
concyclic.

SOLUTION 2:
If $P=A$, then $D=C$ and $E=B$, so both $X$ and $Y$ already lie on $(ABC)$ and the conclusion is immediate.

If $P=F$, the circles $(ABD)$ and $(ACE)$ have the common chord $AF$. The reflection argument below places $A'=BX\cap CY$ on $AF$, so the power equality and the conclusion follow immediately. We may therefore assume
\[
P\notin\{A,F\}.
\]

Because $A,B,D,X$ are concyclic and $DA=DX$, the equal chords $DA$ and $DX$ subtend equal angles at $B$. Hence line $BD=BC$ bisects the angle formed by $BA$ and $BX$, internally or externally, and $BX$ is the reflection of $BA$ in $BC$.

Similarly, $CY$ is the reflection of $CA$ in $BC$. Therefore
\[
A'=BX\cap CY
\]
is the reflection of $A$ in $BC$. Since $AF\perp BC$ and $P\in AF$,
\[
A',P,A
\]
are collinear.

It remains to prove that line $AP$ is the radical axis of the circles
\[
(ABD)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(ACE).
\]

Let $N\ne P$ be the second intersection of line $AP$ with the circumcircle of triangle $PDE$. Using directed angles,
\[
\angle DNA
=
\angle DNP
=
\angle DEP
=
\angle DBA,
\]
because $DE$ lies on $BC$ and $EP\parallel AB$. Hence
\[
A,B,D,N
\]
are concyclic.

Similarly,
\[
\angle ENA
=
\angle ENP
=
\angle EDP
=
\angle ECA,
\]
because $DP\parallel AC$. Therefore
\[
A,C,E,N
\]
are concyclic.

Thus $A$ and $N$ are the two common points of the circles $(ABD)$ and $(ACE)$, so their radical axis is
\[
AN=AP.
\]
The point $A'$ lies on this line, and consequently
\[
A'B\cdot A'X=A'C\cdot A'Y.
\]
It follows that $B,C,X,Y$ are concyclic.
