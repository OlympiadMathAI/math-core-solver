ID: 0298

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Ceiling Function, Infinite Descent, Vieta Jumping, Diophantine Equations

STATEMENT:
Let $a$ and $b$ be positive integers. Prove that the integer
\[
a^2+\left\lceil\frac{4a^2}{b}\right\rceil
\]
is not a perfect square.

SOLUTION 1:
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
a^2+\left\lceil\frac{4a^2}{b}\right\rceil=(a+k)^2
\]
for some positive integer $k$. Then
\[
\left\lceil\frac{4a^2}{b}\right\rceil=(2a+k)k.
\]
Putting
\[
c=2a,
\]
we obtain a positive integer solution $(c,k)$ of
\[
\left\lceil\frac{c^2}{b}\right\rceil=(c+k)k,
\tag{1}
\]
with $c$ even.

Among all positive integer solutions of (1), without imposing any parity condition on $c$, choose one for which $k$ is minimal.

Since
\[
\frac{c^2}{b}
>
\left\lceil\frac{c^2}{b}\right\rceil-1
=ck+k^2-1
\ge ck,
\]
we have
\[
c>bk.
\tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{(c-k)(c+k)}b
<
\frac{c^2}{b}
\le
\left\lceil\frac{c^2}{b}\right\rceil
=(c+k)k.
\]
Cancelling $c+k$,
\[
c-k<bk.
\tag{3}
\]
From (2)--(3), there is an integer $r$ with
\[
c=bk+r,
\qquad
0<r<k.
\tag{4}
\]

Substitute (4) into (1):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\lceil\frac{(bk+r)^2}{b}\right\rceil
&=
bk^2+2kr+\left\lceil\frac{r^2}{b}\right\rceil,\\
(c+k)k
&=
bk^2+2kr+k(k-r).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\left\lceil\frac{r^2}{b}\right\rceil=k(k-r).
\tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is another solution of (1), now with
\[
c'=r,
\qquad
k'=k-r.
\]
Both are positive, and
\[
0<k'<k,
\]
contradicting the minimality of $k$.

Hence the stated integer cannot be a square.

SOLUTION 2:
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
a^2+\left\lceil\frac{4a^2}{b}\right\rceil=c^2
\]
for some positive integer $c>a$.

The defining property of the ceiling function gives
\[
c^2-1
<
a^2+\frac{4a^2}{b}
\le
c^2.
\]
Multiplying by $b$,
\[
0\le bc^2-a^2(b+4)<b.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
d=bc^2-a^2(b+4),
\qquad
x=c+a,
\qquad
y=c-a.
\]
Then
\[
0\le d<b,
\qquad
x,y\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\]
and
\[
c=\frac{x+y}{2},
\qquad
a=\frac{x-y}{2}.
\]
Equation (1) becomes
\[
x^2-(b+2)xy+y^2+d=0.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix $b$ and $d$, and among all positive integer solutions $(x,y)$ of (2), choose one minimizing
\[
x+y.
\]
By symmetry, assume
\[
x\ge y.
\]

Regard (2) as a quadratic equation in $x$. Let $z$ be its other root. By Vieta's formulas,
\[
x+z=(b+2)y
\]
and
\[
xz=y^2+d.
\]
Thus
\[
z=(b+2)y-x=\frac{y^2+d}{x}.
\]
The first expression shows that $z$ is an integer, and the second that $z>0$. Therefore $(z,y)$ is another positive integer solution of (2).

We now prove that $z<x$. First, $x>1$; otherwise $x=y=1$, and (2) would give $d=b$, contrary to $d<b$. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)(z-1)
&=xz-(x+z)+1\\
&=y^2+d-(b+2)y+1\\
&<y^2+b-(b+2)y+1\\
&=(y-1)^2-b(y-1)\\
&\le(y-1)^2\\
&\le(x-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $x-1>0$, this implies
\[
z<x.
\]
Hence
\[
z+y<x+y,
\]
contradicting the minimality of $(x,y)$.

Therefore
\[
a^2+\left\lceil\frac{4a^2}{b}\right\rceil
\]
is never a perfect square.
