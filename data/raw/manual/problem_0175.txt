ID: 0175

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Mixing Variables, Concavity, Jensen's Inequality

STATEMENT:
Let $a,b,c$ be positive real numbers such that
\[
\min\{ab,bc,ca\}\ge 1.
\]
Prove that
\[
\sqrt[3]{(a^2+1)(b^2+1)(c^2+1)}
\le
\left(\frac{a+b+c}{3}\right)^2+1.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We first prove the following two-variable inequality.

\textbf{Claim.} If $x,y>0$ and $xy\ge1$, then
\[
(x^2+1)(y^2+1)
\le
\left(\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2+1\right)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed,
\[
\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2-1\ge xy-1\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
(x^2+1)(y^2+1)
&=(xy-1)^2+(x+y)^2\\
&\le
\left(\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2-1\right)^2+(x+y)^2\\
&=
\left(\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2+1\right)^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
proving the claim.

Without loss of generality, assume
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]
Then $a\ge1$. Put
\[
d=\frac{a+b+c}{3}.
\]
Since $a^2,ab,ac\ge1$,
\[
ad=\frac{a(a+b+c)}3\ge1.
\]
We may therefore apply (1) to the pairs $(a,d)$ and $(b,c)$:
\[
(a^2+1)(d^2+1)(b^2+1)(c^2+1)
\le
\left(\left(\frac{a+d}{2}\right)^2+1\right)^2
\left(\left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)^2+1\right)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{a+d}{2}\cdot\frac{b+c}{2}
\ge \sqrt{ad}\sqrt{bc}\ge1.
\]
Applying (1) once more, now to
\[
\frac{a+d}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{b+c}{2},
\]
and using $a+b+c=3d$, we obtain from (2)
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(a^2+1)(d^2+1)(b^2+1)(c^2+1)\\
&\qquad\le
\left(\left(\frac{a+b+c+d}{4}\right)^2+1\right)^4
=(d^2+1)^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
After cancelling $d^2+1>0$,
\[
(a^2+1)(b^2+1)(c^2+1)\le(d^2+1)^3.
\]
Taking cube roots gives the desired inequality.

SOLUTION 2:
Define
\[
F(x)=\ln(1+x^2)
\qquad(x>0).
\]
The required inequality is equivalent to
\[
\frac{F(a)+F(b)+F(c)}3
\le
F\left(\frac{a+b+c}{3}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

The two-variable claim from Solution 1 says that whenever $xy\ge1$,
\[
\frac{F(x)+F(y)}2
\le
F\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Assume without loss of generality that $a\ge b\ge c$. Then
\[
a\ge1,
\qquad
\frac{b+c}{2}\ge\sqrt{bc}\ge1.
\]
Applying (2) to $b,c$, we get
\[
\frac{F(a)+F(b)+F(c)}3
\le
\frac{F(a)+2F\left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)}3.
\tag{3}
\]

Now
\[
F''(x)=\frac{2(1-x^2)}{(1+x^2)^2},
\]
so $F$ is concave on $[1,\infty)$. Jensen's inequality applied to the three numbers
\[
a,\quad \frac{b+c}{2},\quad \frac{b+c}{2}
\]
gives
\[
\frac{F(a)+2F\left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)}3
\le
F\left(\frac{a+b+c}{3}\right).
\]
Together with (3), this proves (1).
