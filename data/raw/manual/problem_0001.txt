ID: 0001

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations

STATEMENT:
Determine all functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that
\[
f(\lfloor x\rfloor y)=f(x)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor
\tag{1}
\]
holds for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. Here, $\lfloor x\rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer not exceeding $x$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
f(x)=C,
\]
where either $C=0$ or $1\le C<2$.

First, setting $x=0$ in (1), we get
\[
f(0)=f(0)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor
\tag{2}
\]
for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Now two cases are possible.

\textbf{Case 1.} Assume that $f(0)\ne 0$. Then from (2) we conclude that
\[
\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=1
\]
for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Therefore, equation (1) becomes
\[
f(\lfloor x\rfloor y)=f(x),
\]
and substituting $y=0$ gives
\[
f(x)=f(0)=C\ne 0.
\]
Finally, from $\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=1=\lfloor C\rfloor$, we obtain
\[
1\le C<2.
\]

\textbf{Case 2.} Now suppose that $f(0)=0$. We consider two subcases.

\textbf{Subcase 2a.} Suppose that there exists $0<\alpha<1$ such that $f(\alpha)\ne 0$. Setting $x=\alpha$ in (1), we obtain
\[
0=f(0)=f(\alpha)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor
\]
for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Hence,
\[
\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0
\]
for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Substituting $x=1$ in (1) then gives
\[
f(y)=0
\]
for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$, contradicting $f(\alpha)\ne 0$.

\textbf{Subcase 2b.} Consequently,
\[
f(\alpha)=0
\]
for all $0\le \alpha<1$. Consider any real number $z$. There exists an integer $N$ such that
\[
\alpha=\frac{z}{N}\in[0,1).
\]
For example, one may take $N=\lfloor z\rfloor+1$ if $z\ge 0$, and $N=\lfloor z\rfloor-1$ otherwise. Now, from (1),
\[
f(z)=f(\lfloor N\rfloor\alpha)=f(N)\lfloor f(\alpha)\rfloor=0.
\]
Thus $f(z)=0$ for all $z\in\mathbb{R}$.

A straightforward check shows that all the obtained functions satisfy (1).

SOLUTION 2:
Assume that
\[
\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0
\]
for some $y$. Then substituting $x=1$ into (1) gives
\[
f(y)=f(1)\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0.
\]
Hence, if $\lfloor f(y)\rfloor=0$ for every $y$, then $f(y)=0$ for every $y$. This function clearly satisfies the condition.

We are therefore left with the case in which
\[
\lfloor f(a)\rfloor\ne 0
\]
for some $a$. Then
\[
f(\lfloor x\rfloor a)=f(x)\lfloor f(a)\rfloor,
\]
so
\[
f(x)=\frac{f(\lfloor x\rfloor a)}{\lfloor f(a)\rfloor}.
\tag{3}
\]
This means that $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$ whenever $\lfloor x_1\rfloor=\lfloor x_2\rfloor$. Hence,
\[
f(x)=f(\lfloor x\rfloor),
\]
and we may assume that $a$ is an integer.

Now,
\[
f(a)
=f\left(2a\cdot\frac12\right)
=f(2a)\left\lfloor f\left(\frac12\right)\right\rfloor
=f(2a)\lfloor f(0)\rfloor.
\]
This implies that $\lfloor f(0)\rfloor\ne 0$, so we may take $a=0$. Equation (3) then gives
\[
f(x)=\frac{f(0)}{\lfloor f(0)\rfloor}=C\ne 0
\]
for every $x$. The original condition becomes
\[
C=C\lfloor C\rfloor,
\]
which holds exactly when
\[
\lfloor C\rfloor=1,
\]
i.e.
\[
1\le C<2.
\]

Together with the zero function, these are all the solutions.
