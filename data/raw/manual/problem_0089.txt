ID: 0089

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Recurrence Relations, Symmetric Polynomials, Matrices

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ be a positive integer and let $a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}$ be arbitrary real numbers. Define the sequences $u_0,\ldots,u_n$ and $v_0,\ldots,v_n$ inductively by
\[
u_0=u_1=v_0=v_1=1,
\]
and
\[
u_{k+1}=u_k+a_k u_{k-1},
\qquad
v_{k+1}=v_k+a_{n-k}v_{k-1}
\]
for $k=1,\ldots,n-1$. Prove that
\[
u_n=v_n.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For $k\ge 0$, let $\mathcal I_k$ be the family of all subsets of $\{1,2,\ldots,k-1\}$ containing no two consecutive integers. We claim that
\[
u_k=\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_k}\prod_{i\in I}a_i,
\tag{1}
\]
where the product corresponding to $I=\varnothing$ is $1$.

For $k=0,1$, formula (1) is immediate. Suppose it holds for $u_k$ and $u_{k-1}$. Every set in $\mathcal I_{k+1}$ belongs to exactly one of the following two classes:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(i)}&k\notin I,\quad\text{in which case }I\in\mathcal I_k,\\[2mm]
\text{(ii)}&k\in I,\quad\text{in which case }I\setminus\{k\}\in\mathcal I_{k-1}.
\end{array}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_{k+1}}\prod_{i\in I}a_i
=
\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_k}\prod_{i\in I}a_i
+a_k\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_{k-1}}\prod_{i\in I}a_i
=u_k+a_k u_{k-1}=u_{k+1}.
\]
Thus (1) holds for every $k$.

Applying the same formula to the recurrence for $v_k$, whose coefficients occur in the reverse order, gives
\[
v_n=
\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\\
\text{no two elements of }J\text{ are consecutive}}}
\prod_{j\in J}a_{n-j}.
\]
The map
\[
J\longmapsto \{n-j:j\in J\}
\]
is a bijection from the admissible subsets in this sum to the admissible subsets in (1) for $k=n$, and it preserves the corresponding products. Hence $v_n=u_n$.

SOLUTION 2:
Define multivariate polynomials $P_k$ recursively by
\[
P_0=P_1=1
\]
and, for $k\ge1$,
\[
P_{k+1}(x_1,\ldots,x_k)
=P_k(x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1})
+x_kP_{k-1}(x_1,\ldots,x_{k-2}),
\tag{1}
\]
with empty lists of variables omitted. The recurrences in the problem give
\[
u_n=P_n(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}),
\qquad
v_n=P_n(a_{n-1},\ldots,a_1).
\tag{2}
\]
It remains to prove that $P_n$ is unchanged when its variables are reversed.

We first establish the recurrence from the other end:
\[
P_r(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1})
=P_{r-1}(x_2,\ldots,x_{r-1})
+x_1P_{r-2}(x_3,\ldots,x_{r-1})
\tag{3}
\]
for every $r\ge2$. The cases $r=2,3$ are immediate. For $r\ge4$, use (1) and the induction hypothesis twice:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_r(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1})
={}&P_{r-1}(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-2})
+x_{r-1}P_{r-2}(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-3})\\
={}&P_{r-2}(x_2,\ldots,x_{r-2})
+x_1P_{r-3}(x_3,\ldots,x_{r-2})\\
&+x_{r-1}P_{r-3}(x_2,\ldots,x_{r-3})
+x_1x_{r-1}P_{r-4}(x_3,\ldots,x_{r-3})\\
={}&P_{r-1}(x_2,\ldots,x_{r-1})
+x_1P_{r-2}(x_3,\ldots,x_{r-1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (3).

We now prove by induction on $n$ that
\[
P_n(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})=P_n(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_1).
\tag{4}
\]
The assertion is clear for $n\le2$. For $n\ge3$, formula (3) and the induction hypothesis yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})
&=P_{n-1}(x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1})
+x_1P_{n-2}(x_3,\ldots,x_{n-1})\\
&=P_{n-1}(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_2)
+x_1P_{n-2}(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_3)\\
&=P_n(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_1),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is the defining recurrence (1) applied to the reversed list. Combining (2) and (4) gives $u_n=v_n$.

SOLUTION 3:
For $1\le k\le n-1$, define
\[
A_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+a_k&-a_k\\
a_k&-a_k
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The recurrence for $u_k$ can be written as
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
u_{k+1}\\
u_{k+1}-u_k
\end{pmatrix}
=A_k
\begin{pmatrix}
u_k\\
u_k-u_{k-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]
Similarly, the recurrence for $v_k$ gives the row-vector identity
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
v_{k+1}&v_k-v_{k+1}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
v_k&v_{k-1}-v_k
\end{pmatrix}A_{n-k}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since
\[
\begin{pmatrix}u_1\\u_1-u_0\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}v_1&v_0-v_1\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
iterating (1) and (2) yields
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
u_n\\u_n-u_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
=A_{n-1}A_{n-2}\cdots A_1
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}
\]
and
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
v_n&v_{n-1}-v_n
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}
A_{n-1}A_{n-2}\cdots A_1.
\]
Therefore both $u_n$ and $v_n$ are equal to the upper-left entry of the matrix product $A_{n-1}\cdots A_1$. Hence $u_n=v_n$.
