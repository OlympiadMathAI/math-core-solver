ID: 0407

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Online Strategies, Majorization, Invariants, Adversarial Processes

STATEMENT:
Elisa has $2023$ treasure chests, all initially unlocked and empty. Each day, Elisa puts one new gem into an unlocked chest of her choice. Afterwards, a fairy acts as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item if more than one chest is unlocked, the fairy locks one unlocked chest;
\item if exactly one chest is unlocked, the fairy unlocks all the chests.
\end{itemize}

The process continues forever. Prove that there is a constant $C$ such that Elisa can ensure that the numbers of gems in any two chests always differ by at most $C$, regardless of the fairy's choices.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove the stronger statement for every odd number $n$ of chests. Elisa can take
\[
C=n-1.
\]
For the problem, this gives
\[
C=2022.
\]

Elisa always places the new gem into an unlocked chest having the fewest gems.

At the end of day $t$, write the chest contents in nondecreasing order:
\[
a_1^t\le a_2^t\le\cdots\le a_n^t.
\]
Thus
\[
a_1^0=\cdots=a_n^0=0,
\qquad
a_1^t+\cdots+a_n^t=t.
\]

Since one gem is added each day, there is a unique index $m=m(t)$ such that the sorted vector at time $t+1$ is obtained from the sorted vector at time $t$ by increasing the $m$th entry by $1$. We use two observations.

First,
\[
a_j^t>a_m^t
\qquad(j>m).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, after the increment, the new value $a_m^t+1$ still occurs no later than position $m$, while the later entries are unchanged.

Second, let
\[
r\equiv t\pmod n,
\qquad
0\le r<n.
\]
Exactly $r$ chests are locked at the end of day $t$. If $j>r$, then among the $j$ chests with the fewest gems, at least one is unlocked. Elisa chooses a minimum among all unlocked chests, and hence
\[
a_j^t\ge a_m^t
\qquad(j>r).
\tag{2}
\]

We compare $(a_i^t)$ with an explicit sequence. Since $n$ is odd, define
\[
b_i^0=i-\frac{n+1}{2}
\qquad(1\le i\le n),
\]
and for $t\ge0$ let
\[
b_i^t
=
b_i^0+
\left\lfloor\frac{t+n-i}{n}\right\rfloor.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, when $t$ changes to $t+1$, precisely the coordinate
\[
i\equiv t+1\pmod n,
\qquad1\le i\le n,
\]
increases by $1$. We have
\[
b_1^t\le\cdots\le b_n^t,
\qquad
b_1^t+\cdots+b_n^t=t,
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
b_i^t<b_{i+1}^t
\quad\text{unless}\quad
t\equiv i\pmod n.
\tag{5}
\]

For nondecreasing sequences $x_1\le\cdots\le x_n$ and
$y_1\le\cdots\le y_n$ with equal total sum, say that $x$ majorizes $y$ if
\[
x_1+\cdots+x_k\le y_1+\cdots+y_k
\qquad(1\le k<n).
\]

We claim that
\[
(b_1^t,\ldots,b_n^t)
\quad\text{majorizes}\quad
(a_1^t,\ldots,a_n^t)
\tag{6}
\]
for every $t$.

For $t=0$,
\[
b_1^0+\cdots+b_k^0
=
\frac{k(k-n)}2
\le0
=
a_1^0+\cdots+a_k^0,
\]
so the claim holds.

Assume it holds at time $t$ and suppose it fails at time $t+1$ for some $k<n$. Let
\[
j\equiv t+1\pmod n,
\qquad1\le j\le n.
\]
For a partial-sum inequality to change from true to false, all of the following must hold:

\[
b_1^t+\cdots+b_k^t
=
a_1^t+\cdots+a_k^t,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
j\le k,
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
m(t)>k.
\tag{9}
\]

From (7) and the majorization inequality at $k-1$,
\[
b_k^t\ge a_k^t.
\tag{10}
\]
Condition (8) says that $r=t\bmod n$ satisfies $r<k$, so (2) gives
\[
a_k^t\ge a_m^t.
\]
Together with (9) and the sorted order, this forces
\[
a_k^t=a_{k+1}^t=\cdots=a_m^t.
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover, $r\ne k$, so (5) gives
\[
b_k^t<b_{k+1}^t.
\]
Using (7), (10), and (11),
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_1^t+\cdots+b_{k+1}^t
&>
a_1^t+\cdots+a_k^t+a_{k+1}^t\\
&=
a_1^t+\cdots+a_{k+1}^t,
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting the induction hypothesis. Thus (6) holds for all $t$.

Taking $k=1$ in (6) gives
\[
b_1^t\le a_1^t.
\]
Taking $k=n-1$ and using equality of total sums gives
\[
a_n^t\le b_n^t.
\]
Therefore
\[
a_n^t-a_1^t
\le
b_n^t-b_1^t
\le
b_n^0-b_1^0
=
n-1.
\]
Hence Elisa's strategy keeps the difference between any two chests at most $n-1$ forever.

SOLUTION 2:
We give another strategy that works for any positive integer $n$ of chests.

Divide time into blocks of $n$ days. At the beginning of day $nt+1$, label the chests
\[
C_1^t,C_2^t,\ldots,C_n^t
\]
so that their numbers of gems satisfy
\[
c_1^t\le c_2^t\le\cdots\le c_n^t.
\]
During the next $n$ days, Elisa always adds a gem to the unlocked chest $C_i^t$ with the smallest possible index $i$.

Let $\delta_i^t$ be the number of gems added to $C_i^t$ during this block. Then
\[
\delta_1^t+\cdots+\delta_n^t=n.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\delta_1^t+\cdots+\delta_k^t\ge k
\qquad(1\le k\le n).
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, before the additions in one complete block, the number of locked chests successively takes the values
\[
0,1,\ldots,n-1.
\]
On each of the $k$ days when fewer than $k$ chests are locked, at least one of
\[
C_1^t,\ldots,C_k^t
\]
is unlocked, and Elisa chooses among these first $k$ chests.

At the next block boundary, the sorted sequence
\[
(c_i^{t+1})
\]
is a permutation of
\[
c_1^t+\delta_1^t,\ldots,c_n^t+\delta_n^t.
\tag{3}
\]

Define a comparison sequence
\[
d_i^0=3n\left(i-\frac{n+1}{2}\right),
\qquad
d_i^t=d_i^0+t.
\tag{4}
\]
Both $(c_i^t)$ and $(d_i^t)$ have total sum $nt$.

We claim that, in the ascending majorization convention,
\[
d_1^t+\cdots+d_k^t
\le
c_1^t+\cdots+c_k^t
\qquad(1\le k<n)
\tag{5}
\]
for every $t$. We interpret a partial sum with zero terms as $0$; for $k=n$, the two total sums are equal. The claim is clear for $t=0$.

Assume (5) at time $t$, and fix $k<n$. Write
\[
x_i=c_i^t+\delta_i^t.
\]

If the $k$ smallest numbers among the $x_i$ are exactly
\[
x_1,\ldots,x_k,
\]
then by (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1^{t+1}+\cdots+c_k^{t+1}
&=
c_1^t+\cdots+c_k^t
+\delta_1^t+\cdots+\delta_k^t\\
&\ge
d_1^t+\cdots+d_k^t+k\\
&=
d_1^{t+1}+\cdots+d_k^{t+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Otherwise, there are indices
\[
i\le k<j
\]
such that
\[
x_i>x_j.
\]
Since every $\delta_i^t\le n$,
\[
c_{k+1}^t-c_k^t<n.
\tag{6}
\]
Adding nonnegative quantities to the coordinates and then sorting cannot decrease any order statistic, so
\[
c_1^{t+1}+\cdots+c_k^{t+1}
\ge
c_1^t+\cdots+c_k^t.
\]
Using (6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1^t+\cdots+c_k^t
&>
c_1^t+\cdots+c_{k-1}^t
+\frac{c_k^t+c_{k+1}^t}{2}
-\frac n2\\
&=
\frac12\left(c_1^t+\cdots+c_{k-1}^t\right)
+
\frac12\left(c_1^t+\cdots+c_{k+1}^t\right)
-\frac n2\\
&\ge
\frac12\left(d_1^t+\cdots+d_{k-1}^t\right)
+
\frac12\left(d_1^t+\cdots+d_{k+1}^t\right)
-\frac n2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consecutive terms of $(d_i^t)$ differ by $3n$, so the last expression equals
\[
d_1^t+\cdots+d_k^t+n
\ge
d_1^t+\cdots+d_k^t+k
=
d_1^{t+1}+\cdots+d_k^{t+1}.
\]
This proves (5) by induction.

As in the first solution, (5) implies
\[
c_n^t-c_1^t
\le
d_n^t-d_1^t
=
3n(n-1)
\tag{7}
\]
at every block boundary. During a block of $n$ days, any one chest gains at most $n$ gems, while no chest loses gems. Hence at every time the difference between the fullest and emptiest chest is at most
\[
3n(n-1)+n.
\]
For $n=2023$, this is a fixed constant independent of the fairy's play, completing the proof.
