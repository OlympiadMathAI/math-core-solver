ID: 0410

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Area Ratios, Similar Triangles, Spiral Similarity

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDE$ be a convex pentagon such that
\[
\angle ABC=\angle AED=90^\circ.
\]
Suppose that the midpoint of $CD$ is the circumcentre of triangle $ABE$. Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $ACD$.

Prove that line $AO$ passes through the midpoint of segment $BE$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
M
\]
be the midpoint of $CD$, and put
\[
X=BC\cap ED.
\]
Since
\[
\angle ABX=\angle AEX=90^\circ,
\]
the segment $AX$ is a diameter of the circumcircle of triangle $ABE$. Its midpoint is therefore the circumcentre of triangle $ABE$, namely $M$. Hence $M$ is the midpoint of both $AX$ and $CD$, so
\[
ACXD
\]
is a parallelogram. In particular,
\[
AD\parallel BC,
\qquad
AC\parallel ED.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $N=AO\cap BE$. It is enough to show that $N$ is the midpoint of $BE$.

Let $C_1$ and $D_1$ be the midpoints of $AC$ and $AD$, respectively. Since $O$ is the circumcentre of triangle $ACD$,
\[
OC_1\perp AC,
\qquad
OD_1\perp AD.
\]
Using (1) together with
\[
AB\perp BC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AE\perp ED,
\]
we obtain
\[
OD_1\parallel AB,
\qquad
OC_1\parallel AE.
\tag{2}
\]

Write $[UVW]$ for the ordinary area of triangle $UVW$. From the first parallelism in (2),
\[
[OAB]=[D_1AB].
\]
The triangles $D_1AB$ and $D_1AC$ have equal areas, because $B$ and $C$ lie on the line $BC\parallel AD_1$. Moreover, $D_1$ is the midpoint of $AD$. Consequently,
\[
[OAB]
=
[D_1AB]
=
[D_1AC]
=
\frac12[ACD].
\tag{3}
\]

Similarly,
\[
[OAE]
=
[C_1AE]
=
[C_1AD]
=
\frac12[ACD].
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)-(4) give
\[
[OAB]=[OAE].
\]
The points $B$ and $E$ lie on opposite sides of $AO$ in the convex configuration. Thus their perpendicular distances from $AO$ are equal, and line $AO$ bisects $BE$. Therefore
\[
N
\]
is the midpoint of $BE$.

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
M
\]
be the midpoint of $CD$, and let
\[
X=BC\cap ED.
\]
The right angles
\[
\angle ABX=\angle AEX=90^\circ
\]
show that $AX$ is a diameter of the circumcircle of triangle $ABE$. Since $M$ is its centre, $M$ is the midpoint of $AX$. As $M$ is also the midpoint of $CD$, the quadrilateral $ACXD$ is a parallelogram. Hence
\[
AD\parallel BC,
\qquad
AC\parallel ED.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $N$ be the midpoint of $BE$. We prove that
\[
A,N,O
\]
are collinear.

The quadrilateral $ABXE$ is cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle ABE=\angle AXE=\angle XAC
\]
and
\[
\angle BEA=\angle BXA=\angle CXA.
\]
It follows that
\[
\triangle ABE\sim\triangle CAX.
\]
Under this similarity, the side $BE$ corresponds to $AX$. Hence their midpoints $N$ and $M$ correspond, and
\[
\angle NAB=\angle ACM.
\tag{2}
\]

Because $AD\parallel BC$ and $AB\perp BC$,
\[
\angle DAB=90^\circ.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle OAB=90^\circ-\angle DAO.
\tag{3}
\]
In the isosceles triangle $AOD$,
\[
OA=OD.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle AOD=2\angle ACD,
\]
so
\[
\angle DAO=90^\circ-\angle ACD.
\]
Substituting into (3),
\[
\angle OAB=\angle ACD=\angle ACM.
\]
Together with (2), this gives
\[
\angle OAB=\angle NAB,
\]
and therefore $A,N,O$ are collinear.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $N$ be the midpoint of $BE$. Let $F$ and $G$ be the perpendicular projections of $C$ and $D$ onto $AD$ and $AC$, respectively.

Since
\[
\angle CFD=\angle CGD=90^\circ,
\]
the points
\[
C,G,F,D
\]
are concyclic. Hence
\[
\angle AGF
=
\angle CDF
=
\angle CDA.
\]
Because $O$ is the circumcentre of triangle $ACD$,
\[
\angle CDA=90^\circ-\angle OAC.
\]
Therefore
\[
AO\perp FG.
\tag{1}
\]

It remains to prove
\[
AN\perp FG.
\tag{2}
\]

As in the elementary construction below, let
\[
X=BC\cap ED.
\]
The segment $AX$ is a diameter of the circumcircle of triangle $ABE$, and the given midpoint of $CD$ is the midpoint of $AX$. Thus
\[
AD\parallel BC,
\qquad
AC\parallel ED.
\tag{3}
\]
It follows that
\[
AEDG
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AFCB
\]
are rectangles.

The rectangles give
\[
\angle AGE=\angle DAG=\angle FAC=\angle BFA.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle GAE\sim\triangle FAB.
\tag{4}
\]

Let $F'$ be the reflection of $F$ in $A$. Since reversing the ray $AF$ changes both corresponding orientations, (4) shows that there is a direct spiral similarity centred at $A$ which sends
\[
F'\longmapsto G,
\qquad
B\longmapsto E.
\tag{5}
\]

Let $P$ be the midpoint of $F'G$. With $A$ as the origin, write the complex multiplier of the similarity in (5) as $\lambda$. Then
\[
G=\lambda F',
\qquad
E=\lambda B,
\]
while
\[
P=\frac{F'+G}{2}
=\frac{1+\lambda}{2}F',
\qquad
N=\frac{B+E}{2}
=\frac{1+\lambda}{2}B.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\triangle APN\sim\triangle AGE.
\]
Since $AEDG$ is a rectangle,
\[
\angle GAE=90^\circ,
\]
and therefore
\[
\angle PAN=90^\circ.
\tag{6}
\]

Finally, $A$ is the midpoint of $FF'$, while $P$ is the midpoint of $F'G$. In triangle $F'FG$, the midpoint theorem gives
\[
AP\parallel FG.
\]
Together with (6), this proves (2). Combining (1) and (2), the lines $AO$ and $AN$ coincide. Hence $AO$ passes through the midpoint $N$ of $BE$.
