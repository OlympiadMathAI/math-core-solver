ID: 0256

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2018

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Directed Angles, Spiral Similarity, Concurrency

STATEMENT:
A point $T$ is chosen inside a triangle $A B C$. Let $A_{1}, B_{1}$, and $C_{1}$ be the
reflections of $T$ in $B C, C A$, and $A B$, respectively. Let $\Omega$ be the circumcircle
of the triangle $A_{1} B_{1} C_{1}$. The lines $A_{1} T, B_{1} T$, and $C_{1} T$ meet
$\Omega$ again at $A_{2}, B_{2}$, and $C_{2}$, respectively. Prove that the lines $A A_{2},
B B_{2}$, and $C C_{2}$ are concurrent on $\Omega$.

SOLUTION 1:
By $\measuredangle(\ell, n)$ we always mean the directed angle of the lines $\ell$ and $n$, taken
modulo $180^{\circ}$.

Let $C C_{2}$ meet $\Omega$ again at $K$ (as usual, if $C C_{2}$ is tangent to $\Omega$, we
set $K=C_{2}$ ). We show that the line $B B_{2}$ contains $K$; similarly, $A A_{2}$ will
also pass through $K$. For this purpose, it suffices to prove that

\[
\measuredangle\left(C_{2} C, C_{2} A_{1}\right)=\measuredangle\left(B_{2} B, B_{2} A_{1}\right) .
\tag{1}
\]

By the problem condition, $C B$ and $C A$ are the perpendicular bisectors of $T A_{1}$ and
$T B_{1}$, respectively. Hence, $C$ is the circumcentre of the triangle $A_{1} T B_{1}$.
Therefore,

\[
\measuredangle\left(C A_{1}, C B\right)=\measuredangle(C B, C T)=\measuredangle\left(B_{1} A_{1}, B_{1} T\right)=\measuredangle\left(B_{1} A_{1}, B_{1} B_{2}\right) .
\]

In circle $\Omega$ we have $\measuredangle\left(B_{1} A_{1}, B_{1}
B_{2}\right)=\measuredangle\left(C_{2} A_{1}, C_{2} B_{2}\right)$. Thus,

\[
\measuredangle\left(C A_{1}, C B\right)=\measuredangle\left(B_{1} A_{1}, B_{1} B_{2}\right)=\measuredangle\left(C_{2} A_{1}, C_{2} B_{2}\right) .
\tag{2}
\]

Similarly, we get

\[
\measuredangle\left(B A_{1}, B C\right)=\measuredangle\left(C_{1} A_{1}, C_{1} C_{2}\right)=\measuredangle\left(B_{2} A_{1}, B_{2} C_{2}\right) .
\tag{3}
\]

The two obtained relations yield that the triangles $A_{1} B C$ and $A_{1} B_{2} C_{2}$ are
similar and equioriented, hence

\[
\frac{A_{1} B_{2}}{A_{1} B}=\frac{A_{1} C_{2}}{A_{1} C} \quad \text { and } \quad \measuredangle\left(A_{1} B, A_{1} C\right)=\measuredangle\left(A_{1} B_{2}, A_{1} C_{2}\right)
\]

The second equality may be rewritten as $\measuredangle\left(A_{1} B, A_{1}
B_{2}\right)=\measuredangle\left(A_{1} C, A_{1} C_{2}\right)$, so the triangles $A_{1} B B_{2}$
and $A_{1} C C_{2}$ are also similar and equioriented. This establishes (1).
