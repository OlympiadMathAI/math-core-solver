ID: 0353

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Isogonal Conjugates, Inversion, Radical Axes, Circumcentres, Directed Angles

STATEMENT:
Let $D$ be a point inside an acute triangle $ABC$ with
\[
AB>AC
\]
and
\[
\angle BAD=\angle DAC.
\]
A point $E$ is chosen on segment $AC$ so that
\[
\angle ADE=\angle DCB.
\]
Similarly, a point $F$ is chosen on segment $AB$ so that
\[
\angle ADF=\angle DBC.
\]
A point $X$ is chosen on line $AC$ so that
\[
CX=BX.
\]

Let $O_1$ and $O_2$ be the circumcentres of triangles $ADC$ and $DXE$, respectively. Prove that the lines
\[
BC,\qquad EF,\qquad O_1O_2
\]
are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$.

Let $Q$ be the isogonal conjugate of $D$ with respect to triangle $ABC$. Since $D$ lies on the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$, the point $Q$ also lies on $AD$.

By isogonality and the defining condition for $F$,
\[
\angle QBA=\angle DBC=\angle ADF.
\]
As $A,B,F$ are collinear and $A,D,Q$ are collinear, this gives
\[
Q,D,F,B
\]
concyclic. Similarly,
\[
Q,D,E,C
\]
are concyclic. Taking powers from $A$,
\[
AF\cdot AB=AD\cdot AQ=AE\cdot AC,
\]
so
\[
B,C,E,F
\]
are concyclic.

Put
\[
T=BC\cap EF.
\]

We need the following claim:
\[
TD^2=TB\cdot TC=TE\cdot TF.
\tag{1}
\]

To prove it, observe first that the circles
\[
(DEF)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(DBC)
\]
are tangent at $D$. Indeed, an angle chase gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BDF
&=\angle AFD-\angle ABD\\
&=180^\circ-\angle FAD-\angle ADF
  -(\angle ABC-\angle DBC)\\
&=180^\circ-\angle FAD-\angle ABC\\
&=180^\circ-\angle DAE-\angle FEA\\
&=\angle FED+\angle ADE\\
&=\angle FED+\angle DCB.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly the equality of the tangent directions at $D$.

Since $B,C,E,F$ are concyclic,
\[
TB\cdot TC=TE\cdot TF.
\]
Thus $T$ has equal powers with respect to the two tangent circles above. Their radical axis is their common tangent at $D$, so $T$ lies on that tangent and (1) follows.

Let $TA$ meet the circumcircle $(ABC)$ again at $M$. By (1),
\[
TM\cdot TA=TB\cdot TC=TE\cdot TF=TD^2.
\tag{2}
\]
The equality
\[
TM\cdot TA=TE\cdot TF
\]
also shows that
\[
A,M,E,F
\]
are concyclic.

Perform the inversion $\iota$ centred at $T$ with radius $TD$. Equation (2) shows that
\[
\iota(M)=A,\qquad \iota(B)=C,
\]
while $D$ is fixed. Hence $\iota$ maps the circle $(MBD)$ to the circle $(ACD)$.

Let $K\ne D$ be the second intersection of these two circles. Since the inversion swaps the two circles and fixes their common point $D$, it also fixes $K$. Therefore
\[
TK=TD.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus $T$ and the circumcentre $O_1$ of $(ADCK)$ both lie on the perpendicular bisector of $DK$.

It remains to prove that
\[
D,K,E,X
\]
are concyclic, because then $O_2$ also lies on that same perpendicular bisector.

The lines
\[
BM,\qquad DK,\qquad AC
\]
are the pairwise radical axes of the circles
\[
(ABCM),\qquad (ACDK),\qquad (BMDK).
\]
They are therefore concurrent; call their common point $P$.

We next show that
\[
M,E,X,B
\]
are concyclic. Since $E,C,X$ are collinear and $CX=BX$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(EX,XB)
&=\measuredangle(CX,XB)\\
&=\measuredangle(XC,CB)+\measuredangle(BC,BX)\\
&=2\measuredangle(AC,CB).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Because $B,C,E,F$ are concyclic and $A,B,F$ are collinear,
\[
\measuredangle(EF,FA)=\measuredangle(AC,CB).
\tag{5}
\]
Furthermore, from the circles $(ABCM)$ and $(AEFM)$,
\[
\measuredangle(AM,BM)=\measuredangle(AC,CB)
\]
and
\[
\measuredangle(EM,MA)=\measuredangle(EF,FA).
\]
Combining these equalities with (4)--(5),
\[
\measuredangle(EX,XB)=\measuredangle(EM,BM),
\]
which proves the cyclicity of $M,E,X,B$.

Taking powers from $P$,
\[
PE\cdot PX
=
PM\cdot PB
=
PK\cdot PD.
\]
Hence
\[
D,K,E,X
\]
are concyclic.

By (3), the points
\[
T,\ O_1,\ O_2
\]
all lie on the perpendicular bisector of $DK$. Therefore
\[
T\in O_1O_2.
\]
Since $T=BC\cap EF$, the three required lines are concurrent.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $Q$ be the isogonal conjugate of $D$ with respect to triangle $ABC$. Because $D$ lies on the angle bisector of $\angle BAC$, the point $Q$ lies on $AD$. Isogonality and the definitions of $E,F$ give
\[
Q,D,F,B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Q,D,E,C
\]
cyclic. Hence
\[
AF\cdot AB=AD\cdot AQ=AE\cdot AC,
\]
so
\[
B,C,E,F
\]
are cyclic. Put
\[
T=BC\cap EF.
\]

Let the circle $(CDE)$ meet $BC$ again at $E_1$. Since
\[
\angle E_1CQ=\angle DCE,
\]
the arcs $DE$ and $QE_1$ of the circle $(CDQE)$ are equal. It follows that
\[
DQ\parallel EE_1.
\tag{1}
\]
The cyclicity of $B,C,E,F$ shows that $AD$ forms equal angles with the lines $BC$ and $EF$. By (1), the same is true of $EE_1$. Hence triangle $EE_1T$ is isosceles:
\[
TE=TE_1.
\]
Thus $T$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $EE_1$. Since $EE_1$ and $DQ$ are parallel chords of the same circle $(CDQE)$, their perpendicular bisectors coincide. Therefore $T$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $DQ$.

Let
\[
U=\text{centre of }(ADE),
\qquad
V=\text{centre of }(CDQE).
\]
The points
\[
U,V,O_2
\]
all lie on the perpendicular bisector of $DE$. Also,
\[
UO_1\perp AD
\]
because $AD$ is the common chord of $(ADE)$ and $(ADC)$, while
\[
VT\perp DQ.
\]
Since $D,Q,A$ are collinear,
\[
UO_1\parallel VT.
\tag{2}
\]

We prove
\[
\frac{O_2U}{O_2V}
=
\frac{O_1U}{TV}.
\tag{3}
\]
Together with the collinearity of $U,O_2,V$ and the parallelism (2), this makes the triangles
\[
O_2UO_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
O_2VT
\]
similar, and hence gives
\[
T,O_1,O_2
\]
collinear.

Denote the angles of triangle $ABC$ by
\[
\alpha=\angle A,\qquad
\beta=\angle B,\qquad
\gamma=\angle C.
\]

Use directed lengths in the following projection computations. Project the collinear points $O_2,U,V$ orthogonally onto $AC$. Since the projections of circle centres onto chords are the corresponding chord midpoints,
\[
\frac{O_2U}{O_2V}
=
\frac{(XE-AE)/2}{(XE+EC)/2}
=
\frac{AX}{CX}.
\]
Because $CX=BX$, the sine rule in triangle $ABX$ gives
\[
\frac{AX}{CX}
=
\frac{AX}{BX}
=
\frac{\sin(\gamma-\beta)}{\sin\alpha}.
\tag{4}
\]

The projection of $O_1U$ onto $AC$ has length
\[
\frac{EC}{2}.
\]
The angle between $O_1U$ and $AC$ is
\[
90^\circ-\frac{\alpha}{2},
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{O_1U}{EC}
=
\frac{1}{2\sin(\alpha/2)}.
\tag{5}
\]

We next note that
\[
E,V,C,T
\]
are concyclic. The line $TV$ is the common perpendicular bisector of the parallel chords $EE_1$ and $DQ$. Since $EE_1$ forms equal angles with $TC=BC$ and $TF=EF$, its perpendicular $TV$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle CTF$. The point $V$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $CE$. Hence $V$ is the midpoint of the corresponding arc $CE$ of the circle $(TCE)$.

The relevant angles are
\[
\angle EVC=180^\circ-(\gamma-\beta)
\]
and
\[
\angle VET=90^\circ-\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\]
The sine rule in the cyclic quadrilateral gives
\[
\frac{EC}{TV}
=
\frac{\sin(\gamma-\beta)}{\cos(\alpha/2)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Multiplying (5) and (6),
\[
\frac{O_1U}{TV}
=
\frac{1}{2\sin(\alpha/2)}
\cdot
\frac{\sin(\gamma-\beta)}{\cos(\alpha/2)}
=
\frac{\sin(\gamma-\beta)}{\sin\alpha}.
\]
Together with (4), this proves (3). Hence
\[
T\in O_1O_2,
\]
and the desired concurrency follows.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $Q$ be the isogonal conjugate of $D$ with respect to triangle $ABC$. Then $Q\in AD$, and the angle conditions give
\[
Q,D,F,B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Q,D,E,C
\]
cyclic. It follows from powers at $A$ that
\[
B,C,E,F
\]
are cyclic.

Put
\[
T=BC\cap EF.
\]
An angle chase shows that the circles $(DEF)$ and $(DBC)$ are tangent at $D$. Since
\[
TB\cdot TC=TE\cdot TF,
\]
the point $T$ lies on their common tangent at $D$. Therefore
\[
TD^2=TB\cdot TC=TE\cdot TF.
\tag{1}
\]

If $D$ is replaced by $Q$ in the construction, the points $E,F,T$ remain unchanged, because
\[
\angle AQE=\angle QCB
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle AQF=\angle QBC.
\]
Applying (1) to $Q$ gives
\[
TQ^2=TB\cdot TC=TD^2.
\]
Thus there is a circle $\Gamma$ centred at $T$ and passing through $D$ and $Q$.

Let $K\ne D$ be the second intersection of $\Gamma$ and the circle $(ADC)$. Let the circle $(DEK)$ meet $AC$ again at $Y$. We prove that
\[
Y=X.
\tag{2}
\]
Once this is done, the points $D,K,E,X$ are concyclic. Moreover,
\[
TD=TK,
\]
so $T$, $O_1$, and $O_2$ all lie on the perpendicular bisector of $DK$.

Let
\[
L=AD\cap BC.
\]
Invert about a circle centred at $C$, and denote images by primes.

The images $A',D',Q',L'$ lie with $C$ on the circle
\[
\Omega=(A'L'C),
\]
and the angle conditions give
\[
A'L'\parallel D'Q'.
\tag{3}
\]
The point
\[
E'=A'C\cap D'Q'
\]
is the image of $E$.

The image $\Gamma'$ of $\Gamma$ is a circle through $D'$ and $Q'$ whose centre lies on $CL'$. We claim that its centre is $B'$. Indeed,
\[
\angle A'B'C
=
\angle BAC
=
2\angle LAC
=
2\angle A'L'C,
\]
so
\[
B'A'=B'L'.
\]
Thus $B'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $A'L'$. Because the two chords in (3) are parallel chords of $\Omega$, this is also the perpendicular bisector of $D'Q'$. Hence $B'$ is the centre of $\Gamma'$.

The point $K'$ is the second intersection of $A'D'$ with $\Gamma'$. Let $Y'$ be the second intersection of $A'E'$ with the circle $(D'K'E')$.

We have
\[
\measuredangle(Y'K',K'A')
=
\measuredangle(Y'E',E'D')
=
\measuredangle(Y'A',A'L').
\]
Therefore $A'L'$ is tangent at $A'$ to the circle
\[
\omega=(Y'A'K').
\]

Let $O$ and $O^*$ be the centres of $\Omega$ and $\omega$, respectively. Both vectors
\[
\overrightarrow{O^*A'}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\overrightarrow{B'O}
\]
are perpendicular to $A'L'$, hence parallel.

Project these vectors onto the line $K'D'=A'D'$. The projection of $\overrightarrow{O^*A'}$ is
\[
\frac12\overrightarrow{K'A'},
\]
because the projection of $O^*$ onto the chord $A'K'$ is its midpoint. The projection of $\overrightarrow{B'O}$ is also
\[
\frac12\overrightarrow{K'A'},
\]
because the projections of $B'$ and $O$ are the midpoints of $D'K'$ and $D'A'$, respectively. Thus
\[
\overrightarrow{O^*A'}=\overrightarrow{B'O},
\]
or equivalently,
\[
\overrightarrow{A'O}=\overrightarrow{O^*B'}.
\tag{4}
\]

Project (4) onto $A'C$. The projection of $O$ is the midpoint of $A'C$, and the projection of $O^*$ is the midpoint of $A'Y'$. Hence the projection of $B'$ onto $A'C$ is the midpoint of $CY'$. It follows that
\[
B'C=B'Y',
\]
so
\[
\angle B'Y'C=\angle B'CY'.
\]
After inverting back, this becomes
\[
\angle CBY=\angle BCY.
\]
Therefore
\[
BY=CY.
\]
Since $Y\in AC$, the defining property of $X$ gives $Y=X$, proving (2).

Hence the perpendicular bisector of $DK$ contains
\[
T,\ O_1,\ O_2.
\]
Thus $BC$, $EF$, and $O_1O_2$ are concurrent at $T$.
