ID: 0398

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality, AM-GM Inequality, Integer Sequences, Gap Estimates

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{2023}
\]
be distinct positive real numbers. For
\[
n=1,2,\ldots,2023,
\]
define
\[
a_n=
\sqrt{
(x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n)
\left(
\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}+\cdots+\frac1{x_n}
\right)
}.
\]
Assume that every $a_n$ is an integer.

Prove that
\[
a_{2023}\ge3034.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
S_n=x_1+\cdots+x_n,
\qquad
T_n=\frac1{x_1}+\cdots+\frac1{x_n}.
\]
Then
\[
a_n^2=S_nT_n.
\]

For every $n<2023$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1}^2
&=(S_n+x_{n+1})
\left(T_n+\frac1{x_{n+1}}\right)\\
&=
a_n^2+1+\frac{S_n}{x_{n+1}}+x_{n+1}T_n\\
&\ge
a_n^2+1+2\sqrt{S_nT_n}\\
&=(a_n+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus
\[
a_{n+1}\ge a_n+1.
\]
Also,
\[
a_1=1,
\]
while Cauchy-Schwarz and $x_1\ne x_2$ give
\[
a_2>2,
\]
so
\[
a_2\ge3.
\tag{2}
\]

We claim that two consecutive increments cannot both equal $1$. Suppose
\[
a_{n+1}=a_n+1.
\]
Equality must then hold in the AM-GM step in (1), so
\[
\frac{S_n}{x_{n+1}}=x_{n+1}T_n.
\tag{3}
\]
If also
\[
a_{n+2}=a_{n+1}+1,
\]
the analogous equality gives
\[
\frac{S_n+x_{n+1}}{x_{n+2}}
=
x_{n+2}
\left(T_n+\frac1{x_{n+1}}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Using (3), both sides of (4) contain the common positive factor
\[
x_{n+1}T_n+1,
\]
and (4) reduces to
\[
\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{n+2}}
=
\frac{x_{n+2}}{x_{n+1}}.
\]
Hence
\[
x_{n+1}=x_{n+2},
\]
contrary to distinctness.

Therefore, among every two consecutive increments
\[
a_{j+1}-a_j,
\]
at least one is at least $2$, and the first increment is already at least $2$ by (2). Pairing the $2022$ increments gives
\[
a_{2023}
\ge
1+1011(2+1)
=
3034.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
With
\[
S_n=x_1+\cdots+x_n,
\qquad
T_n=\frac1{x_1}+\cdots+\frac1{x_n},
\]
we have
\[
a_n^2=S_nT_n.
\]

Fix $n\le2021$, and put
\[
u=x_{n+1},
\qquad
v=x_{n+2}.
\]
Expanding,
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+2}^2
&=
a_n^2
+
(u+v)\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)
+
S_n\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)
+
(u+v)T_n\\
&\ge
a_n^2
+
(u+v)\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)\\
&\quad+
2a_n
\sqrt{
(u+v)\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)
}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
a_{n+2}
\ge
a_n+
\sqrt{
(u+v)\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)
}.
\tag{1}
\]
Since $u\ne v$,
\[
(u+v)\left(\frac1u+\frac1v\right)>4.
\]
Therefore
\[
a_{n+2}>a_n+2.
\]
Both sides are integers, so
\[
a_{n+2}\ge a_n+3.
\tag{2}
\]

Starting from $a_1=1$ and applying (2) to the odd-indexed terms,
\[
a_{2k+1}\ge1+3k.
\]
Taking $k=1011$ gives
\[
a_{2023}\ge1+3\cdot1011=3034.
\]
