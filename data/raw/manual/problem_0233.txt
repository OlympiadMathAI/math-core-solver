ID: 0233

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Sums, Prime Moduli, Pigeonhole Principle, Counterexamples

STATEMENT:
Determine all integers $n\ge2$ with the following property: for any integers
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n
\]
whose sum is not divisible by $n$, there exists an index $1\le i\le n$ such that none of the numbers
\[
a_i,\quad a_i+a_{i+1},\quad\ldots,\quad a_i+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+n-1}
\]
is divisible by $n$. Indices are read cyclically, so $a_i=a_{i-n}$ when $i>n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The required integers are exactly the primes:
\[
\boxed{n\text{ is prime}}.
\]

Suppose first that $n$ is composite. Write
\[
n=ab,
\qquad a,b\ge2,
\]
and set
\[
a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_{n-1}=a,
\qquad
a_n=0.
\]
The total sum is
\[
a(n-1),
\]
which is not divisible by $n=ab$.

Nevertheless, starting from any index, there is a cyclic block with sum $n$. If the starting index is at most $n-b$, take the next $b$ terms. Otherwise, take the next $b+1$ terms. In either case the chosen block contains exactly $b$ copies of $a$ and possibly the single zero $a_n$, so its sum is
\[
ab=n.
\]
Thus no composite $n$ has the property.

Now let $n$ be prime. Suppose, for contradiction, that the property fails. Then there are integers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ with
\[
A=a_1+\cdots+a_n\not\equiv0\pmod n
\tag{1}
\]
such that from every starting index there is a nonempty cyclic block of length at most $n-1$ whose sum is divisible by $n$.

Construct integers
\[
0=i_0<i_1<\cdots<i_n
\]
recursively. Having chosen $i_s$, start at the cyclic index $i_s+1$ and choose
\[
1\le i_{s+1}-i_s\le n-1
\]
so that
\[
a_{i_s+1}+a_{i_s+2}+\cdots+a_{i_{s+1}}
\equiv0\pmod n.
\tag{2}
\]

Among the $n+1$ integers $i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_n$, two are congruent modulo $n$. Choose
\[
0\le r<s\le n
\]
with
\[
i_s\equiv i_r\pmod n.
\]
Summing (2) for $r\le j<s$ gives
\[
a_{i_r+1}+a_{i_r+2}+\cdots+a_{i_s}
\equiv0\pmod n.
\tag{3}
\]
Write
\[
i_s-i_r=kn.
\]
Since each increment is at most $n-1$ and there are at most $n$ increments,
\[
1\le k\le n-1.
\]
The block from $i_r+1$ to $i_s$ consists of exactly $k$ full turns around the cyclic sequence, so its sum is
\[
kA.
\]
Because $n$ is prime, neither $k$ nor $A$ is divisible by $n$, contradicting (3).

Hence every prime $n$ has the stated property.
