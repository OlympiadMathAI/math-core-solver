ID: 0323

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Convex Geometry, Orthogonal Projections, Extremal Principle, Packing Estimates

STATEMENT:
Prove that there is a positive constant $c$ with the following property.

For every integer $n\ge2$ and every set $S$ of $n$ points in the plane such that the distance between any two distinct points of $S$ is at least $1$, there exists a line $\ell$ separating $S$ such that the distance from every point of $S$ to $\ell$ is at least
\[
cn^{-1/3}.
\]

A line $\ell$ is said to separate $S$ if some segment joining two points of $S$ crosses $\ell$.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove that the statement holds with
\[
\boxed{c=\frac18}.
\]
Put
\[
\delta=\frac18n^{-1/3}.
\]
For a line $g$ and a point $X$, write $X_g$ for the orthogonal projection of $X$ onto $g$; use the analogous notation for point sets.

Suppose that, for some line $g$, two adjacent points $X_g,Y_g$ of the projected set $S_g$ satisfy
\[
X_gY_g=2d.
\]
The line perpendicular to $g$ through the midpoint of $X_gY_g$ separates $S$. Since no projected point lies strictly between $X_g$ and $Y_g$, every point of $S$ is at distance at least $d$ from this separating line.

Thus, if $d\ge\delta$, we are done. Assume, for contradiction, that this never happens for any projection. Therefore every gap between adjacent projected points, in every direction, is smaller than
\[
2\delta.
\tag{1}
\]

Choose $A,B\in S$ with maximal distance, and put
\[
M=AB.
\]
Then
\[
M\ge1.
\]
Let $g=AB$. Every point of $S$ lies in each of the two disks of radius $M$ centred at $A$ and $B$, so the projection $S_g$ is contained in segment $AB$. By (1), the projected points divide $AB$ into fewer than $n$ gaps, each shorter than $2\delta$. Hence
\[
M<2n\delta.
\tag{2}
\]

Choose $H\in AB$ such that
\[
AH=\frac12.
\]
Let $\mathcal P$ be the closed strip bounded by the lines through $A$ and $H$ perpendicular to $AB$. Put
\[
T=S\cap\mathcal P,
\qquad
t=|T|.
\]
Starting with the projection of $A$, condition (1) forces enough projected points into the interval $AH$ to give
\[
t>\frac1{4\delta}.
\tag{3}
\]

The set $T$ lies in the intersection of $\mathcal P$ with the disk of radius $M$ centred at $B$. Let $a$ be the boundary line of $\mathcal P$ through $A$. The projection of this circular segment onto $a$ has length
\[
2\sqrt{M^2-\left(M-\frac12\right)^2}
<
2\sqrt M.
\tag{4}
\]

For any distinct $X,Y\in T$, we know
\[
XY\ge1,
\]
while the difference of their projections onto $AB$ is at most $1/2$. Therefore their projections onto $a$ differ by at least
\[
\sqrt{1-\frac14}
=
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the $t$ projected points of $T_a$ lie on a segment of length less than $2\sqrt M$ and are pairwise separated, in their natural order, by at least $\sqrt3/2$. Hence
\[
2\sqrt M
>
(t-1)\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]
which gives
\[
t
<
1+\frac{4\sqrt M}{\sqrt3}
<
4\sqrt M,
\tag{6}
\]
because $M\ge1$.

Combining (2), (3), and (6),
\[
\frac1{4\delta}
<
t
<
4\sqrt M
<
4\sqrt{2n\delta}.
\]
Squaring the outer inequality gives
\[
512n\delta^3>1.
\]
But for
\[
\delta=\frac18n^{-1/3},
\]
the left-hand side equals $1$. This contradiction proves that a desired separating line must exist.

Therefore the constant
\[
c=\frac18
\]
works.
