ID: 0234

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Multiplicative Orders, Terminating Decimals, LTE, Euler Totient Function

STATEMENT:
Call a rational number \emph{short} if it has finitely many digits in its decimal expansion. For a positive integer $m$, a positive integer $t$ is called \emph{$m$-tastic} if there exists
\[
c\in\{1,2,\ldots,2017\}
\]
such that
\[
\frac{10^t-1}{cm}
\]
is short, while
\[
\frac{10^k-1}{cm}
\]
is not short for every $1\le k<t$. Let $S(m)$ be the set of all $m$-tastic numbers. As $m$ ranges over the positive integers, determine the maximum possible value of $|S(m)|$.

SOLUTION 1:
The maximum is
\[
\boxed{807}.
\]

A rational number is short exactly when, after reduction, its denominator has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$. Write
\[
m=2^u5^v s,
\qquad
\gcd(s,10)=1.
\]
The powers of $2$ and $5$ do not affect whether the quotients in the statement are short, so replacing $m$ by $s$ leaves $S(m)$ unchanged. We may therefore assume
\[
\gcd(m,10)=1.
\]
Likewise, replacing $c$ by its factor coprime to $10$ does not change the first exponent for which the quotient becomes short. Define
\[
\mathcal C=\{c:1\le c\le2017,\ \gcd(c,10)=1\}.
\]
Then
\[
S(m)=\{\operatorname{ord}_{cm}(10):c\in\mathcal C\},
\tag{1}
\]
where $\operatorname{ord}_N(10)$ is the multiplicative order of $10$ modulo $N$.

There are $201$ complete blocks of ten integers up to $2010$, each contributing the residues $1,3,7,9$, and the additional integers $2011,2013,2017$. Hence
\[
|\mathcal C|=4\cdot201+3=807.
\]
Equation (1) gives
\[
|S(m)|\le807.
\tag{2}
\]

We now attain equality. Let
\[
\mathcal P=\{p:1<p\le2017,\ p\text{ prime},\ p\notin\{2,5\}\}.
\]
Choose a positive integer $\alpha$ such that
\[
p\mid10^\alpha-1
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P).
\]
For example, one may take $\alpha=\varphi(T)$, where $T$ is the product of all primes in $\mathcal P$. Put
\[
m=10^\alpha-1.
\]
We claim that, for every $c\in\mathcal C$,
\[
\operatorname{ord}_{cm}(10)=c\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]

First,
\[
\operatorname{ord}_m(10)=\alpha,
\]
because $m=10^\alpha-1$ and no smaller positive number $10^t-1$ can be divisible by $m$. Thus, if
\[
cm\mid10^t-1,
\]
then $\alpha\mid t$; write $t=k\alpha$.

Every prime divisor of $c$ belongs to $\mathcal P$. For such a prime $p$, the lifting-the-exponent lemma gives
\[
v_p(10^{k\alpha}-1)
=v_p(10^\alpha-1)+v_p(k)
=v_p(m)+v_p(k).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
cm\mid10^{k\alpha}-1
&\iff v_p(c)+v_p(m)\le v_p(m)+v_p(k)
   \quad\text{for every }p\mid c\\
&\iff c\mid k.
\end{aligned}
\]
The smallest possible $k$ is $c$, proving (3).

Thus the $807$ elements of $\mathcal C$ give the distinct orders
\[
c\alpha\qquad(c\in\mathcal C),
\]
so $|S(m)|=807$. Together with (2), this proves the answer.
