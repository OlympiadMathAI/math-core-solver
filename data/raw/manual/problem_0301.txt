ID: 0301

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, AM-GM, Homogeneity, Extremal Values

STATEMENT:
Suppose that $a,b,c,d$ are positive real numbers satisfying
\[
(a+c)(b+d)=ac+bd.
\]
Find the smallest possible value of
\[
\frac ab+\frac bc+\frac cd+\frac da.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The smallest possible value is
\[
\boxed{8}.
\]

Put
\[
S=\frac ab+\frac bc+\frac cd+\frac da.
\]
By the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality,
\[
\frac ab+\frac cd
\ge2\sqrt{\frac{ac}{bd}},
\]
and
\[
\frac bc+\frac da
\ge2\sqrt{\frac{bd}{ac}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
S\ge
2\sqrt{\frac{ac}{bd}}
+2\sqrt{\frac{bd}{ac}}.
\tag{1}
\]

The condition gives
\[
ac+bd=(a+c)(b+d)
\ge4\sqrt{abcd}.
\]
Set
\[
x=\sqrt{\frac{ac}{bd}}>0.
\]
After division by $\sqrt{abcd}$, the preceding inequality becomes
\[
x+\frac1x\ge4.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2),
\[
S\ge2\left(x+\frac1x\right)\ge8.
\]

Equality is attainable. Take
\[
a=c=1,
\qquad
b=d=2+\sqrt3.
\]
Then
\[
(a+c)(b+d)=4(2+\sqrt3)=1+(2+\sqrt3)^2=ac+bd,
\]
and
\[
S=2\left((2+\sqrt3)+(2-\sqrt3)\right)=8.
\]
Thus the minimum is $8$.

SOLUTION 2:
By homogeneity, scale the variables so that
\[
abcd=1.
\]
Define
\[
A=bc,
\qquad
B=ac,
\qquad
C=ab.
\]
Then
\[
ad=\frac1A,
\qquad
bd=\frac1B,
\qquad
cd=\frac1C.
\]
The condition becomes
\[
A+\frac1A+C+\frac1C=B+\frac1B.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S
&=\left(\frac ab+\frac cd\right)
 +\left(\frac bc+\frac da\right)\\
&=B\left(A+\frac1A\right)
 +\frac1B\left(C+\frac1C\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using (1),
\[
S=
\left(B-\frac1B\right)
\left(A+\frac1A\right)
+1+\frac1{B^2}.
\tag{2}
\]

A cyclic relabelling of $a,b,c,d$ replaces $B$ by $1/B$ and leaves both the condition and $S$ unchanged. Hence we may assume
\[
B\ge1.
\]
Since
\[
A+\frac1A\ge2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C+\frac1C\ge2,
\]
equation (1) gives
\[
B+\frac1B\ge4.
\]
Thus
\[
B\ge2+\sqrt3.
\tag{3}
\]
From (2),
\[
S\ge
2\left(B-\frac1B\right)+1+\frac1{B^2}
=:F(B).
\]
For $B\ge1$,
\[
F'(B)=2+\frac2{B^2}-\frac2{B^3}>0.
\]
Using (3),
\[
S\ge F(2+\sqrt3)=8.
\]
The equality example from Solution 1 shows that this bound is sharp.

SOLUTION 3:
We give another direct proof. As in Solution 1,
\[
S\ge
2\sqrt{\frac{ac}{bd}}
+2\sqrt{\frac{bd}{ac}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
y=\sqrt{ac},
\qquad
z=\sqrt{bd}.
\]
Set
\[
x=\max\left\{\frac yz,\frac zy\right\}\ge1.
\]
Then $x+1/x=y/z+z/y$.
The condition and AM-GM give
\[
y^2+z^2=ac+bd=(a+c)(b+d)\ge4yz.
\]
Hence
\[
x^2+1\ge4x.
\]
For $x\ge1$, this is equivalent to
\[
x\ge2+\sqrt3.
\]
The function $x+1/x$ is increasing on $[1,\infty)$, so (1) yields
\[
S\ge2\left(x+\frac1x\right)
\ge2\left((2+\sqrt3)+(2-\sqrt3)\right)=8.
\]
Again, equality is attained by the example in Solution 1.
