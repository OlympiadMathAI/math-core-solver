ID: 0423

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Linear Recurrences, Difference Sequences, Congruences, Multiplicative Steps

STATEMENT:
A sequence of integers
\[
a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots
\]
is called \emph{kawaii} if
\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_1=1,
\]
and, for every positive integer $n$,
\[
\bigl(a_{n+1}-3a_n+2a_{n-1}\bigr)
\bigl(a_{n+1}-4a_n+3a_{n-1}\bigr)=0.
\]

An integer is called \emph{kawaii} if it belongs to a kawaii sequence.

Suppose that two consecutive positive integers $m$ and $m+1$ are both kawaii, not necessarily in the same sequence. Prove that
\[
3\mid m
\]
and that
\[
\frac m3
\]
is kawaii.

SOLUTION 1:
The recurrence says that at every step,
\[
a_{n+1}=3a_n-2a_{n-1}
\]
or
\[
a_{n+1}=4a_n-3a_{n-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

Modulo $3$, these two possibilities give
\[
a_{n+1}\equiv a_{n-1}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
a_{n+1}\equiv a_n.
\]
Since
\[
a_0\equiv0,\qquad a_1\equiv1\pmod3,
\]
every term of every kawaii sequence is congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo $3$.

Both $m$ and $m+1$ have this property. The only possibility is
\[
m\equiv0\pmod3.
\tag{2}
\]

We also note that
\[
a_2\in\{3,4\}.
\]
If $a_2=3$, then $a_1$ and $a_2$ are both odd, and (1) shows inductively that every $a_n$ with $n\ge1$ is odd.

If $a_2=4$, then
\[
a_1\equiv a_2\equiv1\pmod3,
\]
and (1) shows inductively that
\[
a_n\equiv1\pmod3
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]

A kawaii sequence containing the multiple $m$ of $3$ therefore cannot have $a_2=4$. Hence $m$ is odd, and $m+1$ is even.

Take a kawaii sequence $(a_n)$ containing $m+1$, say
\[
a_t=m+1.
\]
Since this term is even, this sequence cannot have $a_2=3$. Therefore
\[
a_n\equiv1\pmod3
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Define
\[
a'_n=\frac{a_{n+1}-1}{3}
\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Then
\[
a'_0=0,\qquad a'_1=1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
a'_{n+1}-3a'_n+2a'_{n-1}
&=
\frac{a_{n+2}-3a_{n+1}+2a_n}{3},\\
a'_{n+1}-4a'_n+3a'_{n-1}
&=
\frac{a_{n+2}-4a_{n+1}+3a_n}{3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $(a'_n)$ is also a kawaii sequence. Finally,
\[
a'_{t-1}
=
\frac{a_t-1}{3}
=
\frac m3.
\]
Therefore $m/3$ is kawaii.

SOLUTION 2:
For a kawaii sequence, define
\[
b_n=a_n-a_{n-1}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Then
\[
b_1=1,
\]
and the recurrence is equivalent to
\[
b_{n+1}=2b_n
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
b_{n+1}=3b_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
a_t=b_1+b_2+\cdots+b_t.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, an integer $A\ge2$ is kawaii if and only if it can be written as
\[
A=1+b_2+\cdots+b_t,
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
b_2\in\{2,3\}
\]
and each quotient
\[
\frac{b_{i+1}}{b_i}
\]
is either $2$ or $3$. For the converse, use these numbers as successive differences and continue multiplying each new difference by either $2$ or $3$.

Modulo $3$, either recurrence branch gives
\[
a_{n+1}\equiv a_n
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
a_{n+1}\equiv a_{n-1}.
\]
Starting from $a_0\equiv0$ and $a_1\equiv1$, every kawaii integer is therefore congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo $3$. Applied to the consecutive kawaii integers $m,m+1$, this gives
\[
3\mid m.
\tag{4}
\]

In any representation (3) of $m$, we must have $b_2=2$; otherwise all $b_i$ for $i\ge2$ would be divisible by $3$, giving
\[
m\equiv1\pmod3.
\]
Hence all $b_i$ for $i\ge2$ are even, so $m$ is odd and $m+1$ is even.

Now take a representation
\[
m+1=1+c_2+\cdots+c_t.
\]
Since $m+1$ is even, we must have
\[
c_2=3;
\]
otherwise all $c_i$ would be even and the sum would be odd. Hence every $c_i$ for $i\ge2$ is divisible by $3$.

Therefore
\[
\frac m3
=
\frac{c_2+\cdots+c_t}{3}
=
1+\frac{c_3}{3}+\cdots+\frac{c_t}{3}.
\]
The successive numbers
\[
1,\frac{c_3}{3},\ldots,\frac{c_t}{3}
\]
again have consecutive ratios equal to $2$ or $3$. If $t=2$, the resulting value is $1$, which is kawaii. If $t\ge3$, characterization (3) shows that $m/3$ is kawaii.

SOLUTION 3:
Put
\[
b_n=a_n-a_{n-1}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Then
\[
b_1=1,
\qquad
\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n}\in\{2,3\}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
a_t=\sum_{j=1}^t b_j.
\tag{2}
\]

The original recurrence modulo $3$ shows that every term is congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo $3$. Therefore, if $m$ and $m+1$ are kawaii,
\[
m\equiv0\pmod3.
\tag{3}
\]

A kawaii sequence beginning with
\[
b_2=2
\]
has every $b_n$ for $n\ge2$ even, so all of its positive-index terms are odd. A sequence beginning with
\[
b_2=3
\]
has every $b_n$ for $n\ge2$ divisible by $3$, so all of its positive-index terms are congruent to $1$ modulo $3$.

Since $m$ is divisible by $3$, a sequence containing $m$ must be of the first type. Thus $m$ is odd and $m+1$ is even.

Take a kawaii sequence with
\[
a_t=m+1.
\]
It must be of the second type, so
\[
3\mid b_n
\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
Define
\[
b'_n=\frac{b_{n+1}}3
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Then
\[
b'_1=1
\]
and
\[
\frac{b'_{n+1}}{b'_n}\in\{2,3\}.
\]
Set
\[
a'_0=0,
\qquad
a'_n=b'_1+\cdots+b'_n.
\]
By (1), $(a'_n)$ is kawaii. Finally,
\[
a'_{t-1}
=
\frac{b_2+\cdots+b_t}{3}
=
\frac{a_t-b_1}{3}
=
\frac{m+1-1}{3}
=
\frac m3.
\]
Hence $m/3$ is kawaii.
