YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Graph Transformations, Degree Parity, Connectivity, Trees

STATEMENT:
A social network has $2019$ users and a symmetric friendship relation. Initially, $1010$ users have exactly $1009$ friends each, while the other $1009$ users have exactly $1010$ friends each.

The following event may be performed repeatedly. If $A$ is friends with both $B$ and $C$, while $B$ and $C$ are not friends, then the friendships $AB$ and $AC$ are deleted and the friendship $BC$ is created.

Prove that some sequence of such events leaves every user with at most one friend.

SOLUTION 1:
Use graph terminology. A permitted operation, called a \emph{refriending}, removes $AB,AC$ and adds $BC$.

The initial graph is connected. Indeed, two nonadjacent vertices have degree sum at least $2018$, while only $2017$ other vertices are available, so they have a common neighbor.

The initial graph satisfies:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{(P)} Every connected component with at least three vertices is noncomplete and contains a vertex of odd degree.
\end{quote}
It is noncomplete because every degree is below $2018$, and it has odd-degree vertices because $1009$ is odd. A refriending preserves the parity of every degree.

We show that whenever a graph satisfies (P) and has a vertex of degree at least $2$, a refriending preserving (P) exists. Choose a component $H$ with such a vertex. We may choose a vertex $A$ having two nonadjacent neighbors: take a maximal clique in $H$; because $H$ is connected and noncomplete, some $A$ in the clique has a neighbor outside it, which is nonadjacent to another vertex of the clique.

Delete $A$ temporarily, obtaining components $H_1,\ldots,H_t$.

\textbf{Case 1: $t\ge2$, and $A$ has at least two neighbors in some $H_i$.}
Choose $B\in H_i$ adjacent to $A$ and choose $C$ adjacent to $A$ in another component. Then $B,C$ are nonadjacent. Refriending at $A$ keeps $H$ connected; the missing edges $AB,AC$ keep it noncomplete, and degree parity is preserved.

\textbf{Case 2: $t\ge2$, and $A$ has exactly one neighbor in each $H_i$.}
In the graph induced by $H_i\cup\{A\}$, the vertex $A$ has degree $1$. By the handshaking lemma, $H_i$ contains a vertex of odd degree. Refriend any two distinct neighbors $B,C$ of $A$. Every new component of size at least three contains an odd-degree vertex and is visibly noncomplete, so (P) is preserved.

\textbf{Case 3: $t=1$, and $A$ has at least three neighbors.}
Choose nonadjacent neighbors $B,C$. The refriending keeps the component connected, and Case 1 applies.

\textbf{Case 4: $t=1$, and $A$ has exactly two neighbors $B,C$.}
They are nonadjacent. The obvious refriending isolates $A$ and leaves another component with an odd-degree vertex. The only problem would be if this second component became complete on at least three vertices. Then $H-A$ was a complete graph with only $BC$ missing. It has at least four vertices; with three, the original component would be a $4$-cycle and have no odd-degree vertex. Choose a third vertex $D$ of $H-A$. Refriend at $B$ using the nonadjacent neighbors $A,D$: remove $BA,BD$ and add $AD$. The component remains connected and noncomplete, and parity is preserved.

Every refriending decreases the number of edges by one. Repeating the construction must terminate, and termination is possible only when every degree is at most $1$.

SOLUTION 2:
The initial graph is connected, noncomplete, and has an odd-degree vertex. Refriending preserves degree parity and reduces the number of edges.

\textbf{Stage 1: reduce the graph to a tree while keeping it connected.}
Suppose a cycle remains. We find a cycle $Z$, an edge $AB$ of $Z$, and a vertex $C\notin Z$ adjacent to $A$ but not $B$. Then replacing $AB,AC$ by $BC$ keeps the graph connected: the rest of $Z$ still joins $A$ to $B$, and the new edge reconnects the part containing $C$.

If the graph contains a triangle, take a maximal clique $K$. Since the graph is connected and noncomplete, some $A\in K$ has a neighbor $C\notin K$. Maximality gives $B\in K$ not adjacent to $C$, and a cycle in $K$ can be chosen through $AB$.

If the graph is triangle-free, take a shortest cycle $Z$. It cannot contain all vertices, for then it would have no chord and the whole graph would be that cycle, making every degree even. Choose $C\notin Z$ adjacent to $A\in Z$ and let $B$ be a neighbor of $A$ on $Z$. Triangle-freeness gives $BC\notin E$.

Each connected refriending removes one edge, so eventually the graph becomes a tree.

\textbf{Stage 2: reduce the tree to a matching.}
A refriending in a forest cannot create a cycle: removing $AB,AC$ separates the branches through $B,C$, and the new edge $BC$ merely reconnects those two branches. Continue until no operation is possible. If a final vertex had two neighbors, those neighbors would be nonadjacent in a forest and another refriending would be possible. Hence the final graph is a disjoint union of isolated vertices and single edges.
