ID: 0122

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Rectangular Dissections, Double Counting, Planar Geometry, Induction

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ points be given inside a rectangle $R$, with no two of them lying on a line parallel to either side of $R$. The rectangle is dissected into smaller rectangles whose sides are parallel to the sides of $R$, in such a way that none of the given points lies in the interior of a smaller rectangle. Prove that the dissection contains at least $n+1$ smaller rectangles.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $k$ be the number of smaller rectangles. Consider all vertices occurring in the dissection. There are three possible kinds:

\begin{itemize}
\item the four corners of $R$, at each of which exactly one smaller rectangle has a corner;
\item $b$ points at which exactly two smaller rectangles have corners, including ordinary $T$-junctions and endpoints of internal boundaries on the boundary of $R$;
\item $c$ crossings at which four smaller rectangles have corners.
\end{itemize}

Counting corner incidences of the $k$ smaller rectangles gives
\[
4k=4+2b+4c.
\]
Hence
\[
b=2k-2-2c\le 2k-2.
\tag{1}
\]

Every given point lies on an internal boundary of the dissection, because it lies inside $R$ but in the interior of no smaller rectangle. Through such a point choose one horizontal or vertical internal boundary segment and extend it maximally in both directions along collinear boundaries. The resulting maximal segment ends at two points of the second kind above.

No two given points produce the same maximal segment, since no two given points have the same horizontal or vertical coordinate. Moreover, a point of the second kind can be an endpoint of at most one maximal internal segment: at a $T$-junction only the stem terminates, while the other boundary continues through the point. Therefore the $n$ chosen maximal segments have $2n$ distinct endpoints, and so
\[
b\ge 2n.
\tag{2}
\]

Combining (1) and (2),
\[
2n\le b\le 2k-2,
\]
which yields
\[
k\ge n+1.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
We prove the equivalent assertion that if a rectangle is dissected into $k$ smaller rectangles, then at most $k-1$ points satisfying the conditions can lie on the boundaries of the dissection. We use induction on $k$.

The case $k=1$ is immediate. Assume $k>1$. Call the two side directions horizontal and vertical.

If there is no horizontal internal boundary segment, then the dissection consists of $k$ vertical strips separated by $k-1$ vertical segments. Every given point must lie on one of these segments, and each segment contains at most one given point. Hence there are at most $k-1$ points.

Now suppose that horizontal internal boundary segments do occur. Let $h$ be the lowest horizontal line containing such a segment, and remove the part of $R$ below $h$. The remaining part is a rectangle $R'$. The rectangles lying entirely below $h$ form $r\ge1$ blocks, separated from one another by vertical boundary segments. Suppose that the $i$th block contains $k_i$ rectangles.

The two vertical sides of every block continue above $h$. Any given point lying on one of these continuing sides may be moved slightly upward along that side into $R'$, choosing distinct heights so that the condition on horizontal lines remains valid. Inside the $i$th block there are exactly $k_i-1$ other internal boundary segments, and each of them contains at most one given point. In addition, at most one given point lies on the line $h$ itself.

The rectangle $R'$ is dissected into
\[
k-\sum_{i=1}^r k_i
\]
rectangles. By the induction hypothesis, after the described upward movements it contains at most
\[
\left(k-\sum_{i=1}^r k_i\right)-1
\]
given points. Consequently the total number of given points is at most
\[
\left(k-\sum_{i=1}^r k_i-1\right)
+\sum_{i=1}^r(k_i-1)+1
=k-r\le k-1.
\]
Thus $n\le k-1$, or equivalently $k\ge n+1$.
