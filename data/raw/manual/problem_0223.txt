ID: 0223

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Orthocenter, Tangential Pentagons, Brianchon's Theorem, Congruent Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDE$ be a convex pentagon such that
\[
AB=BC=CD,
\qquad
\angle EAB=\angle BCD,
\qquad
\angle EDC=\angle CBA.
\]
Prove that the line through $E$ perpendicular to $BC$ and the segments $AC$ and $BD$ are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Write $\angle A,\angle B,\angle C,\angle D,\angle E$ for the internal angles of the pentagon.

The perpendicular bisector of $AC$ passes through $B$, and the perpendicular bisector of $BD$ passes through $C$. Let these two perpendicular bisectors meet at $I$. Then
\[
BI\perp AC,
\qquad
CI\perp BD.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
H=AC\cap BD,
\]
then $H$ is the orthocenter of $\triangle BIC$, and therefore
\[
IH\perp BC.
\tag{1}
\]
It remains to prove that $E,I,H$ are collinear.

We have
\[
IA=IC,
\qquad
IB=ID.
\]
Together with $AB=BC=CD$, this shows that
\[
\triangle IAB\cong\triangle ICB\cong\triangle ICD.
\]
Hence $IB$ and $IC$ bisect $\angle B$ and $\angle C$, respectively. Since
\[
\angle A=\angle C
\quad\text{and}\quad
\angle B=\angle D,
\]
the same congruences also show that $IA$ and $ID$ bisect $\angle A$ and $\angle D$.

Thus $I$ is equidistant from the four consecutive side-lines
\[
EA,AB,BC,CD,DE.
\]
In particular, it is equidistant from $EA$ and $ED$, so $IE$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle E$.

Because the sum of the internal angles of the pentagon is $540^\circ$,
\[
\angle E=540^\circ-2\angle A-2\angle B.
\tag{2}
\]
In the quadrilateral $ABIE$, using that $AI$ and $EI$ are angle bisectors, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BIE
&=360^\circ-\angle EAB-\angle ABI-\angle AEI\\
&=360^\circ-\angle A-\frac12\angle B-\frac12\angle E\\
&=90^\circ+\frac12\angle B\\
&=90^\circ+\angle IBC.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore $IE\perp BC$. Comparing with (1), both $IE$ and $IH$ are the line through $I$ perpendicular to $BC$. Hence $E,I,H$ are collinear, and the required three lines are concurrent at $H$.

SOLUTION 2:
Define $I$ and $H$ as in Solution 1. The same congruence argument shows that all five internal angle bisectors of $ABCDE$ pass through $I$. Hence the pentagon has an incircle centered at $I$.

Let this incircle touch $BC$ at $T$. Since $IT\perp BC$ and $IH\perp BC$, the points $I,H,T$ are collinear.

Apply Brianchon's theorem to the degenerate circumscribed hexagon
\[
ABTCDE,
\]
whose consecutive side-lines are all tangent to the incircle; the two consecutive sides $BT$ and $TC$ lie on the same tangent line $BC$. Brianchon's theorem gives the concurrency of the three main diagonals
\[
AC,
\qquad
BD,
\qquad
ET.
\]
The first two meet at $H$, so $E,H,T$ are collinear. As $I,H,T$ are also collinear, the line $EH$ is perpendicular to $BC$. Thus the perpendicular from $E$ to $BC$, together with $AC$ and $BD$, passes through $H$.

SOLUTION 3:
As in Solution 1, let $I$ be the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AC$ and $BD$, and let
\[
H=AC\cap BD.
\]
Then $IH\perp BC$, and the triangles $IAB$ and $ICD$ are congruent with the same orientation.

Since the pentagon is convex,
\[
\angle E<180^\circ.
\]
Using $\angle A=\angle C$ and $\angle B=\angle D$, this implies
\[
\angle A+\angle B>180^\circ.
\]
Therefore the rays $EA$ and $CB$ meet at a point $P$, while the rays $BC$ and $ED$ meet at a point $Q$.

We have
\[
\angle PBA=180^\circ-\angle B
=180^\circ-\angle D
=\angle QDC,
\]
and similarly
\[
\angle PAB=\angle QCD.
\]
Since $AB=CD$,
\[
\triangle PAB\cong\triangle QCD
\]
with the same orientation. In particular,
\[
\angle EPQ=\angle PQE,
\]
so
\[
EP=EQ.
\tag{1}
\]

Combining this congruence with
\[
\triangle IAB\cong\triangle ICD
\]
shows that the quadrilaterals $PBIA$ and $QDIC$ are congruent. Hence
\[
IP=IQ.
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1) and (2) say that both $E$ and $I$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$. Since $P,Q,B,C$ are collinear,
\[
EI\perp PQ,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
EI\perp BC.
\]
But $IH\perp BC$ as well, so $E,I,H$ are collinear. The desired concurrency follows.
