ID: 0347

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Parallelograms, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Radical Axes, Pascal's Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram such that
\[
AC=BC.
\]
A point $P$ is chosen on the extension of segment $AB$ beyond $B$. The circumcircle of triangle $ACD$ meets segment $PD$ again at $Q$, and the circumcircle of triangle $APQ$ meets segment $PC$ again at $R$.

Prove that the lines
\[
CD,\qquad AQ,\qquad BR
\]
are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Since $ABCD$ is a parallelogram and $AC=BC$, we have
\[
AC=BC=AD
\]
and therefore
\[
\angle ABC=\angle BAC=\angle ACD=\angle ADC.
\tag{1}
\]

The quadrilaterals $APRQ$ and $AQCD$ are cyclic. Using the collinearities
\[
P,R,C
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P,Q,D,
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle CRA
&=180^\circ-\angle ARP\\
&=180^\circ-\angle AQP\\
&=\angle DQA\\
&=\angle DCA\\
&=\angle CBA.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
A,B,C,R
\]
are concyclic; denote their circle by $\gamma$.

Let
\[
X=AQ\cap CD.
\]
We shall prove that $B,R,X$ are collinear.

From the cyclic quadrilateral $APRQ$ and the parallelism $AB\parallel CD$,
\[
\angle RQX
=
180^\circ-\angle AQR
=
\angle RPA
=
\angle RCX.
\]
Thus
\[
C,Q,R,X
\]
are concyclic. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle XRC
&=\angle XQC\\
&=180^\circ-\angle CQA\\
&=\angle ADC\\
&=\angle BAC\\
&=180^\circ-\angle CRB,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows from the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCR$.

Therefore the rays $RX$ and $RB$ are opposite, so
\[
B,R,X
\]
are collinear. Since $X\in AQ\cap CD$, the three required lines are concurrent at $X$.

SOLUTION 2:
Since $AC=BC=AD$,
\[
\angle ABC=\angle BAC=\angle ACD=\angle ADC.
\]
The cyclic quadrilaterals $APRQ$ and $AQCD$, together with the collinearities $P,R,C$ and $P,Q,D$, give
\[
\angle CRA
=
180^\circ-\angle ARP
=
180^\circ-\angle AQP
=
\angle DQA
=
\angle DCA
=
\angle CBA.
\]
Hence the points
\[
A,B,C,R
\]
lie on a circle $\gamma$.

Let
\[
\alpha=(APQR).
\]
We first show that $AC$ is tangent to $\alpha$ at $A$. Indeed,
\[
\angle CAP
=
\angle ACD
=
\angle AQD
=
180^\circ-\angle AQP.
\]
This is precisely the tangent--chord criterion.

Let $Y\ne A$ be the second intersection of $AD$ with $\alpha$. Using the circle $\gamma$ and the tangency of $AC$ to $\alpha$,
\[
\angle ARY
=
\angle CAD
=
\angle ACB
=
\angle ARB.
\]
Hence
\[
Y,B,R
\]
are collinear.

Apply Pascal's theorem to the degenerate hexagon
\[
A,A,Y,R,P,Q
\]
inscribed in $\alpha$, where the side $AA$ is interpreted as the tangent to $\alpha$ at $A$. The three intersections of opposite sides are

\[
AA\cap RP=AC\cap PC=C,
\]
\[
AY\cap PQ=AD\cap PD=D,
\]
and
\[
YR\cap QA=BR\cap AQ.
\]

Pascal's theorem says that these three points are collinear. Therefore
\[
BR\cap AQ\in CD,
\]
which proves the required concurrency.

SOLUTION 3:
As $AC=BC=AD$,
\[
\angle ABC=\angle BAC=\angle ACD=\angle ADC.
\]
Using the cyclic quadrilaterals $APRQ$ and $AQCD$ as in the angle chain
\[
\angle CRA
=
180^\circ-\angle ARP
=
180^\circ-\angle AQP
=
\angle DQA
=
\angle DCA
=
\angle CBA,
\]
we obtain a circle $\gamma$ through
\[
A,B,C,R.
\]
Let
\[
\alpha=(APQR),
\qquad
X=AQ\cap CD.
\]

Let $Y\ne R$ be the second intersection of $BR$ with $\alpha$. By (1),
\[
\angle DCA=\angle CBA,
\]
so the tangent--chord theorem shows that $CD$ is tangent to $\gamma$ at $C$. Therefore, using the circle $\alpha$, the parallelism $AB\parallel CD$, and this tangency,
\[
\angle RYA
=
\angle RPA
=
\angle RCX
=
\angle RBC.
\tag{2}
\]
It follows that
\[
AY\parallel BC.
\]
Since $AD\parallel BC$ and both lines pass through $A$, we get
\[
Y\in AD.
\]

Equation (2) also gives
\[
\angle RYD=\angle RCX,
\]
so
\[
C,D,Y,R
\]
are concyclic; denote this circle by $\beta$.

Now consider the three circles
\[
(AQCD),\qquad \alpha,\qquad \beta.
\]
Their pairwise radical axes are respectively
\[
AQ,\qquad CD,\qquad YR=BR.
\]
By the radical-axis theorem, these three lines are concurrent.
