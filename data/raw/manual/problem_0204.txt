ID: 0204

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Divisibility, Prime Powers, Bounding Divisors

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb N$ denote the set of positive integers. Determine all functions
\[
f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N
\]
such that, for all positive integers $m,n$, the nonzero integer
\[
f(m)+f(n)-mn
\]
divides
\[
mf(m)+nf(n).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The unique solution is
\[
\boxed{f(n)=n^2\quad(n\in\mathbb N).}
\]

The condition is
\[
f(m)+f(n)-mn\mid mf(m)+nf(n).
\tag{1}
\]

Taking $m=n=1$,
\[
2f(1)-1\mid2f(1).
\]
Therefore
\[
2f(1)-1\mid1,
\]
and since $f(1)$ is positive,
\[
f(1)=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $p\ge7$ be a prime. Setting $m=p$ and $n=1$ in (1), and using (2), we obtain
\[
f(p)-p+1\mid pf(p)+1.
\]
Subtracting $p$ times the divisor,
\[
f(p)-p+1\mid p^2-p+1.
\tag{3}
\]

We first show that
\[
f(p)-p+1>0.
\]
If it were negative, then
\[
f(p)\le p-2.
\]
Using $m=n=p$ in (1),
\[
2f(p)-p^2\mid2pf(p),
\]
and subtracting $p(2f(p)-p^2)$ gives
\[
2f(p)-p^2\mid p^3.
\tag{4}
\]
But in the present case,
\[
-p^2<2f(p)-p^2\le2p-4-p^2<-p.
\]
Its absolute value lies strictly between $p$ and $p^2$, so it cannot divide $p^3$. This contradiction proves the claim.

If the positive divisor in (3) equals $p^2-p+1$, then
\[
f(p)=p^2.
\]
Suppose it is a proper divisor. Since $p^2-p+1$ is odd, the quotient is at least $3$, and hence
\[
p^2-p+1\ge3(f(p)-p+1).
\]
Thus
\[
f(p)\le\frac{p^2+2p-2}{3}.
\tag{5}
\]
Again use (4). From $f(p)\ge1$ and (5),
\[
-p^2<2f(p)-p^2
\le
\frac{2(p^2+2p-2)}3-p^2
<-p
\]
for $p\ge7$. This again gives a divisor of $p^3$ whose absolute value lies strictly between $p$ and $p^2$, impossible.

Therefore
\[
f(p)=p^2
\qquad\text{for every prime }p\ge7.
\tag{6}
\]

Now fix any positive integer $n$, and choose a sufficiently large prime $p$. Taking $m=p$ in (1) and subtracting $n$ times the divisor from the dividend gives
\[
f(p)+f(n)-pn
\mid
(p-n)f(p)+pn^2.
\]
Using (6),
\[
p^2-pn+f(n)
\mid
p(p^2-pn+n^2).
\tag{7}
\]

Choose $p>f(n)$. Then
\[
\gcd(p,p^2-pn+f(n))
=\gcd(p,f(n))
=1.
\]
Equation (7) therefore implies
\[
p^2-pn+f(n)\mid p^2-pn+n^2.
\]
Subtracting,
\[
p^2-pn+f(n)\mid n^2-f(n).
\tag{8}
\]

The number on the right of (8) is fixed, while the positive divisor on the left tends to infinity with $p$. Hence
\[
n^2-f(n)=0.
\]
Since $n$ was arbitrary,
\[
f(n)=n^2
\qquad(n\in\mathbb N).
\]

Finally, this function works because
\[
f(m)+f(n)-mn=m^2+n^2-mn>0
\]
and
\[
mf(m)+nf(n)
=m^3+n^3
=(m+n)(m^2+n^2-mn).
\]
