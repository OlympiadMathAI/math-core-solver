ID: 0228

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Regular Polygons, Convexity, Homothety, Half-Planes, Cyclic Order

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge3$ be an integer. Two regular $n$-gons $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ are given in the plane. Prove that the vertices of $\mathcal A$ that lie inside $\mathcal B$ or on its boundary are consecutive in the cyclic order of the vertices of $\mathcal A$.

Equivalently, prove that a line separates those vertices from the remaining vertices of $\mathcal A$.

SOLUTION 1:
Throughout the proof, a polygon includes its boundary.

We first construct a regular $n$-gon $\mathcal C$ inscribed in $\mathcal B$ and aligned with $\mathcal A$. Let $O_A,O_B$ be the centers of $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$. Choose a vertex $A$ of $\mathcal A$, and choose the point $C$ on the boundary of $\mathcal B$ for which
\[
\overrightarrow{O_BC}
\]
has the same direction as
\[
\overrightarrow{O_AA}.
\]
Rotate $C$ about $O_B$ through the multiples of $2\pi/n$. The resulting vertices form a regular $n$-gon $\mathcal C$ inscribed in $\mathcal B$. Corresponding vertices of $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal A$ are related either by a translation, or by a positive homothety.

It is therefore enough to prove the following more general assertion:

\begin{quote}
If $\mathcal C$ is a convex polygon inscribed in a convex polygon $\mathcal B$, and $\mathcal A$ is obtained from $\mathcal C$ by a translation or a positive homothety, then the vertices of $\mathcal A$ lying in $\mathcal B$ are consecutive.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Case 1: a translation.}
Let $\tau$ be the translation taking $\mathcal C$ to $\mathcal A$. If its vector is zero, all vertices lie on the boundary of $\mathcal B$ and the result is immediate. Otherwise, choose coordinates so that the translation vector points to the right.

Let $B_T$ and $B_B$ be the top and bottom vertices of $\mathcal B$, choosing the rightmost one if there is a tie. They split the boundary of $\mathcal B$ into a left chain $B_L$ and a right chain $B_R$. Accordingly, the vertices of the inscribed polygon $\mathcal C$ split into consecutive sets
\[
C_L\subseteq B_L,
\qquad
C_R\subseteq B_R.
\]
Every point of $C_R$ moves outside $\mathcal B$, because it is the rightmost point of $\mathcal B$ on its horizontal line.

It remains to consider $C_L$. Let $C_1,C_2,C_3$ occur in this order on $C_L$, and suppose
\[
A_1=\tau(C_1),
\qquad
A_3=\tau(C_3)
\]
lie in $\mathcal B$. Put $A_2=\tau(C_2)$. By the convexity of the left boundary, the horizontal line through $C_2$ meets the chord $C_1C_3$ to the right of $C_2$. After translation, the same line meets $A_1A_3$ to the right of $A_2$. Hence $A_2$ lies in the triangle
\[
A_1C_2A_3,
\]
which is contained in the convex polygon $\mathcal B$. Therefore $A_2\in\mathcal B$. This proves consecutiveness in the translation case.

\textbf{Case 2: a positive homothety.}
Let $h$ be the homothety taking $\mathcal C$ to $\mathcal A$, with center $X$ and ratio $k>0$.

If $X\in\mathcal B$, the assertion is immediate. For $0<k\le1$, every image vertex lies on a segment from $X$ to a boundary point of $\mathcal B$ and hence lies in $\mathcal B$. For $k>1$, the image vertices lie beyond the corresponding boundary points; none lies inside, except possibly one or two boundary vertices in limiting supporting directions, which are consecutive.

Assume now that
\[
X\notin\mathcal B.
\]
Choose two boundary vertices $B_T,B_B$ so that $\mathcal B$ lies in the angle $\angle B_TXB_B$. They divide the boundary into a chain $B_R$ farther from $X$ and a chain $B_L$ nearer to $X$. The vertices of $\mathcal C$ again split into consecutive subsets
\[
C_R\subseteq B_R,
\qquad
C_L\subseteq B_L.
\]

Suppose first that $k>1$. All vertices on the far chain $C_R$ move outside $\mathcal B$. Let $C_1,C_2,C_3$ occur in this order on $C_L$, and suppose that
\[
A_1=h(C_1),
\qquad
A_3=h(C_3)
\]
lie in $\mathcal B$. The ray $XC_2$ meets the chord $C_1C_3$ beyond $C_2$. After applying the homothety, it meets $A_1A_3$ beyond $A_2=h(C_2)$. Hence
\[
A_2\in\triangle A_1C_2A_3\subseteq\mathcal B.
\]
Thus the surviving vertices are consecutive.

If $0<k<1$, the points of the near chain $C_L$ move toward the exterior point $X$ and leave $\mathcal B$. The same argument is applied to three vertices $C_1,C_2,C_3$ in the far chain $C_R$. In this case $A_2$ lies on $XC_2$, and the segments $XA_2$ and $A_1A_3$ intersect; again
\[
A_2\in\triangle A_1C_2A_3\subseteq\mathcal B.
\]
Therefore the vertices of $\mathcal A$ lying in $\mathcal B$ form one consecutive block in every case.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $O_A,O_B$ be the centers of $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$, and write
\[
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\},
\]
with all indices interpreted cyclically modulo $n$.

Enumerate the side-lines of $\mathcal B$ clockwise as
\[
\ell_1,\ell_2,\ldots,\ell_n.
\]
Let $H_i$ be the closed half-plane bounded by $\ell_i$ that contains $\mathcal B$. Let $B_i$ be the midpoint of the corresponding side, and put
\[
\mathbf b_i=\overrightarrow{B_iO_B}.
\]

Choose a vertex $A_1$ of $\mathcal A$ so that
\[
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_AA_1},\mathbf b_1\right\rangle
\]
is minimal among all vertices. Enumerate the vertices clockwise as
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n.
\]
Rotational symmetry gives, for every $i$,
\[
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_AA_i},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle
=
\min_{j\in[n]}
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_AA_j},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

Call a subset of $[n]$ \emph{connected} if its elements are consecutive in the cyclic order. The set of vertices of a convex polygon lying in any fixed half-plane is connected. The union of two connected cyclic sets that have a common element is connected.

Define
\[
M=\{j\in[n]:A_j\notin\mathcal B\},
\qquad
M_i=\{j\in[n]:A_j\notin H_i\}.
\]
Since
\[
\mathcal B=\bigcap_{i=1}^nH_i,
\]
we have
\[
M=\bigcup_{i=1}^nM_i.
\tag{2}
\]

We establish three properties.

\textbf{Property 1.} Every $M_i$ is connected.

This follows because $M_i$ consists of the vertices of $\mathcal A$ lying in the open half-plane complementary to $H_i$.

\textbf{Property 2.} If $M_i\ne\varnothing$, then $i\in M_i$.

Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
j\in M_i
&\iff
\left\langle\overrightarrow{B_iA_j},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle<0\\
&\iff
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_AA_j},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle
<
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_AB_i},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The right-hand side of the last inequality is independent of $j$. If it holds for some $j$, then by the minimality in (1) it also holds for $j=i$.

Put
\[
M'=\{i\in[n]:i\in M_i\}
=\{i\in[n]:M_i\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

\textbf{Property 3.} The set $M'$ is connected.

From (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
i\in M'
&\iff
\left\langle\overrightarrow{B_iA_i},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle<0\\
&\iff
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_BO_A},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle
<
\left\langle\overrightarrow{O_BB_i},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle
+
\left\langle\overrightarrow{A_iO_A},\mathbf b_i\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
By the rotational symmetry of both regular polygons, the right-hand side of (4) is a constant, say $\mu$, independent of $i$. Thus $i\in M'$ exactly when the side midpoint $B_i$ lies in a certain open half-plane whose boundary is perpendicular to $O_BO_A$. The corresponding side indices form a connected set, proving Property 3.

Finally, by Property 2 every nonempty connected set $M_i$ contains the index $i\in M'$. Starting from the connected set $M'$ and adjoining all the sets $M_i$, one by one, always adjoins a connected set meeting the union already present. Hence, by (2), $M$ is connected.

The complement $[n]\setminus M$, which indexes precisely the vertices of $\mathcal A$ lying in $\mathcal B$, is also connected in the cyclic order. Therefore those vertices are consecutive.
