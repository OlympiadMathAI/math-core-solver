ID: 0105

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Rhombi, Distances to Lines, Trigonometric Inequalities, Affine Geometry

STATEMENT:
In a triangle $ABC$, let $D$ and $E$ be the feet of the angle bisectors of angles $A$ and $B$, respectively. A rhombus is inscribed in the quadrilateral $AEDB$, with its four vertices lying on four different sides of $AEDB$. Let $\varphi$ be the non-obtuse angle of the rhombus. Prove that
\[
\varphi\le \max\{\angle BAC,\angle ABC\}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $K,L,M,N$ be the vertices of the rhombus lying on $AE,ED,DB,BA$, respectively. Put
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,
\qquad
\beta=\angle ABC,
\qquad
\psi=\angle NKL=\varphi,
\]
where $\psi$ is chosen to be the non-obtuse angle of the rhombus. Let the common side length of the rhombus be
\[
a=KL=LM=MN=NK.
\]

For a point $X$ and a line $YZ$, write $d(X,YZ)$ for the distance from $X$ to $YZ$. Since $D$ lies on the bisector of $\angle A$ and $E$ lies on the bisector of $\angle B$,
\[
d(D,AB)=d(D,AC),
\qquad
d(E,AB)=d(E,BC).
\]
Also,
\[
d(D,BC)=0,
\qquad
d(E,AC)=0.
\]
Hence
\[
d(D,AC)+d(D,BC)=d(D,AB)
\]
and
\[
d(E,AC)+d(E,BC)=d(E,AB).
\]
Inside triangle $ABC$, signed distance to a fixed side-line is an affine function of the point. Since $L$ lies on $DE$, the preceding two equalities imply
\[
d(L,AC)+d(L,BC)=d(L,AB).
\tag{1}
\]

Define the angles $\mu,\nu,\delta,\varepsilon$ by
\[
\mu=\angle CKL,
\qquad
\nu=\angle CML,
\qquad
\delta=\angle ANK,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\angle MNB.
\]
Then
\[
d(L,AC)=a\sin\mu,
\qquad
d(L,BC)=a\sin\nu.
\]
Because $KLMN$ is a parallelogram and $N\in AB$, the affine-distance identity for its opposite vertices gives
\[
d(L,AB)+d(N,AB)=d(K,AB)+d(M,AB).
\]
Since $d(N,AB)=0$, this becomes
\[
d(L,AB)=a(\sin\delta+\sin\varepsilon).
\]
Substituting in (1), we obtain
\[
\sin\mu+\sin\nu=\sin\delta+\sin\varepsilon.
\tag{2}
\]

If either $\alpha$ or $\beta$ is non-acute, then
\[
\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\ge90^\circ\ge\psi,
\]
and there is nothing to prove. Thus assume
\[
\alpha,\beta<90^\circ.
\]
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\[
\psi>\max\{\alpha,\beta\}.
\tag{3}
\]
At $K$ we have
\[
\mu+\psi=\angle CKN=\alpha+\delta,
\]
so (3) gives
\[
\mu<\delta.
\tag{4}
\]
Similarly, at $M$,
\[
\nu+\psi=\beta+\varepsilon,
\]
and hence
\[
\nu<\varepsilon.
\tag{5}
\]

Since $KN\parallel LM$,
\[
\beta=\delta+\nu,
\]
so
\[
0<\delta<\beta<90^\circ.
\]
Likewise, since $KL\parallel MN$,
\[
\alpha=\mu+\varepsilon,
\]
and therefore
\[
0<\varepsilon<\alpha<90^\circ.
\]
Thus (4) and (5), together with the strict increase of sine on $(0,90^\circ)$, give
\[
\sin\mu<\sin\delta
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sin\nu<\sin\varepsilon.
\]
Adding these inequalities contradicts (2). Hence (3) is impossible, and
\[
\varphi=\psi\le\max\{\angle BAC,\angle ABC\}.
\]
