ID: 0159

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Orthocenter, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Isosceles Triangles, Congruence

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with orthocenter $H$. Let $G$ be the point such that the quadrilateral $ABGH$ is a parallelogram. Let $I$ be the point on the line $GH$ such that $AC$ bisects the segment $HI$. Suppose that the line $AC$ intersects the circumcircle of triangle $GCI$ at $C$ and $J$. Prove that
\[
IJ=AH.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
M=AC\cap GH.
\]
Since $ABGH$ is a parallelogram,
\[
HG\parallel AB
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
BG\parallel AH.
\]
Because $H$ is the orthocenter of $ABC$, we have $AH\perp BC$ and $CH\perp AB$. Hence
\[
BG\perp BC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
CH\perp GH.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle GBC=\angle GHC=90^\circ,
\]
so $B,G,C,H$ are concyclic.

Also,
\[
\angle HAC=90^\circ-\angle ACB=\angle CBH.
\]
Using the cyclic quadrilaterals $BGCH$ and $CGJI$, we obtain, with directed angles modulo $180^\circ$,
\[
\angle CJI=\angle CGI=\angle CGH=\angle CBH=\angle HAC.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $D\ne A$ be the second point on the line $AC$ satisfying
\[
HD=HA.
\]
Then triangle $AHD$ is isosceles, and since $A,M,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle MDH=\angle HAD=\angle HAC.
\tag{2}
\]
The line $AC$ bisects the segment $HI$, so $M$ is the midpoint of $HI$ and
\[
IM=MH.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, because $I,M,H$ are collinear and $J,M,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle IMJ=\angle HMD.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (2), (3), and (4) show that triangles $IMJ$ and $HMD$ are congruent. Consequently,
\[
IJ=HD=AH.
\]
