ID: 0021

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incenter, Equilateral Triangle, Incircles, Arc Estimates

STATEMENT:
The vertices $X,Y,Z$ of an equilateral triangle lie respectively on the sides $BC,CA,AB$ of an acute triangle $ABC$. Prove that the incenter of triangle $ABC$ lies inside triangle $XYZ$.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove the stronger statement that the incenter $I$ of triangle $ABC$ lies inside the incircle of the equilateral triangle $XYZ$.

Let $O$ be the center of triangle $XYZ$. Denote by
\[
r,\qquad r',\qquad R'
\]
the inradius of $ABC$, the inradius of $XYZ$, and the circumradius of $XYZ$, respectively. Since $XYZ$ is equilateral,
\[
R'=2r'.
\]
Thus it is enough to prove
\[
OI\le r'.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the incircle of $ABC$ touch $BC,CA,AB$ at $A_1,B_1,C_1$, respectively. The three perpendicular lines
\[
IA_1,\quad IB_1,\quad IC_1
\]
divide the plane into six acute sectors. We may suppose that $O$ lies in the sector bounded by $IA_1$ and $IC_1$ that contains $C_1$.

Let $A'$ and $C'$ be the perpendicular projections of $O$ onto $IA_1$ and $IC_1$, respectively.

Because $X\in BC$ and $OX=R'$,
\[
d(O,BC)\le R'.
\]
Since $OA'\parallel BC$,
\[
d(A',BC)=IA'+r\le R',
\]
and hence
\[
IA'\le R'-r.
\tag{2}
\]

The incircle of $XYZ$ lies inside triangle $XYZ$, and therefore inside triangle $ABC$. Consequently,
\[
d(O,AB)\ge r'.
\]
Since $OC'\parallel AB$,
\[
d(O,AB)=C'C_1=r-IC',
\]
so
\[
IC'\le r-r'.
\tag{3}
\]

The quadrilateral $IA'OC'$ is cyclic, because
\[
\angle IA'O=\angle IC'O=90^\circ.
\]
Also, the angle $\angle A'IC'$ is acute. Since $OI$ is a diameter of this circle, comparison of the corresponding arcs gives
\[
A'O<IC'.
\tag{4}
\]

Finally, by the triangle inequality and (2)--(4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
OI
&\le IA'+A'O\\
&<IA'+IC'\\
&\le(R'-r)+(r-r')\\
&=R'-r'=r'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $I$ lies inside the incircle of $XYZ$, and hence inside triangle $XYZ$.

SOLUTION 2:
Assume, for a contradiction, that the incenter $I$ lies outside triangle $XYZ$. Since $XYZ$ is contained in $ABC$, the point $I$ must lie in one of the three corner triangles
\[
AYZ,\qquad BXZ,\qquad CXY.
\]
Without loss of generality, assume
\[
I\in\triangle AYZ.
\]

Let the incircle $\omega$ of $ABC$ touch $BC$ and $CA$ at $A_1$ and $B_1$, respectively. By symmetry of the argument, assume that $A_1$ lies on the segment $CX$. We shall prove that
\[
\angle C>90^\circ,
\]
contradicting the fact that $ABC$ is acute.

The circle $\omega$ meets each of the segments $XY$ and $YZ$ twice. Denote the intersection points by
\[
U,U'\in XY,\qquad V,V'\in YZ,
\]
chosen so that
\[
YU>YU',\qquad YV>YV'.
\]
Because $\angle XYZ=60^\circ$, the intersecting-secants angle formula gives
\[
60^\circ
=\frac12\left(\widehat{UV}-\widehat{U'V'}\right)
\le\frac12\widehat{UV}.
\]
Hence
\[
\widehat{UV}\ge120^\circ.
\tag{1}
\]

Since $I$ lies in triangle $AYZ$, the relevant arc $\widehat{VUV'}$ is less than $180^\circ$. Therefore
\[
\widehat{UA_1U'}
\le\widehat{UA_1V'}
<
180^\circ-\widehat{UV}
\le60^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]

There are two possible orders of $Y$ and $B_1$ on $AC$.

\textbf{Case 1.} Suppose that $Y$ lies on the segment $AB_1$.

Using (2),
\[
\angle YXC
=\frac12\left(\widehat{A_1U'}-\widehat{A_1U}\right)
\le\frac12\widehat{UA_1U'}
<30^\circ.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\angle XYC<30^\circ.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle YCX
=180^\circ-\angle YXC-\angle XYC
>120^\circ,
\]
which is impossible in an acute triangle.

\textbf{Case 2.} Suppose that $Y$ lies on the segment $CB_1$.

As above,
\[
\angle YXC<30^\circ.
\tag{3}
\]
Since $I$ lies inside $\angle XYZ$ and $Z,Y,I$ occur in the relevant order,
\[
\angle IYX>\angle ZYX=60^\circ.
\]
The line $YB_1$ is tangent to $\omega$, while $YX$ is a secant, so
\[
\angle IYX<\angle IYB_1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
120^\circ
&<\angle IYB_1+\angle IYX\\
&=\angle B_1YX\\
&=\angle YXC+\angle YCX\\
&<30^\circ+\angle YCX.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\angle YCX>90^\circ,
\]
again contradicting the acuteness of $ABC$.

Both cases are impossible. Therefore $I$ lies inside triangle $XYZ$.
