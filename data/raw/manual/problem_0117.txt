ID: 0117

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Iterated Functions, Dynamical Systems, Growth Arguments

STATEMENT:
Define the function $f:(0,1)\to(0,1)$ by
\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x+\dfrac12,&x<\dfrac12,\\[2mm]
x^2,&x\ge\dfrac12.
\end{cases}
\]
Let $0<a<b<1$, and define
\[
a_0=a,\qquad b_0=b,
\]
and
\[
a_n=f(a_{n-1}),\qquad b_n=f(b_{n-1})
\]
for every $n\ge1$. Prove that there exists a positive integer $n$ such that
\[
(a_n-a_{n-1})(b_n-b_{n-1})<0.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
I_1=\left(0,\frac12\right),
\qquad
I_2=\left[\frac12,1\right).
\]
For $x\in I_1$,
\[
f(x)-x=\frac12>0,
\]
whereas for $x\in I_2$,
\[
f(x)-x=x^2-x<0.
\]
Therefore
\[
(a_n-a_{n-1})(b_n-b_{n-1})<0
\]
exactly when $a_{n-1}$ and $b_{n-1}$ belong to different intervals among $I_1,I_2$.

Assume for contradiction that $a_k$ and $b_k$ always belong to the same one of these two intervals. Set
\[
d_k=|a_k-b_k|.
\]
Since $a_0\ne b_0$, we have $d_0>0$.

If $a_k,b_k\in I_1$, then
\[
d_{k+1}
=\left|a_k+\frac12-b_k-\frac12\right|
=d_k.
\tag{1}
\]
If $a_k,b_k\in I_2$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{k+1}
&=|a_k^2-b_k^2|\\
&=|a_k-b_k|(a_k+b_k).
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing the larger of $a_k,b_k$ as the smaller plus $d_k$, and using that the smaller is at least $1/2$, we get
\[
a_k+b_k\ge1+d_k.
\]
Hence
\[
d_{k+1}\ge d_k(1+d_k)\ge d_k.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus $(d_k)$ is nondecreasing, so $d_k\ge d_0$ for all $k$.

We now compare terms two steps apart. If $a_k,b_k\in I_2$, then by (2) and the monotonicity of $(d_k)$,
\[
d_{k+2}\ge d_{k+1}\ge d_k(1+d_k)\ge d_k(1+d_0).
\]
If $a_k,b_k\in I_1$, then (1) gives $d_{k+1}=d_k$, and both $a_{k+1}$ and $b_{k+1}$ lie in $I_2$. Therefore,
\[
d_{k+2}\ge d_{k+1}(1+d_{k+1})
=d_k(1+d_k)
\ge d_k(1+d_0).
\]
In either case,
\[
d_{k+2}\ge d_k(1+d_0).
\]
Inductively,
\[
d_{2m}\ge d_0(1+d_0)^m.
\]
The right-hand side eventually exceeds $1$, while $a_{2m},b_{2m}\in(0,1)$ always give
\[
d_{2m}<1.
\]
This contradiction proves that at some stage $a_{n-1}$ and $b_{n-1}$ lie in different intervals. For that positive integer $n$, the required product is negative.
