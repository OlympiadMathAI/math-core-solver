ID: 0221

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Set Operations, Increasing Functions, Functional Composition, Associativity

STATEMENT:
For a finite set $X$ of positive integers, let $f_X(k)$ be the $k$th smallest positive integer not belonging to $X$. For finite sets $X,Y$ of positive integers, define
\[
X*Y=X\cup\{f_X(y):y\in Y\}.
\]

Let $A$ contain $a>0$ positive integers and $B$ contain $b>0$ positive integers. Prove that if
\[
A*B=B*A,
\]
then
\[
\underbrace{A*A*\cdots*A}_{b\text{ copies of }A}
=
\underbrace{B*B*\cdots*B}_{a\text{ copies of }B}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For a function $g:\mathbb Z_{>0}\to\mathbb Z_{>0}$ and a set $X$, write
\[
g(X)=\{g(x):x\in X\}.
\]
The range of $f_X$ is
\[
f_X(\mathbb Z_{>0})=\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus X.
\]

We first prove that the operation $*$ is associative.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
For finite sets $X,Y$,
\[
f_{X*Y}=f_X\circ f_Y.
\tag{1}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Because $f_X$ is injective and has range $\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus X$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{X*Y}(\mathbb Z_{>0})
&=
\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus(X*Y)\\
&=
(\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus X)\setminus f_X(Y)\\
&=
f_X(\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus Y)\\
&=
f_X(f_Y(\mathbb Z_{>0})).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both $f_{X*Y}$ and $f_X\circ f_Y$ are strictly increasing functions with this same range. A strictly increasing function from the positive integers is uniquely determined by its range, so (1) follows. $\square$

Consequently,
\[
f_{(X*Y)*Z}
=
f_X\circ f_Y\circ f_Z
=
f_{X*(Y*Z)},
\]
and therefore
\[
(X*Y)*Z=X*(Y*Z).
\]
We may hence omit parentheses. Write
\[
X^{*k}=\underbrace{X*X*\cdots*X}_{k\text{ copies}}.
\]

We need one more lemma.

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
Suppose finite sets $X,Y$ satisfy
\[
X*Y=Y*X
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|X|=|Y|.
\]
Then
\[
X=Y.
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Assume otherwise, and let $s$ be the largest element belonging to exactly one of $X,Y$. Without loss of generality,
\[
s\in X\setminus Y.
\]
Put
\[
t=f_X(s).
\]
Since $t$ is the $s$th positive integer not in $X$,
\[
t=s+|X\cap\{1,2,\ldots,t\}|.
\tag{2}
\]
We have $t>s$. The sets $X,Y$ agree above $s$ and have equal cardinality, so
\[
|X\cap\{1,\ldots,t\}|
=
|Y\cap\{1,\ldots,t\}|.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) show that exactly $s$ positive integers not exceeding $t$ are absent from $Y$. Moreover, $t\notin Y$: it is not in $X$, and $t>s$, where the two sets agree. Hence
\[
t=f_Y(s).
\tag{4}
\]

Now $t\notin X*Y$. Indeed, $t\notin X$, and if
\[
t=f_X(y)
\]
for some $y\in Y$, the injectivity of $f_X$ and $t=f_X(s)$ would give $y=s$, contrary to $s\notin Y$.

On the other hand, (4) and $s\in X$ give
\[
t=f_Y(s)\in Y*X.
\]
This contradicts $X*Y=Y*X$. $\square$

The sets $A^{*b}$ and $B^{*a}$ both have cardinality $ab$, because
\[
|X*Y|=|X|+|Y|.
\]
Associativity and the relation $A*B=B*A$ allow every adjacent $A$ and $B$ to be interchanged, so
\[
A^{*b}*B^{*a}=B^{*a}*A^{*b}.
\]
Lemma 2 now gives
\[
A^{*b}=B^{*a},
\]
as required.

SOLUTION 2:
We use Lemma 1 from Solution 1. Put
\[
g=f_A,
\qquad
h=f_B.
\]
Since a finite set is determined by the range of its corresponding increasing function, the hypothesis
\[
A*B=B*A
\]
is equivalent, by Lemma 1, to
\[
g\circ h=h\circ g.
\tag{1}
\]
Similarly, the desired conclusion is equivalent to
\[
g^b=h^a.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
p=\max A,
\qquad
q=\max B.
\]
For every sufficiently large $m$,
\[
g(m)=m+a,
\qquad
h(m)=m+b.
\]
Therefore
\[
g^b(m)=m+ab=h^a(m)
\]
for all sufficiently large $m$.

Suppose (2) fails somewhere, and let $s$ be the largest positive integer for which
\[
g^b(s)\ne h^a(s).
\tag{3}
\]
Both $g(s)$ and $h(s)$ are at least $s$. If both were equal to $s$, then (3) would be false. Interchanging $A$ and $B$ if necessary, assume
\[
g(s)>s.
\]
By the maximality of $s$, equation (2) holds at $g(s)$. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
g(g^b(s))
&=g^b(g(s))\\
&=h^a(g(s))\\
&=g(h^a(s)),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality follows from (1). Since $g$ is injective,
\[
g^b(s)=h^a(s),
\]
contradicting (3).

Thus (2) holds for every positive integer, and hence
\[
A^{*b}=B^{*a}.
\]
