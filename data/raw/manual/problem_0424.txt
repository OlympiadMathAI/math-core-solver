ID: 0424

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Square-Free Integers, Divisibility, Rational Identities, Parametric Constructions

STATEMENT:
Let $a,b,c,d$ be positive integers satisfying
\[
\frac{ab}{a+b}
+
\frac{cd}{c+d}
=
\frac{(a+b)(c+d)}{a+b+c+d}.
\]
Determine all possible values of
\[
a+b+c+d.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The possible values are exactly the positive integers that are not square-free.

First, let $k,\ell$ be positive integers and take
\[
a=\ell,\qquad
b=k\ell,\qquad
c=k\ell,\qquad
d=k^2\ell.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{ab}{a+b}
+
\frac{cd}{c+d}
=
\frac{k\ell}{k+1}
+
\frac{k^2\ell}{k+1}
=
k\ell,
\]
while
\[
\frac{(a+b)(c+d)}{a+b+c+d}
=
\frac{(k+1)\ell\cdot k(k+1)\ell}{(k+1)^2\ell}
=
k\ell.
\]
Thus the equality holds, and
\[
a+b+c+d=\ell(k+1)^2.
\]
Every non-square-free positive integer has this form: if
\[
p^2\mid N
\]
for some prime $p$, take
\[
k=p-1,\qquad \ell=\frac{N}{p^2}.
\]
Hence every non-square-free integer is attainable.

Conversely, suppose the displayed equality holds, and write
\[
S=a+b+c+d.
\]
After clearing denominators,
\[
\bigl(ab(c+d)+cd(a+b)\bigr)S
=
(a+b)^2(c+d)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Assume for contradiction that $S$ is square-free. Let $p$ be any prime divisor of $S$. Equation (1) implies
\[
p\mid(a+b)(c+d).
\]
If $p\mid a+b$, then
\[
p\mid S-(a+b)=c+d;
\]
and the same conclusion follows if $p\mid c+d$. Thus every prime divisor of $S$ divides both
\[
a+b
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c+d.
\]
Since $S$ is square-free, this implies
\[
S\mid a+b.
\]
But
\[
0<a+b<S,
\]
a contradiction.

Therefore $S$ is not square-free, and the stated set of values is complete.
