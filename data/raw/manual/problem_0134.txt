ID: 0134

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Locus, Radical Axes, Miquel's Theorem, Directed Angles, Inversion, Pappus' Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $\Gamma$ be a fixed circle and let $A,B,C$ be three fixed points on it. Fix a real number $\lambda\in(0,1)$. For a variable point
\[
P\in\Gamma\setminus\{A,B,C\},
\]
let $M$ be the point of the segment $CP$ satisfying
\[
CM=\lambda\,CP.
\]
Let $Q$ be the second common point of the circumcircles of triangles $AMP$ and $BMC$. Prove that, as $P$ varies, $Q$ lies on a fixed circle.

SOLUTION 1:
All directed angles in this solution are taken modulo $180^\circ$.

Let $D$ be the fixed point of $AB$ such that
\[
BD=\lambda\,BA.
\]
We prove that either $Q=D$, or
\[
\measuredangle(DQ,QB)=\measuredangle(AB,BC).
\tag{1}
\]
Equation (1) says precisely that $Q$ belongs to the fixed circle through $B,D$ whose tangent at $B$ is parallel to $BC$.

Let
\[
\omega_A=(AMP),
\qquad
\omega_B=(BMC).
\]
The pairwise radical axes of $\Gamma,\omega_A,\omega_B$ are, respectively,
\[
AP,
\qquad
BC,
\qquad
MQ.
\]
They are therefore either concurrent or parallel.

\textbf{Parallel case.}
Suppose that
\[
AP\parallel BC\parallel MQ.
\]
The parallel chords $AP$ and $BC$ of $\Gamma$ have the same perpendicular bisector. This line is also the perpendicular bisector of the parallel chords $AP$ and $MQ$ of $\omega_A$. Reflection in this common perpendicular bisector sends
\[
A\longleftrightarrow P,
\qquad
B\longleftrightarrow C,
\qquad
M\longleftrightarrow Q.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{BQ}{BA}=\frac{CM}{CP}=\lambda=\frac{BD}{BA},
\]
and hence $Q=D$.

\textbf{Concurrent case.}
Let
\[
X=AP\cap BC\cap MQ.
\]
By Miquel's theorem applied to triangle $XPC$, the points
\[
A,Q,B,X
\]
are concyclic; call their circle $\Omega$.

Let $Y$ be the reflection of $X$ in the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Reflection interchanges $A$ and $B$, so it preserves the circle through $A,B,X$; hence $Y\in\Omega$. The triangles $XPC$ and $XBA$ are directly similar because $A,B,C,P$ lie on $\Gamma$. The reflection also gives
\[
\triangle YAB\cong\triangle XBA.
\]
Thus the triangles $YAB$ and $XPC$ are directly similar, with the correspondence
\[
Y\leftrightarrow X,
\qquad
A\leftrightarrow P,
\qquad
B\leftrightarrow C.
\]
Since
\[
\frac{BD}{BA}=\frac{CM}{CP}=\lambda,
\]
the points $D$ and $M$ correspond under this similarity. Therefore
\[
\measuredangle(MX,XP)=\measuredangle(DY,YA).
\tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, $A,Q,X,Y$ are concyclic, so
\[
\measuredangle(QY,YA)=\measuredangle(QX,XA)
=\measuredangle(MX,XP).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2) and (3) show that $D,Y,Q$ are collinear.

Finally, using the cyclic quadrilateral $A,B,Q,Y$ and then the reflection symmetry,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(DQ,QB)
&=\measuredangle(YQ,QB)\\
&=\measuredangle(YA,AB)\\
&=\measuredangle(AB,BX)\\
&=\measuredangle(AB,BC).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (1) holds, and the locus of $Q$ is contained in the required fixed circle.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\omega_A,\omega_B$ be as in Solution 1. If the three radical axes $AP,BC,MQ$ are parallel, the reflection argument from the parallel case above gives the fixed point $Q=D$. We may therefore assume that they meet at a finite point
\[
X=AP\cap BC\cap MQ.
\]
Again, Miquel's theorem gives a circle $\Omega$ through $A,Q,B,X$.

We first note that $Q$ always lies on the same side of $BC$ as $A$. Indeed, if $P$ lies on the arc $BAC$ of $\Gamma$, then $X$ lies outside $\Gamma$, and $Q$ lies on the ray $XM$; hence $A$ and $Q$ are on the same side of $BC$. If $P$ lies on the other arc $BC$, then $X$ lies inside $\Gamma$ and $M,Q$ are on opposite sides of $BC$, which again places $Q$ on the same side of $BC$ as $A$.

We prove that
\[
\frac{QB}{\sin\angle QBC}
\tag{1}
\]
is constant. Since $A,Q,B,X$ are concyclic, the sine rule in $\Omega$ gives
\[
\frac{QX}{\sin\angle QBX}
=
\frac{AX}{\sin\angle ABX}
=
\frac{AX}{\sin\angle ABC},
\tag{2}
\]
because $B,X,C$ are collinear.

The cyclic quadrilateral $B,Q,M,C$, together with $X\in BC\cap QM$, gives
\[
\triangle XBQ\sim\triangle XMC,
\]
so
\[
\frac{QB}{QX}=\frac{CM}{CX}
=\lambda\frac{CP}{CX}.
\tag{3}
\]
Also, from the cyclic quadrilateral $A,B,C,P$,
\[
\triangle XCP\sim\triangle XAB,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{CP}{CX}=\frac{AB}{AX}.
\tag{4}
\]
Multiplying (2)--(4), we obtain
\[
\frac{QB}{\sin\angle QBC}
=
\lambda\frac{AB}{\sin\angle ABC},
\tag{5}
\]
which is independent of $P$.

For a circle through $B$ tangent to $BC$ at $B$, the quantity $QB/\sin\angle QBC$ is its diameter. Equation (5), together with the fact that $Q$ always lies on the same side of $BC$, therefore determines one fixed circle. Hence $Q$ lies on a fixed circle.

SOLUTION 3:
Perform an inversion $\iota$ with center $C$, and write $X'$ for the image of a point $X$.

The fixed circle $\Gamma$ becomes a fixed line $g$ through $A'$ and $B'$, while $P'$ varies on $g$. The locus of $M$ before inversion is the circle obtained from $\Gamma$ by the homothety with center $C$ and ratio $\lambda$. Consequently, after inversion $M'$ varies on a fixed line $h$ parallel to $g$, and
\[
M'=CP'\cap h.
\]
The circle $(AMP)$ becomes the circle
\[
\omega_A'=(A'M'P'),
\]
whereas the circle $(BMC)$, which passes through the inversion center $C$, becomes the line $B'M'$. Thus $Q'$ is the second intersection of $B'M'$ with $\omega_A'$.

Let
\[
J=h\cap CA',
\]
and let $\ell$ be the fixed line through $J$ parallel to $CB'$. Put
\[
V=\ell\cap B'M'.
\]
Apply Pappus' theorem to the two collinear triples
\[
(C,J,A')
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(V,B',M').
\]
Two of the three Pappus intersection points are points at infinity:
\[
CB'\parallel JV
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
JM'=h\parallel A'B'=g.
\]
Hence the third one is also at infinity, and therefore
\[
CM'\parallel A'V.
\tag{1}
\]

Because $A',M',P',Q'$ are concyclic,
\[
\measuredangle(Q'A',A'P')
=
\measuredangle(Q'M',M'P').
\]
Now $A',B',P'$ are collinear, $B',Q',M',V$ are collinear, and $M'P'\parallel A'V$ by (1). Hence
\[
\measuredangle(Q'A',A'B')
=
\measuredangle(B'V,VA').
\]
Together with the common angle at $B'$, this proves
\[
\triangle B'Q'A'\sim\triangle B'A'V.
\]
Consequently,
\[
(B'A')^2=B'Q'\cdot B'V.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus $Q'$ is the image of $V$ under the fixed inversion $\jmath$ with center $B'$ and radius $B'A'$. Since $V$ moves on the fixed line $\ell$, the point $Q'$ moves on the fixed circle
\[
\Theta=\jmath(\ell).
\]

The circle $\Theta$ does not pass through $C$. Indeed, the inverse of $C$ under $\jmath$ lies on the line $B'C$, while $\ell\parallel B'C$ and is distinct from it. Therefore the first inversion $\iota^{-1}$ maps $\Theta$ to a circle, not to a line. This image is fixed and contains every possible point $Q$. Hence $Q$ lies on a fixed circle.
