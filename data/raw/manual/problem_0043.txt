ID: 0043

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Circle Geometry, Tangency, Reflection, Circumcenters

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle. Let $\omega$ be a circle whose center $L$ lies on the side $BC$. Suppose that $\omega$ is tangent to $AB$ at $B'$ and to $AC$ at $C'$. Suppose also that the circumcenter $O$ of triangle $ABC$ lies on the shorter arc $B'C'$ of $\omega$. Prove that the circumcircle of $ABC$ and $\omega$ intersect at two points.

SOLUTION 1:
The tangency points $B'$ and $C'$ are the perpendicular projections of $L$ onto $AB$ and $AC$. Since $ABC$ is acute and $L\in BC$, both points lie in the interiors of the corresponding sides. The shorter arc $B'C'$ of $\omega$ lies inside triangle $AB'C'$, so the point $O$ lies inside that triangle. In particular,
\[
\angle BOC<\angle B'OC'.
\tag{1}
\]

Put
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC.
\]
Since $O$ is the center of the circumcircle of $ABC$,
\[
\angle BOC=2\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, $O$ lies on $\omega$, so $\angle B'OC'$ is an inscribed angle of $\omega$. It subtends the major arc $B'C'$, and hence
\[
2\angle B'OC'=360^\circ-\angle B'LC'.
\tag{3}
\]
The quadrilateral $AB'LC'$ is a kite with right angles at $B'$ and $C'$. Therefore
\[
\angle B'LC'=180^\circ-\angle B'AC'=180^\circ-\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (1)--(4), we obtain
\[
2\alpha
<\frac{360^\circ-(180^\circ-\alpha)}2
=90^\circ+\frac\alpha2.
\]
Thus
\[
\alpha<60^\circ.
\tag{5}
\]

Let $O'$ be the reflection of $O$ in the line $BC$. Since the center $L$ of $\omega$ lies on $BC$ and $O\in\omega$, reflection in $BC$ preserves $\omega$ and therefore
\[
O'\in\omega.
\]
The point $O$ is inside the circumcircle of $ABC$. We claim that $O'$ is outside it. Indeed,
\[
\angle BO'C=\angle BOC=2\alpha,
\]
and by (5),
\[
\angle BO'C+\angle BAC=3\alpha<180^\circ.
\]
For a point on the side of $BC$ opposite to $A$, equality with $180^\circ$ characterizes the circumcircle of $ABC$; the strict inequality shows that $O'$ lies outside that circle.

Thus the circle $\omega$ contains one point, namely $O$, inside the circumcircle of $ABC$, and another point, namely $O'$, outside it. Each of the two arcs of $\omega$ joining $O$ to $O'$ must cross the circumcircle. The two crossings are distinct, so the two circles intersect at two points.
