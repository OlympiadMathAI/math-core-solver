ID: 0146

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Sequences, Telescoping Sums, Induction

STATEMENT:
Suppose that a sequence $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ of positive real numbers satisfies
\[
a_{k+1}\ge \frac{k a_k}{a_k^2+(k-1)}
\tag{1}
\]
for every positive integer $k$. Prove that
\[
a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n\ge n
\]
for every integer $n\ge2$.

SOLUTION 1:
From (1),
\[
\frac{k}{a_{k+1}}
\le \frac{a_k^2+(k-1)}{a_k}
=a_k+\frac{k-1}{a_k},
\]
and therefore
\[
a_k\ge \frac{k}{a_{k+1}}-\frac{k-1}{a_k}.
\tag{2}
\]
Summing (2) for $k=1,2,\ldots,m$ gives the telescoping estimate
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_m
&\ge \left(\frac1{a_2}-\frac0{a_1}\right)
 +\left(\frac2{a_3}-\frac1{a_2}\right)
 +\cdots
 +\left(\frac m{a_{m+1}}-\frac{m-1}{a_m}\right)\\
&=\frac m{a_{m+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

We now use induction on $n$. For $n=2$, condition (1) with $k=1$ gives
\[
a_2\ge \frac1{a_1},
\]
so
\[
a_1+a_2\ge a_1+\frac1{a_1}\ge2.
\]

Assume that the assertion is true for some $n\ge2$. If $a_{n+1}\ge1$, then
\[
a_1+\cdots+a_n+a_{n+1}\ge n+1.
\]
If $a_{n+1}<1$, then (3), with $m=n$, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_1+\cdots+a_n+a_{n+1}
&\ge \frac n{a_{n+1}}+a_{n+1}\\
&=\frac{n-1}{a_{n+1}}
 +\left(\frac1{a_{n+1}}+a_{n+1}\right)\\
&>(n-1)+2=n+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the induction closes, proving the result for every $n\ge2$.
