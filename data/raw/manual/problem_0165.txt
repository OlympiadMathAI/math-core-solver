ID: 0165

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangential Quadrilaterals, Menelaus' Theorem, Similar Triangles, Incenter Geometry

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral, and let $P,Q,R,S$ lie on the sides $AB,BC,CD,DA$, respectively. Let the segments $PR$ and $QS$ meet at $O$. Suppose that each of the quadrilaterals $APOS$, $BQOP$, $CROQ$, and $DSOR$ has an incircle. Prove that the lines $AC$, $PQ$, and $RS$ are either concurrent or parallel.

SOLUTION 1:
Using directed lengths, Menelaus' theorem applied to triangles $ABC$ and $ACD$ shows that the lines $AC$, $PQ$, and $RS$ pass through one common point, possibly a point at infinity, if and only if
\[
\frac{AP}{PB}\cdot
\frac{BQ}{QC}\cdot
\frac{CR}{RD}\cdot
\frac{DS}{SA}=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus it is enough to prove (1).

We first establish a lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} Let $EFGH$ be a tangential quadrilateral with incenter $N$. Then
\[
\frac{EF\cdot FG}{GH\cdot HE}
=
\frac{FN^2}{HN^2}.
\tag{2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Since $N$ lies on all four angle bisectors,
\[
\angle ENH+\angle GNF
=
\angle FNE+\angle HNG
=
180^\circ,
\]
while
\[
\angle FGN=\angle NGH
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle HEN=\angle NEF.
\]
Applying the sine rule in the four triangles adjacent to the sides of the quadrilateral, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{EF}{FN}\cdot\frac{FG}{FN}
&=
\frac{\sin\angle FNE\,\sin\angle GNF}
     {\sin\angle NEF\,\sin\angle FGN}\
&=
\frac{\sin\angle HNG\,\sin\angle ENH}
     {\sin\angle NGH\,\sin\angle HEN}\
&=
\frac{GH}{HN}\cdot\frac{HE}{HN}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is equivalent to (2). $\square$

Let $I,J,K,L$ be the incenters of $APOS$, $BQOP$, $CROQ$, and $DSOR$, respectively. Applying the lemma to these four quadrilaterals gives
\[
\frac{AP\cdot PO}{OS\cdot SA}
=
\frac{PI^2}{SI^2},
\]
\[
\frac{BQ\cdot QO}{OP\cdot PB}
=
\frac{QJ^2}{PJ^2},
\]
\[
\frac{CR\cdot RO}{OQ\cdot QC}
=
\frac{RK^2}{QK^2},
\]
and
\[
\frac{DS\cdot SO}{OR\cdot RD}
=
\frac{SL^2}{RL^2}.
\]
Multiplying these equalities and cancelling the factors containing $O$, we find
\[
\frac{AP}{PB}\cdot
\frac{BQ}{QC}\cdot
\frac{CR}{RD}\cdot
\frac{DS}{SA}
=
\frac{PI^2}{PJ^2}\cdot
\frac{QJ^2}{QK^2}\cdot
\frac{RK^2}{RL^2}\cdot
\frac{SL^2}{SI^2}.
\tag{3}
\]

At $P$, the internal angles of the two tangential quadrilaterals $APOS$ and $BQOP$ are supplementary, so their bisectors $PI$ and $PJ$ are perpendicular. Similarly, $OI\perp OJ$. Hence
\[
\angle IPJ=\angle IOJ=90^\circ,
\]
and $I,P,J,O$ are cyclic.

Likewise, at $Q$ and $O$ we have
\[
\angle JQK=\angle JOK=90^\circ,
\]
so $J,Q,K,O$ are cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle QKJ
=
\angle QOJ
=
\angle JOP
=
\angle JIP,
\]
where the middle equality holds because $OJ$ bisects $\angle QOP$. The right triangles $IPJ$ and $KQJ$ are thus similar, and
\[
\frac{PI}{PJ}=\frac{QK}{QJ}.
\tag{4}
\]
By the analogous argument at $R$ and $S$,
\[
\frac{RK}{RL}=\frac{SI}{SL}.
\tag{5}
\]
Substituting (4) and (5) into (3), the right-hand side becomes $1$. Hence (1) holds.

Therefore the lines $AC$, $PQ$, and $RS$ are concurrent, with the parallel case corresponding to a common point at infinity.
