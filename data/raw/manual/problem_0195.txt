ID: 0195

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Euler Line, Nine-Point Circle, Spiral Similarity, Power of a Point, Circumcentres

STATEMENT:
Let $D$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to the Euler line of an acute scalene triangle $ABC$. A circle $\omega$ with centre $S$ passes through $A$ and $D$, and it meets the sides $AB$ and $AC$ again at $X$ and $Y$, respectively. Let $P$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that the circumcentre of triangle $XSY$ is equidistant from $P$ and $M$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $H$ and $O$ be the orthocentre and circumcentre of $\triangle ABC$. Let $Q$ be the midpoint of $AH$, and let $N$ be the nine-point centre. Then $Q$ lies on the nine-point circle,
\[
N\ \text{is the midpoint of }QM,
\qquad
QM\parallel AO.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the perpendicular from $S$ to $XY$ meet $QM$ at $S_0$. Let $E$ be the foot of the altitude from $B$ to $AC$. Since both $Q$ and $S$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $AD$, directed-angle calculations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle SDQ
&=\angle QAS\\
&=\angle XAS-\angle XAQ\\
&=\left(\frac\pi2-\angle AYX\right)-\angle BAP\\
&=\angle CBA-\angle AYX\\
&=(\angle CBA-\angle ACB)-\angle BCA-\angle AYX\\
&=\angle PEM-(\angle BCA+\angle AYX)\\
&=\angle PQM-\angle(BC,XY)\\
&=\frac\pi2-\angle(S_0Q,BC)-\angle(BC,XY)\\
&=\angle SS_0Q.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
D,S_0,S,Q\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let the line through $N$ perpendicular to $BC$ meet $SS_0$ at $O_1$. In the special case in which these two lines coincide, $S$ is the midpoint of $AO$ and the desired assertion is immediate. We therefore assume that $O_1$ is defined as an ordinary intersection.

Because
\[
SQ\parallel OD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
QA\parallel O_1N,
\]
we have
\[
\angle DS_0O_1
=\angle DQS
=\angle SQA
=\angle(OD,O_1N)
=\angle DNO_1.
\]
Thus
\[
D,O_1,S_0,N
\]
are concyclic. Combining this circle with (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle SDS_0
&=\angle SQS_0\\
&=\angle(SQ,QS_0)\\
&=\angle(ND,NS_0)\\
&=\angle DNS_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore $SD$ is tangent at $D$ to the circle through $D,O_1,S_0,N$. It follows that
\[
SS_0\cdot SO_1=SD^2=SX^2.
\tag{3}
\]

We next show that $S$ and $S_0$ are reflections in $XY$. The sine rule in the two cyclic configurations yields
\[
\frac{SS_0}{\sin\angle SQS_0}
=\frac{SQ}{\sin\angle SS_0Q}
=\frac{SQ}{\sin\angle SDQ}
=\frac{SQ}{\sin\angle SAQ}
=\frac{SA}{\sin\angle SQA}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
SS_0
&=SA\cdot
\frac{\sin\angle SQS_0}{\sin\angle SQA}\\
&=SA\cdot
\frac{\sin\angle HOA}{\sin\angle OHA}\\
&=SA\cdot\frac{AH}{AO}\\
&=2SA\cos A.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The last quantity is twice the distance from $S$ to $XY$.

It remains only to identify on which side of $XY$ the point $S_0$ lies. The points $S$ and $C$ lie on the same side of the perpendicular bisector of $PM$ exactly when
\[
\angle SAC<\angle OAC,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
\angle YXA>\angle CBA.
\]
This shows that $S$ and $O_1$ lie on opposite sides of $XY$. Since $S_0$ lies on the ray $SO_1$, the points $S$ and $S_0$ cannot lie on the same side of $XY$. Together with (4), they are reflections in $XY$.

Let $d$ be the diameter of the circumcircle of $\triangle XSY$. Since $SX=SY$ and $SS_0$ is twice the altitude from $S$ to $XY$,
\[
SS_0=\frac{2SX^2}{d}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) give
\[
d=2SO_1.
\]
The line $SO_1$ is the perpendicular bisector of $XY$, so $O_1$ is the circumcentre of $\triangle XSY$.

Finally, $N$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PM$, because $P$ and $M$ lie on the nine-point circle. By construction $NO_1\perp BC$, and hence $NO_1$ is that same perpendicular bisector. Therefore
\[
O_1P=O_1M,
\]
as required.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $H$ and $O$ be the orthocentre and circumcentre of $\triangle ABC$, and let $O_1$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle XSY$.

The centre $S$ of any circle through $A$ and $D$ lies on the fixed perpendicular bisector of $AD$. Choose two other possible centres $S'$ and $S''$ on this line, and denote the corresponding intersection points and circumcentres by
\[
X',Y',O_1'
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X'',Y'',O_1''.
\]

The lines $XX'$ and $YY'$ meet at $A$, while the circumcircles of $AXY$ and $AX'Y'$ meet at $A$ and $D$. Therefore there is a spiral similarity centred at $D$ taking $XY$ to $X'Y'$. Moreover,
\[
\angle SXY
=\frac\pi2-\angle YAX
=\frac\pi2-\angle Y'AX'
=\angle S'X'Y',
\]
and similarly
\[
\angle SYX=\angle S'Y'X'.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle SXY\sim\triangle S'X'Y'
\]
directly. The same spiral similarity centred at $D$ maps
\[
S,X,Y,O_1
\quad\text{to}\quad
S',X',Y',O_1'.
\]
The analogous statement holds for $S''$. Hence there is a spiral similarity taking the collinear points
\[
S,S',S''
\]
to
\[
O_1,O_1',O_1''.
\]
Therefore the circumcentre $O_1$ always lies on a fixed straight line as $S$ varies.

We identify this line by considering two special choices.

First, take $S$ to be the midpoint of $AH$. Then $X$ and $Y$ are the feet of the altitudes from $C$ and $B$, and the circumcircle of $\triangle XSY$ is the nine-point circle of $\triangle ABC$. Thus $O_1$ is the nine-point centre, and
\[
O_1P=O_1M
\]
because $P$ and $M$ both lie on the nine-point circle.

Second, take $S'$ to be the midpoint of $AO$. Then $X'$ and $Y'$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, so
\[
X'Y'\parallel BC.
\]
The point $S'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PM$. Since the perpendicular bisectors of $X'Y'$ and $PM$ are parallel and both pass through $S'$, they coincide. Hence
\[
O_1'P=O_1'M.
\]

The fixed line containing all possible positions of $O_1$ contains these two distinct points $O_1$ and $O_1'$, both of which lie on the perpendicular bisector of $PM$. It is therefore exactly that perpendicular bisector. Thus every possible circumcentre of $\triangle XSY$ is equidistant from $P$ and $M$.
