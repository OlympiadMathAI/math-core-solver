ID: 0385

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Homothety Centres, Monge's Theorem, Hyperbolas, Brianchon's Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle, and let $H$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$.

A point $P$ varies so that the internal angle bisectors $k$ and $\ell$ of
\[
\angle PBC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle PCB,
\]
respectively, meet at a point of segment $AH$.

Let
\[
E=k\cap AC,\qquad
F=\ell\cap AB,\qquad
Q=EF\cap AH.
\]

Prove that, as $P$ varies, line $PQ$ passes through a fixed point.

SOLUTION 1:
Reflect line $BC$ in $AB$ and in $AC$. Let the two reflected lines meet at $K$. We prove that
\[
P,Q,K
\]
are collinear; hence $K$ is the required fixed point.

Let
\[
I=BE\cap CF.
\]
Then $I$ is the incenter of triangle $PBC$, and by hypothesis
\[
A,I,H
\]
are collinear.

For a point $U$ and a positive number $r$, write $(U,r)$ for the circle with centre $U$ and radius $r$. Define

\[
\omega_A=(A,AH),\qquad
\omega_I=(I,IH),
\]
let $\omega_K$ be the incircle of triangle $KBC$, and let $\omega_P$ be the $P$-excircle of triangle $PBC$.

The circles $\omega_A$ and $\omega_I$ are tangent at $H$. Thus $H$ is their external homothety centre. The complete quadrilateral formed by
\[
AB,\ AC,\ BE,\ CF
\]
gives the harmonic relation
\[
(A,I;Q,H)=-1.
\tag{1}
\]
For two internally tangent circles, the second homothety centre is the harmonic conjugate of the tangency point with respect to their centres. Hence $Q$ is the internal homothety centre of
\[
\omega_A
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\omega_I.
\tag{2}
\]

Because $AB$ and $AC$ are the external angle bisectors at $B$ and $C$ of triangle $KBC$, the circle $\omega_A$ is the $K$-excircle of that triangle. Therefore $K$ is the external homothety centre of $\omega_A$ and $\omega_K$.

Likewise, $P$ is the external homothety centre of $\omega_I$ and $\omega_P$.

Let $T$ be the point where $\omega_P$ touches $BC$, and let $T'$ be the point where $\omega_K$ touches $BC$. In triangle $PBC$, the incircle touches $BC$ at $H$, so the standard tangent-length formulas for the incircle and the $P$-excircle give
\[
CT=BH,\qquad BT=CH.
\tag{3}
\]
In triangle $KBC$, the $K$-excircle touches $BC$ at $H$, and its incircle touches $BC$ at $T'$. The same formulas give
\[
CT'=BH,\qquad BT'=CH.
\]
Thus
\[
T'=T,
\]
and $\omega_K$ and $\omega_P$ are tangent at $T$.

Let $S$ be the internal homothety centre of $\omega_A$ and $\omega_P$, and let $S'$ be the internal homothety centre of $\omega_I$ and $\omega_K$. Both points lie on $BC$.

Denote the radii of the four circles by
\[
r_A,r_I,r_P,r_K.
\]
For a triangle with incircle radius $r$ and opposite excircle radius $r_a$, one has
\[
rr_a=(s-b)(s-c),
\tag{4}
\]
where $s$ is the semiperimeter and $b,c$ are the two sides adjacent to the corresponding vertex.

Applying (4) to triangle $PBC$ gives
\[
r_Ir_P=BH\cdot CH.
\]
Applying it to triangle $KBC$ gives
\[
r_Kr_A=BT\cdot CT.
\]
By (3),
\[
r_Ir_P=r_Kr_A,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{r_A}{r_P}=\frac{r_I}{r_K}.
\tag{5}
\]

Since the circles in each pair touch the same line $BC$ at $H$ and $T$,
\[
\frac{HS}{ST}=\frac{r_A}{r_P},
\qquad
\frac{HS'}{S'T}=\frac{r_I}{r_K}.
\]
Equation (5) therefore gives
\[
S=S'.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply the generalized Monge theorem to the circles
\[
\omega_A,\omega_I,\omega_K.
\]
Their two internal homothety centres and their external homothety centre are
\[
Q,\ S,\ K,
\]
so these three points are collinear.

Apply the same theorem to
\[
\omega_A,\omega_I,\omega_P.
\]
The corresponding homothety centres are
\[
Q,\ S,\ P,
\]
so these points are also collinear.

Consequently,
\[
P,Q,K
\]
are collinear. The point $K$ depends only on triangle $ABC$, completing the proof.

SOLUTION 2:
If $AB=AC$, then $AH$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. Since the incenter $I=BE\cap CF$ of triangle $PBC$ lies on $AH$, the half-angles at $B$ and $C$ are equal, so
\[
PB=PC.
\]
Hence $P\in AH$. The entire construction is symmetric about $AH$, so $E$ and $F$ are reflections of one another and $Q\in AH$. Thus
\[
PQ=AH,
\]
and the result is immediate.

Assume from now on that
\[
AB\ne AC.
\]

Let
\[
I=BE\cap CF.
\]
Then $I$ is the incenter of triangle $PBC$, and $PI$ is its third internal angle bisector.

Since the incircle of triangle $PBC$ touches $BC$ at $H$, equal tangent lengths give
\[
PB-PC=BH-CH.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the possible points $P$ lie on one fixed branch $\mathcal H$ of the hyperbola with foci $B,C$. The point $H$ is a vertex of this branch.

The internal bisector $PI$ of the angle between the focal radii $PB$ and $PC$ is tangent to $\mathcal H$ at $P$. Also, the tangent to $\mathcal H$ at the vertex $H$ is
\[
AH=AI.
\]

Let $K\ne P$ be the second intersection of $PQ$ with $\mathcal H$. We prove that $AK$ is tangent to $\mathcal H$ at $K$. Since $AH$ is already one tangent from $A$ to the fixed hyperbola, this identifies $K$ as the other, fixed, point of tangency from $A$.

Define
\[
G=KI\cap AP,
\qquad
M=PI\cap AK.
\]
The complete quadrilateral $BCEF$ gives
\[
(H,Q;I,A)=-1.
\tag{2}
\]
In the complete quadrangle $APIK$, the three diagonal points are
\[
Q=AI\cap PK,\qquad
G=AP\cap IK,\qquad
M=AK\cap PI.
\]
The harmonic property of a complete quadrangle and (2) imply
\[
H,G,M
\]
are collinear.

Now consider the degenerate hexagon
\[
A,H,I,P,M,K.
\]
Its three Brianchon diagonals are
\[
AP,\qquad HM,\qquad IK,
\]
and they concur at $G$. By the converse of Brianchon's theorem, there is a conic tangent to
\[
AI\ \text{at }H,\qquad
IM\ \text{at }P,\qquad
AM\ \text{at }K.
\]
This conic has the points $H,P,K$ and the tangents at $H,P$ in common with $\mathcal H$. These five incidence-and-tangency conditions determine a conic uniquely, so it is $\mathcal H$ itself. Therefore
\[
AK
\]
is tangent to $\mathcal H$ at $K$.

The point $K$ is consequently fixed, and by its definition
\[
P,Q,K
\]
are collinear for every admissible $P$. This proves the assertion.
