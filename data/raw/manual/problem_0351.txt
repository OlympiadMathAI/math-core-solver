ID: 0351

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Homothety, Pascal's Theorem, Radical Axes, Circle Centres

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral whose four sides have pairwise different lengths, and let $O$ be its circumcentre.

The internal angle bisectors of $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ADC$ meet $AC$ at $B_1$ and $D_1$, respectively. Let $O_B$ be the centre of the circle that passes through $B$ and is tangent to $AC$ at $D_1$. Similarly, let $O_D$ be the centre of the circle that passes through $D$ and is tangent to $AC$ at $B_1$.

Assume that
\[
BD_1\parallel DB_1.
\]
Prove that
\[
O\in O_BO_D.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
\Omega=(ABCD),
\]
and let $\gamma_B,\gamma_D$ be the circles with centres $O_B,O_D$, respectively.

Assume without loss of generality that
\[
AB>BC.
\]
The hypothesis then forces
\[
AD<DC.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if also $AD>DC$, the two internal angle-bisector rays $BB_1$ and $DD_1$ lie on the same side of $BD$, whereas the parallelism
\[
BD_1\parallel DB_1
\]
forces $B_1$ and $D_1$ to be separated by
\[
H=AC\cap BD.
\]
This is impossible.

Let $\gamma_B$ meet $\Omega$ again at $T_B$, and let $\gamma_D$ meet $\Omega$ again at $T_D$. Their common chords give
\[
BT_B\perp OO_B,
\qquad
DT_D\perp OO_D.
\]
Consequently, it is enough to prove
\[
BT_B\parallel DT_D.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
H=AC\cap BD,
\]
and let $h$ be the homothety centred at $H$ that sends $B$ to $D$. Since
\[
BD_1\parallel DB_1,
\]
the triangles $HBD_1$ and $HDB_1$ are homothetic, and therefore
\[
h(D_1)=B_1.
\tag{3}
\]

Let the tangents to $\Omega$ at $B$ and $D$ meet $AC$ at $L_B$ and $L_D$, respectively.

We first show that
\[
L_BB=L_BB_1.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle L_BBB_1
&=\angle L_BBC+\angle CBB_1\\
&=\angle BAL_B+\angle B_1BA\\
&=\angle BB_1L_B.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus triangle $L_BBB_1$ is isosceles.

The power of $L_B$ with respect to $\Omega$ is
\[
L_BB^2,
\]
while its power with respect to $\gamma_D$, which is tangent to $AC$ at $B_1$, is
\[
L_BB_1^2.
\]
By (4), the powers are equal. Hence $L_B$ lies on the radical axis of $\Omega$ and $\gamma_D$, so
\[
D,T_D,L_B
\]
are collinear. Similarly,
\[
B,T_B,L_D
\]
are collinear.

We now prove that
\[
h(L_D)=L_B.
\tag{5}
\]
Apply the sine rule in triangles $BHL_B$ and $DHL_D$. Using (4) and its analogous relation
\[
L_DD=L_DD_1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{HL_B}{\sin\angle HBL_B}
=
\frac{B_1L_B}{\sin\angle BHL_B}
\]
and
\[
\frac{HL_D}{\sin\angle HDL_D}
=
\frac{D_1L_D}{\sin\angle DHL_D}.
\]
The angles at $H$ have equal sines. Moreover, the tangents at $B$ and $D$ form equal angles with the chord $BD$, so
\[
\sin\angle HBL_B=\sin\angle HDL_D.
\]
Therefore, using directed lengths on $AC$,
\[
\frac{HL_B}{HL_D}
=
\frac{B_1L_B}{D_1L_D}
=
\frac{HL_B-B_1L_B}{HL_D-D_1L_D}
=
\frac{HB_1}{HD_1}.
\tag{6}
\]
By (3), the last ratio is the ratio of the homothety $h$. Hence (6) proves (5).

The homothety $h$ maps the line $BL_D$ to the line $DL_B$, so these lines are parallel. Since
\[
B,L_D,T_B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D,L_B,T_D
\]
are collinear, we obtain (2). It follows that
\[
OO_B\parallel OO_D.
\]
Both lines pass through $O$, so they coincide, and therefore
\[
O\in O_BO_D.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
\Omega=(ABCD),
\]
and let $\gamma_B,\gamma_D$ be the circles with centres $O_B,O_D$, respectively. Let $\gamma_B$ meet $\Omega$ again at $T_B$, and let $\gamma_D$ meet $\Omega$ again at $T_D$.

The common chords satisfy
\[
BT_B\perp OO_B,
\qquad
DT_D\perp OO_D.
\]
Thus it is enough to prove
\[
BT_B\parallel DT_D.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the line $BD_1$ meet $\Omega$ again at $X_B$, and let the line $T_BD_1$ meet $\Omega$ again at $Y_B$. The tangent--chord theorem for $\gamma_B$, followed by the cyclicity of $\Omega$, gives
\[
\angle BD_1C
=
\angle BT_BD_1
=
\angle BT_BY_B
=
\angle BX_BY_B.
\]
Since $B,D_1,X_B$ are collinear, this shows that
\[
X_BY_B\parallel AC.
\tag{2}
\]

Similarly, let $DB_1$ and $T_DB_1$ meet $\Omega$ again at $X_D$ and $Y_D$, respectively. Then
\[
X_DY_D\parallel AC.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $M_D$ and $M_B$ be the midpoints of the arcs $ABC$ and $ADC$ of $\Omega$, respectively. Then
\[
D,D_1,M_D
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B,B_1,M_B
\]
are collinear. Let $K$ be the midpoint of $AC$; then
\[
K\in M_BM_D.
\]

Apply Pascal's theorem to the hexagon
\[
M_D,D,X_D,X_B,B,M_B
\]
inscribed in $\Omega$. The intersections of two pairs of opposite sides are
\[
M_DD\cap X_BB=D_1
\]
and
\[
DX_D\cap BM_B=B_1.
\]
The third intersection is therefore on the line $D_1B_1=AC$. Since it also lies on $M_BM_D$, it is $K$. Hence
\[
K\in X_BX_D.
\tag{4}
\]

By (2)--(3), the quadrilateral
\[
X_BY_BX_DY_D
\]
is an isosceles trapezoid inscribed in $\Omega$. Its symmetry axis is $M_BM_D$. Its diagonals meet on that axis, and (4) shows that their intersection is $K$. Therefore
\[
K\in Y_BY_D
\]
as well.

Let $b$ and $d$ be the distances from the lines $X_BY_B$ and $X_DY_D$, respectively, to $AC$. The similar triangles with vertex $K$ give
\[
\frac{X_BY_B}{X_DY_D}=\frac bd.
\tag{5}
\]
The assumption
\[
BD_1\parallel DB_1
\]
means
\[
D_1X_B\parallel B_1X_D.
\]
Measuring these two transversals between the parallel lines in (2)--(3) and $AC$, we also have
\[
\frac{D_1X_B}{B_1X_D}=\frac bd.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
\triangle D_1X_BY_B
\sim
\triangle B_1X_DY_D.
\tag{7}
\]

Because $B,T_B,X_B,Y_B$ lie on $\Omega$ and the two secants through $D_1$ are
\[
D_1BX_B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_1T_BY_B,
\]
the secant theorem gives
\[
\triangle D_1T_BB
\sim
\triangle D_1X_BY_B.
\tag{8}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\triangle B_1T_DD
\sim
\triangle B_1X_DY_D.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (7)--(9),
\[
\triangle D_1T_BB
\sim
\triangle B_1T_DD.
\]
The corresponding sides $D_1B$ and $B_1D$ are parallel by hypothesis. Hence this similarity has no rotational component and is a homothety. The other corresponding sides are therefore parallel:
\[
BT_B\parallel DT_D.
\]
By the reduction at the beginning, this proves
\[
O\in O_BO_D.
\]
