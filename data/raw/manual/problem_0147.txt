ID: 0147

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Integer-Valued Functions, Periodicity, Additive Structure

STATEMENT:
Determine all functions $f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ such that
\[
f\bigl(x-f(y)\bigr)=f\bigl(f(x)\bigr)-f(y)-1
\tag{1}
\]
for all integers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=-1\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb Z}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+1\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb Z}.
\]
Both are readily checked.

Let $f$ be any solution. Put $x=0$ and $y=f(0)$ in (1). Then the integer
\[
z=-f(f(0))
\]
satisfies $f(z)=-1$. Substituting $y=z$ into (1) gives
\[
f(x+1)=f(f(x))
\tag{2}
\]
for every $x\in\mathbb Z$. Hence (1) becomes
\[
f\bigl(x-f(y)\bigr)=f(x+1)-f(y)-1.
\tag{3}
\]

Using (3) with $(x,y)=(x,x)$, followed by (2), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x+1)-f(x)
&=f\bigl(x-f(x)\bigr)+1\\
&=f\bigl(f(x-1-f(x))\bigr)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
But (3), with $(x,y)=(x-1,x)$, gives
\[
f(x-1-f(x))=f(x)-f(x)-1=-1.
\]
Consequently
\[
f(x+1)-f(x)=f(-1)+1=:A
\]
is independent of $x$. Thus, by induction in both directions,
\[
f(x)=Ax+B
\]
for all integers $x$, where $B=f(0)$.

Substituting this expression into (2) gives
\[
Ax+(A+B)=A^2x+(AB+B)
\]
for every integer $x$. Hence
\[
A^2=A,
\qquad
A+B=AB+B.
\]
Thus $A=0$ or $A=1$. If $A=1$, then $B=1$, so $f(x)=x+1$. If $A=0$, then $f$ is constant, and (1) forces its constant value to be $-1$. These are exactly the two stated solutions.

SOLUTION 2:
As in Solution 1, we first derive
\[
f(x+1)=f(f(x))
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
f\bigl(x-f(y)\bigr)=f(x+1)-f(y)-1.
\tag{3}
\]

If $f$ is injective, then (2) immediately implies
\[
f(x)=x+1
\]
for every integer $x$.

Suppose now that $f$ is not injective. Choose integers $a>b$ such that
\[
f(a)=f(b).
\]
Using (2) inductively, we get
\[
f(a+n)=f(b+n)
\]
for every $n\ge0$. Thus the sequence
\[
\gamma_n=f(b+n),\qquad n\ge0,
\]
is periodic and bounded. Let
\[
\varphi=\min_{n\ge0}\gamma_n,
\qquad
\psi=\max_{n\ge0}\gamma_n.
\]

Choose $y$ with $f(y)=\varphi$. By periodicity, we may choose an integer $x\ge a$ such that
\[
f(x-f(y))=\varphi.
\]
Since $x+1\ge a$, the value $f(x+1)$ lies between $\varphi$ and $\psi$. Equation (3) gives
\[
\varphi\le f(x+1)=\varphi+\varphi+1=2\varphi+1,
\]
so $\varphi\ge-1$.

Applying the same argument to the maximum $\psi$, we obtain
\[
f(x+1)=2\psi+1\le\psi,
\]
and hence $\psi\le-1$. Therefore
\[
\varphi=\psi=-1.
\]
It follows that
\[
f(t)=-1
\]
for every sufficiently large integer $t$.

Finally, fix any $y\in\mathbb Z$ and choose $x$ so large that both $x+1$ and $x-f(y)$ lie in this constant tail. Then (3) yields
\[
f(y)=f(x+1)-f(x-f(y))-1=(-1)-(-1)-1=-1.
\]
Thus $f$ is the constant function $-1$. Together with the injective case, this gives precisely the two solutions.

SOLUTION 3:
Let
\[
d=f(0).
\]
Putting $x=f(y)$ in (1), we obtain
\[
f^3(y)=f(y)+d+1,
\tag{2}
\]
where $f^3=f\circ f\circ f$. Replacing $x$ by $f(x)$ in (1) and then using (2), we get
\[
f\bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr)=f(x)-f(y)+d.
\tag{3}
\]

Consider the set
\[
E=\{f(x)-d:x\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
If $a,b\in E$, choose $x,y$ such that $f(x)=a+d$ and $f(y)=b+d$. Equation (3) gives
\[
f(a-b)=a-b+d,
\]
so $a-b\in E$. Hence $E$ is closed under taking differences.

If $E=\{0\}$, then $f$ is constant, and (1) shows that $f\equiv-1$. Assume henceforth that $E\ne\{0\}$. Since $E$ is a nonzero additive subgroup of $\mathbb Z$, there is a positive integer $k$ such that
\[
E=k\mathbb Z.
\]
Consequently the range of $f$ is
\[
\{kt+d:t\in\mathbb Z\},
\tag{4}
\]
and, because every $kt\in E$,
\[
f(kt)=kt+d
\tag{5}
\]
for every $t\in\mathbb Z$.

In particular, $f(k)=k+d$. Comparing equation (1) with $y=0$ and with $y=k$, and using $f(0)=d$, gives
\[
f(z+k)=f(z)+k
\tag{6}
\]
for every integer $z$. Thus on every residue class modulo $k$, the function $f$ has slope $1$.

All values of $f$ belong to the single residue class $d\pmod k$ by (4). Hence there is a constant $c$ such that
\[
f(f(x))=f(x)+c
\]
for every $x$. Equation (1) therefore becomes
\[
f\bigl(x-f(y)\bigr)=f(x)-f(y)+c-1.
\tag{7}
\]

Reducing (1) modulo $k$ and using (4), we get
\[
d\equiv d-d-1\pmod k,
\]
so
\[
d\equiv-1\pmod k.
\]
Thus $-1$ occurs as a value of $f$. Choose $y$ with $f(y)=-1$ in (7). Then
\[
f(x+1)=f(x)+c
\]
for every integer $x$, so $f$ is linear with slope $c$. Comparing with (6) gives $c=1$. Therefore
\[
f(x)=x+d
\]
for all integers $x$. Finally, substituting this into (2) gives
\[
y+3d=y+2d+1,
\]
so $d=1$. Hence $f(x)=x+1$.

Together with the constant solution $f\equiv-1$, this completes the classification.
