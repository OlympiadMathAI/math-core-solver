ID: 0044

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Power of a Point, Signed Lengths, Circle Equations, Coordinates

STATEMENT:
Let $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ be a non-cyclic quadrilateral. Let $O_1$ and $r_1$ be the circumcenter and circumradius of triangle $A_2A_3A_4$. Define $O_2,O_3,O_4$ and $r_2,r_3,r_4$ analogously. Prove that
\[
\frac1{O_1A_1^2-r_1^2}
+\frac1{O_2A_2^2-r_2^2}
+\frac1{O_3A_3^2-r_3^2}
+\frac1{O_4A_4^2-r_4^2}
=0.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
M=A_1A_3\cap A_2A_4.
\]
Choose an orientation on each diagonal, and denote the corresponding signed distances by
\[
MA_1=x,\qquad MA_2=y,\qquad MA_3=z,\qquad MA_4=w.
\]
Since the quadrilateral is not cyclic,
\[
\Delta=yw-xz\ne0;
\]
indeed, $yw=xz$ would be the intersecting-chords criterion for cyclicity.

Let $\omega_1$ be the circumcircle of $A_2A_3A_4$, and let $B_1$ be its second intersection with the line $A_1A_3$. By the power-of-a-point relation at $M$,
\[
MB_1\cdot MA_3=MA_2\cdot MA_4,
\]
so
\[
MB_1=\frac{yw}{z}.
\]
The power of $A_1$ with respect to $\omega_1$ is therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
O_1A_1^2-r_1^2
&=A_1B_1\cdot A_1A_3\\
&=\left(\frac{yw}{z}-x\right)(z-x)\\
&=\frac{z-x}{z}\,(yw-xz)\\
&=\frac{z-x}{z}\,\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The three analogous calculations give
\[
O_2A_2^2-r_2^2=-\frac{w-y}{w}\,\Delta,
\]
\[
O_3A_3^2-r_3^2=\frac{x-z}{x}\,\Delta,
\]
and
\[
O_4A_4^2-r_4^2=-\frac{y-w}{y}\,\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^4\frac1{O_iA_i^2-r_i^2}
&=\frac1\Delta
\left(
\frac{z}{z-x}-\frac{w}{w-y}
+\frac{x}{x-z}-\frac{y}{y-w}
\right)\\
&=\frac1\Delta\bigl(1-1\bigr)\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the identity.

SOLUTION 2:
Introduce Cartesian coordinates. For each $i\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, let the circumcircle of the three vertices other than $A_i$ have equation
\[
p_i(X,Y)=X^2+Y^2+\ell_i(X,Y)=0,
\]
where $\ell_i$ is affine linear. Put
\[
q_i=O_iA_i^2-r_i^2.
\]
The number $q_i$ is the power of $A_i$ with respect to this circle, so
\[
q_i=p_i(A_i).
\]
It is nonzero because the four vertices are not concyclic.

Consider
\[
F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^4\frac{p_i(X,Y)}{q_i}.
\]
For a fixed $j$, the point $A_j$ lies on each circle $p_i=0$ with $i\ne j$. Hence
\[
F(A_j)=\frac{p_j(A_j)}{q_j}=1.
\]
Thus all four vertices lie on the locus
\[
F(X,Y)=1.
\tag{1}
\]
The left side of (1), after moving $1$ to the left, has the form
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^4\frac1{q_i}\right)(X^2+Y^2)
+\text{an affine-linear expression}.
\]
If this polynomial were nonzero, its zero set would be either a circle or a line. It contains all four vertices, but the vertices form a non-cyclic quadrilateral and do not all lie on one line. Therefore the polynomial must vanish identically. Its quadratic coefficient is consequently zero:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\frac1{q_i}=0.
\]
Since $q_i=O_iA_i^2-r_i^2$, this is exactly the required identity.
