ID: 0342

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Tournaments, Edge-Disjoint Paths, Menger Theorem, Network Cuts

STATEMENT:
The kingdom of Anisotropy consists of $n$ cities. Between every two cities there is exactly one direct one-way road.

A path from a city $X$ to a city $Y$ is a sequence of roads along which one can travel from $X$ to $Y$ without visiting a city more than once. A collection of paths is called \emph{diverse} if no road belongs to two paths in the collection.

Fix two distinct cities $A$ and $B$. Let $N_{AB}$ be the maximum number of paths in a diverse collection from $A$ to $B$, and define $N_{BA}$ analogously.

Prove that
\[
N_{AB}=N_{BA}
\]
if and only if the outdegrees of $A$ and $B$ are equal.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove the stronger identity
\[
N_{BA}-N_{AB}=d^+(B)-d^+(A),
\tag{1}
\]
where $d^+(V)$ denotes the number of roads leaving city $V$.

Write
\[
X\to Y
\]
when the road between $X$ and $Y$ is directed from $X$ to $Y$. By interchanging $A$ and $B$ if necessary, assume
\[
A\to B.
\]

Partition the other cities into four classes:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I&=\{C:A\to C\leftarrow B\},\\
\mathcal O&=\{C:A\leftarrow C\to B\},\\
\mathcal A&=\{C:A\to C\to B\},\\
\mathcal B&=\{C:A\leftarrow C\leftarrow B\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Call a path \emph{short} if it contains one or two roads.

We first prove a lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Every diverse collection of $p$ paths from $A$ to $B$ can be replaced by a diverse collection of at least $p$ such paths which contains every short path from $A$ to $B$.

\textit{Proof.}
If the one-road path $A\to B$ is absent, add it; no longer $A$-to-$B$ path can use this edge.

Now fix $C\in\mathcal A$ for which the path
\[
A\to C\to B
\]
is absent. If at most one current path uses one of the two edges
\[
A\to C,\qquad C\to B,
\]
remove that path if necessary and insert the short path.

Otherwise, two paths have the forms
\[
A\to C\rightsquigarrow B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A\rightsquigarrow C\to B.
\]
Replace them by
\[
A\to C\to B
\]
and the route obtained by concatenating the remaining part from $A$ to $C$ with the remaining part from $C$ to $B$. Delete any directed cycles from this route to obtain a path. No road is duplicated, and the number of paths does not decrease.

Doing this for every $C\in\mathcal A$ proves the lemma. $\square$

Start with a maximum diverse collection of $N_{AB}$ paths and apply the lemma. Let the resulting collection be $\mathcal P$. It contains the
\[
|\mathcal A|+1
\]
short paths from $A$ to $B$.

Every non-short path in $\mathcal P$ has the form
\[
A\to C\rightsquigarrow D\to B,
\qquad
C\in\mathcal I,\quad D\in\mathcal O.
\]
Replace it by
\[
B\to C\rightsquigarrow D\to A.
\]
These new paths are mutually edge-disjoint. Add all
\[
|\mathcal B|
\]
short paths
\[
B\to C\to A,
\qquad C\in\mathcal B.
\]
No path from $A$ to $B$ could have used an edge leaving $B$ or entering $A$, so the resulting collection from $B$ to $A$ is diverse. Its size is at least
\[
N_{AB}-(|\mathcal A|+1)+|\mathcal B|.
\tag{3}
\]

Let
\[
a=d^+(A),
\qquad
b=d^+(B).
\]
From (2) and $A\to B$,
\[
a=|\mathcal I|+|\mathcal A|+1,
\qquad
b=|\mathcal I|+|\mathcal B|.
\]
Thus (3) gives
\[
N_{BA}\ge N_{AB}+b-a.
\]
Interchanging $A$ and $B$ yields the reverse inequality, so (1) follows.

Therefore
\[
N_{AB}=N_{BA}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^+(A)=d^+(B).
\]

SOLUTION 2:
We again prove
\[
N_{BA}-N_{AB}=d^+(B)-d^+(A).
\tag{1}
\]

We use the edge form of Menger's theorem: in a finite directed graph, the maximum number of pairwise edge-disjoint paths from $s$ to $t$ equals the minimum number of edges directed from $S$ to $V\setminus S$, where $S$ ranges over all vertex sets containing $s$ but not $t$.

For a vertex set $S$ in a tournament, let
\[
e(S,V\setminus S)
\]
be the number of roads directed from $S$ to its complement. Since every pair of vertices of $S$ contributes exactly one internal road,
\[
e(S,V\setminus S)
=
\sum_{v\in S}d^+(v)-\binom{|S|}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Take any $(A,B)$-cut
\[
V=S_A\sqcup T_A,
\qquad
A\in S_A,\quad B\in T_A.
\]
Form the corresponding $(B,A)$-cut by swapping $A$ and $B$:
\[
S_B=(S_A\setminus\{A\})\cup\{B\},
\qquad
T_B=(T_A\setminus\{B\})\cup\{A\}.
\]
The sets $S_A$ and $S_B$ have the same size. Hence (2) gives
\[
e(S_B,T_B)
=
e(S_A,T_A)+d^+(B)-d^+(A).
\tag{3}
\]

Choose $(S_A,T_A)$ to be a minimum $(A,B)$-cut. By Menger's theorem and (3),
\[
N_{BA}
\le
N_{AB}+d^+(B)-d^+(A).
\tag{4}
\]
Applying the same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged gives
\[
N_{AB}
\le
N_{BA}+d^+(A)-d^+(B).
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) force (1), and the required equivalence follows immediately.
