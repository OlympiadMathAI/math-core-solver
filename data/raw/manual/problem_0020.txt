ID: 0020

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Convex Pentagons, Central Symmetry, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Trigonometry

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDE$ be a convex pentagon such that
\[
BC\parallel AE,\qquad
AB=BC+AE,\qquad
\angle ABC=\angle CDE.
\]
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $CE$, and let $O$ be the circumcenter of triangle $BCD$. Given that
\[
\angle DMO=90^\circ,
\]
prove that
\[
2\angle BDA=\angle CDE.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Choose $T$ on the ray $AE$ so that
\[
AT=AB.
\]
Since $AT=AB$, triangle $ABT$ is isosceles, and therefore
\[
\angle ATB=\angle ABT.
\]
Since $AT\parallel BC$, we also have
\[
\angle CBT=\angle ATB.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle CBT=\angle ABT,
\]
so $BT$ bisects $\angle ABC$. 
Thus $BT$ bisects $\angle ABC$.

Moreover,
\[
ET=AT-AE=AB-AE=BC.
\]
Together with $ET\parallel BC$, this shows that $BCTE$ is a parallelogram. Hence its diagonals $BT$ and $CE$ bisect each other, so $M$ is also the midpoint of $BT$.

Let $K$ be the reflection of $D$ in $M$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $DK$ and
\[
DM\perp OM,
\]
the line $OM$ is the perpendicular bisector of $DK$. Therefore
\[
OD=OK.
\]
As $O$ is the circumcenter of triangle $BCD$, the point $K$ also lies on the circumcircle of $BCD$.

Thus
\[
\angle BKC=\angle BDC.
\]
The half-turn about $M$ sends
\[
B\leftrightarrow T,\qquad
C\leftrightarrow E,\qquad
K\leftrightarrow D,
\]
so
\[
\angle TDE=\angle BKC=\angle BDC.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle BDT
=\angle BDE+\angle EDT
=\angle BDE+\angle BDC
=\angle CDE.
\]
By hypothesis,
\[
\angle BDT=\angle ABC.
\]
Since $AT\parallel BC$,
\[
\angle BAT+\angle ABC=180^\circ.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle BDT+\angle BAT=180^\circ,
\]
so $A,B,D,T$ are concyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle ADB=\angle ATB
=\frac12\angle ABC
=\frac12\angle CDE.
\]
This is the desired result.

SOLUTION 2:
Set
\[
\angle CBD=\alpha,\qquad
\angle BDC=\beta,\qquad
\angle ADE=\gamma,
\]
and write
\[
\angle ABC=\angle CDE=2\varphi.
\]
Angle chasing gives
\[
\angle ADB=2\varphi-\beta-\gamma,
\]
\[
\angle BCD=180^\circ-\alpha-\beta,
\]
\[
\angle AED=180^\circ-2\varphi+\alpha+\beta,
\]
and
\[
\angle DAE=2\varphi-\alpha-\beta-\gamma.
\]

Let $N$ be the midpoint of $CD$. Since $O$ is the circumcenter of triangle $BCD$,
\[
ON\perp CD.
\]
Together with $OM\perp DM$, this implies that $D,M,O,N$ are cyclic on the circle with diameter $DO$. In either possible configuration,
\[
\angle DMN=\alpha.
\]
Because $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of $CE$ and $CD$ in triangle $CDE$,
\[
MN\parallel DE.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle MDE=\alpha,
\qquad
\angle NDM=2\varphi-\alpha.
\]

Apply the sine rule in the triangles $ABD,ADE,BCD$, and $MND$. We obtain
\[
\frac{AB}{AD}
=
\frac{\sin(2\varphi-\beta-\gamma)}
     {\sin(2\varphi-\alpha)},
\]
\[
\frac{AE}{AD}
=
\frac{\sin\gamma}
     {\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta)},
\]
\[
\frac{DE}{AD}
=
\frac{\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta-\gamma)}
     {\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta)},
\]
\[
\frac{BC}{CD}=\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha},
\]
and
\[
\frac{CD}{DE}
=
\frac{ND}{NM}
=
\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(2\varphi-\alpha)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{BC}{AD}
=
\frac{\sin\beta\,
\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta-\gamma)}
{\sin(2\varphi-\alpha)\,
\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta)}.
\]

The condition $AB=AE+BC$, after substitution and multiplication by the common denominator, becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta)
 \sin(2\varphi-\beta-\gamma)\\
&\qquad=
\sin\gamma\,\sin(2\varphi-\alpha)
+
\sin\beta\,\sin(2\varphi-\alpha-\beta-\gamma).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using product-to-sum formulas, this is equivalent to
\[
\cos\varphi\,
\sin(2\varphi-\beta-\alpha)\,
\sin(\varphi-\beta-\gamma)=0.
\]
Now
\[
0<\varphi<90^\circ
\]
and
\[
0<2\varphi-\beta-\alpha
=180^\circ-\angle AED<180^\circ.
\]
Thus the first two factors are nonzero, and
\[
\varphi=\beta+\gamma.
\]
Finally,
\[
\angle BDA
=2\varphi-\beta-\gamma
=\varphi
=\frac12\angle CDE.
\]
Hence
\[
2\angle BDA=\angle CDE.
\]
