ID: 0397

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Inequalities, Sign Analysis, Symmetry, Contrapositive

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
\]
satisfy
\[
f(x+y)f(x-y)\ge f(x)^2-f(y)^2
\tag{1}
\]
for all real numbers $x,y$. Assume that (1) is strict for at least one pair $(x_0,y_0)$.

Prove that either
\[
f(x)\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R,
\]
or
\[
f(x)\le0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
s=x+y,
\qquad
t=x-y.
\]
Then (1) becomes
\[
f(s)f(t)
\ge
f\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\right)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Replacing $t$ by $-t$ gives
\[
f(s)f(-t)
\ge
f\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Adding (2) and (3),
\[
f(s)\bigl(f(t)+f(-t)\bigr)\ge0
\tag{4}
\]
for all $s,t\in\mathbb R$.

For
\[
s_0=x_0+y_0,
\qquad
t_0=x_0-y_0,
\]
inequality (2) is strict. Therefore (4) is strict at $(s_0,t_0)$, so
\[
f(t_0)+f(-t_0)\ne0.
\]
Fixing $t=t_0$ in (4), we see that every value $f(s)$ has the same weak sign as the nonzero constant
\[
f(t_0)+f(-t_0).
\]
Thus $f$ has constant sign.

SOLUTION 2:
If
\[
f(0)\ne0,
\]
then substituting $y=x$ into (1) gives
\[
f(2x)f(0)\ge0
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\]
Since $2x$ ranges over all real numbers, $f$ has constant sign.

Assume now that
\[
f(0)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Substituting $y=-x$ into (1) gives
\[
f(-x)^2\ge f(x)^2.
\]
Replacing $x$ by $-x$ yields the reverse inequality, and hence
\[
f(-x)^2=f(x)^2
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\tag{3}
\]

Using the variables
\[
s=x+y,\qquad t=x-y,
\]
we have
\[
f(s)f(t)
\ge
f\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\right)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Replace $s$ by $-s$ and use (3):
\[
f(-s)f(t)
\ge
f\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Adding (4) and (5),
\[
\bigl(f(s)+f(-s)\bigr)f(t)\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]

At the transformed pair corresponding to $(x_0,y_0)$, inequality (4) is strict; hence (6) is strict there. Thus there is some $s_0$ for which
\[
f(s_0)+f(-s_0)\ne0.
\]
Fixing this $s_0$ in (6) shows that all values of $f$ have the same weak sign.

SOLUTION 3:
We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that $f$ takes both signs, so there are real numbers $a,b$ with
\[
f(a)<0<f(b).
\tag{2}
\]
We shall prove that (1) is always an equality.

For arbitrary $u$, substitute
\[
x=\frac{a+u}{2},
\qquad
y=\frac{a-u}{2}
\]
into (1). This gives
\[
f(a)f(u)
\ge
f\left(\frac{a+u}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{a-u}{2}\right)^2.
\]
Replace $u$ by $-u$ and add. We obtain
\[
f(a)\bigl(f(u)+f(-u)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
Since $f(a)<0$,
\[
f(u)+f(-u)\le0.
\]
Repeating the same argument with $b$ in place of $a$ and using $f(b)>0$, we obtain the reverse inequality. Therefore
\[
f(-u)=-f(u)
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R);
\]
that is, $f$ is odd.

Apply (1) first to $(x,y)$ and then to $(y,x)$. Oddness gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)^2-f(y)^2
&\le f(x+y)f(x-y)\\
&=-f(y+x)f(y-x)\\
&\le f(x)^2-f(y)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus equality holds throughout:
\[
f(x+y)f(x-y)=f(x)^2-f(y)^2
\]
for all $x,y$. Hence, if $f$ does not have constant sign, the original inequality is never strict. This proves the contrapositive.

SOLUTION 4:
Again suppose that $f$ takes both signs. Choose $a,b$ such that
\[
f(a)f(b)<0.
\tag{2}
\]

We first prove
\[
f(b)f(-b)<0.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
x_1=\frac{a+b}{2},
\qquad
y_1=\frac{a-b}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
f(a)f(b)
\ge
f(x_1)^2-f(y_1)^2.
\]
The left side is negative, so
\[
f(x_1)^2-f(y_1)^2<0.
\tag{4}
\]
Applying (1) to $(y_1,x_1)$ gives
\[
f(a)f(-b)\ge f(y_1)^2-f(x_1)^2>0.
\]
Together with (2), this proves (3).

Next, $f(0)=0$. Indeed, if $f(0)\ne0$, the substitution $y=x$ would force every value of $f$ to have the same sign. With $f(0)=0$, the substitution $y=-x$, followed by replacing $x$ by $-x$, gives
\[
f(x)^2=f(-x)^2
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\tag{5}
\]

In the variables $s=x+y$ and $t=x-y$, inequality (1) becomes
\[
f(s)f(t)\ge\Delta,
\qquad
\Delta=
f\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)^2
-
f\left(\frac{s-t}{2}\right)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Replacing both $s$ and $t$ by their negatives and using (5),
\[
f(-s)f(-t)\ge\Delta.
\tag{7}
\]
After replacing $t$ by $-t$ when necessary, we may assume $\Delta\ge0$. Multiplying (6) and (7) gives
\[
f(s)f(-s)f(t)f(-t)\ge0
\qquad(s,t\in\mathbb R).
\]
Hence the function
\[
g(s)=f(s)f(-s)
\]
has constant weak sign. By (3), this sign is nonpositive:
\[
f(s)f(-s)\le0
\qquad(s\in\mathbb R).
\]
Together with (5), this yields
\[
f(-s)=-f(s),
\]
so $f$ is odd.

Apply (1) to $(x,y)$ and to $(y,x)$. Using oddness,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)^2-f(y)^2
&\le f(x+y)f(x-y)\\
&=-f(y+x)f(y-x)\\
&\le f(x)^2-f(y)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (1) is an equality for every pair $(x,y)$. A sign-changing function therefore cannot satisfy the assumed strict inequality, completing the proof.
