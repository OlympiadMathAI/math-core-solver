ID: 0065

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Max-Min Algebra, Lattice Polynomials, Closure Properties, Piecewise Polynomial Functions

STATEMENT:
A function $f:\mathbb{R}^k\to\mathbb{R}$ is called a metapolynomial if, for some positive integers $m$ and $n$, it can be represented in the form
\[
f(x_1,\ldots,x_k)
=
\max_{1\le i\le m}\ \min_{1\le j\le n} P_{i,j}(x_1,\ldots,x_k),
\]
where all $P_{i,j}$ are multivariate polynomials. Prove that the product of two metapolynomials is also a metapolynomial.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\mathcal{M}$ denote the family of metapolynomials. Write $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ and $[m]=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}$.

If a metapolynomial has a representation with some $m,n$, then either index range may be enlarged by repeating rows or entries. Thus, when comparing finitely many metapolynomials, we may assume that their representations use common values of $m$ and $n$.

We first record a finite max-min identity.

\textbf{Lemma.}
For real numbers $a_{i,j}$, where $i\in[m]$ and $j\in[n]$,
\[
\min_{i\in[m]}\max_{j\in[n]}a_{i,j}
=
\max_{j_1,\ldots,j_m\in[n]}\min_{i\in[m]}a_{i,j_i}.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
For each row $i$, relabel its entries so that $a_{i,n}=\max_j a_{i,j}$, and relabel the rows so that
\[
a_{m,n}=\min_i a_{i,n}.
\]
Then the left-hand side of (1) equals $a_{m,n}$. Choosing
\[
(j_1,\ldots,j_m)=(n,\ldots,n)
\]
shows that the right-hand side is at least $a_{m,n}$. Conversely, for every choice of $(j_1,\ldots,j_m)$,
\[
\min_i a_{i,j_i}\le a_{m,j_m}\le a_{m,n}.
\]
Thus equality holds. $\square$

We next prove several closure properties of $\mathcal{M}$.

\textbf{Maxima and minima.}
A finite maximum of metapolynomials is a metapolynomial, since all the outer maxima can be merged. A finite minimum is also a metapolynomial: after writing each function in max-min form, apply the lemma to interchange the outer minimum and maximum, and then merge the remaining minima.

More explicitly, if
\[
f_s=\max_{i_s}\min_{j_s}P^{(s)}_{i_s,j_s}
\qquad(s=1,\ldots,r),
\]
then the lemma gives
\[
\min_s f_s
=
\max_{i_1,\ldots,i_r}
\min_{s,j_s}P^{(s)}_{i_s,j_s},
\]
which is again of max-min form.

\textbf{Negation.}
If
\[
f=\max_i\min_j P_{i,j},
\]
then
\[
-f=\min_i\max_j(-P_{i,j}),
\]
and the lemma converts this expression to max-min form. Hence $-f\in\mathcal{M}$.

\textbf{Addition.}
If
\[
f=\max_{i_1}\min_{j_1}P_{i_1,j_1},
\qquad
g=\max_{i_2}\min_{j_2}Q_{i_2,j_2},
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
f+g
&=
\max_{i_1,i_2}
\left(
\min_{j_1}P_{i_1,j_1}
+
\min_{j_2}Q_{i_2,j_2}
\right)\\
&=
\max_{i_1,i_2}\min_{j_1,j_2}
\bigl(P_{i_1,j_1}+Q_{i_2,j_2}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $f+g\in\mathcal{M}$. Together with negation, this also gives closure under subtraction.

It remains to prove closure under multiplication. First consider nonnegative metapolynomials $f,g$. For a real-valued function $h$, write
\[
h^+=\max\{h,0\}.
\]
Suppose
\[
f=\max_i\min_jP_{i,j}\ge0,
\qquad
g=\max_i\min_jQ_{i,j}\ge0.
\]
Since
\[
\min_j P_{i,j}^+=\left(\min_j P_{i,j}\right)^+,
\]
we may write
\[
f=\max_i\min_jP_{i,j}^+,
\qquad
g=\max_i\min_jQ_{i,j}^+.
\]
All the displayed quantities are nonnegative, so multiplication preserves maxima and minima:
\[
fg
=
\max_{i_1,i_2}\min_{j_1,j_2}
\left(P_{i_1,j_1}^+Q_{i_2,j_2}^+\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Therefore it is enough to prove that $P^+Q^+$ is a metapolynomial whenever $P$ and $Q$ are polynomials.

For real numbers $u,v$, the following identity holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^+v^+
=
\max\bigl\{&0,
\min\{uv,u,v\},
\min\{uv,uv^2,u^2v\},\\
&\min\{uv,u,u^2v\},
\min\{uv,uv^2,v\}
\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
If $u\le0$ or $v\le0$, both sides of (3) are $0$. If $u,v\ge0$, every term inside the outer maximum is at most $uv$, and one of the four inner minima equals $uv$ according as
\[
0\le u,v\le1,
\qquad
1\le u,v,
\qquad
0\le v\le1\le u,
\qquad
0\le u\le1\le v.
\]
Thus (3) is valid. Replacing $u$ by $P(x)$ and $v$ by $Q(x)$ expresses $P^+Q^+$ using only maxima, minima, and polynomials, so $P^+Q^+\in\mathcal{M}$. By (2), the product of two nonnegative metapolynomials belongs to $\mathcal{M}$.

Finally, for arbitrary $f,g\in\mathcal{M}$, define
\[
f^+=\max\{f,0\},
\qquad
f^-=\max\{-f,0\},
\]
and similarly for $g$. All four functions belong to $\mathcal{M}$ and are nonnegative, while
\[
f=f^+-f^-,
\qquad
g=g^+-g^-.
\]
Hence
\[
fg=f^+g^+-f^+g^--f^-g^++f^-g^-.
\]
Each product on the right belongs to $\mathcal{M}$ by the nonnegative case, and $\mathcal{M}$ is closed under addition and subtraction. Therefore $fg\in\mathcal{M}$.

\textbf{Comment.}
When $k=1$, metapolynomials are exactly the continuous piecewise-polynomial functions. A useful special case is that, for every positive integer $n$,
\[
(x^+)^n
=
\max\left\{0,
\min\{x^{n-1},x^n\},
\min\{x^n,x^{n+1}\}
\right\}.
\]
