ID: 0018

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Convex Polygons, Orthogonal Projections, Circumcircles, Trigonometry

STATEMENT:
Let $A_1A_2\cdots A_n$ be a convex polygon. A point $P$ inside the polygon is chosen so that its perpendicular projections $P_1,\ldots,P_n$ onto the lines
\[
A_1A_2,\ A_2A_3,\ \ldots,\ A_nA_1
\]
lie on the corresponding sides. For arbitrary points
\[
X_i\in A_iA_{i+1}
\qquad (i=1,\ldots,n),
\]
where indices are taken modulo $n$, prove that
\[
\max_{1\le i\le n}\frac{X_iX_{i+1}}{P_iP_{i+1}}\ge1.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We use the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} Let $Q$ be a point inside the convex polygon $A_1A_2\cdots A_n$. Then $Q$ lies inside or on at least one of the circumcircles of the triangles
\[
X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]

If $Q$ lies in one of these triangles, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise $Q$ lies inside the polygon $X_1X_2\cdots X_n$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{i=1}^n
\left(
\angle X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}
+
\angle X_iQX_{i+1}
\right)\\
&\qquad=(n-2)\pi+2\pi=n\pi.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore, for some index $i$,
\[
\angle X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}
+
\angle X_iQX_{i+1}
\ge\pi.
\]
Since the quadrilateral $QX_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}$ is convex, this means that $Q$ lies inside or on the circumcircle of triangle $X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}$. The lemma follows.

Apply the lemma to $Q=P$. For some $i$, the point $P$ lies inside or on the circumcircle $\Omega$ of
\[
X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}.
\]
Let its radius be $R$. Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of
\[
P_iA_{i+1}P_{i+1},
\]
and let its radius be $r$. Since
\[
PP_i\perp A_iA_{i+1},
\qquad
PP_{i+1}\perp A_{i+1}A_{i+2},
\]
the segment $A_{i+1}P$ is a diameter of $\omega$, so
\[
2r=A_{i+1}P.
\]
Because $P$ lies inside $\Omega$ and $A_{i+1}\in\Omega$,
\[
A_{i+1}P\le2R,
\]
hence $r\le R$.

The angles at $A_{i+1}$ in the two triangles are equal, because both are the angle between the adjacent sides of the polygon. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_iP_{i+1}
&=2r\sin\angle P_iA_{i+1}P_{i+1}\\
&\le2R\sin\angle X_iA_{i+1}X_{i+1}\\
&=X_iX_{i+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{X_iX_{i+1}}{P_iP_{i+1}}\ge1,
\]
which proves the claim.

SOLUTION 2:
We prove the stronger statement
\[
\max_{1\le i\le n}
\left(
\frac{X_iX_{i+1}}{P_iP_{i+1}}\cos\alpha_i
\right)\ge1,
\tag{1}
\]
where $\alpha_i$ is the angle between the lines $X_iX_{i+1}$ and $P_iP_{i+1}$.

Define
\[
\beta_i=\angle A_iP_iP_{i-1},
\qquad
\gamma_i=\angle A_{i+1}P_iP_{i+1}.
\]
All these angles are acute.

Suppose that, for some $i$, the point $X_i$ lies on the segment $A_iP_i$ and $X_{i+1}$ lies on the segment $P_{i+1}A_{i+2}$. The orthogonal projection of $X_iX_{i+1}$ onto the line $P_iP_{i+1}$ then contains the whole segment $P_iP_{i+1}$. Hence
\[
X_iX_{i+1}\cos\alpha_i\ge P_iP_{i+1},
\]
and (1) follows.

Thus it remains only to consider the case in which all $X_i$ lie on the segments
\[
P_iA_{i+1};
\]
the opposite uniform orientation is analogous.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
\[
X_iX_{i+1}\cos\alpha_i<P_iP_{i+1}
\tag{2}
\]
for every $i$. Let $Y_i$ and $Y'_{i+1}$ be the perpendicular projections of $X_i$ and $X_{i+1}$ onto the line $P_iP_{i+1}$. Inequality (2) says
\[
Y_iY'_{i+1}<P_iP_{i+1},
\]
which, in the present configuration, is equivalent to
\[
P_iY_i>P_{i+1}Y'_{i+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
X_iP_i\cos\gamma_i
>
X_{i+1}P_{i+1}\cos\beta_{i+1}.
\]
Multiplying these inequalities for all $i$ gives
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n\cos\gamma_i
>
\prod_{i=1}^n\cos\beta_i.
\tag{3}
\]

Now apply the sine rule to triangle $PP_iP_{i+1}$. Its angles at $P_i$ and $P_{i+1}$ are
\[
\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta_{i+1},
\]
respectively, so
\[
\frac{PP_i}{PP_{i+1}}
=
\frac{\cos\beta_{i+1}}{\cos\gamma_i}.
\]
Multiplying these equalities cyclically,
\[
1=
\prod_{i=1}^n
\frac{\cos\beta_{i+1}}{\cos\gamma_i},
\]
or
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n\cos\gamma_i
=
\prod_{i=1}^n\cos\beta_i,
\]
contradicting (3). Hence (1) holds. Since $\cos\alpha_i\le1$, the required inequality follows.
