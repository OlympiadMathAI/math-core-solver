ID: 0193

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Coordinate Geometry, Similarity Classes, Convex Sets, Star-Shaped Sets

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
B=(-1,0),\qquad C=(1,0).
\]
A nonempty bounded subset $S$ of the plane is called \emph{nice} if:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] there is a point $T\in S$ such that for every $Q\in S$, the whole segment $TQ$ lies in $S$;
\item[(ii)] for every triangle $P_1P_2P_3$, there is a unique point $A\in S$ and a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,2,3\}$ such that the triangles $ABC$ and
\[
P_{\sigma(1)}P_{\sigma(2)}P_{\sigma(3)}
\]
are similar.
\end{enumerate}

Prove that there are two distinct nice subsets $S,S'$ of
\[
\{(x,y):x\ge0,\ y\ge0\}
\]
such that, if $A\in S$ and $A'\in S'$ are the unique points corresponding to a given triangle in condition (ii), then
\[
BA\cdot BA'
\]
is a constant independent of the triangle.

SOLUTION 1:
For a point $A=(x,y)$ in the first quadrant,
\[
AB^2=(x+1)^2+y^2,
\qquad
AC^2=(x-1)^2+y^2,
\qquad
BC=2.
\]
Since $x\ge0$, we always have
\[
AB\ge AC.
\tag{1}
\]

First suppose that, in the similarity with the given triangle, the side $BC$ corresponds to its longest side. Then the required ordering is
\[
BC\ge AB\ge AC.
\]
By (1), the only nonautomatic condition is
\[
AB\le2,
\]
or
\[
(x+1)^2+y^2\le4.
\]
Define
\[
S=\{(x,y):(x+1)^2+y^2\le4,\ x\ge0,\ y\ge0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
This is the intersection of a disk and the first quadrant, hence it is bounded and convex; therefore it satisfies condition (i). The ordered ratios of the three side lengths determine the point $A$ uniquely in the upper half-plane. Indeed, after scaling so that $BC=2$, the prescribed values of $AB$ and $AC$ give
\[
x=\frac{AB^2-AC^2}{4},
\qquad
y=\sqrt{AB^2-(x+1)^2},
\]
with the nonnegative square root. Thus condition (ii) also holds.

Next require $BC$ to correspond to the second longest side. Then
\[
AB\ge BC\ge AC.
\]
These inequalities are equivalent to
\[
(x+1)^2+y^2\ge4
\]
and
\[
(x-1)^2+y^2\le4.
\]
Define
\[
S'=\{(x,y):(x+1)^2+y^2\ge4,\ (x-1)^2+y^2\le4,\ x\ge0,\ y\ge0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
This set is bounded. The same side-length argument proves condition (ii).

We verify condition (i) directly. Take
\[
T'=(1,2),
\]
the highest point of the circle $(x-1)^2+y^2=4$. Let $Q=(x,y)\in S'$, and let $R$ be any point of the segment $T'Q$. The first quadrant and the disk
\[
(x-1)^2+y^2\le4
\]
are convex, so $R$ remains in both. It remains to prove that $R$ stays outside the disk centred at $B=(-1,0)$ with radius $2$.

Put
\[
u=T'-B=(2,2),
\qquad
v=Q-B=(x+1,y).
\]
The condition $Q\in S'$ gives $|v|\ge2$. It also implies $x+y\ge1$: otherwise
\[
(x+1)^2+y^2<4.
\]
Hence
\[
u\cdot v=2(x+y+1)\ge4.
\]
Writing $R-B=(1-t)u+tv$ with $0\le t\le1$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
|R-B|^2
&=(1-t)^2|u|^2+t^2|v|^2+2t(1-t)u\cdot v\\
&\ge4\bigl((1-t)^2+t^2+2t(1-t)\bigr)=4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $R\in S'$, proving that $S'$ is star-shaped with respect to $T'$. Clearly $S$ and $S'$ are distinct.

Finally, let the side lengths of the given triangle satisfy
\[
P_1P_2\ge P_2P_3\ge P_3P_1.
\]
For the point $A\in S$, the longest side $P_1P_2$ corresponds to $BC$, while $P_2P_3$ corresponds to $BA$. Therefore
\[
BA
=BC\cdot\frac{P_2P_3}{P_1P_2}.
\]
For the point $A'\in S'$, the second longest side $P_2P_3$ corresponds to $BC$, while $P_1P_2$ corresponds to $BA'$. Hence
\[
BA'
=BC\cdot\frac{P_1P_2}{P_2P_3}.
\]
Multiplying,
\[
BA\cdot BA'=BC^2=4.
\]
Thus the required product is the constant $4$.
