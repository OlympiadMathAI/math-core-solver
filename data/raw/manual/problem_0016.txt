ID: 0016

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Orthic Triangle, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Angle Chasing, Reflection

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle, and let $D,E,F$ be the feet of the altitudes on $BC,CA,AB$, respectively. A point $P$ is one of the two intersections of the line $EF$ with the circumcircle of $ABC$. The lines $BP$ and $DF$ meet at $Q$. Prove that
\[
AP=AQ.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Write
\[
\angle CAB=\alpha,\qquad
\angle ABC=\beta,\qquad
\angle BCA=\gamma.
\]
The quadrilaterals $BCEF$ and $CAFD$ are cyclic, because they contain right angles. Hence
\[
\angle BDF=\alpha,\qquad
\angle AFE=\gamma,\qquad
\angle DFB=\gamma.
\]

There are two possible positions of $P$.

\textbf{Case 1.} Suppose that $P$ lies on the ray $EF$.

Since $P$ lies on the arc $AB$ of the circumcircle,
\[
\angle PBA<\angle BCA=\gamma.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle PBD+\angle BDF
&=\angle PBA+\angle ABD+\angle BDF\\
&<\gamma+\beta+\alpha\\
&=180^\circ.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $Q$ lies on the extensions of $BP$ and $DF$ beyond
$P$ and $F$, respectively.
Since $A,P,B,C$ are concyclic,
\[
\angle QPA
=180^\circ-\angle APB
=\angle BCA
=\gamma.
\]
Also,
\[
\angle QFA=\angle DFB=\gamma.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle QPA=\angle QFA,
\]
so $A,Q,P,F$ are concyclic. It follows that
\[
\angle AQP
=180^\circ-\angle PFA
=\angle AFE
=\gamma.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle AQP=\angle QPA,
\]
and consequently $AP=AQ$.

\textbf{Case 2.} Suppose that $P$ lies on the ray $FE$.

Now $Q$ lies inside the segment $FD$. As before,
\[
\angle QPA=\angle BCA=\gamma.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle DFB=\gamma=180^\circ-\angle AFQ,
\]
and hence
\[
\angle QPA+\angle AFQ=180^\circ.
\]
Thus $A,F,Q,P$ are cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle AQP=\angle AFP=\angle AFE=\gamma=\angle QPA.
\]
Again triangle $AQP$ is isosceles, so $AP=AQ$.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $P$ and $P'$ be the two intersections of $EF$ with the circumcircle of $ABC$, where $P$ lies on arc $AB$ and $P'$ lies on arc $CA$. Let
\[
Q=BP\cap DF,\qquad
Q'=BP'\cap DF.
\]
We prove the stronger relation
\[
AP=AP'=AQ=AQ'.
\]

Put $\gamma=\angle BCA$. As in Solution 1,
\[
\angle AFE=\angle BFP=\angle DFB=\gamma.
\]
For points on the circumcircle, let $\widehat{XY}$ denote the measure of the relevant arc from $X$ to $Y$. We have
\[
\widehat{PB}+\widehat{P'A}
=2\angle AFP'
=2\gamma
=2\angle BCA
=\widehat{AP}+\widehat{PB}.
\]
Hence
\[
\widehat{AP}=\widehat{P'A},
\]
so
\[
AP=AP'
\quad\text{and}\quad
\angle PBA=\angle ABP'.
\tag{1}
\]

The last angle equality means that the lines $BP$ and $BP'=BQ'$ are mirror images in the line $AB$. Also,
\[
\angle BFP=\angle Q'FB,
\]
because $FQ'$ lies on $FD$. Thus $FP$ and $FQ'$ are mirror images in $AB$. Their intersections show that $P$ and $Q'$ are reflections of each other in $AB$. Therefore
\[
AP=AQ'.
\tag{2}
\]

The same reflection argument interchanges $P'$ and $Q$, giving
\[
AP'=AQ.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (3), we obtain
\[
AP=AP'=AQ=AQ',
\]
and in particular $AP=AQ$.
