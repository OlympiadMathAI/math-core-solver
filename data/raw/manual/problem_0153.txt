ID: 0153

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Euclidean Combinatorics, Regular Polygons, Pigeonhole Principle, Double Counting

STATEMENT:
Let $V$ be a finite set of points in the plane. We say that $V$ is \emph{balanced} if, for every two distinct points $A,B\in V$, there exists a point $C\in V$ such that
\[
AC=BC.
\]
We say that $V$ is \emph{center-free} if there do not exist four points $A,B,C,P\in V$, with $A,B,C$ distinct, such that
\[
PA=PB=PC.
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Show that for every integer $n\ge3$, there exists a balanced set consisting of $n$ points.
\item[(b)] Determine all integers $n\ge3$ for which there exists a balanced, center-free set consisting of $n$ points.
\end{enumerate}

SOLUTION 1:
For part (b), the answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{all odd integers }n\ge3}.
\]

\textbf{Part (a): construction of balanced sets.}

First suppose that $n$ is odd. Take the vertices
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n
\]
of a regular $n$-gon, with indices considered modulo $n$. For distinct $i,j$, the congruence
\[
2k\equiv i+j\pmod n
\]
has a unique solution because $n$ is odd. Then the arcs from $A_i$ to $A_k$ and from $A_k$ to $A_j$ contain the same number of sides, so
\[
A_iA_k=A_jA_k.
\]
Thus the vertex set is balanced.

Now suppose that $n$ is even. Put
\[
N=3n-6=3(n-2),
\]
and take a regular $N$-gon with center $O$ and consecutive vertices
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_N.
\]
Set
\[
V=\{O,A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_{n-1}\}.
\]
Any two points $A_i,A_j$ are equidistant from $O$, so only pairs of the form $O,A_i$ need consideration.

Let
\[
d=\frac n2-1=\frac{n-2}{2}.
\]
Vertices whose indices differ by $d$ subtend a central angle
\[
\frac{360^\circ d}{N}=60^\circ,
\]
so together with $O$ they form an equilateral triangle. If $i\le n/2$, use $A_{i+d}$; if $i>n/2$, use $A_{i-d}$. In both cases this auxiliary vertex belongs to $\{A_1,\ldots,A_{n-1}\}$ and is equidistant from $O$ and $A_i$. Hence $V$ is balanced.

\textbf{Part (b): the center-free condition.}

If $n$ is odd, use the vertices of a regular $n$-gon. We already know that this set is balanced. If a point $P$ were equidistant from three distinct vertices, then $P$ would be the circumcenter of the circle through those vertices, hence the center of the regular polygon. That center is not one of the vertices, so the set is center-free.

It remains to prove that no balanced, center-free set can have even cardinality. Suppose that $|V|=n$ is even. For each unordered pair $\{A,B\}$ of distinct points, choose one point $C\in V$ satisfying $AC=BC$, and say that $C$ is associated with the pair $\{A,B\}$.

There are
\[
\binom n2
\]
pairs. By the pigeonhole principle, some point $P\in V$ is associated with at least
\[
\left\lceil\frac{\binom n2}{n}\right\rceil
=\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil
=\frac n2
\]
pairs. None of these pairs contains $P$. If the $n/2$ pairs were pairwise disjoint, their union would contain $n$ points, but only $n-1$ points are available outside $P$. Hence two associated pairs share a point; write them as
\[
\{A,B\},\qquad \{A,C\},
\]
with $B\ne C$. Since both are associated with $P$,
\[
PA=PB=PC,
\]
contradicting center-freeness.

Thus a balanced, center-free set exists exactly for odd $n\ge3$.
