ID: 0210

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Recurrence Relations, Extremal Principle, Bounding Sequences, Induction

STATEMENT:
A sequence of real numbers
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots
\]
satisfies
\[
a_n=-\max_{\substack{i+j=n\\i,j\ge1}}(a_i+a_j)
\]
for every integer $n>2017$. Prove that the sequence is bounded.

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
D=2017.
\]
For $n>D$, define
\[
M_n=\max_{1\le k<n}a_k,
\qquad
m_n=-\min_{1\le k<n}a_k.
\]
Both sequences $(M_n)$ and $(m_n)$ are nondecreasing. It is enough to prove that they are bounded.

Fix $n>D$. By the recurrence, for some positive integers $p,q$ with $p+q=n$,
\[
a_n=-(a_p+a_q).
\]
Since $a_p,a_q\le M_n$,
\[
a_n\ge-2M_n.
\tag{1}
\]

Choose an index $k<n$ such that
\[
a_k=M_n.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_n
&=-\max_{i+j=n}(a_i+a_j)\\
&\le-(a_{n-k}+a_k)\\
&=-a_{n-k}-M_n\\
&\le m_n-M_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1) and (2) yield
\[
-2M_n\le a_n\le m_n-M_n.
\]
Therefore
\[
m_n\le m_{n+1}\le\max\{m_n,2M_n\},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
M_n\le M_{n+1}\le\max\{M_n,m_n-M_n\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Call an index $n>D$ \emph{lucky} if
\[
m_n\le2M_n.
\]

Suppose first that some index $n$ is lucky. From (3),
\[
m_{n+1}\le2M_n.
\]
From (4),
\[
M_{n+1}\le\max\{M_n,m_n-M_n\}=M_n.
\]
Since $M_{n+1}\ge M_n$, we have
\[
M_{n+1}=M_n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
m_{n+1}\le2M_{n+1},
\]
so $n+1$ is lucky. Repeating the argument, $M_j=M_n$ and $m_j\le2M_n$ for every $j\ge n$. Thus both sequences are bounded.

Suppose instead that no index is lucky. Then
\[
2M_n<m_n
\qquad(n>D).
\]
Equation (3) gives
\[
m_{n+1}=m_n
\qquad(n>D).
\]
Hence $m_n$ is eventually constant. Also,
\[
M_n<\frac{m_n}{2},
\]
so $M_n$ is bounded as well.

In both cases, $(M_n)$ and $(m_n)$ are bounded. Therefore the original sequence $(a_n)$ is bounded.

SOLUTION 2:
Again put
\[
D=2017.
\]
If the sequence is bounded above by some real number $Q$, then for every $n>D$,
\[
a_n\ge-2Q.
\]
Together with the finitely many initial terms, this would make the sequence bounded. It therefore suffices to prove that the sequence is bounded above.

Assume, for contradiction, that it is not bounded above. Let
\[
\ell=\min\{a_1,\ldots,a_D\},
\qquad
L=\max\{a_1,\ldots,a_D\}.
\]
Call an index $n$ \emph{good} if
\[
n>D,\qquad
a_n>a_i\ \text{for every }i<n,
\qquad
a_n>-2\ell.
\tag{1}
\]

A good index exists. Indeed, choose $N$ with
\[
a_N>\max\{L,-2\ell\},
\]
and let $n$ be the first index at which the maximum of
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_N
\]
is attained. Then $n$ satisfies (1).

Fix a good index $n$. For every positive $u,v$ with $u+v=n$, the recurrence gives
\[
a_n+a_u+a_v\le0.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose $u\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$ so that $a_u$ is maximal, and put
\[
v=n-u.
\]
Then
\[
a_u\ge a_v.
\]

If $v\le D$, then
\[
a_u\ge a_v\ge\ell.
\]
Equation (2) gives
\[
a_n+2\ell\le0,
\]
contradicting $a_n>-2\ell$.

Hence $v>D$. By the recurrence for $a_v$, there exist positive integers $w_1,w_2$ with
\[
w_1+w_2=v
\]
such that
\[
a_v+a_{w_1}+a_{w_2}=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3),
\[
a_n+a_u\le a_{w_1}+a_{w_2}.
\]
Since $n$ is a record index,
\[
a_n>a_u.
\]
Therefore
\[
a_{w_1}+a_{w_2}>2a_u,
\]
so at least one of $a_{w_1},a_{w_2}$ is greater than $a_u$.

But
\[
w_1,w_2<v<n,
\]
contradicting the choice of $u$ as an index with maximal value among all indices smaller than $n$.

This contradiction proves that $(a_n)$ is bounded above, and hence it is bounded.
