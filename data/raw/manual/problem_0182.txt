ID: 0182

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Best Constants, Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality, Telescoping Sums

STATEMENT:
Determine the largest real number $a$ such that, for every integer $n\ge1$ and all real numbers
\[
0=x_0<x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_n,
\]
one has
\[
\frac1{x_1-x_0}
+
\frac1{x_2-x_1}
+
\cdots
+
\frac1{x_n-x_{n-1}}
\ge
a\left(
\frac2{x_1}+
\frac3{x_2}+
\cdots+
\frac{n+1}{x_n}
\right).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The largest constant is
\[
\boxed{a=\frac49}.
\]

For $2\le k\le n$, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
\[
\left(x_{k-1}+x_k-x_{k-1}\right)
\left(
\frac{(k-1)^2}{x_{k-1}}
+
\frac{3^2}{x_k-x_{k-1}}
\right)
\ge(k+2)^2.
\]
Since the first factor is $x_k$, this is equivalent to
\[
\frac9{x_k-x_{k-1}}
\ge
\frac{(k+2)^2}{x_k}
-
\frac{(k-1)^2}{x_{k-1}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Sum (1) for $k=2,3,\ldots,n$ and add $9/x_1$. The terms telescope to give
\[
9\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{x_k-x_{k-1}}
\ge
4\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k+1}{x_k}
+
\frac{n^2}{x_n}
\ge
4\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k+1}{x_k}.
\]
Thus $a=4/9$ is admissible.

To prove optimality, take
\[
x_0=0,
\qquad
x_k-x_{k-1}=k(k+1).
\]
Then
\[
x_k=\frac{k(k+1)(k+2)}3.
\]
For this sequence,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{x_k-x_{k-1}}
=
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k(k+1)}
=
1-\frac1{n+1},
\]
whereas
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k+1}{x_k}
&=3\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{k(k+2)}\\
&=\frac32\left(1+\frac12-\frac1{n+1}-\frac1{n+2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
As $n\to\infty$, these two expressions tend to $1$ and $9/4$, respectively. Therefore every admissible $a$ satisfies
\[
1\ge\frac94a,
\]
so $a\le4/9$.

SOLUTION 2:
Put
\[
y_k=x_k-x_{k-1}>0,
\qquad
x_k=y_1+y_2+\cdots+y_k.
\]
For each $1\le k\le n$, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\left(\sum_{j=1}^k y_j\right)
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^k
\frac1{y_j}\binom{j+1}{2}^2
\right)
\ge
\left(
\sum_{j=1}^k\binom{j+1}{2}
\right)^2
=
\binom{k+2}{3}^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{k+1}{x_k}
\le
\frac{36}{k^2(k+1)(k+2)^2}
\sum_{j=1}^k
\frac1{y_j}\binom{j+1}{2}^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Summing (1) over $k=1,\ldots,n$ and interchanging the order of summation, we obtain
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k+1}{x_k}
\le
\sum_{m=1}^n\frac{c_m}{y_m},
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_m
&=36\binom{m+1}{2}^2
\sum_{k=m}^n\frac1{k^2(k+1)(k+2)^2}\\
&=\frac{9m^2(m+1)^2}{4}
\sum_{k=m}^n
\left(
\frac1{k^2(k+1)^2}
-
\frac1{(k+1)^2(k+2)^2}
\right)\\
&=\frac{9m^2(m+1)^2}{4}
\left(
\frac1{m^2(m+1)^2}
-
\frac1{(n+1)^2(n+2)^2}
\right)\\
&<\frac94.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows from (2) that
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k+1}{x_k}
<
\frac94\sum_{m=1}^n\frac1{y_m}.
\]
Thus the desired inequality holds with $a=4/9$. The example from Solution 1 shows that no larger constant is possible.
