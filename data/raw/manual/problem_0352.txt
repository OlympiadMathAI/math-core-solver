ID: 0352

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Equilateral Triangles, Convex Polygons, Perimeter Monotonicity, Extremal Diagonals

STATEMENT:
Determine all integers $n\ge3$ with the following property:

Every convex $n$-gon whose sides all have length $1$ contains an equilateral triangle of side length $1$.

Every polygon is understood to contain its boundary.

SOLUTION 1:
The required integers are exactly
\[
\boxed{\text{all odd }n\ge3}.
\]

We first construct a counterexample for every even $n$.

Let
\[
n=2h.
\]
Choose a regular $q$-gon
\[
A_0A_1\cdots A_{q-1}
\]
of side length $1$, where $q$ is sufficiently large and $q>h$. For
\[
1\le i<h,
\]
let $B_i$ be the reflection of $A_i$ in the line $A_0A_h$.

Then
\[
A_0A_1\cdots A_hB_{h-1}B_{h-2}\cdots B_1
\]
is a convex $n$-gon, and every one of its sides has length $1$. Since $h$ is fixed, as $q$ tends to infinity this polygon is contained in a strip of arbitrarily small width. Choose $q$ so large that the width is less than $1/2$.

The minimum, over all directions, of the width of an equilateral triangle of side $1$ is its altitude
\[
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
Hence such a triangle cannot be contained in a strip of width $1/2$. Thus no even $n$ has the required property.

Now let
\[
n=2k+1.
\]
For $k=1$, the polygon itself is an equilateral triangle, so assume
\[
k\ge2.
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal P=A_0A_1\cdots A_{2k}
\]
be a convex $(2k+1)$-gon with all sides equal to $1$. Choose a longest diagonal, and label the vertices so that it is
\[
A_0A_\ell
\]
and the boundary chain
\[
A_0A_1\cdots A_\ell
\]
has at least $k+1$ edges. Thus
\[
\ell\ge k+1.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose first that
\[
\angle A_\ell A_0A_1\ge60^\circ.
\]
Since $A_0A_\ell$ is a longest diagonal,
\[
A_1A_\ell\le A_0A_\ell.
\]
Therefore, in triangle $A_0A_1A_\ell$,
\[
\angle A_0A_1A_\ell
\ge
\angle A_\ell A_0A_1
\ge60^\circ.
\]
The unit equilateral triangle erected on $A_0A_1$ toward the interior of $\mathcal P$ is consequently contained in triangle $A_0A_1A_\ell$, and hence in $\mathcal P$.

The same argument works if
\[
\angle A_{\ell-1}A_\ell A_0\ge60^\circ.
\]
We may therefore assume
\[
\angle A_\ell A_0A_1<60^\circ,
\qquad
\angle A_{\ell-1}A_\ell A_0<60^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]

Construct an isosceles trapezoid
\[
A_0YZA_\ell
\]
on the same side of $A_0A_\ell$ as the chain, such that
\[
A_0A_\ell\parallel YZ,
\qquad
A_0Y=ZA_\ell=1,
\]
and
\[
\angle A_\ell A_0Y=\angle ZA_\ell A_0=60^\circ.
\tag{3}
\]

Assume for a moment that the whole chain
\[
A_0A_1\cdots A_\ell
\]
is contained in this trapezoid. The convex polygon formed by this chain and the segment $A_\ell A_0$ has perimeter
\[
\ell+A_0A_\ell.
\]
The trapezoid has perimeter
\[
2A_0A_\ell+1.
\]
The perimeter of a convex polygon contained in another convex polygon cannot exceed the perimeter of the latter, so
\[
\ell+A_0A_\ell\le2A_0A_\ell+1,
\]
or
\[
\ell-1\le A_0A_\ell.
\tag{4}
\]

On the other hand, the complementary boundary chain has
\[
2k+1-\ell
\]
unit edges. By the strict triangle inequality,
\[
A_0A_\ell<2k+1-\ell.
\]
Using (1),
\[
2k+1-\ell\le\ell-1,
\]
contradicting (4). Hence some vertex $A_m$ of the chain lies outside the trapezoid.

Let $U$ be the perpendicular projection of $A_m$ onto $A_0A_\ell$. Conditions (2)--(3) imply
\[
UA_m\ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\qquad
A_0U>\frac12,
\qquad
UA_\ell>\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose points
\[
Q\in A_0U,\qquad R\in UA_\ell,\qquad S\in UA_m
\]
such that
\[
UQ=UR=\frac12,
\qquad
US=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
Then $QRS$ is an equilateral triangle of side length $1$.

The segment $A_0A_\ell$ and the vertex $A_m$ lie in the convex polygon $\mathcal P$, so the entire triangle $QRS$ lies in $\mathcal P$. Thus every odd-sided equilateral convex polygon contains a unit equilateral triangle.

Therefore the answer is precisely all odd integers $n\ge3$.
