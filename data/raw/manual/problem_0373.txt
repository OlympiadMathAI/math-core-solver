ID: 0373

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Combinatorial Games, Board Colourings, Pigeonhole Principle, Scheduling Strategies

STATEMENT:
Every square of a $2022\times2022$ garden initially contains a tree of height $0$. A gardener and a lumberjack play alternately, with the gardener moving first.

On each gardener move, one square is chosen, and the tree in that square together with all trees in its surrounding squares, at most eight of them, grows by one unit.

On each lumberjack move, four different squares are chosen, and every chosen tree of positive height loses one unit of height.

A tree is called \emph{majestic} if its height is at least
\[
10^6.
\]
Determine the largest integer $K$ such that the gardener can guarantee that eventually at least $K$ trees are majestic, regardless of the lumberjack's play.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{
K=\frac{5\cdot2022^2}{9}=2271380
}.
\]

More generally, we solve the game on a $3N\times3N$ board and prove that the answer is
\[
5N^2.
\tag{1}
\]

First we give a strategy for the lumberjack. Number the rows and columns from $1$ to $3N$, and colour a square if at least one of its two coordinates is divisible by $3$. There are
\[
(3N)^2-(2N)^2=5N^2
\]
coloured squares.

Any three consecutive row indices contain exactly one multiple of $3$, and the same is true of any three consecutive column indices. Therefore any $3\times3$ square contains exactly four uncoloured squares. A gardener move affects a subset of such a $3\times3$ square, so it increases the heights of at most four uncoloured trees. The lumberjack cuts precisely those trees, using any unused choices arbitrarily if fewer than four occur.

After every lumberjack move, every uncoloured tree has height $0$. Thus only the $5N^2$ coloured trees can ever become majestic, proving
\[
K\le5N^2.
\tag{2}
\]

We now give a strategy for the gardener. It is enough to win a harder modified game. In this game, after the gardener grows the trees in a chosen $3\times3$ subboard, the lumberjack may cut four of those nine trees and may additionally cut every positive-height tree outside that subboard. Thus, in the chosen subboard, five trees gain one unit and four have no net change; outside it, every positive tree may lose one unit.

Let
\[
M=\binom95=126,
\]
the number of possible sets of five trees that can be left uncut inside a $3\times3$ subboard.

Partition the $3N\times3N$ board into $N^2$ disjoint $3\times3$ subboards, numbered
\[
0,1,\ldots,N^2-1.
\]
Process them in descending order. For
\[
b=N^2-1,N^2-2,\ldots,0,
\]
the gardener repeatedly chooses the centre square of subboard $b$, thereby growing exactly that full $3\times3$ subboard. He does this exactly
\[
10^6M(M+1)^b
\tag{3}
\]
times.

Among the $M$ possible five-square sets, one is left uncut at least
\[
10^6(M+1)^b
\tag{4}
\]
times during the moves on subboard $b$. Hence five trees in that subboard reach height at least the quantity in (4).

Before subboard $b$ is first used, all of its trees still have height $0$, so earlier cuts cannot have lowered them. After the gardener finishes with subboard $b$, the number of remaining moves is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}10^6M(M+1)^j
=
10^6\bigl((M+1)^b-1\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
Each of the five selected trees can lose at most one unit on each of these later moves. Combining (4) and (5), its final height is at least
\[
10^6(M+1)^b
-
10^6\bigl((M+1)^b-1\bigr)
=
10^6.
\]
Thus every subboard ends with five majestic trees, giving $5N^2$ in total. Together with (2), this proves (1).

Finally,
\[
2022=3\cdot674,
\]
so $N=674$ and
\[
K=5\cdot674^2=2271380.
\]
