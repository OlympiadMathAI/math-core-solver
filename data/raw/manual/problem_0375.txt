ID: 0375

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Set Systems, Weighted Subset Sums, Injective Constructions, Pigeonhole Principle

STATEMENT:
Let $m,n\ge2$, let $X$ be a set with $n$ elements, and let
\[
X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_m
\]
be pairwise distinct nonempty subsets of $X$.

A function
\[
f:X\to\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
\]
is called \emph{nice} if there is an index $k$ such that
\[
\sum_{x\in X_k}f(x)
>
\sum_{x\in X_i}f(x)
\qquad\text{for every }i\ne k.
\]

Prove that there are at least
\[
n^n
\]
nice functions.

SOLUTION 1:
For a subset $Y\subseteq X$, write
\[
f(Y)=\sum_{y\in Y}f(y).
\]

Consider the set $\mathcal F$ of all functions
\[
f:X\to\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.
\]
It has exactly
\[
|\mathcal F|=n^n
\]
elements.

For each $f\in\mathcal F$, choose an index $\ell$ for which
\[
f(X_\ell)=\max_{1\le i\le m}f(X_i).
\]
Define
\[
f^+(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x)+1,&x\in X_\ell,\\
f(x),&x\notin X_\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
f^+:X\to\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}.
\]

We prove that $f^+$ is nice and that $X_\ell$ is its unique maximizing set. For $j\ne\ell$,
\[
f^+(X_j)=f(X_j)+|X_j\cap X_\ell|,
\]
whereas
\[
f^+(X_\ell)=f(X_\ell)+|X_\ell|.
\]
The containment
\[
X_\ell\subseteq X_j
\]
is impossible: because the sets are distinct and all values of $f$ are positive, it would imply
\[
f(X_j)>f(X_\ell),
\]
contrary to the choice of $\ell$. Hence
\[
|X_j\cap X_\ell|<|X_\ell|.
\]
Together with
\[
f(X_j)\le f(X_\ell),
\]
this gives
\[
f^+(X_j)<f^+(X_\ell).
\]
Thus $f^+$ is nice.

Finally, the original function $f$ can be recovered uniquely from $f^+$. Its unique maximizing set identifies $X_\ell$, and then one subtracts $1$ from $f^+(x)$ exactly for
\[
x\in X_\ell.
\]
Therefore the map
\[
f\longmapsto f^+
\]
is injective.

Since there are $n^n$ functions in $\mathcal F$, there are at least $n^n$ nice functions.
