ID: 0191

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Similar Triangles, Isosceles Triangles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Radical Axis, Desargues' Theorem

STATEMENT:
In a convex pentagon $ABCDE$, let $F$ be a point on $AC$ such that
\[
\angle FBC=90^\circ.
\]
Suppose that the triangles $ABF$, $ACD$, and $ADE$ are similar isosceles triangles and
\[
\angle FAB=\angle FBA=\angle DAC=\angle DCA=\angle EAD=\angle EDA.
\]
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $CF$. Choose $X$ so that $AMXE$ is a parallelogram. Prove that the lines $BD$, $EM$, and $FX$ are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Let the common angle in the statement be $\theta$.

From
\[
\triangle ABF\sim\triangle ACD
\]
we have
\[
\frac{AB}{AC}=\frac{AF}{AD}.
\]
Since the included angles at $A$ are equal, this gives
\[
\triangle ABC\sim\triangle AFD.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle AFD=\angle ABC=90^\circ+\theta.
\]
Because $EA=ED$,
\[
90^\circ+\theta
=180^\circ-\frac12\angle AED.
\]
Hence $F$ lies on the circle with centre $E$ and radius $EA$, so
\[
EF=EA=ED.
\tag{1}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\angle EFA=\angle EAF=2\theta=\angle BFC,
\]
and since $A,F,C$ are collinear, the points $B,F,E$ are collinear.

The equality
\[
\angle EDA=\angle MAD
\]
implies
\[
ED\parallel AM.
\]
As $AM\parallel EX$, we obtain that $E,D,X$ are collinear.

Since $M$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $CF$ of the right triangle $CBF$,
\[
MF=MB.
\tag{2}
\]
The triangles $EFA$ and $MFB$ are isosceles, and
\[
\angle EFA=\angle MFB,
\qquad
AF=BF.
\]
Thus they are congruent. In particular,
\[
BM=AE=XM
\]
and
\[
BE=BF+FE=AF+FM=AM=EX.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle EMB\cong\triangle EMX.
\]
Reflection in $EM$ interchanges $B$ and $X$. By (1), it also interchanges the points $F\in EB$ and $D\in EX$. Hence the reflected images of the lines $BD$ and $XF$ are one another. Their intersection must lie on the axis of reflection $EM$, proving the desired concurrency.

SOLUTION 2:
Let the common angle be $\theta$. From
\[
\angle CAD=\angle EDA
\]
we have
\[
AC\parallel ED.
\]
Also, $AM\parallel EX$ and $A,M,C$ are collinear, so
\[
E,D,X\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]

As in Solution 1,
\[
\triangle ABC\sim\triangle AFD.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle AFD=\angle ABC=90^\circ+\theta,
\]
and therefore
\[
\angle FDC=90^\circ.
\]
Hence $B,C,D,F$ are concyclic. Let their circle be $\Gamma_1$. Since
\[
\angle CBF=90^\circ,
\]
the segment $CF$ is a diameter of $\Gamma_1$, and $M$, the midpoint of $CF$, is its centre.

Similarly,
\[
\triangle ABF\sim\triangle ADE
\]
gives
\[
\triangle ABD\sim\triangle AFE.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle AFE
=\angle ABD
=\theta+\angle FBD
=\theta+\angle FCD
=2\theta
=180^\circ-\angle BFA.
\]
Thus $B,F,E$ are collinear. In fact $F$ is the incentre of $\triangle DAB$. The point $E$ lies both on the internal bisector of $\angle DBA$ and on the perpendicular bisector of $AD$, so $E$ lies on the circumcircle $\Gamma_2$ of $\triangle ABD$, and
\[
EA=EF=ED.
\tag{2}
\]

Since $M$ is the centre of $\Gamma_1$,
\[
\angle AMD=2\theta=\angle ABD,
\]
so $M\in\Gamma_2$. Furthermore,
\[
\angle MDX=\angle MAE=\angle DXM
\]
by the parallelogram $AMXE$. Hence
\[
MD=MX,
\]
and therefore $X\in\Gamma_1$.

We now finish in either of two equivalent ways.

First, (2), $XM=EA$, and $EX\parallel FM$ show that $EFMX$ is an isosceles trapezoid, hence cyclic; call its circle $\Gamma_3$. The lines
\[
BD,\qquad EM,\qquad FX
\]
are the three pairwise radical axes of $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2,\Gamma_3$, respectively. The radical-axis theorem says that they are concurrent or parallel. The lines $BD$ and $EM$ plainly meet in the given configuration, so the parallel case is impossible. Hence the three lines are concurrent.

Alternatively,
\[
\angle DMF=2\theta=\angle BFM
\]
gives $DM\parallel EB$. A direct angle calculation gives
\[
\angle BFD+\angle XBF=180^\circ,
\]
so $DF\parallel XB$. Thus the corresponding sides of the triangles $DMF$ and $BEX$ are pairwise parallel. By the converse of Desargues' theorem, the lines joining corresponding vertices,
\[
DB,\qquad ME,\qquad FX,
\]
are concurrent or parallel. Again $DB$ and $ME$ meet, so they are concurrent.

SOLUTION 3:
Let the common angle be $\theta$. As before,
\[
\triangle ABC\sim\triangle AFD.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle ADF=\angle ACB=90^\circ-2\theta
=90^\circ-\angle BAD,
\]
so
\[
DF\perp AB.
\]
Since $FA=FB$, the line through $F$ perpendicular to $AB$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Therefore $D$ lies on this perpendicular bisector and
\[
DA=DB.
\]
The point $F$ is consequently the incentre of the isosceles triangle $DAB$.

Also,
\[
\angle AED=180^\circ-2\theta
=180^\circ-\angle DBA,
\]
so $A,B,D,E$ are concyclic. Since $EA=ED$, it follows that $B,E,F$ are collinear and
\[
EA=EF=ED.
\tag{1}
\]

The point $C$ lies on the internal angle bisector of $\angle DAB$ and on the external angle bisector of $\angle DBA$; hence it is the $A$-excentre of $\triangle DAB$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $CF$, the standard incenter-excenter symmetry in an isosceles triangle shows that $M$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle DAB$ and is the centre of the circle through $D,F,B,C$. It follows, by the symmetry of the configuration, that $DEFM$ is a rhombus.

The midpoints of the three segments $AX$, $EM$, and $DF$ coincide; hence $DAFX$ is a parallelogram.

Let
\[
P=BD\cap EM,
\qquad
Q=AD\cap BE.
\]
Because
\[
DC\parallel AB\parallel EM,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{DP}{PB}=\frac{CM}{MA}.
\]
Using the rhombus and (1),
\[
CM=DM=DE=AE,
\qquad
MA=BE,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{DP}{PB}=\frac{AE}{BE}.
\tag{2}
\]

The triangles $AEQ$ and $BEA$ are similar, so
\[
\frac{AE}{BE}=\frac{AQ}{BA}.
\]
Since $F$ is the incentre of $\triangle DAB$, the angle-bisector theorem gives
\[
\frac{AQ}{BA}=\frac{QF}{FB}.
\]
Together with (2),
\[
\frac{DP}{PB}=\frac{QF}{FB},
\]
which implies
\[
QD\parallel FP.
\]
But $DAFX$ is a parallelogram, so $QD$ is parallel to $FX$. Hence $F,P,X$ are collinear. Since $P$ also lies on $BD$ and $EM$, the three required lines are concurrent.
