ID: 0035

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Holder Inequality, Schur Inequality, Symmetry

STATEMENT:
Let $a,b,c$ be positive real numbers satisfying
\[
\min\{a+b,b+c,c+a\}>\sqrt2
\]
and
\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=3.
\]
Prove that
\[
\frac{a}{(b+c-a)^2}
+
\frac{b}{(c+a-b)^2}
+
\frac{c}{(a+b-c)^2}
\ge
\frac{3}{(abc)^2}.
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
First, all three denominators are positive. Indeed, from $b+c>\sqrt2$ we get
\[
b^2+c^2>1,
\]
so
\[
a^2=3-b^2-c^2<2,
\]
and hence
\[
a<\sqrt2<b+c.
\]
The other two inequalities follow cyclically.

We use Holder's inequality in the form
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{x_i^{p+1}}{y_i^p}
\ge
\frac{(x_1+\cdots+x_n)^{p+1}}
     {(y_1+\cdots+y_n)^p}
\]
for positive $p,x_i,y_i$. Taking $p=2$ and applying it cyclically with
\[
x_1=a^2,
\qquad
y_1=a^{5/2}(b+c-a),
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\rm cyc}\frac{a}{(b+c-a)^2}
&=
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{(a^2)^3}{\bigl(a^{5/2}(b+c-a)\bigr)^2}\\
&\ge
\frac{(a^2+b^2+c^2)^3}
{\left(\sum_{\rm cyc}a^{5/2}(b+c-a)\right)^2}\\
&=
\frac{27}
{\left(\sum_{\rm cyc}a^{5/2}(b+c-a)\right)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Schur's inequality gives
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}a^{3/2}(a-b)(a-c)\ge0.
\]
After expansion, this is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}a^{5/2}(b+c-a)
\le
abc(\sqrt a+\sqrt b+\sqrt c).
\tag{3}
\]
By the power-mean inequality,
\[
\left(\frac{\sqrt a+\sqrt b+\sqrt c}{3}\right)^4
\le
\frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{3}=1,
\]
so
\[
\sqrt a+\sqrt b+\sqrt c\le3.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (2)--(4),
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}\frac{a}{(b+c-a)^2}
\ge
\frac{27}{(3abc)^2}
=
\frac{3}{(abc)^2}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
As in Solution 1, the three quantities
\[
b+c-a,
\qquad c+a-b,
\qquad a+b-c
\]
are positive. Also, by the power-mean inequality,
\[
a^5+b^5+c^5\ge3.
\tag{2}
\]
By symmetry, assume
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]
It is enough to prove
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{a^3b^2c^2}{(b+c-a)^2}
\ge
\sum_{\rm cyc}a^5,
\tag{3}
\]
because division by $a^2b^2c^2$ and the use of (2) then give (1).

Subtracting the right side of (3), we need to show
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{a^3}{(b+c-a)^2}
\left((bc)^2-a^2(b+c-a)^2\right)
\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
For positive $x,y,z$ satisfying the triangle inequalities,
\[
(yz)^2-x^2(y+z-x)^2
\]
has the same sign as
\[
yz-x(y+z-x)=(x-y)(x-z),
\]
because the other factor is positive. Hence the terms of (4) corresponding to $a$ and $c$ are nonnegative. It remains to show that the $a$-term is at least the absolute value of the possibly negative $b$-term.

Factoring the two relevant terms, it is enough to prove
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{a^3(a-c)}{(b+c-a)^2}
\bigl(bc+a(b+c-a)\bigr)\\
&\qquad\ge
\frac{b^3(b-c)}{(c+a-b)^2}
\bigl(ac+b(c+a-b)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
We have
\[
a^3\ge b^3,
\qquad
a-c\ge b-c,
\qquad
0<b+c-a\le c+a-b.
\]
Thus it suffices to show
\[
\frac{ab+ac+bc-a^2}{b+c-a}
\ge
\frac{ab+ac+bc-b^2}{c+a-b}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since the denominators are positive, the difference after cross-multiplication is
\[
(a-b)\left(2ab-a^2-b^2+ac+bc\right)
=(a-b)\left(c(a+b)-(a-b)^2\right).
\]
Now $a\ge b$, and the triangle inequality gives
\[
c>a-b.
\]
Also $a+b>a-b$, so
\[
c(a+b)>(a-b)^2.
\]
Therefore the last expression is nonnegative, proving (6), then (5), then (4). Consequently (3) holds, and the desired inequality follows.
