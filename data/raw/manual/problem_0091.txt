ID: 0091

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Inequalities, Superadditivity, Positive Rational Numbers

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ be the set of positive rational numbers. Let
\[
f:\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
\]
be a function satisfying
\[
f(x)f(y)\ge f(xy)
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
f(x+y)\ge f(x)+f(y)
\tag{2}
\]
for all $x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$. Given that $f(a)=a$ for some rational number $a>1$, prove that
\[
f(x)=x
\]
for every $x\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$.

SOLUTION 1:
Putting $x=1$ and $y=a$ in (1), and using $f(a)=a>0$, gives
\[
f(1)\ge1.
\tag{3}
\]
By repeated application of (2),
\[
f(nx)\ge n f(x)
\tag{4}
\]
for every positive integer $n$ and every $x\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$. In particular,
\[
f(n)\ge n f(1)\ge n
\tag{5}
\]
for every positive integer $n$.

We next show that $f$ is positive everywhere. Write an arbitrary positive rational as $q=m/n$, where $m,n$ are positive integers. From (1),
\[
f(m/n)f(n)\ge f(m).
\]
Both $f(m)$ and $f(n)$ are positive by (5), so
\[
f(q)>0
\tag{6}
\]
for all $q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$.

It follows from (2) and (6) that $f$ is strictly increasing: if $y>x>0$, then
\[
f(y)=f(x+(y-x))\ge f(x)+f(y-x)>f(x).
\tag{7}
\]
Hence, for every rational $x\ge1$,
\[
f(x)\ge f(\lfloor x\rfloor)\ge\lfloor x\rfloor>x-1.
\tag{8}
\]
Repeated use of (1) gives
\[
f(x)^N\ge f(x^N)
\]
for every positive integer $N$. If $x>1$, then (8) applied to $x^N$ yields
\[
f(x)^N\ge f(x^N)>x^N-1,
\]
so
\[
f(x)\ge (x^N-1)^{1/N}.
\tag{9}
\]
We claim that this implies
\[
f(x)\ge x
\qquad (x>1).
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, fix a rational $y$ with $1<y<x$. For sufficiently large $N$,
\[
x^N-y^N
=(x-y)(x^{N-1}+x^{N-2}y+\cdots+y^{N-1})
>N(x-y)>1.
\]
Thus $x^N-1>y^N$, and (9) gives $f(x)>y$. Since rationals $y<x$ can be chosen arbitrarily close to $x$, (10) follows.

Now, for every positive integer $N$, conditions (1) and (10) give
\[
a^N=f(a)^N\ge f(a^N)\ge a^N.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(a^N)=a^N.
\tag{11}
\]
Let $x>1$ be rational, and choose $N$ so large that $a^N-x>1$. By (2), (10), and (11),
\[
\begin{aligned}
a^N=f(a^N)
&=f\bigl(x+(a^N-x)\bigr)\\
&\ge f(x)+f(a^N-x)\\
&\ge x+(a^N-x)=a^N.
\end{aligned}
\]
All inequalities are therefore equalities, and in particular
\[
f(x)=x
\qquad (x>1).
\tag{12}
\]

Finally, let $x\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ be arbitrary. Choose an integer $n\ge2$ such that $nx>1$. We already know from (12) that $f(n)=n$ and $f(nx)=nx$. Using (1) and (4),
\[
nf(x)=f(n)f(x)\ge f(nx)\ge n f(x).
\]
Thus equality holds throughout, so
\[
nx=f(nx)=nf(x).
\]
Hence $f(x)=x$ for every positive rational $x$.
