ID: 0169

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: 2-adic Valuations, Products, Divisibility, Powers of Two

STATEMENT:
Let $m$ and $n$ be positive integers with $m>n$. For
\[
k=1,2,\ldots,n+1,
\]
define
\[
x_k=\frac{m+k}{n+k}.
\]
Prove that if all the numbers $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{n+1}$ are integers, then
\[
x_1x_2\cdots x_{n+1}-1
\]
is divisible by an odd prime.

SOLUTION 1:
For each $k$, put
\[
a_k=x_k-1=\frac{m-n}{n+k}.
\]
By hypothesis, every $a_k$ is a positive integer. Let
\[
P=x_1x_2\cdots x_{n+1}-1
=\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}(a_k+1)-1.
\]
It is enough to prove that $P$ is not a power of $2$.

Let
\[
v_2(m-n)=d,
\]
and choose $c$ so that
\[
2^c\le2n+1<2^{c+1}.
\]
Since
\[
2n+1\le2^{c+1}-1,
\]
we have $n+1\le2^c$. Thus $2^c$ occurs among
\[
n+1,n+2,\ldots,2n+1.
\]
It is the only multiple of $2^c$ in this interval. Let $\ell$ be the unique index such that
\[
n+\ell=2^c.
\]

Because $a_\ell=(m-n)/2^c$ is an integer, we have $d\ge c$. Moreover,
\[
v_2(a_\ell)=d-c,
\]
while for every $k\ne\ell$ the number $n+k$ is not divisible by $2^c$, so
\[
v_2(a_k)\ge d-c+1.
\]
Set
\[
q=2^{d-c+1}.
\]
Then
\[
q\nmid a_\ell,
\qquad
q\mid a_k\quad(k\ne\ell).
\]
Therefore
\[
P
=\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}(a_k+1)-1
\equiv a_\ell\not\equiv0\pmod q.
\tag{1}
\]

There is at least one index $k\ne\ell$, and for such an index $a_k\ge q$. Hence
\[
P\ge a_k\ge q.
\tag{2}
\]
A power of $2$ which is at least $q$ must be divisible by $q$, contradicting (1). Thus $P$ is not a power of $2$.

Since $P>1$, it follows that $P$ has an odd prime divisor, as required.
