ID: 0123

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Invariants, Products, AM-GM Inequality, Extremal Processes

STATEMENT:
Let $m$ be a positive integer. There are $2^m$ sheets of paper, with the number $1$ written on each sheet. At every step, two distinct sheets carrying numbers $a$ and $b$ are chosen, and the number $a+b$ is written on both of them in place of the old numbers. Prove that after
\[
m2^{m-1}
\]
steps, the sum of all numbers on the sheets is at least $4^m$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $P_t$ be the product of the $2^m$ numbers after $t$ steps. Initially,
\[
P_0=1.
\]

Suppose that at some step the numbers $a$ and $b$ are both replaced by $a+b$. In the total product, the factor $ab$ is replaced by $(a+b)^2$. Since
\[
(a+b)^2\ge4ab,
\]
we obtain
\[
P_{t+1}\ge4P_t.
\]
Therefore, by induction,
\[
P_t\ge4^t
\]
for every $t\ge0$.

Set
\[
T=m2^{m-1}.
\]
Then
\[
P_T\ge4^{m2^{m-1}}
=2^{m2^m}
=(2^m)^{2^m}.
\]
There are $2^m$ positive numbers on the sheets. By the AM-GM inequality, their sum is at least
\[
2^m\sqrt[2^m]{P_T}
\ge2^m\cdot2^m
=4^m.
\]
This proves the assertion.
