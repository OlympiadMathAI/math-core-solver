ID: 0138

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Additive Semigroups, Binary Expansions, Induction, Frobenius-Type Problems

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge 2$ be an integer, and let
\[
A_n=\{2^n-2^k\mid k\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le k<n\}.
\]
Determine the largest positive integer that cannot be written as the sum of one or more, not necessarily distinct, elements of $A_n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{(n-2)2^n+1}.
\]

We first prove, by induction on $n$, that every integer
\[
m>(n-2)2^n+1
\tag{1}
\]
is representable.

For $n=2$, we have $A_2=\{2,3\}$, so every integer greater than $1$ is representable.

Now let $n>2$ and assume the assertion for $n-1$. If $m$ is even, then
\[
\frac m2\ge (n-2)2^{n-1}+1>(n-3)2^{n-1}+1.
\]
By induction,
\[
\frac m2=\sum_{i=1}^r\bigl(2^{n-1}-2^{k_i}\bigr)
\qquad(0\le k_i<n-1).
\]
Multiplying by $2$ gives
\[
m=\sum_{i=1}^r\bigl(2^n-2^{k_i+1}\bigr),
\]
a representation using elements of $A_n$.

If $m$ is odd, then $m\ge (n-2)2^n+3$, and hence
\[
\frac{m-(2^n-1)}2>(n-3)2^{n-1}+1.
\]
The induction hypothesis represents this integer using $A_{n-1}$. Doubling that representation and then adding the element $2^n-1\in A_n$ represents $m$.

It remains to show that
\[
M=(n-2)2^n+1
\tag{2}
\]
is not representable. Let $N$ be the smallest positive representable integer satisfying
\[
N\equiv1\pmod{2^n}.
\]
Such an integer exists, since
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2^n-2^k)
=n2^n-(2^n-1)
=(n-1)2^n+1.
\]
Choose a representation
\[
N=\sum_{i=1}^r(2^n-2^{k_i}),
\qquad 0\le k_i<n.
\tag{3}
\]
No two exponents $k_i$ can be equal. Indeed, if two equal exponents are $n-1$, deleting the corresponding two summands decreases the represented number by $2^n$. If two equal exponents are $k<n-1$, replacing the two summands by the single summand $2^n-2^{k+1}$ again decreases the represented number by $2^n$. Either operation would produce a smaller positive representable integer congruent to $1$ modulo $2^n$, contradicting the definition of $N$.

Thus the $k_i$ are distinct, and
\[
0<\sum_{i=1}^r2^{k_i}\le 1+2+\cdots+2^{n-1}=2^n-1.
\]
Reducing (3) modulo $2^n$ gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r2^{k_i}\equiv-1\pmod{2^n}.
\]
The preceding bounds force
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r2^{k_i}=2^n-1.
\]
By uniqueness of binary expansion, every exponent $0,1,\ldots,n-1$ occurs. Consequently
\[
N=(n-1)2^n+1.
\]
Therefore no smaller positive integer congruent to $1$ modulo $2^n$ is representable. In particular, the number in (2) is not representable. Together with (1), this proves the answer.

SOLUTION 2:
We again obtain
\[
\boxed{(n-2)2^n+1}.
\]
First, every larger integer is representable by induction on $n$. The case $n=2$ uses $A_2=\{2,3\}$. For $n>2$, if $m>(n-2)2^n+1$ is even, represent $m/2$ using $A_{n-1}$ and double every summand. If $m$ is odd, represent $(m-(2^n-1))/2$ using $A_{n-1}$, double every summand, and add $2^n-1$. The same inequalities as in Solution 1 ensure that the numbers to which induction is applied exceed $(n-3)2^{n-1}+1$.

We now give a different proof that the displayed number is not representable.

We prove the following stronger claim.

\textbf{Claim.} If $a,b$ are integers satisfying
\[
a\ge0,\qquad b\ge1,\qquad a+b<n,
\]
then $a2^n+b$ cannot be represented as a sum of elements of $A_n$.

We use induction on $n$. For $n=2$, the only possible pair is $(a,b)=(0,1)$, and $1$ is not representable by $2$ and $3$.

Suppose $n>2$ and, contrary to the claim, write
\[
a2^n+b=m_1+\cdots+m_r,
\qquad m_i\in A_n,
\]
with
\[
m_1\ge m_2\ge\cdots\ge m_r.
\]
Let $\ell$ be the number of summands equal to $2^n-1$. Since all the remaining summands are even, $\ell\equiv b\pmod2$. Removing these $\ell$ summands and dividing the remaining equality by $2$ gives
\[
(a-\ell)2^{n-1}+\frac{b+\ell}{2}
=\frac{m_{\ell+1}}2+\cdots+\frac{m_r}2.
\tag{1}
\]
Every term on the right belongs to $A_{n-1}$.

We must have $a-\ell\ge0$. Otherwise $\ell\ge a+1$, and the original equality would imply
\[
a2^n+b\ge(a+1)(2^n-1),
\]
so
\[
a+b+1\ge2^n,
\]
contrary to $a+b+1\le n<2^n$.

The second coefficient in (1) is a positive integer. By the induction hypothesis, its representability forces
\[
a-\ell+\frac{b+\ell}{2}\ge n-1.
\tag{2}
\]
But $b+\ell$ is a positive even integer, so
\[
\frac{b+\ell}{2}\le b+\ell-1.
\]
Hence the left-hand side of (2) is at most
\[
a-\ell+b+\ell-1=a+b-1<n-1,
\]
a contradiction. The claim follows.

Taking $a=n-2$ and $b=1$ proves that $(n-2)2^n+1$ is not representable, completing the solution.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $B_n$ be the set of all positive integers representable as sums of elements of $A_n$. For a nonnegative integer $t$, let $\sigma_2(t)$ denote the number of $1$'s in its binary expansion.

Every positive integer has a unique form
\[
m=s2^n-t,
\qquad s\ge1,\quad 0\le t\le2^n-1.
\]
We prove the characterization
\[
\boxed{m\in B_n\iff s\ge\sigma_2(t)}.
\tag{1}
\]

If $t=0$, then
\[
m=s2^n=2s(2^n-2^{n-1}),
\]
so $m\in B_n$. Now let $t>0$ and write its binary expansion as
\[
t=2^{k_1}+\cdots+2^{k_q},
\qquad 0\le k_1<\cdots<k_q<n,
\qquad q=\sigma_2(t).
\]
If $s\ge q$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
m
&=(s-q)2^n+(q2^n-t)\\
&=2(s-q)(2^n-2^{n-1})+
  \sum_{i=1}^q(2^n-2^{k_i}),
\end{aligned}
\]
so $m\in B_n$.

For the converse, suppose that (1) fails, and choose the smallest representable number
\[
m=s2^n-t
\]
with $s<\sigma_2(t)$. Write
\[
m=\sum_{i=1}^d(2^n-2^{\ell_i}),
\qquad 0\le\ell_i<n.
\tag{2}
\]
If all $\ell_i$ are distinct, then their powers of $2$ sum to an integer between $0$ and $2^n-1$. Comparing (2) with the unique form $s2^n-t$ modulo $2^n$ and within this range yields
\[
t=\sum_{i=1}^d2^{\ell_i},
\qquad s=d.
\]
Thus $s=\sigma_2(t)$, a contradiction.

Hence two exponents in (2) are equal. Combining the two corresponding summands replaces
\[
2(2^n-2^\ell)
\]
by $2^n-2^{\ell+1}$ and decreases the represented number by $2^n$; when $\ell=n-1$, the two summands are simply deleted. Therefore
\[
m-2^n=(s-1)2^n-t
\]
is also representable. The repeated pair has total at least $2^n$, so $s\ge2$ and this number is positive. Since $s-1<\sigma_2(t)$, this contradicts the minimal choice of $m$. Thus (1) is proved.

For a fixed $t\ge1$, every nonrepresentable positive integer congruent to $-t$ modulo $2^n$ is at most
\[
(\sigma_2(t)-1)2^n-t.
\]
Thus the global maximum is obtained by maximizing this expression. At $t=2^n-1$, where $\sigma_2(t)=n$, it equals
\[
(n-1)2^n-(2^n-1)=(n-2)2^n+1.
\]
For every other $t$, one has $\sigma_2(t)\le n-1$, so the corresponding value is smaller. Hence the required largest integer is
\[
\boxed{(n-2)2^n+1}.
\]
