ID: 0355

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Cube-Free Integers, Modular Arithmetic, Prime Valuations, Divisibility

STATEMENT:
Determine all integers $n\ge1$ for which there exist positive integers $a,b$ such that no cube of a prime divides
\[
a^2+b+3
\]
and
\[
\frac{ab+3b+8}{a^2+b+3}=n.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{n=2}.
\]

Put
\[
D=a^2+b+3.
\]
Because
\[
b\equiv-a^2-3\pmod D,
\]
we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
ab+3b+8
&\equiv
a(-a^2-3)+3(-a^2-3)+8\\
&=-(a+1)^3
\pmod D.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, if the displayed fraction is an integer, then
\[
D\mid(a+1)^3.
\tag{1}
\]

No prime cube divides $D$. Hence, for every prime $p$, the exponent of $p$ in $D$ is at most $2$. From (1), it follows prime by prime that
\[
D\mid(a+1)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, if $p^e\mid D$ with $e\in\{1,2\}$, then
\[
3v_p(a+1)\ge e,
\]
so $v_p(a+1)\ge1$ and therefore
\[
2v_p(a+1)\ge e.
\]

Furthermore,
\[
0<(a+1)^2<2D,
\]
because
\[
2D-(a+1)^2=(a-1)^2+2b+4>0.
\]
Together with (2), this forces
\[
(a+1)^2=D=a^2+b+3.
\]
Consequently,
\[
b=2(a-1).
\]
Substituting this into the numerator,
\[
ab+3b+8
=
2(a-1)(a+3)+8
=
2(a+1)^2
=
2D.
\]
Therefore
\[
n=2.
\]

Conversely, take
\[
(a,b)=(2,2).
\]
Then
\[
a^2+b+3=9,
\]
which is not divisible by the cube of any prime, and
\[
\frac{ab+3b+8}{a^2+b+3}
=
\frac{18}{9}
=
2.
\]
Hence $n=2$ is indeed attainable.
