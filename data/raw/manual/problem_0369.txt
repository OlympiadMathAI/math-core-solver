ID: 0369

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Decimal Digit Sums, Polynomial Values, Carrying, Place-Value Blocks

STATEMENT:
For a positive integer $m$, let $s(m)$ denote the sum of its decimal digits. Let
\[
P(x)=x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0,
\]
where $n\ge2$ and every $a_i$ is a positive integer.

Can it happen that $s(k)$ and $s(P(k))$ have the same parity for every positive integer $k$?

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{No}}.
\]
We construct two positive integers having the same digit sum, while the digit sums of their polynomial values differ by $9$.

For convenience, put
\[
a_n=1
\]
and let
\[
M=\max\{a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n\}.
\]
Choose a positive integer $t$ so large that the interval
\[
I=
\left[
\left(\frac9{a_{n-1}}10^t\right)^{1/(n-1)},
\left(\frac1{a_{n-1}}10^{t+1}\right)^{1/(n-1)}
\right)
\tag{1}
\]
has length greater than $100$ and has lower endpoint greater than $10M$.
Such a choice is possible because both the lower endpoint and the length of $I$ tend to infinity with $t$.

Choose an integer
\[
X\in I
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
X\equiv1\pmod{100}.
\tag{2}
\]
The interval is long enough to contain such an integer. From (1),
\[
9\cdot10^t
\le a_{n-1}X^{n-1}
<10^{t+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the first decimal digit of
\[
a_{n-1}X^{n-1}
\]
is $9$.

Also, $X>10M$. Hence, for every $0\le i<n$,
\[
a_iX^i<a_{i+1}X^{i+1},
\]
and in fact each successive quantity has strictly more decimal digits than the preceding one. Let
\[
\alpha
\]
be the number of decimal digits of $a_{n-1}X^{n-1}$. Then
\[
10^{\alpha-1}
\le a_{n-1}X^{n-1}
<10^\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]

Consider first
\[
P(10^\alpha X)
=
10^{\alpha n}X^n
+a_{n-1}10^{\alpha(n-1)}X^{n-1}
+\cdots+a_0.
\]
Every block below the leading block occupies at most $\alpha$ decimal places. Hence consecutive blocks do not overlap, and no carrying occurs. Therefore
\[
s(P(10^\alpha X))
=
s(X^n)+s(a_{n-1}X^{n-1})+\cdots+s(a_0).
\tag{5}
\]

Now consider
\[
P(10^{\alpha-1}X)
=
10^{(\alpha-1)n}X^n
+a_{n-1}10^{(\alpha-1)(n-1)}X^{n-1}
+\cdots+a_0.
\]
For $i<n-1$, the number $a_iX^i$ has at most $\alpha-1$ digits, so all lower blocks remain disjoint. The only overlap is between the leading digit of
\[
a_{n-1}10^{(\alpha-1)(n-1)}X^{n-1}
\]
and the units digit of the shifted block
\[
10^{(\alpha-1)n}X^n.
\]

By (3), the first of these digits is $9$. By (2),
\[
X^n\equiv1\pmod{100},
\]
so the last two digits of $X^n$ are $01$. At the overlap, the digits $9$ and $1$ add to $10$: the $9$ and $1$ become $0$, and the following $0$ becomes $1$. No other digit is affected. Hence the total digit sum decreases by exactly $9$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
s(P(10^{\alpha-1}X))
&=s(X^n)+s(a_{n-1}X^{n-1})+\cdots+s(a_0)-9\\
&=s(P(10^\alpha X))-9.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

However,
\[
s(10^\alpha X)=s(X)=s(10^{\alpha-1}X).
\]
Thus the two inputs have digit sums of the same parity, while by (6) their polynomial values have digit sums of opposite parity. Therefore it is impossible for
\[
s(k)\equiv s(P(k))\pmod2
\]
to hold for every positive integer $k$.
