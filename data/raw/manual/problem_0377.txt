ID: 0377

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Integer Tuples, Coordinatewise Maximum, Tropical Algebra, Invariant Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Lucy initially writes $s$ integer-valued $2022$-tuples on a blackboard. She may then repeatedly choose two tuples
\[
v=(v_1,\ldots,v_{2022}),
\qquad
w=(w_1,\ldots,w_{2022}),
\]
not necessarily distinct, that are already on the board and write either
\[
v+w=(v_1+w_1,\ldots,v_{2022}+w_{2022})
\]
or
\[
v\vee w=
\bigl(\max(v_1,w_1),\ldots,\max(v_{2022},w_{2022})\bigr).
\]

Determine the smallest $s$ for which Lucy can eventually write every integer-valued $2022$-tuple.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{s=3}.
\]

We prove the corresponding statement for integer-valued $n$-tuples for every $n\ge3$.

First we show that three starting tuples suffice. Let
\[
a_i=-i^2,\qquad b_i=i,\qquad c_i=-1
\qquad(1\le i\le n),
\]
so that
\[
a=(-1^2,-2^2,\ldots,-n^2),\quad
b=(1,2,\ldots,n),\quad
c=(-1,-1,\ldots,-1).
\]

For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, Lucy can form
\[
d^{(j)}=2a+4j\,b+(2j^2-1)c.
\]
Its $i$th coordinate is
\[
d^{(j)}_i
=
-2i^2+4ij-(2j^2-1)
=
1-2(i-j)^2.
\]
Thus
\[
d^{(j)}_j=1,
\qquad
d^{(j)}_i\le-1\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Taking the coordinatewise maximum,
\[
\mathbf 1=d^{(1)}\vee d^{(2)}\vee\cdots\vee d^{(n)}
=(1,1,\ldots,1).
\]
Hence Lucy can form
\[
\mathbf 0=\mathbf 1+c.
\]
For each $j$,
\[
e^{(j)}=d^{(j)}\vee\mathbf0
\]
has a $1$ in coordinate $j$ and $0$ in every other coordinate.

Now let
\[
v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)
\]
be any integer tuple. Choose a positive integer $K$ such that
\[
K+v_i\ge0
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Then Lucy can form
\[
Kc+\sum_{i=1}^n(K+v_i)e^{(i)}=v.
\]
Therefore three tuples suffice.

We now show that two cannot suffice. We use the following invariant.

Suppose $A\ge0$ and two tuples $u,v$ satisfy
\[
u_j\ge A u_k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_j\ge A v_k
\tag{1}
\]
for two coordinates $j,k$. Then both $u+v$ and $u\vee v$ also satisfy the same inequality. For addition this is immediate. For the maximum, if
\[
m=u\vee v,
\]
then
\[
m_j\ge u_j\ge A u_k
\quad\text{and}\quad
m_j\ge v_j\ge A v_k,
\]
so
\[
m_j\ge A\max(u_k,v_k)=A m_k.
\]

Suppose Lucy starts with only two tuples $u,v$. If, in some coordinate $i$, both $u_i$ and $v_i$ are nonnegative, then every generated tuple has nonnegative $i$th coordinate. If both are nonpositive, every generated tuple has nonpositive $i$th coordinate. Either case prevents Lucy from generating every integer tuple.

Thus, in every coordinate, $u_i$ and $v_i$ have opposite signs. Since $n\ge3$, two coordinates $j\ne k$ have the same sign pattern. After interchanging $u$ and $v$ if necessary,
\[
u_j,u_k>0,
\qquad
v_j,v_k<0.
\]
Put
\[
A=\frac{u_j}{u_k}>0.
\]
If
\[
\frac{v_j}{v_k}\le A,
\]
then, because $v_k<0$,
\[
v_j\ge A v_k,
\]
and both starting tuples satisfy (1).

If instead
\[
\frac{v_j}{v_k}>A,
\]
then
\[
u_k=\frac1A u_j
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v_k\ge\frac1A v_j,
\]
so both starting tuples satisfy the analogous invariant with $j$ and $k$ interchanged.

In either case, every generated tuple obeys a fixed nontrivial coordinate inequality, whereas some integer tuples violate it. Hence two starting tuples cannot generate all integer tuples.

Therefore the minimum is
\[
\boxed{3}.
\]
