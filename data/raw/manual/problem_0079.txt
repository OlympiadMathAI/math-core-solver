ID: 0079

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangential Quadrilaterals, Incircles and Excircles, Tangent Lines, Angle Bisectors, Similarity

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral with non-parallel sides $BC$ and $AD$. Assume that there is a point $E$ on the side $BC$ such that the quadrilaterals $ABED$ and $AECD$ are circumscribed. Prove that there is a point $F$ on the side $AD$ such that the quadrilaterals $ABCF$ and $BCDF$ are circumscribed if and only if
\[
AB\parallel CD.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ be the incircles of the tangential quadrilaterals $ABED$ and $AECD$, and let their centers be $O_1$ and $O_2$, respectively.

Let the tangent from $B$ to $\omega_2$ other than $BC$ meet $AD$ at $F_1$, and let the tangent from $C$ to $\omega_1$ other than $BC$ meet $AD$ at $F_2$.

If a point $F$ has the required property, then $\omega_1$ must also be the incircle of $ABCF$, so $CF$ is the second tangent from $C$ to $\omega_1$ and therefore $F=F_2$. Similarly, $\omega_2$ must be the incircle of $BCDF$, so $BF$ is the second tangent from $B$ to $\omega_2$ and $F=F_1$. Conversely, if $F_1=F_2$, the two circles are incircles of the required quadrilaterals. Hence it is enough to prove
\[
F_1=F_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
AB\parallel CD.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
O=AD\cap BC.
\]
The circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ are tangent to both sides of the angle formed by the lines $AD$ and $BC$. Put
\[
p=OO_1\cdot OO_2.
\]
We use the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} Two circles $\omega_1,\omega_2$, with centers $O_1,O_2$, are inscribed in an angle with vertex $O$. Points $P,S$ lie on one side of the angle and $Q,R$ on the other. Suppose that $\omega_1$ is the incircle of triangle $PQO$, while $\omega_2$ is the excircle of triangle $RSO$ opposite $O$. Then exactly one of the following holds:
\[
OP\cdot OR<p<OQ\cdot OS,
\]
\[
OP\cdot OR>p>OQ\cdot OS,
\]
or
\[
OP\cdot OR=p=OQ\cdot OS.
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Proof of the lemma.} Let
\[
\angle OPO_1=u,
\quad
\angle OQO_1=v,
\quad
\angle OO_2R=x,
\quad
\angle OO_2S=y,
\quad
\angle POQ=2\varphi.
\]
Because the indicated lines are internal or external angle bisectors in triangles $PQO$ and $RSO$,
\[
u+v=x+y=90^\circ-\varphi.
\tag{3}
\]
By the sine rule,
\[
\frac{OP}{OO_1}=\frac{\sin(u+\varphi)}{\sin u},
\qquad
\frac{OO_2}{OR}=\frac{\sin(x+\varphi)}{\sin x}.
\]
Since $u,x,\varphi$ are acute,
\[
\begin{aligned}
OP\cdot OR\ge p
&\Longleftrightarrow
\frac{OP}{OO_1}\ge\frac{OO_2}{OR}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\sin x\sin(u+\varphi)\ge \sin u\sin(x+\varphi)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\sin(x-u)\ge 0\\
&\Longleftrightarrow x\ge u.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Equality holds exactly when $x=u$.

Analogously,
\[
p\ge OQ\cdot OS
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v\ge y,
\tag{5}
\]
with equality exactly when $v=y$. By (3), the inequalities $x\ge u$ and $v\ge y$ are equivalent, and equality in one is equivalent to equality in the other. This proves (2). \hfill$\square$

We now apply the lemma three times.

First use the tangent lines $ED$ to $\omega_1$ and $BF_1$ to $\omega_2$. The lemma compares the products
\[
OB\cdot OD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
OE\cdot OF_1.
\]
Thus
\[
OE\cdot OF_1>p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
OB\cdot OD<p,
\tag{6}
\]
and the analogous reversed implication and equality statement also hold.

Second, use the tangent lines $AB$ to $\omega_1$ and $CD$ to $\omega_2$. The lemma compares
\[
OA\cdot OC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
OB\cdot OD.
\]
Therefore (6) implies
\[
OA\cdot OC>p.
\tag{7}
\]

Third, interchange the roles of the two circles and use the tangent lines $AE$ and $CF_2$. The lemma compares
\[
OA\cdot OC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
OE\cdot OF_2.
\]
Thus (7) implies
\[
OE\cdot OF_2<p.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (6)--(8),
\[
OE\cdot OF_1>p>OE\cdot OF_2,
\]
so $F_1\ne F_2$. The same reasoning with every inequality reversed shows that
\[
OE\cdot OF_1<p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_1\ne F_2.
\]
Hence
\[
F_1=F_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
OE\cdot OF_1=p.
\tag{9}
\]
The same two strict-inequality chains also show that
\[
OA\cdot OC=OB\cdot OD
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
OE\cdot OF_1=p.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, in the equality case the three applications of the lemma give
\[
OB\cdot OD=p=OA\cdot OC
\]
and
\[
OE\cdot OF_1=p=OE\cdot OF_2,
\]
whereas in either strict case the products $OB\cdot OD$ and $OA\cdot OC$ lie on opposite sides of $p$.

Finally, because $A,D$ lie on one ray from $O$ and $B,C$ lie on the other, the lines $AB$ and $CD$ are parallel exactly when triangles $OAB$ and $ODC$ are similar. This is equivalent to
\[
\frac{OA}{OD}=\frac{OB}{OC},
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
OA\cdot OC=OB\cdot OD.
\tag{11}
\]
By (9), (10), and (11),
\[
F_1=F_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
AB\parallel CD.
\]
Together with (1), this completes the proof.
