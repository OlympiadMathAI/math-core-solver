ID: 0022

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangent Circles, Homothety, Ellipses, Loci

STATEMENT:
Three circular arcs $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$ connect two points $A$ and $C$. They lie in the same half-plane bounded by $AC$, with $\gamma_2$ between $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_3$. A point $B$ lies on the segment $AC$.

Let $h_1,h_2,h_3$ be three rays from $B$ in that half-plane, with $h_2$ between $h_1$ and $h_3$. For $i,j\in\{1,2,3\}$, let
\[
V_{ij}=h_i\cap\gamma_j.
\]

For $i<k$ and $j<\ell$, consider the curved quadrilateral bounded by the two ray segments
\[
V_{ij}V_{i\ell},\qquad V_{kj}V_{k\ell},
\]
and the two arc segments
\[
\widehat{V_{ij}V_{kj}}\subset\gamma_j,
\qquad
\widehat{V_{i\ell}V_{k\ell}}\subset\gamma_\ell.
\]
Call it circumscribed if a circle is tangent to all four boundary pieces.

Suppose the following three curved quadrilaterals are circumscribed:
\[
(V_{11},V_{21},V_{22},V_{12}),
\]
\[
(V_{12},V_{22},V_{23},V_{13}),
\]
\[
(V_{21},V_{31},V_{32},V_{22}).
\]
Prove that
\[
(V_{22},V_{32},V_{33},V_{23})
\]
is circumscribed as well.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $O_i$ and $R_i$ be the center and radius of the circle containing $\gamma_i$. Let $H$ be the half-plane containing the configuration. For a point $P\in H$, write
\[
d(P)=d(P,AC).
\]
Let $\Omega(P,r)$ denote the circle with center $P$ and radius $r$.

\textbf{Lemma 1.} Fix $1\le i<j\le3$ and consider circles $\Omega(P,r)$ tangent to both rays $h_i$ and $h_j$.

Their centers lie on the internal angle bisector $\beta_{ij}$ of the two rays. Moreover, there is a constant $u_{ij}>0$, depending only on the two rays, such that
\[
r=u_{ij}d(P).
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, all circles tangent to the two rays are homothetic with center of homothety $B$. Therefore both their radii and their distances from $AC$ change by the same factor, proving (1).

\textbf{Lemma 2.} Fix $1\le i<j\le3$ and consider circles $\Omega(P,r)$ that are externally tangent to $\gamma_i$ and internally tangent to $\gamma_j$.

Their centers lie on an ellipse arc $\varepsilon_{ij}$ joining $A$ and $C$. Moreover, there is a constant $v_{ij}>0$, depending only on the two arcs, such that
\[
r=v_{ij}d(P).
\tag{2}
\]

The tangency conditions are
\[
O_iP=R_i+r,
\qquad
O_jP=R_j-r.
\]
Thus
\[
O_iP+O_jP=R_i+R_j,
\]
so $P$ lies on the ellipse with foci $O_i,O_j$ and major-axis length $R_i+R_j$. The relevant part of this ellipse is the arc $\varepsilon_{ij}$ inside $H$ joining $A$ and $C$.

It remains to prove the linear relation (2). Put
\[
\boldsymbol{\rho}=\overrightarrow{AP},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\rho_i}=\overrightarrow{AO_i},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\rho_j}=\overrightarrow{AO_j}.
\]
Because $A$ lies on both circles,
\[
|\boldsymbol{\rho_i}|=R_i,
\qquad
|\boldsymbol{\rho_j}|=R_j.
\]
The tangency equations yield
\[
|\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho_i}|^2
-
|\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho_j}|^2
=
(R_i+r)^2-(R_j-r)^2.
\]
After expansion and cancellation,
\[
\boldsymbol{\rho}\cdot
(\boldsymbol{\rho_j}-\boldsymbol{\rho_i})
=
r(R_i+R_j).
\tag{3}
\]

The centers $O_i,O_j$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, so $O_iO_j\perp AC$. If
\[
d_{ij}=O_iO_j,
\]
then the left side of (3) equals
\[
d_{ij}d(P).
\]
Therefore
\[
r=\frac{d_{ij}}{R_i+R_j}\,d(P).
\]
Thus
\[
v_{ij}=\frac{d_{ij}}{R_i+R_j}.
\]

\textbf{Lemma 3.} The curved quadrilateral bounded by the consecutive rays $h_i,h_{i+1}$ and consecutive arcs $\gamma_j,\gamma_{j+1}$ is circumscribed if and only if
\[
u_{i,i+1}=v_{j,j+1}.
\tag{4}
\]

If the quadrilateral has an incircle $\Omega(P,r)$, Lemmas 1 and 2 give
\[
r=u_{i,i+1}d(P)
\quad\text{and}\quad
r=v_{j,j+1}d(P),
\]
so (4) follows.

Conversely, suppose (4) holds. Let $P$ be the intersection of the angle bisector $\beta_{i,i+1}$ with the ellipse arc $\varepsilon_{j,j+1}$, and set
\[
r=u_{i,i+1}d(P)=v_{j,j+1}d(P).
\]
By Lemma 1, $\Omega(P,r)$ is tangent to the two rays; by Lemma 2, it is tangent to the two arcs. Hence the curved quadrilateral is circumscribed.

We now translate the three hypotheses by Lemma 3. They give
\[
u_{12}=v_{12},
\qquad
u_{12}=v_{23},
\qquad
u_{23}=v_{12}.
\]
Therefore
\[
u_{23}=v_{23}.
\]
A final application of Lemma 3 shows that the curved quadrilateral bounded by $h_2,h_3$ and $\gamma_2,\gamma_3$, namely
\[
(V_{22},V_{32},V_{33},V_{23}),
\]
is circumscribed.
