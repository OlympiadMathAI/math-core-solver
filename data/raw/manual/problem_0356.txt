ID: 0356

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Two-Colourings, Perfect Squares, Pigeonhole Principle, Overlapping Intervals

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge100$ be an integer. The numbers
\[
n,n+1,\ldots,2n
\]
are written on $n+1$ cards, one number on each card. The cards are shuffled and divided into two piles.

Prove that one of the piles contains two cards whose numbers have a perfect-square sum.

SOLUTION 1:
It is enough to find three cards whose three pairwise sums are perfect squares. Among three cards, two must belong to the same pile.

For a positive integer $k$, consider
\[
a=2k^2-4k,\qquad
b=2k^2+1,\qquad
c=2k^2+4k.
\]
Their pairwise sums are
\[
a+b=(2k-1)^2,
\]
\[
a+c=(2k)^2,
\]
and
\[
b+c=(2k+1)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

The three numbers $a,b,c$ all lie in the interval $[n,2n]$ provided that
\[
n\le2k^2-4k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2k^2+4k\le2n.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
k^2+2k\le n\le2k^2-4k.
\tag{2}
\]

For $k\ge9$, the integer intervals
\[
I_k=
\bigl[k^2+2k,\;2k^2-4k\bigr]\cap\mathbb Z
\]
and $I_{k+1}$ have no gap between them. Indeed,
\[
(k+1)^2+2(k+1)
\le
2k^2-4k+1.
\tag{3}
\]
Also,
\[
I_9=[99,126]\cap\mathbb Z.
\]
It follows from (3) that
\[
I_9\cup I_{10}\cup I_{11}\cup\cdots
=
\{99,100,101,\ldots\}.
\]
Thus, for every $n\ge100$, there is an integer $k\ge9$ satisfying (2).

For this $k$, the cards numbered $a,b,c$ are present, and by (1) every pair among them has a perfect-square sum. Two of the three cards lie in the same pile, and that pair proves the assertion.
