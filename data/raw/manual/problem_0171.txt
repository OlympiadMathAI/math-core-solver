ID: 0171

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Diophantine Equations, Powers of Two, 2-adic Valuations, Case Analysis

STATEMENT:
Determine all triples $(a,b,c)$ of positive integers for which
\[
ab-c,\qquad bc-a,\qquad ca-b
\]
are powers of $2$. A power of $2$ means an integer of the form $2^n$, where $n$ is a nonnegative integer.

SOLUTION 1:
The complete list consists of
\[
(2,2,2),
\]
the three permutations of
\[
(2,2,3),
\]
and the six permutations of each of
\[
(2,6,11)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(3,5,7).
\]
A direct substitution verifies that all these triples work.

Now let $(a,b,c)$ be any solution. None of $a,b,c$ can equal $1$. For example, if $a=1$, then both
\[
b-c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c-b
\]
would have to be positive powers of $2$, which is impossible. Thus
\[
a,b,c\ge2.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Case 1: at least two variables are equal.}

By symmetry, suppose $a=b$. Then
\[
a^2-c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a(c-1)
\]
are powers of $2$. Hence both $a$ and $c-1$ are powers of $2$, so
\[
a=2^\alpha,
\qquad
c=2^\gamma+1
\]
for some $\alpha\ge1$ and $\gamma\ge0$.

Now
\[
a^2-c=2^{2\alpha}-2^\gamma-1
\]
is a power of $2$. If $\gamma\ge2$, this number is congruent to $-1$ modulo $4$, which is impossible. Thus $\gamma\le1$.

If $\gamma=0$, then $2^{2\alpha}-2$ is a power of $2$, forcing $\alpha=1$. If $\gamma=1$, then $2^{2\alpha}-3$ is an odd power of $2$, hence equals $1$, again forcing $\alpha=1$. We obtain
\[
(a,b,c)=(2,2,2)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(2,2,3).
\]

\textbf{Case 2: the variables are distinct.}

By symmetry, assume
\[
2\le a<b<c.
\tag{2}
\]
Write
\[
bc-a=2^\alpha,\qquad
ac-b=2^\beta,\qquad
ab-c=2^\gamma.
\tag{3}
\]
The differences
\[
(bc-a)-(ac-b)=(b-a)(c+1)>0
\]
and
\[
(ac-b)-(ab-c)=(c-b)(a+1)>0
\]
show that
\[
\alpha>\beta>\gamma.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Case 2a: $a=2$.}

We first prove that $\gamma=0$. If $\gamma>0$, then $c$ is even by $2b-c=2^\gamma$. Since $\beta>\gamma>0$, the equation $2c-b=2^\beta$ then shows that $b$ is even. But
\[
bc-2\equiv2\pmod4,
\]
so the power of $2$ equal to $bc-2$ would have to be $2$, giving $bc=4$, contrary to (2).

Thus $\gamma=0$, and
\[
c=2b-1.
\]
The middle equation in (3) becomes
\[
3b-2=2^\beta.
\tag{5}
\]
Because $b>2$, we have $\beta\ge4$. If $\beta=4$, then
\[
b=6,\qquad c=11,
\]
giving the solution $(2,6,11)$.

Suppose $\beta\ge5$. From (3) and (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
9\cdot2^\alpha
&=9b(2b-1)-18\\
&=(3b-2)(6b+1)-16\\
&=2^\beta(2^{\beta+1}+5)-16.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression has 2-adic valuation exactly $4$, so $\alpha=4$, contradicting $\alpha>\beta\ge5$. Therefore $(2,6,11)$ is the only solution in this case.

\textbf{Case 2b: $a\ge3$.}

Choose $\vartheta\in\{-1,1\}$ so that $c-\vartheta$ is not divisible by $4$. Using (3),
\[
2^\alpha+\vartheta2^\beta
=(b+\vartheta a)(c-\vartheta).
\tag{6}
\]
The left side has 2-adic valuation exactly $\beta$. Since $v_2(c-\vartheta)\le1$, equation (6) gives
\[
2^{\beta-1}\mid b+\vartheta a.
\tag{7}
\]

On the other hand,
\[
2^\beta=ac-b>(a-1)c\ge2c.
\]
Thus
\[
a,b<c<2^{\beta-1}.
\]
If $\vartheta=-1$, then
\[
0<b-a<2^{\beta-1},
\]
contradicting (7). Hence $\vartheta=1$. Since
\[
0<a+b<2c<2^\beta,
\]
equation (7) forces
\[
a+b=2^{\beta-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
ac-b=2^\beta=2(a+b).
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) gives
\[
a(c-1)=a+3b.
\]
Since $a<b$ and $c-1\ge b$,
\[
4b>a+3b=a(c-1)\ge ab.
\]
Hence $a<4$, and therefore $a=3$.

Now (8) becomes
\[
c=b+2.
\]
The first equation in (3) gives
\[
bc-a=b(b+2)-3=(b-1)(b+3),
\]
which is a power of $2$. Hence both $b-1$ and $b+3$ are powers of $2$. They differ by $4$, so they must be $4$ and $8$. Thus
\[
b=5,\qquad c=7.
\]

We have obtained only the listed triples and their permutations.

SOLUTION 2:
We again begin with $a,b,c\ge2$.

\textbf{Case 1: $a,b,c$ are all even.}

Let
\[
v_2(a)=A,\qquad v_2(b)=B,\qquad v_2(c)=C,
\]
and, by symmetry, suppose
\[
1\le A\le B\le C.
\]
Since
\[
v_2(ac)>B,
\]
the highest power of $2$ dividing $ac-b$ is $2^B$. As $ac-b$ itself is a power of $2$,
\[
ac-b=2^B\le b.
\]
Similarly,
\[
bc-a=2^A\le a.
\]
Adding these two inequalities gives
\[
(a+b)(c-1)\le a+b,
\]
so $c\le2$. Hence $c=2$, and then $A=B=C=1$. Equality must hold throughout, which yields
\[
a=b=c=2.
\]

\textbf{Case 2: $a,b,c$ are all odd.}

If two variables were equal, say $a=b$, then
\[
ac-b=a(c-1)
\]
would have the odd divisor $a>1$, so it could not be a power of $2$. Thus the variables are distinct; assume
\[
a<b<c.
\]
Write
\[
bc-a=2^\alpha,\qquad ac-b=2^\beta.
\]
Then $\alpha>\beta$. Moreover,
\[
a2^\alpha-b2^\beta
=a(bc-a)-b(ac-b)
=b^2-a^2
=(b+a)(b-a).
\]
Hence $2^\beta$ divides $(b+a)(b-a)$. The even numbers $b+a$ and $b-a$ cannot both be divisible by $4$, so one of them is divisible by $2^{\beta-1}$. Therefore
\[
2^\beta\le2(a+b).
\]
Consequently,
\[
(a-1)b<ac-b=2^\beta\le2(a+b)<4b,
\]
which gives $a<5$. Since $a$ is odd and at least $2$, we get $a=3$.

The inequality
\[
3c-b\le2(3+b)
\]
gives $c\le b+2$. Since $b<c$ and both are odd, we must have
\[
c=b+2.
\]
Then
\[
bc-a=(b-1)(b+3)
\]
is a power of $2$, so $b-1$ and $b+3$ are powers of $2$ differing by $4$. Hence
\[
b=5,\qquad c=7.
\]

\textbf{Case 3: both parities occur.}

By symmetry, take $c$ odd and assume $a\le b$. At least one of $a,b$ is even, so
\[
ab-c
\]
is odd. Being a power of $2$, it must equal $1$:
\[
ab-c=1.
\tag{9}
\]

If $a=b$, then
\[
c=a^2-1
\]
and
\[
ac-b=a(a^2-2)
\]
is a power of $2$. Hence both $a$ and $a^2-2$ are powers of $2$, which forces $a=2$. This gives
\[
(a,b,c)=(2,2,3).
\]

Now suppose $a<b$. Write
\[
bc-a=2^\alpha,\qquad ac-b=2^\beta,
\qquad \alpha>\beta.
\tag{10}
\]
If $\beta=0$, then $ac-b=ab-c=1$, which gives
\[
(a+1)(c-b)=0,
\]
impossible. Thus $\beta>0$. Equations (9) and (10) then show that $a$ and $b$ are both even.

Substituting $c=ab-1$ into (10), we get
\[
2^\alpha=ab^2-(a+b),
\tag{11}
\]
\[
2^\beta=a^2b-(a+b).
\tag{12}
\]
Adding,
\[
2^\alpha+2^\beta=(ab-2)(a+b).
\]
Here $ab-2$ is divisible by $2$ but not by $4$, so
\[
v_2(a+b)=\beta-1.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (11) and (12) then imply
\[
v_2(ab^2)=v_2(a^2b)=\beta-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
v_2(a)=v_2(b)=\tau
\]
for some $\tau\ge1$, and
\[
\beta=3\tau+1.
\]

Write
\[
a=2^\tau A,\qquad
b=2^\tau B,\qquad
a+b=2^{3\tau}C,
\]
where $A,B,C$ are odd and $A<B$. Thus
\[
A+B=2^{2\tau}C\ge4C.
\tag{14}
\]
Dividing (12) by $2^{3\tau}$ gives
\[
A^2B-C=2.
\tag{15}
\]
Using (14) and (15),
\[
\begin{aligned}
8
&=4A^2B-4C\\
&\ge4A^2B-A-B\\
&\ge A^2(3B-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $A$ and $B$ are odd and $A<B$, this forces
\[
A=1,\qquad B=3.
\]
Then (14) yields
\[
\tau=1,\qquad C=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
a=2,\qquad b=6,\qquad c=ab-1=11.
\]

This completes the classification.
