ID: 0162

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Parallelograms, Similar Triangles, Midpoints

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $AC$. A circle $\omega$ passing through $B$ and $M$ meets the sides $AB$ and $BC$ again at $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Let $T$ be the point such that $BPTQ$ is a parallelogram. Suppose that $T$ lies on the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. Determine all possible values of
\[
\frac{BT}{BM}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\sqrt2}.
\]

We first prove a lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} Let $ABCT$ be a cyclic quadrilateral. Let $U$ and $V$ lie on the lines $BA$ and $BC$, respectively, and suppose that $BUTV$ is a parallelogram. Then
\[
BU\cdot BA+BV\cdot BC=BT^2,
\tag{1}
\]
where directed lengths may be used if necessary.

\textit{Proof.}
Let the circumcircle of triangle $VTC$ meet the line $BT$ again at $J$. By the power of $B$ with respect to this circle,
\[
BV\cdot BC=BJ\cdot BT.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle TJV=180^\circ-\angle VCT=\angle TAB
\]
and, because $TV\parallel BA$,
\[
\angle VTJ=\angle ABT.
\]
Thus triangles $TJV$ and $BAT$ are similar, so
\[
\frac{TJ}{TV}=\frac{BA}{BT}.
\]
Since $TV=BU$, we get
\[
TJ\cdot BT=BU\cdot BA.
\tag{3}
\]
Adding (2) and (3), and using $BJ+JT=BT$ on the line $BT$, gives (1). $\square$

Let $X$ and $Y$ be the midpoints of $BA$ and $BC$, respectively. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$, the quadrilateral $BXMY$ is a parallelogram.

The points $P,B,Q,M$ lie on $\omega$. Applying the lemma to the cyclic quadrilateral $PBQM$ and the parallelogram $BXMY$, we obtain
\[
BX\cdot BP+BY\cdot BQ=BM^2.
\tag{4}
\]
The points $A,B,C,T$ are cyclic, and $BPTQ$ is a parallelogram. Applying the lemma again gives
\[
BP\cdot BA+BQ\cdot BC=BT^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Since
\[
BA=2BX
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
BC=2BY,
\]
equations (4) and (5) yield
\[
BT^2=2BM^2.
\]
All lengths are positive, so
\[
\frac{BT}{BM}=\sqrt2.
\]
