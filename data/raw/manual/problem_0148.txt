ID: 0148

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Optimization, Quadratic Forms, Inequalities, Rearrangement Inequality

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ be a fixed positive integer. Find the maximum possible value of
\[
\sum_{1\le r<s\le 2n}(s-r-n)x_rx_s,
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
-1\le x_i\le1
\]
for every $i=1,2,\ldots,2n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The maximum is
\[
\boxed{n(n-1)}.
\]

Let $Z$ denote the expression in (1). Since $Z$ is linear in each variable separately, its maximum on the cube $[-1,1]^{2n}$ is attained at a vertex. Thus it is enough to consider
\[
x_i\in\{-1,1\}
\]
for every $i$.

For $i=1,2,\ldots,2n$, define
\[
y_i=\sum_{r=1}^{i}x_r-\sum_{r=i+1}^{2n}x_r.
\]
Because $x_r^2=1$, expansion gives
\[
y_i^2
=2n
+2\sum_{r<s\le i}x_rx_s
+2\sum_{i<r<s}x_rx_s
-2\sum_{r\le i<s}x_rx_s.
\tag{2}
\]
For fixed $r<s$, the coefficient of $x_rx_s$ in
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}y_i^2
\]
is
\[
2(2n-s+r)-2(s-r)=4(n-s+r).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}y_i^2
=4n^2+4\sum_{1\le r<s\le2n}(n-s+r)x_rx_s
=4n^2-4Z.
\tag{3}
\]

Each $y_i$ is an even integer, and
\[
y_i-y_{i-1}=2x_i\in\{-2,2\}
\]
for $i\ge2$. Hence two consecutive $y$'s are distinct even integers, so
\[
y_{i-1}^2+y_i^2\ge4.
\]
Pairing consecutive terms yields
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n}y_i^2
=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(y_{2j-1}^2+y_{2j}^2\right)
\ge4n.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3) and (4),
\[
4n\le4n^2-4Z,
\]
so
\[
Z\le n(n-1).
\]

Equality is attained by taking
\[
x_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&i\text{ odd},\\
-1,&i\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
For this choice, the pairs $(y_{2j-1},y_{2j})$ are $(2,0)$, so equality holds in (4). Thus the maximum is $n(n-1)$.

SOLUTION 2:
Again, because the expression is linear in each variable, it is enough to consider
\[
x_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Let
\[
A=\{i:x_i=1\},
\qquad
B=\{i:x_i=-1\}.
\]
For subsets $X,Y\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,2n\}$, define
\[
e(X,Y)=\sum_{\substack{r<s\\r\in X,\ s\in Y}}(s-r-n).
\]
Then
\[
Z=e(A,A)-e(A,B)-e(B,A)+e(B,B).
\tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
e(A,A)+e(A,B)+e(B,A)+e(B,B)
=\sum_{1\le r<s\le2n}(s-r-n)
=-\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}3.
\]
Hence
\[
Z=2\bigl(e(A,A)+e(B,B)\bigr)
+\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}3.
\tag{3}
\]

Interchanging $A$ and $B$ does not change $Z$, so write
\[
|A|=n-p,
\qquad
|B|=n+p
\]
with $0\le p\le n$. List the elements as
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_{n-p},
\qquad
b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_{n+p}.
\]
A direct collection of coefficients gives
\[
e(A,A)
=\sum_{i=1}^{n-p}(2i-1-n+p)a_i
-n\binom{n-p}{2},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
e(B,B)
=\sum_{i=1}^{n+p}(2i-1-n-p)b_i
-n\binom{n+p}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus we have to maximize
\[
M=
\sum_{i=1}^{n-p}(2i-1-n+p)a_i
+
\sum_{i=1}^{n+p}(2i-1-n-p)b_i,
\tag{6}
\]
where the numbers $a_i,b_i$ together form a permutation of $1,2,\ldots,2n$.

In descending order, the coefficients in (6) are
\[
n+p+1-2i\quad (i=1,\ldots,p),
\]
then
\[
n-p+1-2i\quad (i=1,\ldots,n-p),
\]
each occurring twice, and finally the negatives of the first $p$ coefficients. By the rearrangement inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
M\le{}&
\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n+p+1-2i)(2n+1-i)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n-p}(n-p+1-2i)
\bigl((2n+2-p-2i)+(2n+1-p-2i)\bigr)\\
&-\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n+p+1-2i)i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Substituting (4), (5), and (7) into (3), and simplifying, gives
\[
Z\le n(n-1)-\frac23p(p-1)(p+1).
\]
Since $p$ is a nonnegative integer,
\[
p(p-1)(p+1)\ge0,
\]
and therefore
\[
Z\le n(n-1).
\]
The alternating choice from Solution 1 attains this value, so the maximum is $n(n-1)$.
