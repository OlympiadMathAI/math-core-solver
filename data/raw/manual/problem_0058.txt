ID: 0058

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Hamiltonian Cycles, Power Residues, Quadratic Residues, Fermat Primes

STATEMENT:
Let $k$ be a positive integer and put
\[
n=2^k+1.
\]
Prove that $n$ is prime if and only if there exist

- a permutation
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_{n-1}
\]
of $1,2,\ldots,n-1$, and

- integers
\[
g_1,g_2,\ldots,g_{n-1},
\]

such that
\[
n\mid g_i^{a_i}-a_{i+1}
\qquad(i=1,\ldots,n-1),
\]
where
\[
a_n=a_1.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
\mathcal N=\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}.
\]
For $a,b\in\mathcal N$, say that $b$ \emph{follows} $a$, and write
\[
a\longrightarrow b,
\]
if there exists an integer $g$ such that
\[
b\equiv g^a\pmod n.
\]
The required permutation exists exactly when this directed graph on $\mathcal N$ has a Hamiltonian cycle.

\textbf{Step 1: Composite $n$ admits no Hamiltonian cycle.}

Suppose that $n$ is composite, and write
\[
n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_s^{\alpha_s}.
\]
All the primes $p_i$ are odd.

Assume first that
\[
\alpha_i>1
\]
for some $i$. For every integer $g$ and every exponent $a\ge2$,
\[
g^a\not\equiv p_i\pmod{p_i^2}.
\]
Indeed, if $p_i\mid g$, then $p_i^2\mid g^a$; if $p_i\nmid g$, then $p_i\nmid g^a$. Thus, in any Hamiltonian cycle, the vertex $p_i$ must immediately follow the vertex $1$.

The same argument applies to $2p_i$, which is also a vertex of $\mathcal N$, and shows that $2p_i$ must immediately follow $1$. A vertex in a directed cycle has only one successor, so this is impossible.

It remains to consider the case in which $n$ is square-free:
\[
n=p_1p_2\cdots p_s,
\qquad
s\ge2.
\]
Then
\[
n>9.
\]
There are exactly
\[
\frac{n-1}{2}
\]
even vertices in $\mathcal N$. If $a$ is even and $a\to b$, then $b$ is a quadratic residue modulo $n$. Since the successors in a Hamiltonian cycle are all distinct, the cycle would require at least $(n-1)/2$ nonzero quadratic residues modulo $n$.

For each prime $p_i$, there are $(p_i+1)/2$ quadratic residues modulo $p_i$, including $0$. By the Chinese remainder theorem, the total number of quadratic residues modulo $n$, again including $0$, is
\[
\prod_{i=1}^s\frac{p_i+1}{2}.
\]
Since $p_i\ge3$,
\[
\frac{p_i+1}{2}\le\frac{2p_i}{3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\prod_{i=1}^s\frac{p_i+1}{2}
\le
\left(\frac23\right)^s n
\le
\frac{4n}{9}
<
\frac{n-1}{2},
\]
where the last inequality uses $n>9$. This is a contradiction. Hence the required Hamiltonian cycle cannot exist when $n$ is composite.

\textbf{Step 2: Structure of the graph when $n$ is prime.}

Assume now that $n$ is prime. Then
\[
|\mathcal N|=n-1=2^k.
\]
For $a\in\mathcal N$, let $\nu_2(a)$ be the exponent of $2$ in the prime factorization of $a$, and define
\[
\mu(a)=
\max\left\{t\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}:2^t\longrightarrow a\right\}.
\]

We need the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} For $a,b\in\mathcal N$,
\[
a\longrightarrow b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\nu_2(a)\le\mu(b).
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
\ell=\nu_2(a),
\qquad
m=\mu(b).
\]

Suppose first that $\ell\le m$. By the definition of $\mu(b)$, there exists $g_0$ such that
\[
b\equiv g_0^{\,2^m}\pmod n.
\tag{2}
\]
Since
\[
\gcd(a,n-1)=2^\ell,
\]
there are integers $u,v$ satisfying
\[
ua-v(n-1)=2^\ell.
\tag{3}
\]
We may choose $u$ nonnegative by replacing $(u,v)$ with another solution if necessary. Put
\[
g=g_0^{\,2^{m-\ell}u}.
\]
Using (3) and Fermat's little theorem,
\[
\begin{aligned}
g^a
&=g_0^{\,2^{m-\ell}ua}\\
&=g_0^{\,2^m+2^{m-\ell}v(n-1)}\\
&\equiv g_0^{\,2^m}\\
&\equiv b
\pmod n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $a\to b$.

Conversely, suppose that $a\to b$. Then
\[
b\equiv g^a\pmod n
\]
for some integer $g$. Writing
\[
a=2^\ell q
\]
with $q$ odd, we get
\[
b\equiv(g^q)^{2^\ell}\pmod n.
\]
Therefore
\[
2^\ell\longrightarrow b,
\]
so
\[
\ell\le\mu(b).
\]
The lemma is proved.

For $0\le i\le k$, define
\[
A_i=\{a\in\mathcal N:\nu_2(a)=i\},
\]
\[
B_i=\{a\in\mathcal N:\mu(a)=i\},
\]
and
\[
C_i=\{a\in\mathcal N:\mu(a)\ge i\}
=B_i\cup B_{i+1}\cup\cdots\cup B_k.
\]

We claim that
\[
|A_i|=|B_i|
\qquad(0\le i\le k).
\tag{4}
\]
Clearly,
\[
|A_i|=2^{k-i-1}
\qquad(0\le i<k),
\]
and
\[
|A_k|=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Now determine the sizes of the sets $C_i$. We have
\[
|C_0|=2^k,
\qquad
C_k=\{1\},
\qquad
|C_k|=1.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
C_{i+1}
=
\{x^2\bmod n:x\in C_i\}.
\tag{7}
\]
For every nonzero residue $y$ modulo the prime $n$, the congruence
\[
x^2\equiv y\pmod n
\]
has at most two solutions. Hence (7) gives
\[
|C_i|\le2|C_{i+1}|.
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) successively and using (6),
\[
2^k=|C_0|
\le2|C_1|
\le\cdots\le2^k|C_k|
=2^k.
\]
Thus equality holds at every step, and
\[
|C_i|=2^{k-i}
\qquad(0\le i\le k).
\tag{9}
\]
It follows that
\[
|B_i|=|C_i|-|C_{i+1}|=2^{k-i-1}
\qquad(0\le i<k),
\]
and
\[
|B_k|=1.
\]
Together with (5), this proves (4).

The equality case in (8) also shows that, for every $0\le i<k$ and every $z\in C_i$, both square roots of $z^2$ modulo $n$ belong to $C_i$. These roots are
\[
z
\quad\text{and}\quad
n-z.
\]
Hence
\[
z\in C_i\Longrightarrow n-z\in C_i.
\tag{10}
\]
The pair $\{z,n-z\}$ contains one odd and one even integer. Thus exactly half of the elements of $C_i$ are odd for $i<k$. For
\[
0\le i\le k-2,
\]
both $C_i$ and $C_{i+1}$ are stable under $z\mapsto n-z$, so the same is true of
\[
B_i=C_i\setminus C_{i+1}.
\]
In particular, every $B_i$ with $0\le i\le k-2$ contains an odd number.

Finally,
\[
B_k=C_k=\{1\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Also, $C_{k-1}$ consists of the two square roots of $1$ modulo $n$, namely
\[
1
\quad\text{and}\quad
n-1=2^k.
\]
Therefore
\[
B_{k-1}=\{2^k\}.
\tag{12}
\]

\textbf{Step 3: Construction of a Hamiltonian cycle.}

For each $i=0,\ldots,k$, choose an arbitrary bijection
\[
A_i\longrightarrow B_i.
\]
Join every $a\in A_i$ to its assigned element of $B_i$. By the lemma, every chosen edge is allowed. Since the sets $A_i$ partition $\mathcal N$ and the sets $B_i$ also partition $\mathcal N$, every vertex has out-degree $1$ and in-degree $1$. Hence the chosen edges form a disjoint union of directed cycles.

We show how to merge cycles until only one remains. For a cycle $\mathcal C$, define
\[
\lambda(\mathcal C)=
\min_{c\in\mathcal C}\nu_2(c).
\]
Choose a cycle $\mathcal C$ for which $\lambda(\mathcal C)$ is maximal.

Suppose first that
\[
\lambda(\mathcal C)=0.
\]
Then every other cycle also contains an odd vertex. Take odd vertices
\[
a\in\mathcal C,
\qquad
a'\in\mathcal C'
\]
from two different cycles, and let their successors be $b$ and $b'$. Since
\[
a,a'\in A_0
\quad\text{and}\quad
b,b'\in B_0,
\]
we may replace the two edges
\[
a\to b,
\qquad
a'\to b'
\]
by
\[
a\to b',
\qquad
a'\to b.
\]
The new edges are still allowed by the lemma, and this operation merges the two cycles.

Now suppose
\[
\lambda=\lambda(\mathcal C)\ge1,
\]
and choose
\[
a\in\mathcal C\cap A_\lambda.
\]
If there is another vertex
\[
a'\in A_\lambda\setminus\mathcal C,
\]
then $a'$ lies in another cycle, and swapping the successors of $a$ and $a'$ merges those two cycles exactly as above.

We are left with the case
\[
A_\lambda\subset\mathcal C.
\]
Because the chosen edges give a bijection from $A_\lambda$ to $B_\lambda$, this implies
\[
B_\lambda\subset\mathcal C.
\tag{13}
\]

If $\lambda\le k-2$, then $B_\lambda$ contains an odd number, contradicting
\[
\lambda(\mathcal C)\ge1.
\]

The case $\lambda=k$ is also impossible, because (11) would place the odd vertex $1$ in $\mathcal C$.

Finally, suppose
\[
\lambda=k-1.
\]
The unique element of $A_{k-1}$ is $2^{k-1}$. By (12), its successor is $2^k$, the unique element of $A_k$, whose successor is $1$ by (11). Thus $\mathcal C$ contains the path
\[
2^{k-1}\longrightarrow2^k\longrightarrow1,
\]
again contradicting $\lambda(\mathcal C)\ge1$.

Therefore, whenever more than one cycle is present, two of them can be merged. Repeating the operation finitely many times produces one directed Hamiltonian cycle
\[
a_1\to a_2\to\cdots\to a_{n-1}\to a_1.
\]
For each edge $a_i\to a_{i+1}$, choose an integer $g_i$ satisfying
\[
g_i^{a_i}\equiv a_{i+1}\pmod n.
\]
Then
\[
n\mid g_i^{a_i}-a_{i+1}
\]
for every $i$, as required.

Thus the stated permutation and integers exist exactly when $n$ is prime.
