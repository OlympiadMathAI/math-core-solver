ID: 0335

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Telescoping Sums, Refinement, Probabilistic Method

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge2$ be an integer, and let
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n
\]
be positive real numbers satisfying
\[
a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n=1.
\]
Prove that
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{a_k}{1-a_k}
\left(a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{k-1}\right)^2
<
\frac13.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
s_k=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k,
\qquad
s_0=0.
\]
We prove, for every $k$, that
\[
\frac{a_ks_{k-1}^2}{1-a_k}
<
\frac{s_k^3-s_{k-1}^3}{3}.
\tag{1}
\]

Write
\[
a=a_k,
\qquad
s=s_{k-1}.
\]
Since
\[
a>0,\qquad s\ge0,\qquad a+s=s_k\le1,
\]
we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{(s+a)^3-s^3}{3}
-\frac{as^2}{1-a}\\
&\quad=
a\left(s^2+as+\frac{a^2}{3}\right)
-\frac{as^2}{1-a}\\
&\quad=
\frac{a^2}{1-a}
\left(
s(1-a-s)+\frac{a(1-a)}3
\right)
>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (1) holds.

Summing (1) from $k=1$ to $n$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{a_ks_{k-1}^2}{1-a_k}
&<
\frac13\sum_{k=1}^{n}(s_k^3-s_{k-1}^3)\\
&=
\frac{s_n^3-s_0^3}{3}\\
&=
\frac13.
\end{aligned}
\]

SOLUTION 2:
For a finite ordered tuple of positive numbers with sum $1$, define
\[
S(a_1,\ldots,a_n)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{a_k}{1-a_k}
\left(a_1+\cdots+a_{k-1}\right)^2.
\]

Fix an index $i$, and put
\[
a=a_i,
\qquad
s=a_1+\cdots+a_{i-1}.
\]
Replace the term $a$ by two consecutive terms
\[
\frac a2,\frac a2.
\]
Only the contribution at that location changes. The increase in $S$ is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta
&=
\frac{a}{2-a}
\left(
s^2+\left(s+\frac a2\right)^2
\right)
-\frac{a}{1-a}s^2\\
&=
\frac{a^2\left(4s(1-a-s)+a(1-a)\right)}
{4(2-a)(1-a)}\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus splitting any part into two equal adjacent parts strictly increases $S$.

Repeat this operation so that the largest part of the resulting ordered partition tends to $0$. Let the refined parts be
\[
c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_N
\]
and let
\[
t_j=c_1+\cdots+c_j,
\qquad t_0=0.
\]
Then
\[
S(c_1,\ldots,c_N)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\frac{c_j}{1-c_j}t_{j-1}^2.
\tag{2}
\]
As
\[
\max_jc_j\longrightarrow0,
\]
the right side of (2) tends to the left Riemann integral
\[
\int_0^1x^2\,dx=\frac13.
\]
Indeed,
\[
\frac{c_j}{1-c_j}=c_j+O(c_j^2)
\]
uniformly, so replacing the coefficient by $c_j$ changes the sum by a quantity tending to $0$.

Every finite refinement has value strictly larger than the original one, while the refined values approach $1/3$. Therefore
\[
S(a_1,\ldots,a_n)<\frac13.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
Choose three points
\[
X_1,X_2,X_3
\]
independently and uniformly at random from $[0,1]$.

Partition $[0,1]$ into consecutive intervals
\[
I_1,I_2,\ldots,I_n
\]
of lengths
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n.
\]
For each $k$, let
\[
J_k=I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_{k-1},
\qquad
s_{k-1}=|J_k|=a_1+\cdots+a_{k-1}.
\]

With probability $1/3$, the point $X_1$ is the largest of the three. Decomposing this event according to the interval containing $X_1$, we get
\[
\frac13
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\left(
a_ks_{k-1}^2
+a_k^2s_{k-1}
+\frac{a_k^3}{3}
\right).
\tag{1}
\]
The three terms correspond respectively to:

\begin{itemize}
\item $X_1\in I_k$ and $X_2,X_3\in J_k$;
\item exactly one of $X_2,X_3$ lies in $J_k$, while the other lies in $I_k$ below $X_1$;
\item all three points lie in $I_k$, with $X_1$ the largest.
\end{itemize}

Since
\[
s_{k-1}\le1-a_k,
\]
we have
\[
a_k^2s_{k-1}
\ge
\frac{a_k^2s_{k-1}^2}{1-a_k}.
\]
The additional term $a_k^3/3$ is strictly positive. Hence every summand in (1) is strictly larger than
\[
a_ks_{k-1}^2
+
\frac{a_k^2s_{k-1}^2}{1-a_k}
=
\frac{a_k}{1-a_k}s_{k-1}^2.
\]
Summing over $k$ proves
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{a_k}{1-a_k}s_{k-1}^2
<
\frac13.
\]
