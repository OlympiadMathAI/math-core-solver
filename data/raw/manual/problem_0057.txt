ID: 0057

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Harmonic Sums, Congruences, Binomial Coefficients

STATEMENT:
Let $p$ be an odd prime. For every integer $a$, define
\[
S_a=\frac a1+\frac{a^2}{2}+\cdots+\frac{a^{p-1}}{p-1}.
\]
Let $m,n$ be integers with $n\ne0$ such that
\[
S_3+S_4-3S_2=\frac mn.
\]
Prove that
\[
p\mid m.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We use congruences modulo $p$ for rational numbers whose denominators are not divisible by $p$. Thus
\[
\frac{u_1}{v_1}\equiv\frac{u_2}{v_2}\pmod p
\]
means
\[
p\mid u_1v_2-u_2v_1,
\qquad
p\nmid v_1v_2.
\]

For $1\le k\le p-1$,
\[
\frac1p\binom pk
=
\frac{(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(p-k+1)}{k!}
\equiv
\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}k
\pmod p.
\tag{1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_a
&=-\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}
(-a)^k\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}k\\
&\equiv
-\frac1p\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom pk(-a)^k
\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using the binomial theorem and the fact that $p$ is odd,
\[
\begin{aligned}
-\frac1p\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom pk(-a)^k
&=
-\frac{(1-a)^p-1-(-a)^p}{p}\\
&=
\frac{(a-1)^p-a^p+1}{p}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
S_a\equiv
\frac{(a-1)^p-a^p+1}{p}
\pmod p.
\tag{2}
\]

Applying (2) for $a=2,3,4$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_3+S_4-3S_2
&\equiv
\frac{(2^p-3^p+1)+(3^p-4^p+1)-3(1-2^p+1)}p\\
&=
\frac{4\cdot2^p-4^p-4}{p}\\
&=
-\frac{(2^p-2)^2}{p}
\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
By Fermat's little theorem,
\[
p\mid2^p-2.
\]
Thus the numerator $(2^p-2)^2$ in (3) is divisible by $p^2$, and consequently
\[
S_3+S_4-3S_2\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{4}
\]

The number on the left of (4) has a denominator not divisible by $p$, because all its summands have denominators among $1,2,\ldots,p-1$. Write it in lowest terms as
\[
\frac AB,
\qquad
p\nmid B.
\]
Relation (4) says that
\[
p\mid A.
\]
Since
\[
\frac mn=\frac AB,
\]
we have
\[
mB=nA.
\]
Thus $p\mid mB$, and because $p\nmid B$,
\[
p\mid m.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
As in Solution 1, all congruences between rational numbers are taken modulo $p$, with denominators coprime to $p$.

We first prove the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} For every integer $a$,
\[
S_{a+1}\equiv S_{-a}\pmod p.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, by the binomial theorem,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{a+1}
&=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac1k
   \sum_{j=0}^k\binom kj a^j\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac1k
+
\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}a^j
\sum_{k=j}^{p-1}\frac1k\binom kj.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The first sum vanishes modulo $p$, because
\[
\frac1k+\frac1{p-k}
=\frac{p}{k(p-k)}
\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{3}
\]

For the second sum, use
\[
\frac1k\binom kj
=
\frac1j\binom{k-1}{j-1}
\]
and the hockey-stick identity:
\[
\sum_{k=j}^{p-1}\binom{k-1}{j-1}
=
\binom{p-1}{j}
\equiv(-1)^j\pmod p.
\]
Substituting into (2),
\[
S_{a+1}
\equiv
\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-a)^j}{j}
=S_{-a}
\pmod p.
\]
This proves the lemma.

Taking $a=2$ in (1),
\[
S_3\equiv S_{-2}\pmod p.
\]
Therefore
\[
S_3-3S_2
\equiv
\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^k-3\cdot2^k}{k}
\pmod p.
\tag{4}
\]

For even $k=2\ell$, the summand in (4) is
\[
-\frac{2\cdot2^{2\ell}}{2\ell}
=-\frac{4^\ell}{\ell}.
\]
For odd $k=2\ell-1$, it is
\[
-\frac{4\cdot2^{2\ell-1}}{2\ell-1}
=-\frac{2^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell-1}.
\]
By Fermat's little theorem,
\[
2^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,
\]
so
\[
-\frac{2^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell-1}
\equiv
-\frac{2^{p+2\ell}}{p+2\ell-1}
\pmod p.
\]
Put
\[
r=\ell+\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
p+2\ell-1=2r
\]
and
\[
-\frac{2^{p+2\ell}}{p+2\ell-1}
=
-\frac{2\cdot4^r}{2r}
=
-\frac{4^r}{r}.
\]

As $\ell$ ranges from $1$ to $(p-1)/2$, the even terms give the indices
\[
1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
while the transformed odd terms give
\[
\frac{p+1}{2},\ldots,p-1.
\]
Thus (4) becomes
\[
S_3-3S_2
\equiv
-\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\frac{4^r}{r}
=-S_4
\pmod p.
\]
Hence
\[
S_3+S_4-3S_2\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

As explained at the end of Solution 1, the left-hand side has a denominator coprime to $p$. Therefore, from
\[
S_3+S_4-3S_2=\frac mn,
\]
it follows that
\[
p\mid m.
\]
