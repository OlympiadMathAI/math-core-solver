ID: 0003

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Cyclic Sequences

STATEMENT:
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_{100}$ be nonnegative real numbers such that
\[
x_i+x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}\le 1
\]
for every $i=1,\ldots,100$, where $x_{101}=x_1$ and $x_{102}=x_2$. Find the maximal possible value of
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^{100}x_i x_{i+2}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The maximum is
\[
\boxed{\frac{25}{2}}.
\]
It is attained, for example, by
\[
x_{2i}=0,\qquad x_{2i-1}=\frac12
\]
for $i=1,\ldots,50$.

It remains to prove the upper bound. For each $i=1,\ldots,50$, the conditions imply
\[
x_{2i-1}\le 1-x_{2i}-x_{2i+1},
\qquad
x_{2i+2}\le 1-x_{2i}-x_{2i+1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&x_{2i-1}x_{2i+1}+x_{2i}x_{2i+2}\\
&\qquad\le (1-x_{2i}-x_{2i+1})x_{2i+1}
+x_{2i}(1-x_{2i}-x_{2i+1})\\
&\qquad=(x_{2i}+x_{2i+1})(1-x_{2i}-x_{2i+1})\\
&\qquad\le \frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing these $50$ inequalities gives
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^{50}
\bigl(x_{2i-1}x_{2i+1}+x_{2i}x_{2i+2}\bigr)
\le 50\cdot\frac14=\frac{25}{2}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
X=\sum_{i=1}^{100}x_i.
\]
By the given inequalities,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S
&=\sum_{i=1}^{100}x_i x_{i+2}\\
&\le \sum_{i=1}^{100}x_i(1-x_i-x_{i+1})\\
&=X-\sum_{i=1}^{100}x_i^2-\sum_{i=1}^{100}x_ix_{i+1}\\
&=X-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{100}(x_i+x_{i+1})^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the quadratic-mean inequality,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{100}(x_i+x_{i+1})^2
\ge \frac1{100}
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{100}(x_i+x_{i+1})\right)^2
=\frac{(2X)^2}{100}.
\]
Hence
\[
S\le X-\frac{X^2}{50}
=\frac{2}{100}X(50-X)
\le \frac{2}{100}\left(\frac{50}{2}\right)^2
=\frac{25}{2}.
\]
The construction in Solution 1 shows that equality is attainable.
