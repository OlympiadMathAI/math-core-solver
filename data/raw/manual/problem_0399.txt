ID: 0399

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Inequalities, Iteration, Telescoping Sums, Multiplicative Invariants

STATEMENT:
Determine all functions
\[
f:\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}
\]
such that
\[
x\bigl(f(x)+f(y)\bigr)
\ge
\bigl(f(f(x))+y\bigr)f(y)
\tag{1}
\]
for all positive real numbers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=\frac{c}{x}}
\]
for arbitrary constants $c>0$.

Let $f^m$ denote the $m$-fold iterate of $f$, with $f^0(x)=x$.

Setting $y=x$ in (1) and dividing by $f(x)>0$ gives
\[
x\ge f^2(x).
\tag{2}
\]
Setting $x=f(y)$ gives
\[
f(y)+f^2(y)\ge y+f^3(y),
\]
or
\[
f(y)-f^3(y)\ge y-f^2(y).
\tag{3}
\]
Replacing $y$ by $f^{n-1}(y)$ in (3),
\[
f^n(y)-f^{n+2}(y)
\ge
f^{n-1}(y)-f^{n+1}(y).
\]
Using (2), induction gives
\[
f^n(y)-f^{n+2}(y)
\ge
y-f^2(y)
\ge0
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{4}
\]

For $m\ge1$, telescope the even iterates:
\[
\begin{aligned}
y-f^{2m}(y)
&=
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
\left(f^{2i}(y)-f^{2i+2}(y)\right)\\
&\ge
m\bigl(y-f^2(y)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $f^{2m}(y)>0$, the left side is smaller than $y$. Letting $m$ be arbitrarily large forces
\[
f^2(y)=y
\qquad(y>0).
\tag{5}
\]

Substituting (5) into (1),
\[
x\bigl(f(x)+f(y)\bigr)\ge(x+y)f(y),
\]
so
\[
xf(x)\ge yf(y)
\qquad(x,y>0).
\]
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ shows equality:
\[
xf(x)=yf(y).
\]
Thus $xf(x)$ is a positive constant $c$, and
\[
f(x)=\frac{c}{x}.
\]

Conversely, every function $f(x)=c/x$ satisfies $f(f(x))=x$, and both sides of (1) differ only by the equality
\[
xf(x)=yf(y)=c.
\]
Hence all such functions work.

SOLUTION 2:
We again write $f^m$ for the $m$-fold iterate.

As in the direct substitutions below,
\[
x\ge f^2(x)
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
f^n(x)\ge f^{n+2}(x)
\qquad(n\ge0).
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, (2) follows by setting $y=x$ in (1), while setting $x=f(y)$ gives
\[
f(y)-f^3(y)\ge y-f^2(y),
\]
from which (3) follows inductively.

Since all values are positive, (3) yields the decreasing chain
\[
xf(x)
\ge
f(x)f^2(x)
\ge
f^2(x)f^3(x)
\ge\cdots.
\tag{4}
\]

Replace $x$ in (1) by $f^n(x)$. Rearranging gives
\[
f^n(x)-f^{n+2}(x)
\ge
\frac{
yf(y)-f^n(x)f^{n+1}(x)
}{f(y)}.
\]
Using (4),
\[
f^n(x)-f^{n+2}(x)
\ge
\frac{yf(y)-xf(x)}{f(y)}.
\tag{5}
\]

Sum (5) for
\[
n=0,2,4,\ldots,2m-2.
\]
Then
\[
x-f^{2m}(x)
\ge
m\frac{yf(y)-xf(x)}{f(y)}.
\]
The left side is smaller than $x$. Therefore the right side cannot be positive for arbitrarily large $m$, so
\[
yf(y)\le xf(x)
\qquad(x,y>0).
\]
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives equality. Hence
\[
xf(x)=c
\]
for a constant $c>0$, and
\[
f(x)=\frac{c}{x}.
\]

Conversely, for this function,
\[
f(f(x))=x
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
xf(x)=yf(y)=c.
\]
After substitution, inequality (1) holds with equality. Thus all and only the displayed functions are solutions.
