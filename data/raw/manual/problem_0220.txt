ID: 0220

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Double Counting, Three-Dimensional Colorings, Induction, Extremal Constructions

STATEMENT:
Let $n>1$ be an integer. An $n\times n\times n$ cube is composed of $n^3$ unit cubes, each painted with one color.

For every $n\times n\times1$ slice, of any of the three orientations, consider the set of colors occurring in that slice, listing each color only once. Thus there are $3n$ color sets, divided into three groups according to orientation.

Suppose that every color set occurring in one orientation also occurs in each of the other two orientations. Determine the maximum possible number of colors in the cube.

SOLUTION 1:
The maximum is
\[
\boxed{\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6}.
\]

Call a slice perpendicular to the $x$-, $y$-, or $z$-axis an $x$-slice, $y$-slice, or $z$-slice.

Let $\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2,\mathcal C_3$ be the sets of colors occurring respectively exactly once, exactly twice, and at least three times in the whole cube. Let $M_i$ be the set of unit cubes whose colors belong to $\mathcal C_i$, and put
\[
n_i=|M_i|.
\]

Fix an $x$-slice $X$. By the hypothesis, there are a $y$-slice $Y$ and a $z$-slice $Z$ having exactly the same color set as $X$.

We claim that
\[
4|X\cap M_1|+|X\cap M_2|\le3n+1.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose first that $X\cap M_1\ne\varnothing$. A color occurring only once but present in all three slices $X,Y,Z$ must occur at their unique common unit cube. Hence
\[
|X\cap M_1|=1,
\]
and that common cube has the singleton color.

The union
\[
(X\cap Y)\cup(X\cap Z)
\]
contains $2n-1$ cubes, one of which is the common singleton cube. Thus at most
\[
2n-2
\]
cubes of $X\cap M_2$ lie in this union.

Take any other cube $a\in X\cap M_2$. Its color occurs in exactly one other cube $a'$. Since both $Y$ and $Z$ contain this color and $a$ lies in neither of them, the cube $a'$ must lie in
\[
(Y\cap Z)\setminus X,
\]
which has $n-1$ cubes. The assignment $a\mapsto a'$ is injective. Therefore
\[
|X\cap M_2|\le(2n-2)+(n-1)=3(n-1),
\]
and (1) follows.

Now suppose
\[
X\cap M_1=\varnothing.
\]
At most $2n-1$ cubes of $X\cap M_2$ lie in
\[
(X\cap Y)\cup(X\cap Z).
\]
Every remaining one has its mate in $Y\cap Z$, which contains $n$ cubes. Hence
\[
|X\cap M_2|\le(2n-1)+n=3n-1,
\]
which again implies (1).

Summing (1) over all $n$ $x$-slices gives
\[
4n_1+n_2\le n(3n+1).
\tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
n_1+n_2+n_3=n^3.
\tag{3}
\]

If $C$ is the total number of colors, then
\[
C
\le
n_1+\frac{n_2}{2}+\frac{n_3}{3}.
\]
Using (2)--(3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
C
&\le
\frac{n_1+n_2+n_3}{3}
+
\frac{4n_1+n_2}{6}\\
&\le
\frac{n^3}{3}
+
\frac{n(3n+1)}6\\
&=
\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to attain this bound. Label the unit cubes by triples
\[
(x,y,z)\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}^3.
\]
Use one new color for each of the following disjoint sets of cubes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the $n$ singletons
\[
S_i=\{(i,i,i)\};
\]

\item for every $1\le i<j\le n$, the three doubletons
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{i,j}^{(1)}&=\{(i,j,j),(j,i,i)\},\\
D_{i,j}^{(2)}&=\{(j,i,j),(i,j,i)\},\\
D_{i,j}^{(3)}&=\{(j,j,i),(i,i,j)\};
\end{aligned}
\]

\item for every ordered triple satisfying either
\[
1\le i<j<k\le n
\]
or
\[
1\le i<k<j\le n,
\]
the triplet
\[
T_{i,j,k}
=
\{(i,j,k),(j,k,i),(k,i,j)\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

These sets partition all unit cubes. For every index $r$, a color occurs in the $r$th $x$-slice if and only if it occurs in the $r$th $y$-slice, and if and only if it occurs in the $r$th $z$-slice. Thus the condition is satisfied.

The number of colors is
\[
n+3\binom n2+2\binom n3
=
\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
We prove the upper bound by induction in a slightly more general setting.

Allow every unit cube either to have a color or to be invisible. Invisible cubes contribute no color to a slice. Require only that every \emph{nonempty} color set occurring in one orientation also occur in the other two orientations. Call such a configuration \emph{strange}.

Let $D(n)$ be the maximum number of colors in a strange $n\times n\times n$ cube. Clearly the original problem has at most $D(n)$ colors.

We claim that
\[
D(n)\le\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6.
\tag{1}
\]
For $n=1$, this is immediate.

Take a strange $n\times n\times n$ cube with $n\ge2$. If all cubes are invisible, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose a nonempty color set $\mathcal S$ occurring in three slices
\[
X,\qquad Y,\qquad Z
\]
of the three different orientations.

Make invisible every cube whose color belongs to $\mathcal S$. The three slices $X,Y,Z$ then become completely invisible. Remove them. The remaining cubes form an $(n-1)\times(n-1)\times(n-1)$ cube.

For every remaining slice, its new color set is its former color set with the colors of $\mathcal S$ removed. Any resulting nonempty set still has matching slices in the other two orientations; those matching slices cannot be among $X,Y,Z$, because those three now have empty color sets. Hence the remaining cube is strange.

By induction, the remaining cube has at most
\[
\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}6
\]
colors. Since $\mathcal S$ occurs in a slice containing only $n^2$ unit cubes,
\[
|\mathcal S|\le n^2.
\]
Therefore the original cube has at most
\[
\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}6+n^2
=
\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6
\]
colors, proving (1).

The construction in Solution 1 contains no invisible cubes and attains this bound. Hence it is also optimal for the original problem.
