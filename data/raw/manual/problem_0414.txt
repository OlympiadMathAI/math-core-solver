ID: 0414

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Similar Triangles, Inversion, Orthocentre, Spiral Similarity

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with circumcircle $\omega$ and circumcentre $O$. Let
\[
D\ne B,\qquad E\ne C
\]
be points on $\omega$ such that
\[
BD\perp AC,
\qquad
CE\perp AB.
\]
Let
\[
X=CO\cap AB,
\qquad
Y=BO\cap AC.
\]

Prove that the circumcircles of triangles $BXD$ and $CYE$ have a common point on line $AO$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\ell$ be the line through $O$ parallel to $AC$. Since
\[
BD\perp AC
\]
and $O$ is equidistant from $B$ and $D$, the line $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BD$. Reflection $\sigma$ in $\ell$ therefore swaps
\[
B\longleftrightarrow D.
\tag{1}
\]

Because $OA=OC$, the line through $O$ parallel to the base $AC$ is the external angle bisector of the two lines $OA$ and $OC$. Hence $\sigma$ also swaps the lines
\[
AO\longleftrightarrow CO=XO.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $\gamma_B=(BXD)$, and let $P\ne X$ be the second intersection of $\gamma_B$ with line $CO$. The centre of $\gamma_B$ lies on the perpendicular bisector $\ell$ of $BD$, so $\gamma_B$ is invariant under $\sigma$. Put
\[
Z=\sigma(P).
\]
By (2),
\[
Z\in AO,
\]
and by invariance,
\[
Z\in\gamma_B.
\tag{3}
\]

We now identify $Z$ independently of the reflection. From (1)-(2) and the cyclicity of $B,D,X,P$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle OZB
&=\angle OPD\\
&=\angle XPD\\
&=\angle XBD\\
&=90^\circ-\angle BAC.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the isosceles triangle $BOC$,
\[
\angle OCB=90^\circ-\angle BAC.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle OZB=\angle OCB,
\]
so
\[
B,O,C,Z
\]
are concyclic. Therefore $Z$ is the second intersection, other than $O$, of line $AO$ with the fixed circle $(BOC)$.

Applying the same argument with
\[
(B,D,X,AC)\longleftrightarrow(C,E,Y,AB)
\]
shows that this same second intersection $Z$ belongs to
\[
(CYE).
\]
Together with (3), $Z$ is the required common point on $AO$.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $B'$ be the reflection of $B$ in line $AC$, and let
\[
Z\ne O
\]
be the second intersection of line $AO$ with the circle $(OBC)$.

We first show that
\[
Z,C,B'
\]
are collinear. Since $B'$ is the reflection of $B$ in $AC$,
\[
\angle B'CA=\angle BCA.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle ACZ=\angle ACB+\angle BCZ.
\]
Because $B,C,O,Z$ are cyclic,
\[
\angle BCZ=\angle BOZ.
\]
The points $A,O,Z$ are collinear, and $\angle AOB=2\angle ACB$, so
\[
\angle BOZ=180^\circ-2\angle ACB.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle B'CA+\angle ACZ=180^\circ,
\]
as claimed.

We prove that
\[
\triangle ZXA\sim\triangle ZDB'.
\tag{1}
\]
Since $X\in AB$, $D\in BB'$, and $Z\in CB'$,
\[
\angle XAZ
=
\angle BAO
=
90^\circ-\angle ACB
=
\angle CBB'
=
\angle DB'Z.
\tag{2}
\]

It remains to compare the adjacent sides. The triangles $ZAB'$ and $CXB$ are similar, because
\[
\angle B'ZA=\angle CZO=\angle CBO=\angle XCB
\]
and
\[
\angle AB'Z=\angle AB'C=\angle CBA=\angle CBX.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{B'Z}{AZ}=\frac{BC}{CX}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $H$ be the orthocentre of triangle $ABC$. The reflection of $H$ in $AC$ is the point $D$, so reflection in $AC$ gives
\[
B'D=BH.
\tag{4}
\]
Applying the sine rule in triangles $ACX$ and $BHC$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AX}{CX}
&=
\frac{\sin\angle ACX}{\sin\angle XAC}\\
&=
\frac{\sin(90^\circ-\angle CBA)}{\sin\angle BAC}\\
&=
\frac{\sin\angle HCB}{\sin\angle BHC}\\
&=
\frac{BH}{BC}
=
\frac{B'D}{BC}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Multiplying (3) and (5) yields
\[
\frac{B'Z}{B'D}=\frac{AZ}{AX}.
\]
Together with (2), this proves (1).

The similarity now gives
\[
\angle ZDB'= \angle ZXA.
\]
Since $B,D,B'$ and $B,X,A$ are collinear,
\[
\angle BDZ
=
180^\circ-\angle ZDB'
=
180^\circ-\angle ZXA
=
\angle BXZ.
\]
Therefore
\[
B,X,D,Z
\]
are concyclic.

The same argument after interchanging $B$ and $C$ shows that
\[
C,Y,E,Z
\]
are concyclic. Thus the two required circumcircles meet at $Z\in AO$.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $\Phi$ be the composition of

\begin{itemize}
\item inversion with centre $A$ and radius $\sqrt{AB\cdot AC}$;
\item reflection in the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$.
\end{itemize}

For a point $U$, write $U^*=\Phi(U)$. The transformation is involutive and swaps
\[
B\longleftrightarrow C.
\]
It sends the circumcircle $\omega$, which passes through $A$, to line $BC$. Hence
\[
D^*\in BC.
\]
It sends line $AB$ to line $AC$, so
\[
X^*\in AC.
\]

Let $H$ be the orthocentre of $ABC$, let $K$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$ to $BC$, and let $L$ be the foot of the altitude from $B$ to $AC$. Denote
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\qquad
\beta=\angle CBA,\qquad
\gamma=\angle ACB,
\]
and let $R$ be the circumradius of $ABC$.

The lines $AH$ and $AO$ are isogonal in $\angle BAC$. Therefore
\[
H^*\in AO.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove that
\[
D^*,H,C,X^*
\]
are concyclic.

Reflection in the angle bisector sends the ray $AD$ to the ray $AD^*$ and the ray $AO$ to the ray $AH=AK$. Hence
\[
\angle D^*AK=\angle OAD.
\]
By the tangent-chord relation for chord $AD$,
\[
\angle OAD
=
90^\circ-\angle DBA
=
\alpha,
\]
because $BD\perp AC$. Since $D^*,K,C$ are collinear,
\[
D^*K=AK\tan\alpha.
\]
Using the standard altitude formulas,
\[
AK=2R\sin\beta\sin\gamma,
\qquad
HK=2R\cos\beta\cos\gamma.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{D^*K}{HK}
=
\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma.
\tag{2}
\]

Similarly, conformality of $\Phi$ gives
\[
\angle AX^*B=\angle ACX=\angle ACO=90^\circ-\beta.
\]
As $X^*,L,C$ are collinear,
\[
X^*L=BL\tan\beta.
\]
Now
\[
BL=2R\sin\alpha\sin\gamma,
\qquad
HL=2R\cos\alpha\cos\gamma,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{X^*L}{HL}
=
\tan\alpha\tan\beta\tan\gamma.
\tag{3}
\]

The triangles $D^*HK$ and $X^*HL$ are right-angled at $K$ and $L$, respectively. Equations (2)-(3) make them similar. Therefore
\[
\angle KD^*H=\angle LX^*H.
\]
Since $C,D^*,K$ and $C,X^*,L$ are collinear,
\[
\angle CD^*H=\angle CX^*H.
\]
Thus
\[
D^*,H,C,X^*
\]
are cyclic.

Apply the inverse transformation $\Phi$. The four points become
\[
D,\ H^*,\ B,\ X,
\]
so
\[
D,H^*,B,X
\]
are cyclic. Therefore
\[
H^*\in(BXD).
\tag{4}
\]

The same argument with $B$ and $C$ interchanged gives
\[
H^*\in(CYE).
\tag{5}
\]
By (1), the point $H^*$ lies on $AO$. Equations (4)-(5) prove the result.

SOLUTION 4:
Let $F$ be the point of $\omega$ diametrically opposite $A$, and define
\[
J=DF\cap CO.
\]
Invert about the circle $\omega$, whose centre is $O$, and write $U'$ for the inverse of $U$.

Since $X\in CO$,
\[
X'\in CO.
\]
Also,
\[
OX\cdot OX'=R^2=OB^2.
\]
Thus triangles $OBX$ and $OX'B$ give
\[
\angle BX'O=\angle OBX.
\]
Using $X\in AB$, $A,O,F$ collinear, and $D,F,J$ collinear,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BX'J
&=\angle BX'O\\
&=\angle OBX\\
&=\angle OBA\\
&=\angle BAO\\
&=\angle BAF\\
&=\angle BDF\\
&=\angle BDJ.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
B,X',D,J\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
K=AF\cap BC.
\]
We show that triangles $BOK$ and $DOJ$ are congruent. First,
\[
OB=OD.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\angle KBO
=
90^\circ-\angle BAC
=
\angle DBA
=
\angle DFA
=
\angle DFO
=
\angle ODJ,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\angle BOK
=
2\angle OBA
=
2\angle CBD
=
\angle COD
=
\angle JOD.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle BOK\cong\triangle DOJ.
\]
In particular,
\[
BK=DJ.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)-(3) also give
\[
\angle KBD
=
\angle KBO+\angle OBD
=
\angle ODJ+\angle BDO
=
\angle BDJ.
\tag{5}
\]
The quadrilateral $BDJK$ is therefore an isosceles trapezoid, and hence it is cyclic. Together with (1),
\[
B,X',D,J,K
\]
lie on one circle.

Invert back. Since $B$ and $D$ lie on $\omega$, they are fixed, while $X'$ maps to $X$ and $K$ maps to $K'$. We obtain
\[
B,X,D,K'\ \text{are cyclic}.
\tag{6}
\]

Repeating the argument with $B$ and $C$ interchanged yields
\[
C,Y,E,K'\ \text{are cyclic}.
\tag{7}
\]
Finally, $K\in AF=AO$, so inversion centred at $O$ also gives
\[
K'\in AO.
\]
Equations (6)-(7) show that $K'$ is the desired common point.
