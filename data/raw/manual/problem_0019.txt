ID: 0019

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incenter, Menelaus Theorem, Similar Triangles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $I$ be the incenter of triangle $ABC$, and let $\Gamma$ be its circumcircle. The line $AI$ meets $\Gamma$ again at $D\ne A$. Let $F$ be a point on $BC$, and let $E$ be a point on the arc $BDC$, such that
\[
\angle BAF=\angle CAE<\frac12\angle BAC.
\]
Let $G$ be the midpoint of $IF$. Prove that the lines $DG$ and $EI$ meet on $\Gamma$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $X\ne E$ be the second intersection of $EI$ with $\Gamma$, and let
\[
L=AI\cap BC.
\]
Let
\[
G'=DX\cap IF,
\qquad
T=DX\cap AF.
\]
It is enough to prove that $G'=G$, or equivalently
\[
IG'=G'F.
\]

Apply Menelaus' theorem to triangle $AIF$ and the transversal $D,G',T$. In directed ratios,
\[
\frac{G'F}{IG'}
=
\frac{TF}{AT}\cdot\frac{AD}{ID}.
\]
Thus it suffices to prove
\[
\frac{TF}{AT}=\frac{ID}{AD}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $K\ne A$ be the second intersection of $AF$ with $\Gamma$. Since
\[
\angle BAK=\angle CAE,
\]
the arcs $\widehat{BK}$ and $\widehat{CE}$ are equal, and therefore
\[
KE\parallel BC.
\tag{2}
\]

Also,
\[
\angle IAT
=\angle DAK
=\angle EAD
=\angle EXD
=\angle IXT.
\]
Hence $I,A,X,T$ are concyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle ITA
=\angle IXA
=\angle EXA
=\angle EKA,
\]
so
\[
IT\parallel KE\parallel BC.
\tag{3}
\]

In triangle $AFL$, relation (3) gives
\[
\frac{TF}{AT}=\frac{IL}{AI}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since $CI$ bisects $\angle ACL$, the angle-bisector theorem in triangle $ACL$ gives
\[
\frac{IL}{AI}=\frac{CL}{AC}.
\tag{5}
\]

Furthermore,
\[
\angle DCL=\angle DCB=\angle DAB=\angle CAD.
\]
Thus triangles $DCL$ and $DAC$ are similar, so
\[
\frac{CL}{AC}=\frac{DC}{AD}.
\tag{6}
\]

The point $D$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$, and the standard incenter relation gives
\[
DB=DC=DI.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{DC}{AD}=\frac{ID}{AD}.
\tag{7}
\]

Combining (4)--(7), we obtain (1). Therefore $IG'=G'F$, so $G'=G$. Since $X\in\Gamma$, the lines $DG$ and $EI$ meet at the point $X$ on $\Gamma$.

SOLUTION 2:
Use the same points $X,T,K$ as in Solution 1. Menelaus reduces the problem to
\[
\frac{TF}{AT}=\frac{DI}{AD}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{AT}{AD}=\frac{AF}{AD+DI}.
\tag{1}
\]

We first compute the left-hand side. Since
\[
\angle FAD=\angle EAI
\]
and
\[
\angle TDA=\angle XDA=\angle XEA=\angle IEA,
\]
the triangles $ATD$ and $AIE$ are similar. Hence
\[
\frac{AT}{AD}=\frac{AI}{AE}.
\tag{2}
\]

Next, use the relation
\[
DB=DC=DI.
\]
Choose a point $J$ on the extension of $AD$ beyond $D$ such that
\[
DJ=DI=DC.
\]
Then
\[
\angle DJC=\angle JCD
=\frac12\left(180^\circ-\angle JDC\right)
=\frac12\angle ADC
=\frac12\angle ABC
=\angle ABI.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle BAI=\angle JAC.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle ABI\sim\triangle AJC,
\]
and
\[
\frac{AB}{AJ}=\frac{AI}{AC}.
\]
Since $AJ=AD+DI$,
\[
AB\cdot AC=(AD+DI)\cdot AI.
\tag{3}
\]

On the other hand,
\[
\angle ABF=\angle ABC=\angle AEC
\]
and
\[
\angle BAF=\angle CAE.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle ABF\sim\triangle AEC,
\]
which yields
\[
\frac{AF}{AC}=\frac{AB}{AE},
\]
or
\[
AB\cdot AC=AF\cdot AE.
\tag{4}
\]

From (3) and (4),
\[
(AD+DI)\cdot AI=AF\cdot AE,
\]
so
\[
\frac{AI}{AE}=\frac{AF}{AD+DI}.
\]
Together with (2), this proves (1), and hence the required concurrency on $\Gamma$.
