ID: 0392

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Decimal Digits, Cyclic Rotations, Modular Arithmetic, Double Counting

STATEMENT:
For each
\[
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,9\}
\]
and each positive integer $T$, let $d_i(T)$ be the total number of occurrences of the digit $i$ when all positive multiples of $1829$ not exceeding $T$ are written in base $10$.

Prove that there are infinitely many positive integers $T$ for which precisely two distinct values occur among
\[
d_1(T),d_2(T),\ldots,d_9(T).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
n=1829.
\]
Since $\gcd(n,10)=1$, there are arbitrarily large positive integers $k$ such that
\[
n\mid10^k-1;
\]
for example, every positive multiple of $\varphi(n)$ has this property.

Fix such a $k$. We prove that either
\[
T=10^k-1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
T=10^k-2
\]
has the required property.

It is enough to establish
\[
\left|
\{d_i(10^k-1):1\le i\le9\}
\right|
\le2.
\tag{1}
\]
If the cardinality in (1) equals $2$, then $T=10^k-1$ works. If it equals $1$, remove the largest multiple
\[
10^k-1,
\]
whose decimal representation consists of $k$ digits equal to $9$. Then
\[
d_i(10^k-2)=d_i(10^k-1)
\qquad(1\le i\le8),
\]
whereas
\[
d_9(10^k-2)=d_9(10^k-1)-k.
\]
Thus exactly two values occur for $T=10^k-2$.

We now prove (1). Write every integer between $1$ and $10^k-1$ using exactly $k$ digits, allowing leading zeroes. If
\[
N=\overline{a_{k-1}a_{k-2}\cdots a_0},
\]
let
\[
N'=\overline{a_{k-2}\cdots a_0a_{k-1}}
\]
be its cyclic left shift. Then
\[
10N-N'=(10^k-1)a_{k-1}.
\]
Since $n\mid10^k-1$,
\[
n\mid N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n\mid N'.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the set of positive multiples of $n$ not exceeding $10^k-1$ is invariant under cyclic permutation of the $k$ digit positions.

Fix a digit
\[
i\in\{1,\ldots,9\}.
\]
By double counting pairs consisting of a multiple and a position occupied by $i$, the cyclic symmetry gives
\[
d_i(10^k-1)
=
k\,u_i,
\tag{3}
\]
where $u_i$ is the number of $k$-digit strings beginning with $i$ that represent a multiple of $n$.

Those strings represent precisely the integers in the interval
\[
[i10^{k-1},(i+1)10^{k-1}-1],
\]
which contains $10^{k-1}$ consecutive integers. The number of multiples of $n$ in any interval of this length is either
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{10^{k-1}}n\right\rfloor
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
1+\left\lfloor\frac{10^{k-1}}n\right\rfloor.
\]
Therefore the nine values $u_i$, and hence by (3) the nine values
\[
d_i(10^k-1),
\]
assume at most two distinct values. This proves (1).

Since suitable values of $k$ can be arbitrarily large, the resulting values of $T$ are infinite in number.
