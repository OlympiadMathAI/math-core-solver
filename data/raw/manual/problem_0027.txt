ID: 0027

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Squares, Prime Divisibility, Injectivity

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb N$ be the set of positive integers. Find all functions
\[
f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N
\]
such that
\[
\bigl(f(m)+n\bigr)\bigl(m+f(n)\bigr)
\]
is a perfect square for all $m,n\in\mathbb N$.

SOLUTION 1:
All solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(n)=n+c},
\]
where $c$ is a nonnegative integer.

Indeed, if $f(n)=n+c$, then
\[
\bigl(f(m)+n\bigr)\bigl(m+f(n)\bigr)
=(m+n+c)^2,
\]
so every such function works.

We prove that there are no others.

\textbf{Lemma.} Let $p$ be a prime. If
\[
p\mid f(k)-f(\ell),
\]
then
\[
p\mid k-\ell.
\]

First suppose
\[
p^2\mid f(k)-f(\ell).
\]
Write
\[
f(\ell)=f(k)+p^2a
\]
for some integer $a$. Choose a positive integer
\[
D>\max\{f(k),f(\ell)\}
\]
with $p\nmid D$, and put
\[
n=pD-f(k).
\]
Then
\[
n+f(k)=pD,
\qquad
n+f(\ell)=p(D+pa),
\]
and both numbers are divisible by $p$ but not by $p^2$.

The two numbers
\[
\bigl(f(k)+n\bigr)\bigl(f(n)+k\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\bigl(f(\ell)+n\bigr)\bigl(f(n)+\ell\bigr)
\]
are squares. Since their first factors have odd $p$-adic valuation, their second factors must be divisible by $p$. Hence
\[
p\mid f(n)+k
\quad\text{and}\quad
p\mid f(n)+\ell,
\]
so
\[
p\mid k-\ell.
\]

Now suppose that $p\mid f(k)-f(\ell)$ but
\[
p^2\nmid f(k)-f(\ell).
\]
Choose $D$ as above and set
\[
n=p^3D-f(k).
\]
Then
\[
n+f(k)=p^3D
\]
has $p$-adic valuation $3$, while
\[
n+f(\ell)=p^3D+f(\ell)-f(k)
\]
has $p$-adic valuation $1$. Applying the square condition again shows that both
\[
f(n)+k
\quad\text{and}\quad
f(n)+\ell
\]
are divisible by $p$. Hence $p\mid k-\ell$, and the lemma is proved.

Now suppose
\[
f(k)=f(\ell).
\]
Every prime divides $f(k)-f(\ell)$, so the lemma implies that every prime divides $k-\ell$. Therefore $k=\ell$, and $f$ is injective.

For consecutive arguments, if a prime $p$ divided
\[
f(k+1)-f(k),
\]
the lemma would give $p\mid1$, which is impossible. Since injectivity also shows that this difference is nonzero, we obtain
\[
|f(k+1)-f(k)|=1
\tag{1}
\]
for every $k$.

Let
\[
q=f(2)-f(1)\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
We prove by induction that
\[
f(n)=f(1)+q(n-1).
\tag{2}
\]
The formula is true for $n=1,2$. If it is true up to $n$, then by (1),
\[
f(n+1)=f(n)\pm1.
\]
The choice opposite to $q$ would give
\[
f(n+1)=f(n-1),
\]
contradicting injectivity. Thus the same sign $q$ continues, proving (2).

The value $q=-1$ is impossible because $f(n)$ would eventually be nonpositive. Hence $q=1$, and
\[
f(n)=n+f(1)-1.
\]
Since $f(1)\ge1$, the constant
\[
c=f(1)-1
\]
is a nonnegative integer. This completes the proof.
