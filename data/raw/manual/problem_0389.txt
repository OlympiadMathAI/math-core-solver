ID: 0389

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Factorials, Prime Divisibility, Extremal Primes, Prime Gaps

STATEMENT:
Find all positive integers $n>2$ such that
\[
n!
\mid
\prod_{\substack{p<q\le n\\ p,q\text{ prime}}}(p+q).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The only solution is
\[
\boxed{n=7}.
\]

Suppose that $n$ satisfies the condition, and list the primes not exceeding $n$ as
\[
2=p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_m.
\]
Since $p_m\mid n!$, the assumed divisibility implies that $p_m$ divides at least one factor
\[
p_i+p_j
\qquad(i<j).
\]
Neither summand can equal $p_m$, because then the other summand would have to be divisible by $p_m$. Hence
\[
0<p_i+p_j<2p_m.
\]
It follows that
\[
p_i+p_j=p_m.
\]
The right-hand side is odd, so one of the two summands is $2$. Therefore
\[
p_m=p_{m-1}+2.
\tag{1}
\]

Apply the same argument to $p_{m-1}$. It divides some sum $p_k+p_\ell$, and
\[
0<
\frac{p_k+p_\ell}{p_{m-1}}
\le
\frac{p_m+p_{m-1}}{p_{m-1}}
=
2+\frac2{p_{m-1}}
<3.
\]
Thus
\[
p_k+p_\ell=p_{m-1}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
p_k+p_\ell=2p_{m-1}.
\]

In the first case, parity gives
\[
p_{m-1}=2+p_{m-2}.
\]
In the second case, one summand must be larger than $p_{m-1}$, and hence it must be
\[
p_m=p_{m-1}+2.
\]
The other summand is then
\[
2p_{m-1}-p_m=p_{m-1}-2=p_{m-2}.
\]
Thus in either case
\[
p_{m-2},\qquad p_{m-2}+2,\qquad p_{m-2}+4
\]
are three consecutive odd primes in the list.

One of three odd integers differing successively by $2$ is divisible by $3$. Since all three are prime, the divisible one must be $3$. Therefore
\[
p_{m-2}=3,\qquad p_{m-1}=5,\qquad p_m=7.
\]
Hence
\[
7\le n<11.
\]

For all $n$ in this range, the product in the statement is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(2+3)(2+5)(2+7)(3+5)(3+7)(5+7)\\
&\qquad=
5\cdot7\cdot9\cdot8\cdot10\cdot12\\
&\qquad=
2^6\cdot3^3\cdot5^2\cdot7.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is divisible by
\[
7!=2^4\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7,
\]
but it is not divisible by $8!$, whose $2$-adic valuation is $7$. Therefore $n=8,9,10$ do not work, while $n=7$ does.

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{n=7}
\]
is the unique solution.
