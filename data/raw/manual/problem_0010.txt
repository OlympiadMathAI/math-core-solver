ID: 0010

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Hall's Marriage Theorem, Induction, Binary Strings

STATEMENT:
On some planet there are $2^N$ countries, where $N\ge 4$. Each country has a flag consisting of $N$ unit fields in a row, each field being yellow or blue, and no two flags are identical.

A set of $N$ flags is called \emph{diverse} if the flags can be arranged as the rows of an $N\times N$ square so that all fields on the main diagonal have the same color. Determine the least positive integer $M$ such that every collection of $M$ distinct flags contains a diverse set of $N$ flags.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{M=2^{N-2}+1}.
\]

For the lower bound, take all $2^{N-2}$ flags whose first field is yellow and whose second field is blue. Any $N\times N$ square formed from these flags has one diagonal entry from the first column and one from the second column, so its diagonal contains both colors. Thus
\[
M>2^{N-2}.
\]

It remains to prove that $2^{N-2}+1$ flags always suffice. We first establish the case $N=4$.

Write each flag of length $4$ as $LR$, where $L$ and $R$ are its two-field left and right parts. Let
\[
\mathcal{S}=\{BB,BY,YB,YY\}.
\]
We use two elementary facts about pairs of elements of $\mathcal{S}$:

1. For each $A\in\mathcal{S}$, there is exactly one $C\in\mathcal{S}$ such that $A$ and $C$ cannot be arranged as two rows of a $2\times2$ square with a monochromatic diagonal.

2. Among any three distinct elements of $\mathcal{S}$, some pair can form a $2\times2$ square with a yellow diagonal, and some pair can form one with a blue diagonal.

Now take five distinct flags of length $4$. Let $\ell$ and $r$ be the numbers of distinct left and right parts. Since
\[
5\le \ell r,
\qquad
\ell,r\le 4,
\]
one of $\ell,r$ is at least $3$; assume $r\ge 3$. Since there are only four possible right parts, two flags share a right part; write them as
\[
L_1R_1,
\qquad
L_2R_1,
\qquad
L_1\ne L_2.
\]
Choose two more flags $L_3R_3,L_4R_4$ so that $R_1,R_3,R_4$ are distinct, and denote the remaining flag by $L'R'$.

By fact 1, at least one of the pairs $(L',L_1)$ and $(L',L_2)$ can form a monochromatic $2\times2$ diagonal. Suppose its color is blue; the yellow case is identical. Relabeling $L_1,L_2$ if necessary, assume $(L',L_2)$ is such a pair. By fact 2, two of $R_1,R_3,R_4$ can also form a blue diagonal. These two $2\times2$ arrangements can be placed along the diagonal of a $4\times4$ square, producing four flags with a monochromatic diagonal. Thus the case $N=4$ holds.

We now proceed by induction on $N$. Let $N>4$, and take
\[
2^{N-2}+1
\]
distinct flags of length $N$. Since the flags are distinct, some column contains both colors. Choose such a column and call it the first column. At least
\[
\left\lceil\frac{2^{N-2}+1}{2}\right\rceil=2^{N-3}+1
\]
flags have the same color in that column; suppose it is blue, and call those flags good.

Delete the first field from every good flag. By the induction hypothesis, among the resulting flags of length $N-1$ there are $N-1$ whose rows can be arranged with a monochromatic diagonal.

Restore the deleted blue fields. If the smaller diagonal is yellow, add any flag having a yellow first field; such a flag exists and was not used among the good flags. If the smaller diagonal is blue, add an unused good flag. Such a flag exists because
\[
2^{N-3}+1-(N-1)>0
\]
for $N>4$. In either case, the resulting $N\times N$ square has a monochromatic diagonal. Hence
\[
M=2^{N-2}+1.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
We again prove that every collection of
\[
2^{N-2}+1
\]
flags contains a diverse set, this time by Hall's marriage theorem.

Let $V$ be the set of the $N$ columns and let $V'$ be the given set of flags. Construct two bipartite graphs $G_Y$ and $G_B$ on $V\cup V'$. Join a column $c$ to a flag $F$ in $G_Y$ when the field of $F$ in column $c$ is yellow, and join them in $G_B$ when that field is blue.

A matching covering all vertices of $V$ in $G_Y$ gives $N$ distinct flags that can be arranged with a yellow diagonal. Likewise, such a matching in $G_B$ gives a blue diagonal. Suppose, for a contradiction, that neither graph has such a matching.

By Hall's theorem, there are sets of columns $S_Y,S_B\subset V$ such that
\[
|N_Y(S_Y)|\le |S_Y|-1,
\qquad
|N_B(S_B)|\le |S_B|-1,
\tag{1}
\]
where $N_Y$ and $N_B$ denote neighborhoods in the two graphs. Both sets are proper subsets of $V$. Indeed, if for example $S_Y=V$, then at most $N-1$ flags contain any yellow field. Hence at least
\[
2^{N-2}+1-(N-1)\ge2
\]
distinct flags would have to be entirely blue, which is impossible because there is only one all-blue flag. The argument for $S_B$ is identical.

First suppose $S_Y\cap S_B\ne\varnothing$. A flag is joined to every fixed column in exactly one of the two color graphs, so any column in the intersection shows that
\[
N_Y(S_Y)\cup N_B(S_B)=V'.
\]
Therefore, by (1),
\[
2^{N-2}+1=|V'|
\le |S_Y|+|S_B|-2
\le 2N-4,
\]
which is impossible for $N\ge 4$.

Thus $S_Y\cap S_B=\varnothing$. Put
\[
y=|S_Y|,
\qquad
b=|S_B|,
\qquad
c=y+b.
\]
Every flag outside
\[
N_Y(S_Y)\cup N_B(S_B)
\]
has blue fields in all columns of $S_Y$ and yellow fields in all columns of $S_B$. Only the remaining $N-c$ fields are free, so there are at most $2^{N-c}$ such flags. On the other hand, (1) shows that there are at least
\[
2^{N-2}+1-(y-1)-(b-1)=2^{N-2}+3-c
\]
of them. Hence
\[
2^{N-c}+c\ge 2^{N-2}+3.
\tag{2}
\]
But for $2\le c\le N$,
\[
2^{N-c}+c\le 2^{N-2}+2.
\]
Indeed, the left-hand side is maximal at $c=2$. This contradicts (2), completing the proof.
