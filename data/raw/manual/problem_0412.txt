ID: 0412

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Arc Midpoints, Parallelograms, Homothety, Distance Ratios

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral satisfying
\[
\angle BAD<\angle ADC.
\]
Let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $CD$ not containing $A$.

Suppose that there is a point $P$ inside $ABCD$ such that
\[
\angle ADB=\angle CPD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle ADP=\angle PCB.
\]

Prove that the lines
\[
AD,\qquad PM,\qquad BC
\]
are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
X=AM\cap PD,
\qquad
Y=BM\cap PC.
\]
Since $A,B,C,D,M$ lie on the same circle and
\[
CM=MD,
\]
we have
\[
\angle XAD
=
\angle MAD
=
\angle CBM
=
\angle CBY.
\tag{1}
\]
The hypothesis also gives
\[
\angle ADX=\angle YCB.
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)-(2) imply
\[
\angle DXA=\angle BYC.
\]
Using the collinearities
\[
A,X,M,
\qquad
B,Y,M,
\qquad
D,X,P,
\qquad
C,Y,P,
\]
this becomes
\[
\angle PXM=\angle MYP.
\tag{3}
\]

Furthermore,
\[
\angle YPX
=
\angle CPD
=
\angle ADB
=
\angle AMB
=
\angle XMY.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus both pairs of opposite angles of quadrilateral $MXPY$ are equal. Hence $MXPY$ is a parallelogram, and therefore
\[
AM\parallel PC,
\qquad
BM\parallel PD.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
R=AM\cap BC,
\qquad
S=BM\cap AD.
\]
By (5),
\[
\angle ASB
=
\angle ADP
=
\angle PCB
=
\angle ARB.
\]
Consequently,
\[
A,B,R,S
\]
are concyclic. Hence
\[
\angle SRB
=
180^\circ-\angle BAS
=
180^\circ-\angle BAD
=
\angle DCB,
\]
where the last equality uses the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$. Since $R,B,C$ are collinear,
\[
SR\parallel CD.
\tag{6}
\]

Now the corresponding sides of triangles $PCD$ and $MRS$ are parallel:
\[
PC\parallel MR,
\qquad
PD\parallel MS,
\qquad
CD\parallel RS.
\]
The two triangles are therefore homothetic. The lines joining corresponding vertices,
\[
PM,\qquad CR=BC,\qquad DS=AD,
\]
pass through the centre of this homothety. Thus $AD$, $PM$, and $BC$ are concurrent.

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
T=AD\cap BC.
\]
For a point $Z$, denote by
\[
d_a(Z)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
d_b(Z)
\]
its perpendicular distances from the lines $TA=AD$ and $TB=BC$, respectively.

The points $P$ and $M$ lie in the same angle formed by these two lines. Within this angle, the ratio
\[
\frac{d_a(Z)}{d_b(Z)}
\]
determines the ray $TZ$. Hence it is enough to prove
\[
\frac{d_a(P)}{d_b(P)}
=
\frac{d_a(M)}{d_b(M)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Introduce the angles
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC=\angle BDC,
\qquad
\beta=\angle DBA=\angle DCA,
\]
\[
\mu=\angle ADB=\angle AMB=\angle ACB=\angle CPD,
\qquad
\nu=\angle ADP=\angle PCB,
\]
and
\[
\chi
=
\angle MAD
=
\angle CAM
=
\angle MBD
=
\angle CBM.
\]
The equalities involving $\chi$ follow from the fact that $M$ is the midpoint of arc $CD$.

Using the two equal angles $\nu$,
\[
\frac{d_a(P)}{d_b(P)}
=
\frac{PD\sin\nu}{PC\sin\nu}
=
\frac{PD}{PC}.
\tag{2}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\frac{d_a(M)}{d_b(M)}
=
\frac{MA\sin\chi}{MB\sin\chi}
=
\frac{MA}{MB}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus it remains to prove
\[
\frac{PD}{PC}=\frac{MA}{MB}.
\tag{4}
\]

We show that
\[
\triangle PDC\sim\triangle MAB.
\]
Both triangles have the same angle
\[
\angle DPC=\angle AMB=\mu.
\]
For the other two angles, an angle chase in the cyclic quadrilateral gives
\[
\angle PDC+\angle DCP=180^\circ-\mu
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\angle PDC-\angle DCP
=
(\alpha+\mu-\nu)-(\beta+\mu-\nu)
=
\alpha-\beta.
\tag{6}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\angle BAM+\angle MBA=180^\circ-\mu
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
\angle BAM-\angle MBA
=
(\alpha+\chi)-(\beta+\chi)
=
\alpha-\beta.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (5)-(8) imply
\[
\angle PDC=\angle BAM,
\qquad
\angle DCP=\angle MBA.
\]
Hence the triangles are similar, which proves (4).

Combining (2)-(4) gives (1), so
\[
T,P,M
\]
are collinear. Since $T$ already lies on $AD$ and $BC$, the required concurrency follows.
