ID: 0177

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Signed Sums, Induction, Pigeonhole Principle, Absolute Values

STATEMENT:
Find all integers $n\ge3$ with the following property: for all real numbers
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n
\quad\text{and}\quad
b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n
\]
satisfying
\[
|a_k|+|b_k|=1
\qquad(1\le k\le n),
\]
there exist $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\in\{-1,1\}$ such that
\[
\left|\sum_{k=1}^n x_ka_k\right|
+
\left|\sum_{k=1}^n x_kb_k\right|
\le1.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{all odd integers }n\ge3}.
\]

Suppose first that $n\ge4$ is even. Take
\[
a_1=\cdots=a_{n-1}=b_n=0,
\qquad
b_1=\cdots=b_{n-1}=a_n=1.
\]
For every choice of the signs $x_k$, both
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n x_ka_k=x_n
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_{k=1}^n x_kb_k=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}x_k
\]
are odd integers. Hence both have absolute value at least $1$, and their absolute values have sum at least $2$. Thus no even $n$ has the required property.

Now let $n$ be odd. By replacing a pair $(a_k,b_k)$ with $(-a_k,-b_k)$ when necessary, we may suppose that
\[
b_k\ge0
\qquad(1\le k\le n).
\]
We may also reorder the pairs. Arrange them so that, for some $m$,
\[
a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_m\ge0>a_{m+1}\ge\cdots\ge a_n.
\]
Choose
\[
x_k=(-1)^{k+1}.
\]
Set
\[
s=\sum_{k=1}^m x_ka_k,
\qquad
t=-\sum_{k=m+1}^n x_ka_k.
\]
The alternating-sum estimates for a decreasing sequence give
\[
0\le s\le a_1\le1,
\qquad
0\le t\le -a_n\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
Because $b_k=1-a_k$ for $k\le m$ and $b_k=1+a_k$ for $k>m$, and because
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n x_k=1
\]
when $n$ is odd, we have
\[
\sum_{k=1}^n x_ka_k=s-t,
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^n x_kb_k=1-s-t.
\]
It remains to note that for every $s,t\in[0,1]$,
\[
|s-t|+|1-s-t|\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, by symmetry assume $s\ge t$. If $s+t\le1$, the left side of (2) equals $1-2t$; if $s+t\ge1$, it equals $2s-1$. Both quantities are at most $1$.

Thus every odd $n\ge3$ has the desired property.

SOLUTION 2:
The same example as in Solution 1 excludes every even $n$. We prove the assertion for odd $n$ by induction.

First let $n=3$. By changing the sign of each pair when necessary and reordering, assume
\[
1\ge a_1\ge a_2\ge a_3\ge0.
\]
For every $k$, we have $b_k=a_k-1$ or $b_k=1-a_k$. Replacing all $b_k$ by $-b_k$ if necessary, we may suppose
\[
b_1=a_1-1.
\]
There are four cases.

\textbf{Case 1.} If $b_2=a_2-1$ and $b_3=a_3-1$, take
\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(1,-1,1).
\]
With $c=a_1-a_2+a_3\in[0,1]$, the two signed sums are $c$ and $c-1$, so their absolute values sum to $1$.

\textbf{Case 2.} If $b_2=1-a_2$ and $b_3=1-a_3$, use the same signs. Again put $c=a_1-a_2+a_3\in[0,1]$. The second signed sum is
\[
a_1+a_2-a_3-1.
\]
The inequalities $a_3\le a_2$ and $a_1\le1$ give
\[
c-1\le a_1+a_2-a_3-1\le1-c.
\]
Hence its absolute value is at most $1-c$.

\textbf{Case 3.} If $b_2=a_2-1$ and $b_3=1-a_3$, take
\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(-1,1,1)
\]
and put $c=-a_1+a_2+a_3$. The second signed sum is
\[
-a_1+a_2-a_3+1.
\]
If $c\ge0$, it lies between $c-1$ and $1-c$; if $c\le0$, it lies between $-c-1$ and $1+c$. In either case, its absolute value is at most $1-|c|$.

\textbf{Case 4.} If $b_2=1-a_2$ and $b_3=a_3-1$, use the same signs as in Case 3. The second signed sum is
\[
-a_1-a_2+a_3+1.
\]
The same two estimates, now using $a_2\le1$ and $a_3\le a_1$, show that its absolute value is at most $1-|c|$.

Thus the result holds for $n=3$.

Now let $n\ge5$ be odd, and assume the result for $n-2$. By changing signs of individual pairs, suppose
\[
0\le a_k\le1
\qquad(1\le k\le n).
\]
Then each $b_k$ equals either $a_k-1$ or $1-a_k$. At least three of the $b_k$ have the same one of these two forms. Among the corresponding three values of $a_k$, two differ by at most $1/2$. After relabelling and, if necessary, replacing every $b_k$ by $-b_k$, assume
\[
b_1=a_1-1,
\qquad
b_2=a_2-1,
\qquad
0\le d=a_1-a_2\le\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]

By the induction hypothesis, choose $x_3,\ldots,x_n$ so that
\[
A=\sum_{k=3}^n x_ka_k,
\qquad
B=\sum_{k=3}^n x_kb_k
\]
satisfy
\[
|A|+|B|\le1.
\]
Reversing all these signs if necessary, suppose $A\ge0$.

If $B\ge0$, choose $(x_1,x_2)=(-1,1)$. The required sum becomes
\[
|A-d|+|B-d|.
\]
According to the signs of $A-d$ and $B-d$, this equals one of
\[
A+B-2d,
\quad A-B,
\quad B-A,
\quad 2d-A-B,
\]
each of which is at most $1$ by $A+B\le1$ and $2d\le1$.

If $0\ge B\ge-A$, again choose $(x_1,x_2)=(-1,1)$. If $A\ge d$, then
\[
|A-d|+|B-d|=A-B=|A|+|B|\le1.
\]
If $A<d$, then
\[
|A-d|+|B-d|=2d-A-B\le2d\le1,
\]
because $A+B\ge0$.

Finally, if $B\le-A$, choose $(x_1,x_2)=(1,-1)$. If $d+B\ge0$, then
\[
|d+A|+|d+B|=2d+A+B\le2d\le1.
\]
If $d+B<0$, then
\[
|d+A|+|d+B|=A-B=|A|+|B|\le1.
\]

The induction is complete, so precisely the odd integers $n\ge3$ work.
