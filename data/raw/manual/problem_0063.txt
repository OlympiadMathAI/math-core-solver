ID: 0063

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Additive Functions, Multiplicative Functions

STATEMENT:
Find all functions $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that
\[
f(1+xy)-f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)
\]
for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, and
\[
f(-1)\ne 0.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The only solution is
\[
f(x)=x-1
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\]

Set
\[
g(x)=f(x)+1.
\]
Then the equation becomes
\[
g(1+xy)-g(x+y)=\bigl(g(x)-1\bigr)\bigl(g(y)-1\bigr)
\tag{1}
\]
for all real $x,y$, and $g(-1)\ne1$.

Let
\[
C=g(-1)-1\ne0.
\]
Setting $y=-1$ in (1) gives
\[
g(1-x)-g(x-1)=C\bigl(g(x)-1\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]
Taking $x=1$ in (2), we get $C(g(1)-1)=0$, hence
\[
g(1)=1.
\]
Then $x=0$ and $x=2$ in (2) give
\[
g(0)=0,
\qquad
g(2)=2.
\]

We next prove
\[
g(x)+g(2-x)=2
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\tag{3}
\]
Replace $x$ by $1-x$ in (2):
\[
g(x)-g(-x)=C\bigl(g(1-x)-1\bigr).
\]
Now replace $x$ by $-x$ in this relation:
\[
g(-x)-g(x)=C\bigl(g(1+x)-1\bigr).
\]
Adding and using $C\ne0$ gives
\[
g(1-x)+g(1+x)=2,
\]
which is equivalent to (3).

We also prove
\[
g(x+2)-g(x)=2
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\tag{4}
\]
Let $u+v=1$. Applying (1) to $(u,v)$ and to $(2-u,2-v)$ gives
\[
g(1+uv)-g(1)=\bigl(g(u)-1\bigr)\bigl(g(v)-1\bigr),
\]
\[
g(3+uv)-g(3)=\bigl(g(2-u)-1\bigr)\bigl(g(2-v)-1\bigr).
\]
By (3), the right-hand sides are equal. Hence
\[
g(uv+3)-g(uv+1)=g(3)-g(1).
\tag{5}
\]
Every real number $x\le 5/4$ can be written as
\[
x=uv+1
\qquad\text{with }u+v=1,
\]
because $uv$ ranges over $(-\infty,1/4]$. Therefore (5) gives
\[
g(x+2)-g(x)=g(3)-g(1)
\qquad\text{for }x\le 5/4.
\]
Taking $x=0$ and using $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=1$, and $g(2)=2$, we obtain $g(3)=3$, so (4) holds for $x\le5/4$.

If $x>5/4$, then $-x<5/4$, so the already proved case gives
\[
g(2-x)-g(-x)=2.
\]
By (3),
\[
g(x)=2-g(2-x),
\qquad
g(x+2)=2-g(-x).
\]
Thus
\[
g(x+2)-g(x)=g(2-x)-g(-x)=2,
\]
and (4) is proved for all real $x$.

Replacing $x$ by $-x$ in (3) gives
\[
g(-x)+g(2+x)=2.
\]
Using (4), $g(2+x)=g(x)+2$, so
\[
g(-x)=-g(x)
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply (1) to $(-x,y)$ and $(x,-y)$. Using (6), we obtain
\[
g(1-xy)-g(-x+y)=\bigl(g(x)+1\bigr)\bigl(1-g(y)\bigr),
\]
\[
g(1-xy)-g(x-y)=\bigl(1-g(x)\bigr)\bigl(g(y)+1\bigr).
\]
Since $g(-x+y)=-g(x-y)$, adding the two equations gives
\[
g(1-xy)=1-g(x)g(y).
\tag{7}
\]
By (3), applied to $1+xy$,
\[
g(1+xy)=1+g(x)g(y).
\tag{8}
\]
Substituting (8) into (1), we find
\[
g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus $g$ is additive. Combining (8) with additivity gives
\[
1+g(xy)=g(1+xy)=1+g(x)g(y),
\]
so
\[
g(xy)=g(x)g(y).
\tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
g(x^2)=g(x)^2\ge0.
\]
Hence $g(t)\ge0$ for every $t\ge0$. By additivity, this makes $g$ nondecreasing: if $y>x$, then
\[
g(y)-g(x)=g(y-x)\ge0.
\]
For every rational number $q$, additivity and $g(1)=1$ give $g(q)=q$. If $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $q<x<r$ are rational, monotonicity gives
\[
q=g(q)\le g(x)\le g(r)=r.
\]
Letting $q$ and $r$ approach $x$, we obtain
\[
g(x)=x
\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(x)=g(x)-1=x-1.
\]
A direct substitution verifies that this function satisfies the equation and that $f(-1)=-2\ne0$.

\textbf{Comment.}
If the condition $f(-1)\ne0$ is removed, there are other solutions, including $f\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x^2-1$.
