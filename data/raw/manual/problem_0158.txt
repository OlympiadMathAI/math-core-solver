ID: 0158

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Graph Coloring, Odd Cycles, Critical Graphs, Extremal Graph Theory

STATEMENT:
In a company, some pairs of people are enemies. A group is called \emph{unsociable} if it has an odd number of members, at least three, and its members can be arranged around a round table so that every two neighboring members are enemies.

Given that the company has at most $2015$ unsociable groups, prove that the people can be partitioned into $11$ parts such that no two enemies lie in the same part.

SOLUTION 1:
Represent the company by a graph $G=(V,E)$: vertices are people, and edges join enemies. An unsociable group is the vertex set of an odd cycle. The desired partition is a proper coloring of $G$ with at most $11$ colors.

We prove the stronger statement
\[
\boxed{\text{every graph of chromatic number }k\ge3\text{ has at least }2^{k-1}-k\text{ unsociable groups}.}
\tag{1}
\]
Since
\[
2^{11}-12=2036>2015,
\]
a graph satisfying the hypothesis cannot have chromatic number at least $12$. It is therefore $11$-colorable.

Fix a proper $k$-coloring
\[
V=V_1\sqcup V_2\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_k.
\tag{2}
\]
Call it \emph{leximinimal} if the tuple
\[
(|V_1|,|V_2|,\ldots,|V_k|)
\]
is lexicographically minimal among all proper $k$-colorings.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $k\ge3$ be odd, and let (2) be a leximinimal coloring of a graph whose chromatic number is $k$. Then the graph contains an odd cycle meeting every color class $V_1,\ldots,V_k$.

\textit{Proof.}
Choose a vertex $v\in V_1$. We mark vertices and orient some edges as follows. First mark every neighbor of $v$ in $V_2$ and orient the corresponding edges away from $v$. Whenever a marked vertex in $V_i$, where $2\le i\le k$, has an unmarked neighbor in $V_{i+1}$, mark that neighbor and orient the edge toward it; here $V_{k+1}=V_2$. Continue until no further marking is possible.

Let $M_i$ be the marked vertices of $V_i$. Move every marked vertex one color class forward cyclically. Thus set
\[
W_2=(V_2\setminus M_2)\cup M_k,
\]
and, for $3\le i\le k$,
\[
W_i=(V_i\setminus M_i)\cup M_{i-1}.
\]
The coloring
\[
V_1\sqcup W_2\sqcup\cdots\sqcup W_k
\]
is proper. Indeed, there are no edges between $M_{i-1}$ and $V_i\setminus M_i$, because any such neighbor would have been marked, and each original color class is independent.

The vertex $v$ must have a neighbor $w\in W_2$. Otherwise we could move $v$ from $V_1$ to $W_2$, producing a proper coloring with a smaller first color class, contrary to leximinality. This neighbor $w$ cannot lie in $V_2\setminus M_2$, since every neighbor of $v$ in $V_2$ was marked at the beginning. Hence $w\in M_k$.

Because $w$ is marked, there is a directed path from $v$ to $w$. Its color classes run cyclically through
\[
V_2,V_3,\ldots,V_k,V_2,\ldots,
\]
so its length is divisible by $k-1$. Since $k$ is odd, this length is even. Adding the edge $wv$ closes the path to an odd cycle, and the path visits every color class. $\square$

We now prove (1). Choose a leximinimal $k$-coloring. Let
\[
C\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
\]
be any odd-cardinality subset with $|C|\ge3$, and let $G_C$ be the subgraph induced by
\[
\bigcup_{c\in C}V_c.
\]
Its chromatic number is exactly $|C|$: a coloring with fewer colors would combine with the remaining classes to color $G$ with fewer than $k$ colors. The induced coloring is also leximinimal, since any lexicographic improvement inside $C$ would improve the original coloring.

The lemma applied to $G_C$ gives an odd cycle whose vertices lie in, and meet, exactly the color classes indexed by $C$. Different sets $C$ yield different unsociable groups. The number of odd subsets of $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ having size at least $3$ is
\[
2^{k-1}-k.
\]
This proves (1), and therefore the graph is $11$-colorable.

SOLUTION 2:
We give another proof of the bound (1). Call a graph \emph{critical} if deleting any vertex lowers its chromatic number. Every graph contains an induced critical subgraph with the same chromatic number.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $G$ be a critical graph of chromatic number $k\ge3$. Every vertex $v$ of $G$ belongs to at least
\[
2^{k-2}-1
\]
unsociable groups.

\textit{Proof.}
Because $G$ is critical, $G-v$ has chromatic number $k-1$. Choose a proper coloring
\[
V_1\sqcup V_2\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_k
\]
with
\[
V_1=\{v\},
\]
and among all such colorings choose one for which
\[
(n(V_2),n(V_3),\ldots,n(V_k))
\]
is lexicographically minimal, where $n(X)$ denotes the number of neighbors of $v$ in $X$. Every $n(V_i)$ is positive; otherwise $v$ could be moved into $V_i$, giving a $(k-1)$-coloring of $G$.

Take any even set
\[
C\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots,k\},
\qquad |C|\ge2.
\]
Write its elements increasingly as
\[
c_1<c_2<\cdots<c_{2\ell},
\]
and put
\[
U_i=V_{c_i},
\qquad
N_i=\{x\in U_i:xv\in E\}.
\]
We shall construct an odd cycle through $v$ whose other vertices use exactly the color classes indexed by $C$.

For every $1\le i<2\ell$ and every $x\in N_i$, the bipartite subgraph induced by $U_i\cup U_{i+1}$ contains a path from $x$ to some vertex of $N_{i+1}$. Suppose not. Let $S$ be the connected component of $x$ in this bipartite subgraph, with
\[
P=S\cap U_i,
\qquad
Q=S\cap U_{i+1}.
\]
The component contains no vertex of $N_{i+1}$. Interchanging the two colors on $S$ replaces $U_i,U_{i+1}$ by
\[
(U_i\cup Q)\setminus P,
\qquad
(U_{i+1}\cup P)\setminus Q.
\]
This remains a proper coloring, but it decreases $n(U_i)$ and does not change any earlier entry in the lexicographic sequence, a contradiction.

Start with any $v_1\in N_1$. Use the preceding property to connect $v_1$ through $U_1\cup U_2$ to some $v_2\in N_2$, then through $U_2\cup U_3$ to some $v_3\in N_3$, and so on, ending at $v_{2\ell}\in N_{2\ell}$. This gives a walk from $v_1$ to $v_{2\ell}$ in which every edge goes between neighboring classes $U_i,U_{i+1}$. Take a shortest subpath with the same endpoints. It is simple, still visits every class $U_1,\ldots,U_{2\ell}$, and its length is odd: every edge changes the class index by $1$, while the endpoints have indices differing by $2\ell-1$.

Adding the two edges $vv_1$ and $v_{2\ell}v$ produces an odd cycle. Its vertex colors are exactly $\{1\}\cup C$. Distinct choices of $C$ give distinct unsociable groups containing $v$. There are
\[
2^{k-2}-1
\]
nonempty even subsets of $\{2,\ldots,k\}$, proving the lemma. $\square$

We now prove (1) by induction on $k\ge3$. For $k=3$, take a critical $3$-chromatic induced subgraph and apply the lemma; it contains at least one unsociable group, which equals
\[
2^{2}-3.
\]

For the induction step, let $G_0$ be a critical $k$-chromatic induced subgraph of $G$, and choose $v\in V(G_0)$. By the lemma, $G_0$ contains at least
\[
2^{k-2}-1
\]
unsociable groups containing $v$. Since $G_0$ is critical, $G_0-v$ has chromatic number $k-1$. By the induction hypothesis it contains at least
\[
2^{k-2}-(k-1)
\]
unsociable groups, none of which contains $v$. Altogether $G_0$, and hence $G$, contains at least
\[
(2^{k-2}-1)+(2^{k-2}-(k-1))
=2^{k-1}-k
\]
unsociable groups.

Thus the bound (1) holds, and the original graph has chromatic number at most $11$.
