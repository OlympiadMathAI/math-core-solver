ID: 0315

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Reflections, Perpendicular Bisectors, Isosceles Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an isosceles triangle with
\[
BC=CA,
\]
and let $D$ be a point inside side $AB$ such that
\[
AD<DB.
\]
Let $P$ and $Q$ be points inside sides $BC$ and $CA$, respectively, such that
\[
\angle DPB=\angle DQA=90^\circ.
\]
The perpendicular bisector of $PQ$ meets segment $CQ$ at $E$, and the circumcircles of triangles $ABC$ and $CPQ$ meet again at $F\ne C$.

Suppose that $P,E,F$ are collinear. Prove that
\[
\angle ACB=90^\circ.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\ell$ be the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$, and let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $CFPQ$.

Since
\[
DP\perp BC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
DQ\perp AC,
\]
we have
\[
\angle CPD=\angle CQD=90^\circ.
\]
Thus $C,P,D,Q$ lie on the circle with diameter $CD$. Since $C,P,Q,F$ are also concyclic,
\[
C,P,Q,D,F\in\omega,
\]
and $CD$ is a diameter of $\omega$.

Reflection in $\ell$ interchanges $P$ and $Q$. Since $E\in\ell$, the lines $EP$ and $EQ$ are symmetric about $\ell$. The circle $\omega$ is also symmetric about $\ell$, because $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of its chord $PQ$. As
\[
E,P,F\ \text{are collinear}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
E,Q,C\ \text{are collinear},
\]
the reflection in $\ell$ interchanges $F$ and $C$. Hence $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $CF$, and therefore it passes through the circumcentre $O$ of triangle $ABC$.

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Since $BC=CA$,
\[
CM\perp AB.
\]
Because $D,M\in AB$, this gives
\[
CM\perp DM,
\]
so $M\in\omega$.

Furthermore,
\[
\angle PCM=\angle BCM=\angle ACM=\angle QCM.
\]
These equal inscribed angles in $\omega$ subtend equal chords, and hence
\[
MP=MQ.
\]
Thus $M\in\ell$.

The circumcentre $O$ of the isosceles triangle $ABC$ lies on $CM$. The lines $\ell$ and $CM$ are distinct: otherwise $E=C$, and the collinearity of $P,E,F$ would put the three distinct points $C,P,F$ of $\omega$ on one line, which is impossible.

We have now shown that both $O$ and $M$ lie on each of the two distinct lines $\ell$ and $CM$, so
\[
O=M.
\]
Therefore the midpoint of $AB$ is the circumcentre of $ABC$, which means that $AB$ is a diameter of the circumcircle. Hence
\[
\angle ACB=90^\circ.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\ell$ be the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. As in Solution 1,
\[
C,P,Q,D,F,M
\]
lie on one circle $\omega$ with diameter $CD$, and
\[
M\in\ell.
\]

Let
\[
G=BF\cap CM
\]
and put
\[
\alpha=\angle ABF.
\]
Since $A,B,C,F$ are concyclic and $C,P,Q,F$ are concyclic, while reflection in $\ell$ interchanges the chords $CQ$ and $FP$, we have
\[
\angle CQP
=\angle FPQ
=\angle FCQ
=\angle FCA
=\angle FBA
=\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]

The points $P,E,F$ are collinear, and $E$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$. From (1), triangle $EPQ$ is isosceles and
\[
\angle FEQ=2\alpha,
\qquad
\angle QEM=90^\circ-\alpha.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle FEM
=\angle FEQ+\angle QEM
=90^\circ+\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]

Since $BF$ makes an angle $\alpha$ with $AB$, while $CM\perp AB$,
\[
\angle FGM=90^\circ+\alpha.
\]
Together with (2), this shows that
\[
F,E,G,M
\]
are concyclic. Therefore,
\[
\angle EGC
=\angle EFM
=\angle PFM
=\angle PCM,
\]
where the last equality follows from the cyclic quadrilateral $FPCM$. Hence
\[
GE\parallel PC,
\]
and thus
\[
GE\parallel BC.
\tag{3}
\]

Now
\[
\angle FAC=\angle CBF=\angle EGF,
\]
using the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCF$ and (3). Since $A,E,C$ are collinear, it follows that
\[
F,E,G,A
\]
are concyclic. The circles through $F,E,G,M$ and through $F,E,G,A$ coincide, so
\[
A,F,G,M
\]
are concyclic. Hence
\[
\angle ACB
=\angle AFB
=\angle AFG
=180^\circ-\angle AMG.
\]
But
\[
AM\perp MG,
\]
because $AM\subset AB$ and $MG\subset CM$. Thus $\angle AMG=90^\circ$, and therefore
\[
\angle ACB=90^\circ.
\]
