ID: 0213

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Integer Recurrences, Induction, Positivity, Growth Estimates

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots
\]
be a sequence of integers and
\[
b_0,b_1,b_2,\ldots
\]
a sequence of positive integers such that
\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_1=1,
\]
and
\[
a_{n+1}=
\begin{cases}
a_nb_n+a_{n-1},&b_{n-1}=1,\\
a_nb_n-a_{n-1},&b_{n-1}>1
\end{cases}
\qquad(n=1,2,\ldots).
\]
Prove that at least one of the two numbers $a_{2017}$ and $a_{2018}$ is at least $2017$.

SOLUTION 1:
The value of $b_0$ is irrelevant because $a_0=0$, so we may assume
\[
b_0=1.
\]

We first prove a positivity lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
\[
a_n\ge1
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Suppose otherwise, and let $n\ge2$ be the smallest index with
\[
a_n\le0.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
a_{n-1}\ge1,
\qquad
a_{n-2}\ge0.
\]
The recurrence for $a_n$ cannot use the plus sign, so
\[
a_n=a_{n-1}b_{n-1}-a_{n-2}.
\]
Since $a_n\le0$,
\[
a_{n-2}\ge a_{n-1}.
\]
Thus there is an index $r\le n-2$ such that
\[
a_r\ge a_{r+1}\ge a_{r+2}.
\]
Choose the smallest such $r$. We have $r\ge2$: if $b_1=1$, then
\[
a_2=1,\qquad a_3=a_2b_2+a_1>a_2,
\]
while if $b_1>1$, then
\[
a_2=b_1>a_1.
\]

By the minimality of $r$,
\[
a_{r-1}<a_r.
\tag{2}
\]
Also, all of
\[
a_{r-1},a_r,a_{r+1}
\]
are positive by the minimality of $n$.

For $a_{r+2}\le a_{r+1}$ to be possible, the recurrence for $a_{r+2}$ must use the minus sign. Hence
\[
b_r>1.
\]
The recurrence for $a_{r+1}$ then gives, regardless of its sign choice,
\[
a_{r+1}=a_rb_r\pm a_{r-1}
\ge2a_r-a_{r-1}
>a_r
\]
by (2). This contradicts $a_r\ge a_{r+1}$. $\square$

We now prove by induction that
\[
\max\{a_n,a_{n+1}\}\ge n
\qquad(n\ge0).
\tag{3}
\]
The cases $n=0,1$ are immediate. Let $n\ge2$, and assume (3) for all smaller indices.

\textbf{Case 1: $b_{n-1}=1$.}

Then
\[
a_{n+1}=a_nb_n+a_{n-1}\ge a_n+a_{n-1}.
\]
By the induction hypothesis,
\[
\max\{a_{n-1},a_n\}\ge n-1,
\]
while the other term is at least $1$ by the lemma. Therefore
\[
a_{n+1}\ge n.
\]

\textbf{Case 2: $b_{n-1}>1$.}

Because $b_0=1$, there is an index $r$ with
\[
1\le r\le n-1
\]
such that
\[
b_{r-1}=1,
\qquad
b_r,b_{r+1},\ldots,b_{n-1}\ge2.
\]
At the first step of this block,
\[
a_{r+1}=a_rb_r+a_{r-1}\ge2a_r+a_{r-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
a_{r+1}-a_r\ge a_r+a_{r-1}.
\]
By the induction hypothesis and the positivity lemma,
\[
a_r+a_{r-1}\ge r.
\]
In particular,
\[
a_{r+1}\ge r+1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_{r+1}>a_r.
\tag{4}
\]

For every $m=r+1,\ldots,n-1$, the recurrence uses the minus sign and $b_m\ge2$. Thus
\[
a_{m+1}
=a_mb_m-a_{m-1}
\ge2a_m-a_{m-1}.
\]
If $a_m>a_{m-1}$, then
\[
a_{m+1}>a_m.
\]
Starting from (4), we obtain
\[
a_n>a_{n-1}>\cdots>a_{r+1}\ge r+1.
\]
All terms are integers, so
\[
a_n\ge n.
\]

This completes the induction and proves (3). Taking $n=2017$ gives
\[
\max\{a_{2017},a_{2018}\}\ge2017,
\]
as required.

SOLUTION 2:
Again the value of $b_0$ does not affect the sequence, so this time assume
\[
b_0>1.
\]

Call an index $n>1$ \emph{bad} if
\[
b_{n-1}=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b_{n-2}>1;
\]
call it \emph{good} otherwise.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
For every $n\ge1$,
\[
a_n\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, if $n>1$ is good, then
\[
a_n>a_{n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
We use induction on $n$. We have
\[
a_1=1,\qquad a_2=b_1\ge1.
\]
If $2$ is good, then $b_1>1$ because $b_0>1$, so $a_2>a_1$.

Assume the result through index $k\ge2$, and consider $a_{k+1}$.

If $k$ is bad, then $b_{k-1}=1$, so
\[
a_{k+1}=b_ka_k+a_{k-1}>a_k\ge1.
\]

Suppose $k$ is good. Then
\[
a_k>a_{k-1}\ge1.
\]
There are three possibilities.

If $b_k>1$, then
\[
a_{k+1}\ge2a_k-a_{k-1}>a_k.
\]
If
\[
b_k=b_{k-1}=1,
\]
then
\[
a_{k+1}=a_k+a_{k-1}>a_k.
\]
Finally, if
\[
b_k=1,\qquad b_{k-1}>1,
\]
then $k+1$ is bad, so only positivity is required; and
\[
a_{k+1}=a_k-a_{k-1}\ge1
\]
because the two terms are integers and $a_k>a_{k-1}$.

This proves both assertions. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
If $n>1$ is bad, then there exists
\[
j\in\{1,2,3\}
\]
such that
\[
a_{n+j}\ge a_{n-1}+j+1
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
a_{n+i}\ge a_{n-1}+i
\qquad(1\le i<j).
\tag{4}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Since $n$ is bad,
\[
b_{n-1}=1.
\]
Let
\[
m=\inf\{i>0:b_{n+i-1}>1\},
\]
where $m=+\infty$ if the set is empty. We show that
\[
j=\min\{m,3\}
\]
works.

If $m=1$, then $b_n>1$, and
\[
a_{n+1}=b_na_n+a_{n-1}\ge a_{n-1}+2.
\]

If $m=2$, then $b_n=1$ and $b_{n+1}>1$. Hence
\[
a_{n+1}=a_n+a_{n-1}\ge a_{n-1}+1,
\]
and
\[
a_{n+2}
=b_{n+1}a_{n+1}+a_n
\ge2(a_{n-1}+1)+a_n
\ge a_{n-1}+4.
\]

If $m>2$, then $b_n=b_{n+1}=1$. Therefore
\[
a_{n+1}=a_n+a_{n-1}\ge a_{n-1}+1,
\]
\[
a_{n+2}=a_{n+1}+a_n\ge a_{n-1}+2,
\]
and
\[
a_{n+3}
\ge a_{n+2}+a_{n+1}
\ge(a_{n-1}+2)+(a_{n-1}+1)
\ge a_{n-1}+4.
\]
Thus (3)--(4) hold in every case. $\square$

We now prove
\[
\max\{a_n,a_{n+1}\}\ge n
\qquad(n\ge0).
\tag{5}
\]
Start with
\[
a_1=1.
\]
More generally, suppose that for some $n>1$ we have
\[
a_{n-1}\ge n-1.
\tag{6}
\]

If $n$ is good, Lemma 1 gives
\[
a_n>a_{n-1},
\]
so, since the terms are integers,
\[
a_n\ge n.
\]
We may continue with the next index.

If $n$ is bad, choose $j$ from Lemma 2. For every $0\le i<j$, equations (3)--(4) give
\[
a_{n+i+1}\ge n+i.
\]
Hence (5) holds for the successive indices
\[
n,n+1,\ldots,n+j-1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
a_{n+j}\ge a_{n-1}+j+1\ge n+j,
\]
so the same argument can be restarted after this block.

Proceeding indefinitely proves (5) for every $n$. In particular,
\[
\max\{a_{2017},a_{2018}\}\ge2017.
\]
