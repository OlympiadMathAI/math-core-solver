ID: 0285

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Directed Angles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Pappus' Theorem, Radical Axis, Isosceles Trapezoids

STATEMENT:
In triangle $ABC$, let $A_1$ and $B_1$ lie on $BC$ and $AC$, respectively. Let $P\in AA_1$ and $Q\in BB_1$ satisfy
\[
PQ\parallel AB.
\]
On the ray $PB_1$ beyond $B_1$, choose $P_1$ so that
\[
\angle PP_1C=\angle BAC.
\]
On the ray $QA_1$ beyond $A_1$, choose $Q_1$ so that
\[
\angle CQ_1Q=\angle CBA.
\]
Prove that $P,Q,P_1,Q_1$ are concyclic.

SOLUTION 1:
All angles are directed modulo $\pi$.

Let the rays $AA_1$ and $BB_1$ meet the circumcircle of $ABC$ again at $A_2$ and $B_2$, respectively. Since $PQ\parallel AB$,
\[
\angle QPA_2
=\angle BAA_2
=\angle BB_2A_2
=\angle QB_2A_2.
\]
Thus $P,Q,A_2,B_2$ lie on one circle; call it $\omega$.

Furthermore,
\[
\angle CA_2A_1
=\angle CA_2A
=\angle CBA
=\angle CQ_1Q
=\angle CQ_1A_1.
\]
Hence $C,Q_1,A_2,A_1$ are concyclic. It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle QQ_1A_2
&=\angle A_1Q_1A_2\\
&=\angle A_1CA_2\\
&=\angle BCA_2\\
&=\angle BAA_2\\
&=\angle QPA_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore $Q_1\in\omega$. By the same argument with $A$ and $B$ interchanged, $P_1\in\omega$. Hence
\[
P,Q,P_1,Q_1
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 2:
First suppose that the lines $PP_1$ and $QQ_1$ meet at a finite point $R$.

Let $PQ$ meet $AC$ and $BC$ at $E$ and $F$, respectively. Since
\[
\angle PP_1C=\angle BAC=\angle PEC,
\]
the points $C,E,P,P_1$ are concyclic; denote their circle by $\omega_P$. Similarly, $C,F,Q,Q_1$ lie on a circle $\omega_Q$.

Let
\[
T=AQ\cap BP.
\]
Apply Pappus' theorem to the two collinear triples
\[
A,A_1,P
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B,B_1,Q.
\]
The three intersections of corresponding cross-lines are
\[
C=AB_1\cap BA_1,
\quad
R=A_1Q\cap B_1P,
\quad
T=AQ\cap BP,
\]
so $C,R,T$ are collinear.

Let this line meet $PQ$ and $AB$ at $S$ and $U$, respectively. Since $PQ\parallel AB$, elementary projective ratios give
\[
\frac{SP}{SQ}=\frac{UB}{UA}=\frac{SF}{SE}.
\]
Therefore
\[
SP\cdot SE=SQ\cdot SF.
\tag{1}
\]
The left side of (1) is the power of $S$ with respect to $\omega_P$, and the right side is its power with respect to $\omega_Q$. Hence $S$ lies on the radical axis of these two circles. Their common point $C$ also lies on this radical axis, so the radical axis is the line $CR$. Thus $R$ has equal powers to the two circles:
\[
RP\cdot RP_1=RQ\cdot RQ_1.
\]
This is exactly the criterion that $P,P_1,Q,Q_1$ be concyclic.

It remains to treat the case $PP_1\parallel QQ_1$. Pappus' theorem in its limiting form gives
\[
CT\parallel PP_1\parallel QQ_1.
\]
Define $S,U,E,F$ as above. Equation (1) is unchanged, so $CT$ is the radical axis of $\omega_P$ and $\omega_Q$. Let $\ell$ be the line joining the centres of these circles. Then
\[
CT\perp\ell,
\]
and therefore both chords $PP_1$ and $QQ_1$ are perpendicular to $\ell$. Since $\ell$ passes through both circle centres, it is the perpendicular bisector of both chords. Reflection in $\ell$ interchanges $P$ with $P_1$ and $Q$ with $Q_1$. Thus $PP_1Q_1Q$ is an isosceles trapezoid, and hence it is cyclic.
