ID: 0224

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangent-Chord Theorem, Similar Triangles, Reflections, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $R$ and $S$ be distinct points on a circle $\Omega$, and let $t$ be the tangent to $\Omega$ at $R$. Let $R'$ be the reflection of $R$ in $S$. Choose a point $I$ on the smaller arc $RS$ of $\Omega$ so that the circumcircle $\Gamma$ of $\triangle ISR'$ meets $t$ at two distinct points. Let $A$ be the common point of $\Gamma$ and $t$ that is closer to $R$. The line $AI$ meets $\Omega$ again at $J$. Prove that $JR'$ is tangent to $\Gamma$.

SOLUTION 1:
All angles are understood as directed angles modulo $180^\circ$.

From the cyclic quadrilaterals $RSIJ$ and $ASIR'$, and from the collinearity of $A,I,J$, we have
\[
\angle JRS=\angle JIS=\angle AR'S.
\tag{1}
\]
Since $RA$ is tangent to $\Omega$ at $R$, the tangent-chord theorem gives
\[
\angle SJR=\angle SRA.
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) imply
\[
\triangle ARR'\sim\triangle SJR.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{R'R}{RJ}
=
\frac{AR'}{SR}
=
\frac{AR'}{SR'},
\tag{3}
\]
because $S$ is the midpoint of $RR'$.

Also, by (1),
\[
\angle AR'S=\angle JRR'.
\]
Together with (3), this yields
\[
\triangle ASR'\sim\triangle R'JR.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle SAR'=\angle RR'J.
\tag{4}
\]
The angle on the left of (4) is an angle subtended by the chord $SR'$ of $\Gamma$, while the angle on the right is the angle between $JR'$ and $R'S$. Thus the tangent-chord theorem shows that $JR'$ is tangent to $\Gamma$ at $R'$.

SOLUTION 2:
As in Solution 1,
\[
\angle JRS=\angle JIS=\angle AR'S.
\]
Since $R,S,R'$ are collinear, this gives
\[
RJ\parallel AR'.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $A'$ be the reflection of $A$ in $S$. Since $S$ is the midpoint of both $AA'$ and $RR'$, the quadrilateral
\[
ARA'R'
\]
is a parallelogram. Thus
\[
RA'\parallel AR'.
\]
By (1), the point $J$ lies on the line $RA'$.

Furthermore,
\[
\angle SR'A'
=
\angle SRA
=
\angle SJR.
\]
Consequently,
\[
S,J,A',R'
\]
are concyclic. Hence
\[
\angle SR'J
=
\angle SA'J
=
\angle SA'R
=
\angle SAR'.
\]
The last equality uses the central symmetry about $S$ that sends $A$ to $A'$ and $R$ to $R'$. Since $A,S,I,R'$ lie on $\Gamma$, the final angle equality is precisely the tangent-chord condition at $R'$. Therefore $JR'$ is tangent to $\Gamma$.
