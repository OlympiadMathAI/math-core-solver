ID: 0061

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Weighted AM-GM, Product Constraints

STATEMENT:
Let $a_2,\ldots,a_n$ be $n-1$ positive real numbers, where $n\ge 3$, such that
\[
a_2a_3\cdots a_n=1.
\]
Prove that
\[
(1+a_2)^2(1+a_3)^3\cdots(1+a_n)^n>n^n.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Choose positive real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}$ such that
\[
a_2=\frac{x_2}{x_1},\qquad
a_3=\frac{x_3}{x_2},\qquad
\ldots,\qquad
a_n=\frac{x_1}{x_{n-1}}.
\]
Such a choice is possible because $a_2a_3\cdots a_n=1$. The desired inequality becomes
\[
(x_1+x_2)^2(x_2+x_3)^3\cdots(x_{n-1}+x_1)^n
>n^n x_1^2x_2^3\cdots x_{n-1}^n.
\tag{1}
\]

Apply AM-GM to each factor:
\[
(x_1+x_2)^2\ge 2^2x_1x_2,
\]
\[
(x_2+x_3)^3
=\left(2\cdot\frac{x_2}{2}+x_3\right)^3
\ge 3^3\left(\frac{x_2}{2}\right)^2x_3,
\]
\[
(x_3+x_4)^4
=\left(3\cdot\frac{x_3}{3}+x_4\right)^4
\ge 4^4\left(\frac{x_3}{3}\right)^3x_4,
\]
and similarly, ending with
\[
(x_{n-1}+x_1)^n
=\left((n-1)\frac{x_{n-1}}{n-1}+x_1\right)^n
\ge n^n\left(\frac{x_{n-1}}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}x_1.
\]
Multiplying these inequalities gives (1) with $\ge$ in place of $>$. Indeed, all intermediate constants telescope, leaving $n^n$, and the powers of the variables become
\[
x_1^2x_2^3\cdots x_{n-1}^n.
\]

It remains to rule out equality. Equality in all the AM-GM inequalities would require
\[
x_1=x_2,
\qquad
x_2=2x_3,
\qquad
x_3=3x_4,
\qquad
\ldots,
\qquad
x_{n-1}=(n-1)x_1.
\]
Combining these relations yields
\[
x_1=(n-1)!\,x_1,
\]
which is impossible because $x_1>0$ and $n\ge 3$. Therefore the inequality is strict.

SOLUTION 2:
For each $k=2,3,\ldots,n$, apply AM-GM to the $k$ positive numbers consisting of $k-1$ copies of $1/(k-1)$ and one copy of $a_k$:
\[
1+a_k=(k-1)\frac{1}{k-1}+a_k.
\]
This gives
\[
(1+a_k)^k
\ge
k^k\left(\frac{1}{k-1}\right)^{k-1}a_k
=
\frac{k^k}{(k-1)^{k-1}}a_k.
\]
Multiplying for $k=2,3,\ldots,n$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1+a_2)^2(1+a_3)^3\cdots(1+a_n)^n
&\ge
\left(\prod_{k=2}^n\frac{k^k}{(k-1)^{k-1}}\right)
\left(\prod_{k=2}^n a_k\right)\\
&=n^n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equality would require
\[
a_k=\frac{1}{k-1}
\qquad\text{for every }k=2,\ldots,n.
\]
But then
\[
a_2a_3\cdots a_n=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\ne 1
\]
for $n\ge 3$. Hence equality is impossible, and the desired inequality is strict.
