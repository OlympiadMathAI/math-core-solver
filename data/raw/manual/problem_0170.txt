ID: 0170

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: GCD and LCM, Recurrence Relations, Invariants, Periodicity

STATEMENT:
Suppose that $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ and $b_0,b_1,\ldots$ are sequences of positive integers satisfying
\[
a_0,b_0\ge2
\]
and
\[
a_{n+1}=\gcd(a_n,b_n)+1,
\qquad
b_{n+1}=\operatorname{lcm}(a_n,b_n)-1
\]
for every $n\ge0$. Prove that the sequence $(a_n)$ is eventually periodic.

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
s_n=a_n+b_n.
\]
If $a_n\mid b_n$, then
\[
a_{n+1}=a_n+1,
\qquad
b_{n+1}=b_n-1,
\qquad
s_{n+1}=s_n.
\tag{1}
\]

For each $n$, define
\[
W_n=\{m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:m\ge a_n\text{ and }m\nmid s_n\}
\]
and
\[
w_n=\min W_n.
\]
The set $W_n$ is nonempty, since every integer greater than $s_n$ belongs to it.

We first show that $(w_n)$ is non-increasing.

If $a_n\mid b_n$, then $a_n\mid s_n$, so $a_n\notin W_n$. By (1), the sum $s_n$ stays unchanged and the lower bound in the definition of $W_n$ increases from $a_n$ to $a_n+1$. Hence
\[
W_{n+1}=W_n,
\qquad
w_{n+1}=w_n.
\]

If $a_n\nmid b_n$, then $a_n\nmid s_n$, so
\[
w_n=a_n.
\]
Also,
\[
a_{n+1}=\gcd(a_n,b_n)+1\le a_n.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
s_{n+1}
=\gcd(a_n,b_n)+\operatorname{lcm}(a_n,b_n).
\]
The second summand is divisible by $a_n$, whereas the first is a positive proper divisor of $a_n$. Thus
\[
a_n\nmid s_{n+1}.
\]
Therefore $a_n\in W_{n+1}$ and
\[
w_{n+1}\le a_n=w_n.
\]

Hence $(w_n)$ is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers. It is eventually constant; choose $N$ and $w$ such that
\[
w_n=w
\qquad(n\ge N).
\]

For $n\ge N$, define
\[
g_n=\gcd(w,s_n).
\]
Starting from any such $n$, the values of $a$ increase by $1$ while the sum $s$ remains fixed, until $a$ reaches $w$, the first integer at least $a_n$ which does not divide $s_n$. At that point,
\[
a_{n+1}=\gcd(w,s_n)+1=g_n+1.
\tag{2}
\]

We now show that $g_n$ is constant for $n\ge N$. During a step with $a_n\mid b_n$, the sum $s_n$ does not change, so neither does $g_n$. At a step with $a_n=w$, we have
\[
\gcd(a_n,b_n)=\gcd(w,s_n)=g_n.
\]
Since $b_n=s_n-w$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_{n+1}
&=g_n+\frac{wb_n}{g_n}\\
&=g_n+\frac{w(s_n-w)}{g_n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second term is divisible by $w$, and $g_n\mid w$. Therefore
\[
g_{n+1}
=\gcd(w,s_{n+1})
=\gcd(w,g_n)
=g_n.
\]
Thus $g_n$ is eventually a fixed integer $g$.

By (2), from that point onward the sequence $(a_n)$ repeatedly follows the cycle
\[
g+1,g+2,\ldots,w,g+1,g+2,\ldots.
\]
Hence $(a_n)$ is eventually periodic.

SOLUTION 2:
The first part of Solution 1 shows that
\[
a_n\le w_n\le w_0
\]
for every $n$. Thus $(a_n)$ takes only finitely many values.

Let
\[
M=\operatorname{lcm}\{a_n:n\ge0\},
\]
which is well-defined because the set of possible values of $a_n$ is finite. Let $r_n$ be the residue of $b_n$ modulo $M$, chosen in $\{0,1,\ldots,M-1\}$.

Since $a_n\mid M$,
\[
\gcd(a_n,b_n)=\gcd(a_n,r_n),
\]
so
\[
a_{n+1}=\gcd(a_n,r_n)+1.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{n+1}
&=\operatorname{lcm}(a_n,b_n)-1\\
&=\frac{a_n}{\gcd(a_n,b_n)}\,b_n-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Reducing modulo $M$ and using $\gcd(a_n,b_n)=\gcd(a_n,r_n)$ gives
\[
r_{n+1}\equiv
\frac{a_n}{\gcd(a_n,r_n)}\,r_n-1
\pmod M.
\tag{2}
\]

Equations (1) and (2) show that the pair $(a_n,r_n)$ uniquely determines $(a_{n+1},r_{n+1})$. There are only finitely many possible pairs, so the sequence of pairs is eventually periodic. Consequently, its first component $(a_n)$ is eventually periodic.
