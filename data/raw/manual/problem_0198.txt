ID: 0198

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Orthocentres, Rotations, Convexity, Perimeter Comparison

STATEMENT:
Let $A_1,B_1,C_1$ lie on the sides $BC,CA,AB$, respectively, of an acute triangle $ABC$, and suppose that
\[
AA_1,\qquad BB_1,\qquad CC_1
\]
are the internal angle bisectors of $\triangle ABC$. Let $I$ be the incentre of $\triangle ABC$, and let $H$ be the orthocentre of $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$. Prove that
\[
AH+BH+CH\ge AI+BI+CI.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Write
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\qquad
\beta=\angle CBA,\qquad
\gamma=\angle ACB,
\]
and assume without loss of generality that
\[
\alpha\le\beta\le\gamma.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
a=BC,\qquad b=CA,\qquad c=AB.
\]

We first prove that $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$ is acute. Choose $D,E\in BC$ so that
\[
B_1D\parallel AB
\]
and $B_1E$ bisects $\angle BB_1C$. Since
\[
\angle B_1DB=180^\circ-\beta
\]
is obtuse,
\[
BB_1>B_1D.
\]
The angle-bisector theorem gives
\[
\frac{BE}{EC}
=\frac{BB_1}{B_1C}
>
\frac{DB_1}{B_1C}
=
\frac{BA}{AC}
=
\frac{BA_1}{A_1C}.
\]
Hence
\[
BE>BA_1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac12\angle BB_1C
=\angle BB_1E
>\angle BB_1A_1.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\frac12\angle BB_1A>\angle BB_1C_1.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle A_1B_1C_1
&=\angle BB_1A_1+\angle BB_1C_1\\
&<\frac12\left(\angle BB_1C+\angle BB_1A\right)\\
&=90^\circ.
\end{aligned}
\]
By symmetry, $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$ is acute.

Let
\[
F=BB_1\cap A_1C_1.
\]
From $\alpha\le\gamma$ we get $a\le c$, and the angle-bisector theorem gives
\[
BA_1=\frac{ca}{b+c}
\le
\frac{ac}{a+b}
=BC_1.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle BC_1A_1\le\angle BA_1C_1.
\]
Since $BF$ bisects $\angle A_1BC_1$,
\[
\angle B_1FC_1=\angle BFA_1\le90^\circ.
\]
Therefore $H$ lies on the same side of $BB_1$ as $C_1$, so $H$ lies inside $\triangle BB_1C_1$. In the same way, using $\alpha\le\beta$ and $\beta\le\gamma$, we find that $H$ lies inside
\[
\triangle CC_1B_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\triangle AA_1C_1.
\tag{2}
\]

We record two consequences that will be used below. Since $B_1$ lies on $CA$, the triangle $CC_1B_1$ is contained in $\triangle ACC_1$; hence
\[
H\in\triangle ACC_1.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, $H$ lies inside the acute triangle $A_1B_1C_1$, and thus inside $ABC$. The inclusion $H\in\triangle AA_1C_1$ places $H$ on the same side of $AI=AA_1$ as $B$, while $H\in\triangle BB_1C_1$ places it on the same side of $BI=BB_1$ as $A$. Together with the side $AB$, these are exactly the three half-plane conditions defining $\triangle ABI$. Therefore
\[
H\in\triangle ABI.
\tag{4}
\]

From (1),
\[
\alpha\le60^\circ\le\gamma.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle BIC\le120^\circ\le\angle AIB.
\tag{5}
\]

First suppose
\[
\angle AIC\ge120^\circ.
\tag{6}
\]
Rotate the points $B,I,H$ through $60^\circ$ about $A$, obtaining $B',I',H'$, with $B'$ and $C$ on opposite sides of $AB$. Since $\triangle AII'$ is equilateral,
\[
\begin{aligned}
AI+BI+CI
&=I'I+B'I'+IC,\\
AH+BH+CH
&=H'H+B'H'+HC.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Because
\[
\angle AII'=\angle AI'I=60^\circ,
\]
while
\[
\angle AI'B'=\angle AIB\ge120^\circ
\]
and (6) holds, the quadrilateral
\[
B'I'IC
\]
is convex and lies on the same side of $B'C$ as $A$.

By (3), $H$ lies inside $\triangle ACC_1$, so it lies outside $B'I'IC$. Also, by (4), $H$ lies inside $\triangle ABI$, and therefore its rotated image $H'$ lies inside $\triangle AB'I'$. Hence $H'$ also lies outside $B'I'IC$. It follows that the convex quadrilateral $B'I'IC$ is contained in the convex quadrilateral
\[
B'H'HC.
\]
The perimeter of a convex set cannot decrease when the set is enlarged. Adding the common side $B'C$ to the two path lengths in (7), we obtain
\[
I'I+B'I'+IC
\le
H'H+B'H'+HC.
\]
Thus
\[
AI+BI+CI\le AH+BH+CH.
\]

If instead
\[
\angle AIC<120^\circ,
\]
rotate $B,I,H$ through $60^\circ$ about $C$, choosing the orientation so that the image of $B$ and $A$ lie on opposite sides of $BC$. The same containment and perimeter argument, with the roles of $A$ and $C$ interchanged, gives the same inequality.

Therefore
\[
AH+BH+CH\ge AI+BI+CI.
\]
