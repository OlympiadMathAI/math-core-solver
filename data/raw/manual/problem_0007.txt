ID: 0007

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Sequences, Max-Plus Recurrence

STATEMENT:
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ be positive real numbers. For $n>r$, define recursively
\[
a_n=\max_{1\le k\le n-1}(a_k+a_{n-k}).
\tag{1}
\]
Prove that there exist positive integers $\ell\le r$ and $N$ such that
\[
a_n=a_{n-\ell}+a_\ell
\]
for all $n\ge N$.

SOLUTION 1:
Repeatedly expanding a maximizing term in (1), every $a_n$ with $n>r$ can be represented as
\[
a_n=a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_k},
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
1\le i_j\le r,
\qquad
i_1+\cdots+i_k=n.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, the two terms created in the final expansion have indices whose sum is greater than $r$. After reordering, we may therefore require
\[
i_1+i_2>r.
\tag{4}
\]
Conversely, if indices $i_1,\ldots,i_k$ satisfy (3) and (4), and
\[
s_j=i_1+\cdots+i_j,
\]
then repeated use of (1) gives
\[
a_n=a_{s_k}\ge a_{s_{k-1}}+a_{i_k}
\ge\cdots\ge a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_k}.
\]
Hence
\[
a_n=\max\left\{a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_k}:
(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\text{ satisfies (3) and (4)}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
s=\max_{1\le i\le r}\frac{a_i}{i},
\]
and choose $\ell\le r$ such that
\[
s=\frac{a_\ell}{\ell}.
\tag{6}
\]
Take
\[
n\ge r^2\ell+2r
\]
and choose a representation (2) attaining the maximum in (5). Since
\[
n=i_1+\cdots+i_k\le rk,
\]
we have
\[
k\ge \frac nr\ge r\ell+2.
\]
Suppose none of $i_3,\ldots,i_k$ equals $\ell$. Among these at least $r\ell$ indices, some value $j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$, with $j\ne\ell$, occurs at least $\ell$ times.

Delete $\ell$ copies of $j$ and insert $j$ copies of $\ell$. The sum of the indices remains unchanged, and the first two indices still satisfy (4), so the new list is admissible in (5). By maximality,
\[
\ell a_j\ge j a_\ell.
\]
On the other hand, the choice of $\ell$ in (6) gives
\[
\frac{a_j}{j}\le\frac{a_\ell}{\ell},
\]
so the reverse inequality holds. Thus
\[
\ell a_j=j a_\ell,
\]
and the modified representation also attains $a_n$.

We have therefore shown that, for every $n\ge r^2\ell+2r$, there is a maximizing representation (2) in which one of the indices $i_3,\ldots,i_k$ equals $\ell$. Reorder so that $i_k=\ell$. The remaining indices form an admissible representation of $n-\ell$, so (5) gives
\[
a_{n-\ell}+a_\ell
\ge a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_{k-1}}+a_\ell=a_n.
\]
The opposite inequality follows immediately from the recurrence (1). Hence
\[
a_n=a_{n-\ell}+a_\ell
\]
for every $n\ge r^2\ell+2r$.

SOLUTION 2:
Choose $\ell\le r$ and $s$ as in
\[
\frac{a_\ell}{\ell}=s=\max_{1\le i\le r}\frac{a_i}{i}.
\]
Define
\[
b_n=a_n-sn.
\]
Then $b_\ell=0$. We claim that
\[
b_n\le 0
\]
for all $n$, and that $(b_n)$ satisfies the same recurrence:
\[
b_n=\max_{1\le k\le n-1}(b_k+b_{n-k})
\qquad(n>r).
\tag{1}
\]
For $n\le r$, the inequality follows from the definition of $s$. For $n>r$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_n
&=\max_{1\le k\le n-1}(a_k+a_{n-k})-sn\\
&=\max_{1\le k\le n-1}(b_k+b_{n-k})\le 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
by induction.

If $b_i=0$ for every $1\le i\le r$, then (1) gives $b_n=0$ for all $n$, so $a_n=sn$ and the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, let
\[
M=\max_{1\le i\le r}|b_i|,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\min\{|b_i|:1\le i\le r,\ b_i<0\}.
\]
Because $b_\ell=0$, equation (1) gives, for every $n>r$,
\[
b_n\ge b_\ell+b_{n-\ell}=b_{n-\ell}.
\]
Thus along every residue class modulo $\ell$,
\[
0\ge b_n\ge b_{n-\ell}\ge b_{n-2\ell}\ge\cdots\ge -M.
\tag{2}
\]

As in Solution 1, each $b_n$ is a sum of terms chosen from $b_1,\ldots,b_r$. Hence every $b_n$ belongs to
\[
T=\left\{b_{i_1}+\cdots+b_{i_k}:i_1,\ldots,i_k\le r\right\}\cap[-M,0].
\]
The set $T$ is finite. Indeed, any such sum lying in $[-M,0]$ contains at most $M/\varepsilon$ nonzero terms; otherwise its value would be less than $-M$.

For each $t=1,\ldots,\ell$, the sequence
\[
b_{r+t},\ b_{r+t+\ell},\ b_{r+t+2\ell},\ldots
\]
is nondecreasing by (2), but takes values in the finite set $T$. It is therefore eventually constant. Hence there is an $N$ such that
\[
b_n=b_{n-\ell}
\]
for every $n>N+\ell$. Since $b_\ell=0$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_n
&=b_n+sn\\
&=b_{n-\ell}+s(n-\ell)+b_\ell+s\ell\\
&=a_{n-\ell}+a_\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the assertion.
