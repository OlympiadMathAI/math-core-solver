ID: 0014

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Processes, Invariants, Induction

STATEMENT:
Let $k$ be a positive integer and let $b>w>1$ be integers. Initially there is one string of $b$ black pearls and one string of $w$ white pearls. At every step:

(i) order all strings by nonincreasing length, placing a white string before a black string whenever their lengths are equal; choose the first $k$ strings of length greater than $1$, or all such strings if there are fewer than $k$;

(ii) cut each chosen string into two parts whose lengths differ by at most one.

The process stops immediately after the first isolated white pearl appears. Prove that at that moment there is still a black string containing at least two pearls.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $A_i$ be the position after the $i$th step, with $A_0$ initial. Call a string of length $m$ an $m$-string, and use $m$-black and $m$-white when its color matters.

For the proof, imagine continuing the process until every pearl is isolated. Define:

- $s$: the first stage at which any $1$-string appears;

- $t$: the first stage at which the total number of strings is greater than $k$; if this never occurs, put $t=\infty$;

- $f$: the first stage at which all black pearls are isolated.

It is enough to show that a white $1$-string appears no later than stage $A_{f-1}$.

Clearly $s\le f$. In $A_{f-1}$, every black string has length $1$ or $2$, because all nonisolated black strings are cut in the $f$th step. Also, for every transition $A_i\to A_{i+1}$ with $i\le t-1$, every string of length greater than $1$ is cut; if additionally $i<s$, then every string is cut.

Let $B_i,b_i$ be the maximum and minimum black-string lengths in $A_i$, and let $W_i,w_i$ be the analogous white quantities. We claim that for every
\[
i\le\min\{s,t\},
\]
we have
\[
\text{there are }2^i\text{ strings of each color},
\qquad
B_i\ge W_i,
\qquad
b_i\ge w_i.
\tag{1}
\]
This follows by induction. Before these stages every string is cut, so the number of strings of each color doubles. Moreover,
\[
B_i=\left\lceil\frac{B_{i-1}}2\right\rceil
\ge\left\lceil\frac{W_{i-1}}2\right\rceil=W_i
\]
and
\[
b_i=\left\lfloor\frac{b_{i-1}}2\right\rfloor
\ge\left\lfloor\frac{w_{i-1}}2\right\rfloor=w_i.
\]

We distinguish two cases.

\textbf{Case 1.} Suppose $s\le t$ or $f\le t+1$. Then $s\le t+1$. In $A_{s-1}$, relation (1) gives
\[
B_{s-1}\ge W_{s-1},
\qquad
b_{s-1}\ge w_{s-1}>1.
\]
If $s=f$, then $A_{s-1}$ contains no singleton of either color, because $s$ is the first stage at which any $1$-string appears. Moreover, no black string is longer than $2$, since every remaining black string is cut in the $f$th step and becomes isolated. Hence every black string has length $2$, so
\[
B_{s-1}=b_{s-1}=2.
\]
From (1), $B_{s-1}\ge W_{s-1}$, and therefore $W_{s-1}\le2$. Since there is no white singleton in $A_{s-1}$, we also have $w_{s-1}>1$. Thus
\[
W_{s-1}=w_{s-1}=2.
\]
Consequently every string has length $2$. There are $2^{s-1}$ strings of each color, which would give $b=w$, a contradiction.

Thus $s\le f-1$, and in fact $s\le t$. Therefore every string is cut in the $s$th step. If the first singleton produced is white, we are done. If a black singleton is produced, then $w_{s-1}\le b_{s-1}$ shows that cutting a shortest white string also produces a white singleton. Since $s<f$, not all black pearls are isolated at that time.

\textbf{Case 2.} Suppose
\[
t+1\le s,
\qquad
t+2\le f.
\]
In $A_t$ there are exactly $2^t$ strings of each color, all of length greater than $1$, and
\[
2^{t+1}>k\ge2^t.
\]
At step $t+1$, exactly $k$ strings are cut, and at most $2^t$ of them are black. Hence at least $k-2^t$ white strings are cut, so $A_{t+1}$ contains at least
\[
2^t+(k-2^t)=k
\]
white strings. The number of white strings never decreases, so $A_{f-1}$ also contains at least $k$ white strings.

At stage $A_{f-1}$, all black strings have length at most $2$, and at least one black $2$-string is selected in the $f$th step. Therefore at most $k-1$ white strings are selected, so some white string is not cut. But the tie-breaking rule puts every white string of length at least $2$ before a black $2$-string. Thus every non-single white string would have been selected. The uncut white string must therefore have length $1$. This is the desired white singleton before all black pearls are isolated.

SOLUTION 2:
We prove the following invariant. At every stage there exist a black string of length $u$ and a white string of length $v$ such that either
\[
\text{(i)}\qquad u>v\ge1,
\]
or
\[
\text{(ii)}\qquad 2\le u\le v<2u,
\]
and in case (ii) there are also $k-1$ other strings whose lengths are greater than $v/2$.

In both cases $u>1$. Therefore the invariant immediately implies that, when the first white singleton appears, a non-single black string still exists.

We prove the invariant by induction on the number of steps. Initially condition (i) holds because $b>w$.

\textbf{Case 1.} Suppose condition (i) holds at stage $A_i$. Choose $v$ to be the length of a shortest white string. Before the stopping stage, $v\ge2$.

\textbf{Subcase 1a.} Either no black $u$-string is cut, or both a black $u$-string and a white $v$-string are cut. In the first situation, a black string of length $u$ remains while a white string of length at most $v$ remains, so (i) persists. In the second situation, the cuts produce a black string of length
\[
\left\lceil\frac u2\right\rceil
\]
and a white string of length
\[
\left\lfloor\frac v2\right\rfloor.
\]
Since $u>v$, the former is strictly larger, so (i) again holds.

\textbf{Subcase 1b.} A black $u$-string is cut, but no white $v$-string is cut. Then every selected string has length greater than $v$. Put
\[
u'=\left\lceil\frac u2\right\rceil.
\]
If $u'>v$, condition (i) holds. Otherwise $u'\ge2$, and besides the chosen black string, the other $k-1$ selected strings have length at least $v+1$. The larger child of each has length
\[
\left\lceil\frac{v+1}{2}\right\rceil>\frac v2.
\]
Moreover,
\[
u'\le v<u\le2u'.
\]
Thus condition (ii) holds with the same $v$.

\textbf{Case 2.} Suppose condition (ii) holds at stage $A_i$, together with a set $\mathcal{S}$ of $k-1$ other strings longer than $v/2$.

\textbf{Subcase 2a.} Some black $u$-string is not cut, while some white $v$-string is cut. One of the resulting white strings has length $\lfloor v/2\rfloor<u$, so condition (i) holds.

\textbf{Subcase 2b.} No white $v$-string is cut. Since $u\le v$, no black $u$-string is cut either. Every selected string has length greater than $v$, and each selected string produces a child longer than $v/2$. Hence there are at least $k-1$ suitable other strings, and condition (ii) persists.

\textbf{Subcase 2c.} Every black $u$-string is cut. Then every string of length at least $u$ is selected, so a white $v$-string is also cut. We obtain
\[
u'=\left\lceil\frac u2\right\rceil,
\qquad
v'=\left\lfloor\frac v2\right\rfloor.
\]
If $u'>v'$, condition (i) holds. Otherwise
\[
u'\le v'<u\le2u'.
\]
We also have $u'\ge2$. Indeed, if $u'=1$, then $u=2$; the black $u$-string, the white $v$-string, and the $k-1$ strings in $\mathcal{S}$ would all have length at least $2$ and would all have to be selected, giving at least $k+1$ selected strings, impossible.

Finally construct a new set $\mathcal{S}'$ of $k-1$ strings. For each $s\in\mathcal{S}$, keep $s$ itself if it was not cut, and otherwise keep its larger child. Every chosen string has length greater than
\[
\frac v4\ge\frac{v'}2.
\]
Thus condition (ii) holds with $u',v'$ and $\mathcal{S}'$. The induction is complete.
