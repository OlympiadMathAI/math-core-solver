ID: 0026

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Polynomial Congruences, Chinese Remainder Theorem, Prime Powers

STATEMENT:
Let $a,b$ be integers and define
\[
P(x)=ax^3+bx.
\]
For a positive integer $n$, call the pair $(a,b)$ \emph{$n$-good} if, for all integers $m,k$,
\[
n\mid P(m)-P(k)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n\mid m-k.
\]
Call $(a,b)$ \emph{very good} if it is $n$-good for infinitely many positive integers $n$.

\textbf{(a)} Find a pair $(a,b)$ that is $51$-good but not very good.

\textbf{(b)} Prove that every $2010$-good pair is very good.

SOLUTION 1:
\textbf{Part (a).} Take
\[
\boxed{(a,b)=(1,-51^2)}.
\]
Then
\[
P(x)=x^3-51^2x.
\]

Since
\[
P(51)=P(0),
\]
the pair cannot be $n$-good unless $n\mid51$. Thus it is $n$-good for at most finitely many $n$, and so it is not very good.

Now suppose
\[
P(m)\equiv P(k)\pmod{51}.
\]
The linear terms vanish modulo $51$, so
\[
m^3\equiv k^3\pmod{51}.
\]
Modulo $3$,
\[
m\equiv m^3\equiv k^3\equiv k\pmod3.
\]
Modulo $17$, raising the congruence to the eleventh power gives
\[
m^{33}\equiv k^{33}\pmod{17}.
\]
By Fermat's theorem,
\[
x^{33}\equiv x\pmod{17}
\]
for every integer $x$, hence
\[
m\equiv k\pmod{17}.
\]
Therefore
\[
m\equiv k\pmod{51}.
\]
Thus $(1,-51^2)$ is $51$-good but not very good.

\textbf{Part (b).} We prove that a $2010$-good pair is $67^r$-good for every positive integer $r$.

\textbf{Claim 1.} Every $2010$-good pair is $67$-good.

Suppose
\[
P(m)\equiv P(k)\pmod{67}.
\]
Since $\gcd(30,67)=1$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives integers $m',k'$ such that
\[
m'\equiv m\pmod{67},
\qquad
m'\equiv0\pmod{30},
\]
\[
k'\equiv k\pmod{67},
\qquad
k'\equiv0\pmod{30}.
\]
Then
\[
P(m')\equiv P(k')\pmod{30}
\]
and
\[
P(m')\equiv P(m)\equiv P(k)\equiv P(k')\pmod{67}.
\]
Hence
\[
P(m')\equiv P(k')\pmod{2010}.
\]
Since the pair is $2010$-good,
\[
m'\equiv k'\pmod{2010},
\]
and therefore
\[
m\equiv k\pmod{67}.
\]
This proves Claim 1.

\textbf{Claim 2.} If $(a,b)$ is $67$-good, then
\[
67\mid a.
\]

Assume instead that $67\nmid a$. Consider the two sets of residues
\[
\{at^2\bmod67:0\le t\le33\}
\]
and
\[
\{-3as^2-b\bmod67:0\le s\le33\}.
\]
Each has $34$ elements, because the numbers $0,1,\ldots,33$ represent the distinct square classes up to sign and $a$ is invertible modulo $67$. Therefore the two sets intersect. Choose $s,t$ such that
\[
at^2\equiv-3as^2-b\pmod{67}.
\tag{1}
\]

First take
\[
m=t+s,\qquad k=-2s.
\]
Then
\[
m-k=t+3s
\]
and
\[
m^2+mk+k^2=t^2+3s^2.
\]
Using
\[
P(m)-P(k)
=(m-k)\left(a(m^2+mk+k^2)+b\right)
\]
and (1), we get
\[
67\mid P(m)-P(k).
\]
Since the pair is $67$-good,
\[
t+3s\equiv0\pmod{67}.
\tag{2}
\]

Now take
\[
m=t-s,\qquad k=2s.
\]
The same quadratic factor occurs, so
\[
t-3s\equiv0\pmod{67}.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2) and (3) imply
\[
t\equiv s\equiv0\pmod{67}.
\]
Because $0\le s,t\le33$, we have $s=t=0$. Then (1) gives
\[
b\equiv0\pmod{67}.
\]
But now
\[
P(7)-P(2)
=5(67a+b)
\]
is divisible by $67$, while $7-2=5$ is not, contradicting $67$-goodness. Hence
\[
67\mid a.
\]

\textbf{Claim 3.} Every $2010$-good pair is $67^r$-good for all $r\ge1$.

By Claims 1 and 2,
\[
67\mid a.
\]
We must have
\[
67\nmid b;
\]
otherwise $P(x)\equiv P(0)\pmod{67}$ for every $x$, contradicting $67$-goodness.

Suppose
\[
67^r\mid P(m)-P(k).
\]
Factoring,
\[
P(m)-P(k)
=(m-k)\left(a(m^2+mk+k^2)+b\right).
\]
The second factor is not divisible by $67$, because $67\mid a$ and $67\nmid b$. Therefore
\[
67^r\mid m-k.
\]
Thus the pair is $67^r$-good for every $r\ge1$. Since there are infinitely many such moduli, the pair is very good.
