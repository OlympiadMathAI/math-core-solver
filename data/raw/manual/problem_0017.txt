ID: 0017

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Apollonius Circle, Similar Triangles, Arc Chasing, Tangent-Chord Theorem

STATEMENT:
A point $P$ lies inside triangle $ABC$. The lines $AP,BP,CP$ meet the circumcircle $\omega$ of $ABC$ again at $K,L,M$, respectively. The tangent to $\omega$ at $C$ meets the line $AB$ at $S$. Prove that
\[
SC=SP
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
MK=ML.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We may assume that $CA>CB$, so $S$ lies on the ray $AB$.

The triangles $PKM$ and $PCA$ are similar, and so are the triangles $PLM$ and $PCB$. Hence
\[
\frac{PM}{KM}=\frac{PA}{CA},
\qquad
\frac{LM}{PM}=\frac{CB}{PB}.
\]
Multiplying,
\[
\frac{LM}{KM}
=\frac{CB}{CA}\cdot\frac{PA}{PB}.
\]
Therefore
\[
MK=ML
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{PA}{PB}=\frac{CA}{CB}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $E$ be the point where the internal bisector of $\angle ACB$ meets $AB$. The locus of points $X$ satisfying
\[
\frac{XA}{XB}=\frac{CA}{CB}
\]
is an Apollonius circle $\Omega$ whose center $Q$ lies on $AB$. By the angle-bisector theorem, $E\in\Omega$, and clearly $C\in\Omega$. Thus (1) is equivalent to
\[
P\in\Omega
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
QP=QC.
\tag{2}
\]

It remains to identify $Q$. By the tangent-chord theorem and the fact that $CE$ bisects $\angle ACB$,
\[
\angle CES
=\angle CAE+\angle ACE
=\angle BCS+\angle ECB
=\angle ECS.
\]
Hence
\[
SC=SE.
\]
Thus $S$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $CE$. Since $S$ also lies on $AB$, it is the center $Q$ of the Apollonius circle through $C$ and $E$. Therefore $Q=S$, and (2) becomes
\[
MK=ML
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
SP=SC.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again assume that $S$ lies on the ray $AB$. Let the line $SP$ meet $\omega$ at $E$ and $F$, with $E$ lying on the segment $SP$.

We first prove
\[
SP=SC\quad\Longrightarrow\quad MK=ML.
\]

Because $SC$ is tangent to $\omega$,
\[
SC^2=SA\cdot SB.
\]
If $SP=SC$, then
\[
SP^2=SA\cdot SB,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{SP}{SB}=\frac{SA}{SP}.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle PSA\sim\triangle BSP,
\]
so
\[
\angle BPS=\angle SAP.
\tag{1}
\]

Using the intersecting-chords and inscribed-angle formulas,
\[
2\angle BPS=\widehat{BE}+\widehat{LF},
\qquad
2\angle SAP=\widehat{BE}+\widehat{EK}.
\]
From (1),
\[
\widehat{LF}=\widehat{EK}.
\tag{2}
\]

Also, $SP=SC$ gives
\[
\angle SPC=\angle SCP.
\]
Again using arc measures,
\[
2\angle SPC=\widehat{EC}+\widehat{MF},
\qquad
2\angle SCP=\widehat{EC}+\widehat{EM}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\widehat{MF}=\widehat{EM}.
\tag{3}
\]

Adding (2) and (3),
\[
\widehat{MFL}
=\widehat{MF}+\widehat{FL}
=\widehat{ME}+\widehat{EK}
=\widehat{MEK}.
\]
Equal arcs subtend equal chords, so
\[
ML=MK.
\]

Now assume conversely that $MK=ML$. Since
\[
SC^2=SE\cdot SF,
\]
there is a point $P'$ on the segment $EF$ such that
\[
SP'=SC.
\]
Let $AP',BP',CP'$ meet $\omega$ again at $K',L',M'$, respectively. The forward implication already proved gives
\[
\widehat{M'FL'}=\widehat{M'EK'}.
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose $P'\ne P$. If $P'$ lies on the segment $PF$, the cyclic order of the corresponding intersection points gives
\[
\widehat{MFL}
>
\widehat{M'FL'}
=
\widehat{M'EK'}
>
\widehat{MEK},
\]
contradicting $MK=ML$. If $P'$ lies on the segment $EP$, all inequalities reverse, and we obtain the same contradiction. Hence $P'=P$, and therefore
\[
SP=SP'=SC.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
We prove only the converse implication. Assume that
\[
MK=ML.
\]
Let $E,F$ be the intersections of $SP$ with $\omega$, as in Solution 2. Equality of the chords gives
\[
\widehat{MEK}=\widehat{MFL}.
\tag{1}
\]
We claim that
\[
\widehat{ME}=\widehat{MF}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\widehat{EK}=\widehat{FL}.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that
\[
\widehat{ME}>\widehat{MF}.
\]
Then (1) implies
\[
\widehat{EK}<\widehat{FL}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
2\angle SCM
=\widehat{EC}+\widehat{ME}
>
\widehat{EC}+\widehat{MF}
=2\angle SPC,
\]
so in triangle $SPC$,
\[
SP>SC.
\tag{3}
\]

On the other hand,
\[
2\angle SPK
=\widehat{EK}+\widehat{AF}
<
\widehat{FL}+\widehat{AF}
=2\angle ABL.
\]
Since $A,P,K$ are collinear and $B,P,L$ are collinear,
\[
\angle SPA
=180^\circ-\angle SPK
>
180^\circ-\angle ABL
=\angle SBP.
\]
Choose $A'$ on the ray $SA$ so that
\[
\angle SPA'=\angle SBP.
\]
Then
\[
\triangle SBP\sim\triangle SPA',
\]
and therefore
\[
SP^2=SB\cdot SA'<SB\cdot SA=SC^2,
\]
contradicting (3).

The case $\widehat{ME}<\widehat{MF}$ is analogous. Hence (2) holds. Finally,
\[
2\angle SCM
=\widehat{EC}+\widehat{ME}
=\widehat{EC}+\widehat{MF}
=2\angle SPC.
\]
Because $C,P,M$ are collinear, $\angle SCM=\angle SCP$, so
\[
\angle SCP=\angle SPC.
\]
Thus triangle $SPC$ is isosceles and
\[
SC=SP.
\]
