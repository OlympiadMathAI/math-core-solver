ID: 0358

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Laurent Polynomials, Rational Root Theorem, Geometric Transformations, Game Strategy

STATEMENT:
Alice is given a rational number $r>1$ and a line containing two distinct points $B$ and $R$. A red bead is placed at $R$, and a blue bead is placed at $B$.

In one move, Alice chooses an integer $k$, which need not be positive, and chooses one bead. If the chosen bead is at $X$ and the other bead is at $Y$, she moves the chosen bead to the point $X'$ satisfying
\[
\overrightarrow{YX'}=r^k\overrightarrow{YX}.
\]

Alice's goal is to move the red bead to the original point $B$. Determine all rational numbers $r>1$ for which she can achieve this in at most $2021$ moves.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{
r=\frac{b+1}{b}
\quad\text{for}\quad
b=1,2,\ldots,1010
}.
\]

Choose coordinates on the line so that initially
\[
R=0,\qquad B=1.
\]
Because $r^k>0$, the red bead always remains to the left of the blue bead. Every move multiplies the distance between the beads by a power of $r$. Thus, after the $j$th move, the distance has the form
\[
d_j=r^{\alpha_j}
\qquad(\alpha_j\in\mathbb Z),
\]
with
\[
d_0=1.
\]

If the same bead is moved in two consecutive moves, the two distance multipliers can be combined into one power of $r$. Hence any successful play can be shortened, if necessary, so that the two beads are moved alternately. The final move must move the red bead.

Suppose first that the red bead is moved first and altogether $t$ times. Then the play has $2t-1$ moves, and the total displacement of the red bead is
\[
(d_0-d_1)+(d_2-d_3)+\cdots+(d_{2t-2}-d_{2t-1}).
\]
This displacement must equal $1$. Since $d_0=1$, we obtain
\[
d_1+d_3+\cdots+d_{2t-1}
=
d_2+d_4+\cdots+d_{2t-2}.
\tag{1}
\]
The two sides of (1) contain $t$ and $t-1$ powers of $r$, respectively.

If the blue bead is moved first and the red bead is moved $t$ times, then there are $2t$ moves and
\[
(d_1-d_2)+(d_3-d_4)+\cdots+(d_{2t-1}-d_{2t})=1.
\]
Treating $1$ as $r^0$, this again gives an equality between $t+1$ powers of $r$ and $t$ powers of $r$.

Therefore a successful play in at most $2021$ moves yields an equation
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{\beta_i}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}r^{\gamma_i},
\qquad
\beta_i,\gamma_i\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{2}
\]
for some
\[
N\le1011.
\]
Conversely, any equation (2) can be used as the consecutive distances of an alternating play and makes the total displacement of the red bead equal to $1$. Equal powers may be added to both sides, so it is harmless to increase $N$ up to $1011$.

We now determine the rational numbers $r$ for which (2) is possible. Write
\[
r=\frac ab
\]
in lowest terms, where
\[
a>b\ge1.
\]
Define the Laurent polynomial
\[
L(x)=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x^{\beta_i}
-
\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}x^{\gamma_i}.
\]
Then
\[
L\left(\frac ab\right)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L(1)=1.
\]
Choose an integer $d$ so large that
\[
P(x)=x^dL(x)\in\mathbb Z[x].
\]
We still have
\[
P\left(\frac ab\right)=0,
\qquad
P(1)=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Furthermore, $P(-1)$ is the sum of $2N-1$ numbers, each equal to $1$ or $-1$. Hence it is a nonzero odd integer and
\[
1\le|P(-1)|\le2N-1\le2021.
\tag{4}
\]

By the rational-root theorem, the primitive polynomial
\[
a-bx
\]
divides $P(x)$ in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Evaluating this divisibility at $x=1$ and using (3),
\[
a-b\mid P(1)=1.
\]
Since $a>b$,
\[
a-b=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Evaluating at $x=-1$ and using (4),
\[
a+b\mid P(-1),
\]
so
\[
a+b\le2021.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) give
\[
a=b+1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b\le1010.
\]

It remains to prove that every such number works. Let
\[
r=\frac{b+1}{b}
\qquad(1\le b\le1010)
\]
and put
\[
N=b+1.
\]
Then
\[
\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{N\text{ terms}}
=
\underbrace{r+\cdots+r}_{N-1=b\text{ terms}},
\tag{7}
\]
because
\[
br=b+1=N.
\]
Equation (7) has the form (2). More explicitly, Alice may move the red bead first and alternate the beads while taking the distances
\[
d_0=1,\qquad
d_{2j-1}=1\quad(1\le j\le N),
\qquad
d_{2j}=r\quad(1\le j\le N-1).
\]
Each transition is achievable because the ratio of two consecutive distances is a power of $r$. The red bead moves by
\[
1-N+(N-1)r
=
1-(b+1)+b\cdot\frac{b+1}{b}
=
1.
\]
The number of moves is
\[
2N-1=2b+1\le2021.
\]
Thus the red bead reaches the original point $B$, completing the proof.
