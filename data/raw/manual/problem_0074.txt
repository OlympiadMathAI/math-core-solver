ID: 0074

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Similar Triangles, Parallelograms, Reflections, Directed Angles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral whose diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ meet at $E$. The extensions of the sides $AD$ and $BC$ beyond $A$ and $B$, respectively, meet at $F$. Let $G$ be the point such that $ECGD$ is a parallelogram, and let $H$ be the reflection of $E$ in the line $AD$. Prove that $D,H,F,G$ are concyclic.

SOLUTION 1:
We first prove that triangles $FDG$ and $FBE$ are similar.

Since $ABCD$ is cyclic, triangles $EAB$ and $EDC$ are similar. Hence
\[
\frac{CE}{EB}=\frac{CD}{AB}.
\tag{1}
\]
Also, triangles $FAB$ and $FCD$ are similar, which gives
\[
\frac{FD}{FB}=\frac{CD}{AB}.
\tag{2}
\]
Because $ECGD$ is a parallelogram,
\[
GD=EC
\]
and
\[
\angle CDG=\angle DCE.
\]
Moreover, by the cyclicity of $ABCD$,
\[
\angle DCE=\angle DCA=\angle DBA.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle FDG
&=\angle FDC+\angle CDG\\
&=\angle FBA+\angle ABD\\
&=\angle FBE.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
From (1) and (2),
\[
\frac{GD}{EB}=\frac{CE}{EB}=\frac{CD}{AB}=\frac{FD}{FB}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus (3) and (4) give the similarity
\[
\triangle FDG\sim\triangle FBE.
\]
In particular,
\[
\angle FGD=\angle FEB.
\tag{5}
\]

The line $FD$ is the same line as $AD$. Since $H$ is the reflection of $E$ in $FD$, the reflection fixes $F$ and $D$, and hence
\[
\angle FHD=\angle FED.
\tag{6}
\]
Because $D,E,B$ are collinear, the rays $ED$ and $EB$ are opposite, so
\[
\angle FED=180^\circ-\angle FEB.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (5), (6), and (7), we obtain
\[
\angle FHD+\angle FGD=180^\circ.
\]
Therefore the quadrilateral $DHFG$ is cyclic, as required.
