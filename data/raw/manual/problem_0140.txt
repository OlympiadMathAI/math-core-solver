ID: 0140

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Unit Fractions, Packing, Merging Arguments, Averaging

STATEMENT:
A coin is called a Cape Town coin if its value is $1/n$ for some positive integer $n$. Given a collection of Cape Town coins of total value at most
\[
99+\frac12,
\]
prove that it is possible to split the collection into at most $100$ groups, each of total value at most $1$.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove the more general statement that, for every positive integer $N$, any collection of Cape Town coins whose total value is at most
\[
N-\frac12
\tag{1}
\]
can be divided into $N$ groups of value at most $1$. Taking $N=100$ gives the problem.

Whenever several coins have total value $1/k$ for some positive integer $k$, merge them temporarily into one coin of value $1/k$. This operation preserves the total value, and any grouping of the merged collection can later be expanded to a grouping of the original coins. Since each merger reduces the number of coins, the process terminates.

In the resulting collection:

- for every even $k$, there is at most one coin of value $1/k$, since two such coins could be merged into one coin of value $1/(k/2)$;
- for every odd $k>1$, there are at most $k-1$ coins of value $1/k$, since $k$ such coins could be merged into one coin of value $1$.

Every coin of value $1$ may be put alone in a group. If there are $d$ such coins, remove them and replace $N$ by $N-d$. Condition (1) remains valid for the coins that are left. Hence we may assume from now on that no coin has value $1$.

Create groups $G_1,\ldots,G_N$. For each $k=1,\ldots,N$, put into $G_k$ every coin of value
\[
\frac1{2k-1}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\frac1{2k}.
\]
By the terminal properties above, the total value already placed in $G_k$ is at most
\[
\frac{2k-2}{2k-1}+\frac1{2k}<1.
\tag{2}
\]
This formula also covers $k=1$, because there are no coins of value $1$.

The only undistributed coins now have denominator greater than $2N$, and therefore each has value strictly less than $1/(2N)$. Add these remaining coins one at a time. At any stage, the total value in all $N$ groups is at most $N-1/2$, so at least one group has current value at most
\[
\frac{N-\frac12}{N}=1-\frac1{2N}.
\]
Placing the next small coin into that group keeps its value below $1$. Repeating this distributes every coin.

Finally, undo all temporary mergers. We obtain at most $N$ groups, each of total value at most $1$, as required.
