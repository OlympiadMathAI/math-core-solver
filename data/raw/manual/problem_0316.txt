ID: 0316

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Circumcentres, Isosceles Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral. Suppose that $P$ lies inside $ABCD$ and
\[
\angle PAD:\angle PBA:\angle DPA
=
1:2:3
=
\angle CBP:\angle BAP:\angle BPC.
\]
The internal angle bisectors of $\angle ADP$ and $\angle PCB$ meet at a point $Q$ inside triangle $ABP$.

Prove that
\[
AQ=BQ.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Set
\[
\varphi=\angle PAD,
\qquad
\psi=\angle CBP.
\]
Then
\[
\angle PBA=2\varphi,
\qquad
\angle DPA=3\varphi,
\]
and
\[
\angle BAP=2\psi,
\qquad
\angle BPC=3\psi.
\]

Choose $X\in AD$ such that
\[
\angle XPA=\varphi.
\]
Since $A,X,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle PXD
=
\angle PAX+\angle XPA
=
2\varphi
=
\angle DPA-\angle XPA
=
\angle DPX.
\]
Thus triangle $DPX$ is isosceles and
\[
DX=DP.
\]
Consequently, the internal angle bisector of $\angle ADP=\angle XDP$ is the perpendicular bisector of $XP$.

Similarly, choose $Y\in BC$ such that
\[
\angle BPY=\psi.
\]
The same argument shows that the internal angle bisector of $\angle PCB$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PY$.

It remains to show that the perpendicular bisectors of $XP$, $PY$, and $AB$ are concurrent.

We have
\[
\angle AXP
=
180^\circ-\angle PXD
=
180^\circ-2\varphi
=
180^\circ-\angle PBA.
\]
Hence $A,X,P,B$ are concyclic. Analogously,
\[
A,Y,P,B
\]
are concyclic. Thus
\[
A,B,P,X,Y
\]
lie on one circle.

The centre $O$ of this circle lies on the perpendicular bisectors of the three chords
\[
XP,\qquad PY,\qquad AB.
\]
The first two of these perpendicular bisectors are precisely the two given angle bisectors, so
\[
Q=O.
\]
Since $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$,
\[
AQ=BQ.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again put
\[
\varphi=\angle PAD,
\qquad
\psi=\angle CBP.
\]
Then
\[
\angle PBA=2\varphi,\quad
\angle DPA=3\varphi,\quad
\angle BAP=2\psi,\quad
\angle BPC=3\psi.
\]

Let $O$ be the circumcentre of triangle $APB$. We show that $O$ lies on both given angle bisectors.

First,
\[
\angle ADP
=
180^\circ-\angle PAD-\angle DPA
=
180^\circ-4\varphi.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\angle POA
=
2\angle PBA
=
4\varphi.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle ADP+\angle POA=180^\circ,
\]
so $A,D,P,O$ are concyclic.

Since $OA=OP$, the equal chords $OA$ and $OP$ in the circle $(ADPO)$ subtend equal angles at $D$:
\[
\angle ADO=\angle ODP.
\]
Thus $DO$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle ADP$.

The completely analogous argument gives that
\[
CO
\]
is the internal angle bisector of $\angle PCB$.

Hence the two angle bisectors meet at $O$, so $Q=O$. Finally, because $O$ is the circumcentre of triangle $APB$,
\[
OA=OB,
\]
and therefore
\[
AQ=BQ.
\]
