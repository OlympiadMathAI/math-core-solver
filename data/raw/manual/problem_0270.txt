ID: 0270

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Sign Partitions, Ordered-Pair Sums, Extremal Arguments

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge2$, and let $a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n$ be real numbers such that
\[
a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n=0.
\]
Define
\[
A=\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le n,\ |a_i-a_j|\ge1\}.
\]
Prove that, if $A$ is nonempty, then
\[
\sum_{(i,j)\in A}a_i a_j<0.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Define sets of ordered pairs
\[
B=\{(i,j):1\le i,j\le n,\ |a_i-a_j|\ge1\}
\]
and
\[
C=\{(i,j):1\le i,j\le n,\ |a_i-a_j|<1\}.
\]
Since every unordered pair in $A$ appears twice in $B$,
\[
\sum_{(i,j)\in A}a_i a_j
=\frac12\sum_{(i,j)\in B}a_i a_j.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
\sum_{1\le i,j\le n}a_i a_j
=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\right)^2=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{(i,j)\in B}a_i a_j
=-\sum_{(i,j)\in C}a_i a_j.
\tag{2}
\]
It remains to prove that the last sum in (2) is positive.

Partition the indices into
\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=\{i:a_i\le-1\},&
Q&=\{i:-1<a_i\le0\},\\
R&=\{i:0<a_i<1\},&
S&=\{i:a_i\ge1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{(i,j)\in C}a_i a_j
&\ge
\sum_{i\in P\cup S}a_i^2
+
\sum_{i,j\in Q\cup R}a_i a_j\\
&=
\sum_{i\in P\cup S}a_i^2
+
\left(\sum_{i\in Q\cup R}a_i\right)^2\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Every positive term on the right occurs on the left, while any negative term on the right that is missing from the left only makes the right side smaller.

If equality held throughout (3), then $P=S=\varnothing$, and every pair of indices in $Q\cup R$ with negative product would have distance less than $1$. The same-sign differences inside $Q$ and inside $R$ are automatically less than $1$, so all pairwise differences would be less than $1$, giving $A=\varnothing$. Since $A$ is nonempty, (3) is strict:
\[
\sum_{(i,j)\in C}a_i a_j>0.
\]
Equations (1)-(2) now give the result.

SOLUTION 2:
Use the same partition $P,Q,R,S$, and put
\[
p=\sum_{i\in P}a_i,
\qquad
q=\sum_{i\in Q}a_i,
\qquad
r=\sum_{i\in R}a_i,
\qquad
s=\sum_{i\in S}a_i.
\]
Then
\[
p+q+r+s=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Let $t_+$ be the sum of $a_i a_j$ over pairs $(i,j)\in A$ with nonnegative product, and let $t_-$ be the corresponding sum over pairs with nonpositive product.

All positive contributions are bounded above by including every possible same-sign product:
\[
t_+\le
\frac{p^2}{2}+pq+rs+\frac{s^2}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
This inequality is strict unless
\[
p=s=0,
\tag{3}
\]
because $p^2/2$ and $s^2/2$ include positive square terms.

Every pair from $P\times R$, $P\times S$, or $Q\times S$ belongs to $A$. Any additional pair from $Q\times R$ has negative product, so
\[
t_-\le pr+ps+qs.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (2) and (4), and using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
t_++t_-
&\le
\frac{p^2+s^2}{2}+pq+pr+ps+qs+rs\\
&=
\frac{(p+q+r+s)^2-(q+r)^2}{2}\\
&=-\frac{(q+r)^2}{2}\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
If equality held in (5), then (3) would give $P=S=\varnothing$. Since $A$ is nonempty, there would then be a pair with one index in $Q$ and one in $R$ whose values differ by at least $1$; its negative product makes (4) strict. Therefore equality is impossible, and
\[
t_++t_-<0.
\]
