ID: 0149

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Fixed Points, Symmetry, Odd Functions

STATEMENT:
Find all functions $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfying
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x+y)\bigr)+f(xy)
=x+f(x+y)+yf(x)
\tag{1}
\]
for all real numbers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=2-x}
\]
for all real $x$. Both are easily verified.

Let $f$ satisfy (1). Setting $y=1$, we obtain
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x+1)\bigr)=x+f(x+1).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus $x+f(x+1)$ is a fixed point of $f$ for every real $x$.

We distinguish two cases.

\textbf{Case 1: $f(0)\ne0$.}
Putting $x=0$ in (1) gives
\[
f(f(y))+f(0)=f(y)+yf(0).
\tag{3}
\]
If $y_0$ is a fixed point of $f$, then (3) with $y=y_0$ yields
\[
f(0)=y_0f(0),
\]
so $y_0=1$. By (2),
\[
x+f(x+1)=1
\]
for every $x$, and hence
\[
f(t)=2-t
\]
for every real $t$.

\textbf{Case 2: $f(0)=0$.}
Putting $y=0$ in (1), and then replacing $x$ by $x+1$, gives
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x+1)+1\bigr)=x+f(x+1)+1.
\tag{4}
\]
Putting $x=1$ in (1) gives
\[
f\bigl(1+f(y+1)\bigr)+f(y)
=1+f(y+1)+yf(1).
\tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) with $x=-1$ gives $f(-1)=-1$. Substituting $y=-1$ into (5), we obtain $f(1)=1$. Therefore (5) becomes
\[
f\bigl(1+f(y+1)\bigr)+f(y)
=1+f(y+1)+y.
\tag{6}
\]

If both $y_0$ and $y_0+1$ are fixed points, then (6) shows that $y_0+2$ is also fixed. Equations (2) and (4) show that
\[
x+f(x+1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
x+f(x+1)+1
\]
are consecutive fixed points. Hence
\[
x+f(x+1)+2
\]
is fixed as well. Replacing $x$ by $x-2$, we get
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x-1)\bigr)=x+f(x-1).
\tag{7}
\]
On the other hand, setting $y=-1$ in (1) gives
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x-1)\bigr)+f(-x)
=x+f(x-1)-f(x).
\]
Comparing with (7), we obtain
\[
f(-x)=-f(x)
\tag{8}
\]
for every real $x$.

Finally, substitute $(x,y)=(-1,-y)$ into (1). Using $f(-1)=-1$ and the oddness (8), we get
\[
-f\bigl(1+f(y+1)\bigr)+f(y)
=-1-f(y+1)+y.
\tag{9}
\]
Adding (9) to (6) gives
\[
2f(y)=2y.
\]
Thus $f(y)=y$ for every real $y$.

The two cases yield exactly the two stated solutions.
