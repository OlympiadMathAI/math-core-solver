ID: 0325

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Graph Connectivity, Quadratic Congruences, Quadratic Residues, Euclid-Type Prime Construction

STATEMENT:
For every prime $p$, the kingdom of $p$-Landia consists of $p$ islands numbered
\[
1,2,\ldots,p.
\]
Two distinct islands numbered $n$ and $m$ are connected by a bridge if and only if
\[
p\mid(n^2-m+1)(m^2-n+1).
\]
The bridges may pass over one another, but cannot cross.

Prove that for infinitely many primes $p$, there are two islands in $p$-Landia that are not connected by any chain of bridges.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove that the graph is disconnected for every prime $p>3$ that divides a number of the form
\[
x^2-x+1.
\tag{1}
\]

For distinct residues $m,n$, a bridge joins $m$ and $n$ precisely when
\[
n\equiv m^2+1\pmod p
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
m\equiv n^2+1\pmod p.
\]
Whenever
\[
n\equiv m^2+1\pmod p
\]
with $n\ne m$, direct an arrow from $m$ to $n$. Every bridge carries at least one arrow, while at most one arrow starts from each island.

Suppose that
\[
x^2-x+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Then
\[
x^2+1\equiv x\pmod p,
\]
so no arrow starts at the island $x$. The other root of the same quadratic is
\[
1-x\pmod p,
\]
represented among the island numbers by $p+1-x$, and no arrow starts there either.

These two islands are distinct. Indeed, if
\[
x\equiv1-x\pmod p,
\]
then $2x\equiv1\pmod p$, and
\[
4(x^2-x+1)\equiv3\pmod p,
\]
which is impossible for $p>3$.

Thus at most $p-2$ arrows occur, and consequently the number of bridges is at most $p-2$. A graph with $p$ vertices and fewer than $p-1$ edges is disconnected. Hence two islands are not connected by a chain of bridges.

It remains to prove that infinitely many primes satisfy (1). Start with the prime
\[
3\mid 2^2-2+1.
\]
Suppose that
\[
p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_t
\]
are distinct primes already obtained in this way, including $3$, and put
\[
P=p_1p_2\cdots p_t.
\]
The integer
\[
P^2-P+1
\]
is greater than $1$ and is congruent to $1$ modulo every $p_i$. Therefore it has a prime divisor not among
\[
p_1,\ldots,p_t.
\]
This new prime also divides a value of $x^2-x+1$. Repeating the argument proves that infinitely many such primes exist.

SOLUTION 2:
We prove the stronger assertion that for infinitely many primes $p$, two islands form a connected component by themselves.

Use the same directed-arrow convention as in Solution 1. Let $p>3$, and suppose that
\[
a^2-a+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Put
\[
b\equiv1-a\pmod p,
\qquad 1\le b\le p.
\]
Then $b$ is the other root, $a\ne b$, and
\[
ab\equiv1\pmod p.
\tag{1}
\]

No arrow starts from either $a$ or $b$. If an arrow ends at $a$, its starting point $t$ satisfies
\[
t^2+1\equiv a\equiv a^2+1\pmod p.
\]
Apart from the forbidden loop $t=a$, the only possibility is
\[
t\equiv-a\pmod p.
\]
Thus the only arrow entering $a$ comes from $p-a$. Similarly, the only arrow entering $b$ comes from $p-b$.

The pair
\[
\{a,p-a\}
\]
is therefore isolated from the remaining islands if the congruence
\[
t^2+1\equiv-a\pmod p
\]
has no solution. Likewise, the pair
\[
\{b,p-b\}
\]
is isolated if
\[
t^2+1\equiv-b\pmod p
\]
has no solution.

Now
\[
(-1-a)(-1-b)
\equiv
1+(a+b)+ab
\equiv3\pmod p
\]
by (1). Hence, if $3$ is a quadratic nonresidue modulo $p$, at least one of
\[
-1-a,\qquad -1-b
\]
is a quadratic nonresidue, and one of the two pairs above is an isolated component.

It remains to construct infinitely many primes $p$ for which:

\begin{itemize}
\item $x^2-x+1\equiv0\pmod p$ is solvable; and
\item $3$ is a quadratic nonresidue modulo $p$.
\end{itemize}

The first condition is equivalent to
\[
(2x-1)^2\equiv-3\pmod p.
\]
Thus, once the first condition holds, $3$ is a nonresidue exactly when $-1$ is a nonresidue, that is, when
\[
p\equiv-1\pmod4.
\]

Suppose that
\[
p_1,\ldots,p_t
\]
are primes congruent to $-1$ modulo $4$ and dividing values of $x^2-x+1$. Put
\[
P=p_1p_2\cdots p_t
\]
and consider
\[
(2P)^2-2P+1.
\]
This number is congruent to $1$ modulo every $p_i$, and it is congruent to $-1$ modulo $4$. Therefore it has a prime divisor
\[
p\equiv-1\pmod4
\]
that is not among the $p_i$. Since it is a value of $x^2-x+1$, the new prime has both required properties.

This Euclid-type construction produces infinitely many suitable primes.
