ID: 0287

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Signed Areas, Reflections, Direct Similarity, Power of a Point, Radical Geometry

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDE$ be a convex pentagon such that
\[
CD=DE
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle EDC\ne2\angle ADB.
\]
Let $P$ be an interior point of the pentagon satisfying
\[
AP=AE,
\qquad
BP=BC.
\]
Prove that $P$ lies on the diagonal $CE$ if and only if
\[
[BCD]+[ADE]=[ABD]+[ABP],
\]
where $[XYZ]$ denotes the ordinary area of triangle $XYZ$.

SOLUTION 1:
In the proof, areas and lengths are directed whenever necessary.

Let $P'$ be the reflection of $P$ in $AB$, and let $M,N$ be the midpoints of $P'E,P'C$, respectively. Convexity ensures that $P'$ is different from $C$ and $E$.

We shall prove that each of the two conditions in the problem is equivalent to the direct similarity of the right triangles
\[
AP'M
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
BP'N.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $F$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $P'$ to $AB$. Since $AB$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PP'$, the point $F$ is the midpoint of $PP'$. The homothety with centre $P'$ and ratio $1/2$ sends
\[
P,E,C\quad\text{to}\quad F,M,N.
\]
Consequently,
\[
P,C,E\text{ are collinear}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F,M,N\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{2}
\]
Now $AP'=AP=AE$, so $A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $P'E$; similarly, $B$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $P'C$. Hence $AP'M$ and $BP'N$ are right triangles. Also, the quadrilaterals $AP'FM$ and $BFP'N$ are cyclic. Thus
\[
\angle AFM=\angle AP'M,
\qquad
\angle BFN=\angle BP'N.
\]
Since $A,F,B$ are collinear, (2) is equivalent to
\[
\angle AP'M=\angle BP'N,
\]
which is equivalent to the direct similarity in (1).

We now transform the area condition. By reflection in $AB$ and additivity of signed areas,
\[
[ABD]+[ABP]=[AP'BD].
\]
Using that $M,N$ are the midpoints of $P'E,P'C$, respectively, the required area equality reduces to
\[
[DAM]=[DBN].
\tag{3}
\]
Let $G,H$ be the perpendicular projections of $D$ onto the perpendicular bisectors $AM$ of $P'E$ and $BN$ of $P'C$, respectively. Equation (3) is equivalent to
\[
AM\cdot GD=BN\cdot HD.
\tag{4}
\]

Let $\Gamma$ be the circle with centre $D$ passing through $C$ and $E$, and put
\[
h=\operatorname{Pow}_{\Gamma}(P').
\]
We claim that
\[
GD\cdot P'M=HD\cdot P'N=\frac h4\ne0.
\tag{5}
\]
First, $h\ne0$. Indeed, if $P'\in\Gamma$, then $DA$ and $DB$, being perpendicular bisectors of the chords $EP'$ and $CP'$, would be the internal angle bisectors of $\angle EDP'$ and $\angle P'DC$, respectively. This would give
\[
\angle EDC=2\angle ADB,
\]
contrary to the hypothesis.

To prove the first equality in (5), let $E_1$ be the second intersection of $P'E$ with $\Gamma$, and let $E_2$ be the point antipodal to $E_1$ on $\Gamma$. Then $EE_2\perp P'E$. The point $G$ lies on the perpendicular bisectors of $P'E$ and $EE_2$, so it is the circumcentre of the right triangle $P'EE_2$, hence the midpoint of $P'E_2$. Since $D$ is the midpoint of $E_1E_2$,
\[
GD=\frac12P'E_1,
\qquad
P'M=\frac12P'E.
\]
Therefore
\[
GD\cdot P'M
=\frac14P'E_1\cdot P'E
=\frac h4.
\]
The equality involving $H,N,C$ follows identically.

Combining (4) and (5), and cancelling the nonzero quantity $h/4$, gives
\[
\frac{AM}{P'M}=\frac{BN}{P'N}.
\]
Since the two triangles in (1) are right triangles, this ratio equality is exactly their direct similarity. We have shown that the area condition and the collinearity condition are equivalent.

SOLUTION 2:
We use signed areas. On the perpendicular bisector of $CE$, define the affine-linear function
\[
f(X)=[BCX]+[AXE]-[ABX]-[ABP].
\tag{1}
\]
Because $CD=DE$, the point $D$ lies on this perpendicular bisector, and the area equality in the problem is precisely
\[
f(D)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
We prove that (2) holds exactly when $C,P,E$ are collinear.

Let $P'$ be the reflection of $P$ in $AB$. We first note that $P'$ does not lie on $CE$. Indeed, let $A'$ and $B'$ be the images of $A$ and $B$ under the homothety with centre $P'$ and ratio $2$. Since $A$ and $B$ lie on the perpendicular bisectors of $P'E$ and $P'C$, the triangles $A'EP'$ and $B'CP'$ are right at $E$ and $C$. If $P'$ lay on $CE$, then $A'E$ and $B'C$ would both be perpendicular to $CE$, and the position forced by convexity would make the line $A'B'$ avoid the triangle $CDE$. But $P$ is the orthogonal projection of $P'$ onto $A'B'$, so $P$ could not lie inside $CDE$. It would then lie inside the convex quadrilateral $ABCE$, on the same side of $AB$ as $CE$, whereas its reflection $P'$ lies on the opposite side of $AB$; thus $P'$ could not lie on $CE$. This contradiction proves the claim.

Let $O$ be the centre of the circle through $C,E,P'$. Since $AP'=AE$ and $BP'=BC$, the lines $AO$ and $BO$ are the perpendicular bisectors of $EP'$ and $CP'$. A signed-area calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
[BCO]+[AOE]
&=[OP'B]+[P'OA]\\
&=[P'BOA]\\
&=[ABO]+[BAP']\\
&=[ABO]+[ABP].
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
f(O)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
The points $O$ and $D$ are distinct. For if $O=D$, then the equalities $AP'=AE$ and $BP'=BC$ would place $A$ and $B$ on the two relevant chord bisectors of the circle centred at $D$, giving
\[
\angle EDC=2\angle ADB,
\]
which is excluded.

Since $f$ is affine-linear on the perpendicular bisector of $CE$ and vanishes at $O$, equation (2) holds if and only if $f$ is identically zero on that line.

Suppose first that $C,P,E$ are not collinear, and let $T$ be the centre of the circle through $C,E,P$. The same signed-area computation, now using $P$ instead of $P'$, yields
\[
f(T)=-2[ABP]\ne0.
\]
Thus $f$ is not identically zero, and so $f(D)\ne0$.

Conversely, suppose that $C,P,E$ are collinear. Let $Q$ be the reflection of $P$ in the midpoint of $AB$, so that $PAQB$ is a parallelogram. To see that $Q$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $CE$, take the line $CE$ as an axis and put $P=0$, $C=-cu$, $E=eu$, where $u$ is a unit vector and $c,e>0$. The equalities $AP=AE$ and $BP=BC$ give
\[
A\cdot u=\frac e2,
\qquad
B\cdot u=-\frac c2.
\]
Since $Q=A+B-P$, its coordinate along $u$ is $(e-c)/2$, the coordinate of the midpoint of $CE$. Hence $QC=QE$.

Also
\[
AQ=BP=BC,
\qquad
BQ=AP=AE.
\]
Thus triangles $BCQ$ and $AQE$ are oppositely congruent. Consequently,
\[
[BCQ]+[AQE]=0,
\]
while the parallelogram gives
\[
[ABQ]-[BAP]=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(Q)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The points $O$ and $Q$ are distinct. Indeed, consider the circle centred at $Q$ through $P'$. The triangle $PP'Q$ is right at $P'$, so $P$ lies outside this circle. But $P$ lies between $C$ and $E$; hence $C$ and $E$ cannot both lie on that circle. It is therefore not the circle $CEP'$, and $Q\ne O$.

Equations (3) and (4) give two distinct zeroes of the affine-linear function $f$, so $f$ is identically zero. In particular $f(D)=0$, completing the equivalence.
