ID: 0304

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Iterated Functions, Functional Equations, Orbits, Periodicity

STATEMENT:
Determine all functions
\[
f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z
\]
such that
\[
f^{\,a^2+b^2}(a+b)=af(a)+bf(b)
\tag{1}
\]
for all integers $a,b$. Here $f^m$ denotes the $m$-fold iterate of $f$, with
\[
f^0(x)=x
\]
and
\[
f^{m+1}(x)=f(f^m(x))
\qquad(m\ge0).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb Z}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+1\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb Z}.
\]

Denote equation (1) by $E(a,b)$. From $E(0,b)$,
\[
f^{b^2}(b)=bf(b).
\tag{2}
\]
Taking $b=-1$ gives
\[
f(-1)=0.
\tag{3}
\]

Using $E(a,-1)$ and then (2) with $b=a$, we obtain
\[
f^{a^2+1}(a-1)=af(a)=f^{a^2}(a).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the forward orbits of $a-1$ and $a$ have identical tails. Consequently every two integer orbits have identical tails, and either all orbits are finite or all are infinite.

\textbf{Case 1: all orbits are finite.}

Let
\[
\mathcal O(0)=\{0,f(0),f^2(0),\ldots\}.
\]
This is a finite set. From $E(a,-a)$,
\[
f^{2a^2}(0)=a\bigl(f(a)-f(-a)\bigr)\in\mathcal O(0).
\tag{5}
\]
For all sufficiently large $|a|$, the right side of (5), being a multiple of $a$ and lying in the fixed finite set $\mathcal O(0)$, must be zero. Hence
\[
f(a)=f(-a)
\quad\text{and}\quad
f^{2a^2}(0)=0
\tag{6}
\]
for all sufficiently large $|a|$.

Let $T$ be the least positive integer for which
\[
f^T(0)=0.
\]
For two sufficiently large consecutive integers $a,a+1$, equation (6) shows that $T$ divides both
\[
2a^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2(a+1)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
T\mid\gcd\bigl(2a^2,2(a+1)^2\bigr)=2,
\]
and hence
\[
f^2(0)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
Now (5) and (7) give
\[
f(a)=f(-a)
\qquad(a\ne0).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, by (3),
\[
f(1)=0.
\tag{9}
\]

Take $(a,b)=(n,1-n)$ in (1). Since
\[
a^2+b^2=n^2+(1-n)^2=2n^2-2n+1
\]
and the argument is $1$, equations (7) and (9) give
\[
nf(n)+(1-n)f(1-n)=0
\qquad(n\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{10}
\]

Suppose that $f(m)\ne0$ for some nonzero integer $m$, and choose such an $m$ with $|m|$ minimal. By (8), we may assume $m>0$. Equations (9) and (10) show that $m>1$ and
\[
f(1-m)\ne0.
\]
But
\[
|1-m|<m,
\]
contradicting the minimality of $m$. Thus
\[
f(m)=0
\qquad(m\ne0).
\tag{11}
\]

Finally, $E(0,2)$ gives
\[
f^4(2)=2f(2)=0.
\]
By (11), $f(2)=0$, so
\[
f^4(2)=f^3(0).
\]
Using (7),
\[
f^3(0)=f(0).
\]
Hence $f(0)=0$, and therefore $f$ is the zero function.

\textbf{Case 2: all orbits are infinite.}

For any integers $a,b$, the common tail property following from (4) ensures that there exist nonnegative integers $r,s$ such that
\[
f^r(a)=f^s(b).
\]
We claim that the difference $r-s$ depends only on $a,b$.

Indeed, suppose
\[
f^r(a)=f^s(b)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f^u(a)=f^v(b),
\]
with
\[
r-s>u-v.
\]
Applying suitable further iterates to these equalities gives, for every sufficiently large $k$,
\[
f^{s+u+k}(b)=f^{r+u+k}(a)=f^{r+v+k}(b).
\]
The two exponents on the orbit of $b$ differ by
\[
(r-s)-(u-v)>0.
\]
Thus the orbit of $b$ would be eventually periodic and hence finite, a contradiction.

Define
\[
X(a,b)=r-s
\]
whenever $f^r(a)=f^s(b)$. Then
\[
X(a,b)+X(b,c)=X(a,c).
\tag{12}
\]
Equation (4) gives
\[
X(a-1,a)=1.
\]
Using (12) repeatedly,
\[
X(a,b)=b-a
\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{13}
\]

Apply $f$ to both sides of (4). We get
\[
f^{a^2+1}(f(a-1))=f^{a^2}(f(a)).
\]
Therefore
\[
X(f(a-1),f(a))=1.
\]
By (13),
\[
f(a)-f(a-1)=1
\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Together with $f(-1)=0$, this yields
\[
f(x)=x+1
\qquad(x\in\mathbb Z).
\]

Both obtained functions are readily checked in (1). For $f(x)=x+1$,
\[
f^m(x)=x+m,
\]
so
\[
f^{a^2+b^2}(a+b)
=a+b+a^2+b^2
=a(a+1)+b(b+1).
\]
