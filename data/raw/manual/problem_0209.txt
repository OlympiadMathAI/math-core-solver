ID: 0209

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Finite Functions, Function Composition, Iteration, Stabilization of Images

STATEMENT:
Let $S$ be a finite set, and let $\mathcal A$ be the set of all functions from $S$ to $S$. Let
\[
f\in\mathcal A,
\qquad
T=f(S).
\]
Suppose that
\[
f\circ g\circ f\ne g\circ f\circ g
\]
for every $g\in\mathcal A$ with $g\ne f$. Prove that
\[
f(T)=T.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For a positive integer $m$, write $f^m$ for the $m$-fold composition of $f$ with itself.

We first prove the following claim.

\textbf{Claim.}
If there exists an integer $n\ge3$ such that
\[
f^{n+2}=f^{2n+1},
\tag{1}
\]
then the restriction
\[
f:T\to T
\]
is a bijection.

\textit{Proof.}
Set
\[
g=f^n.
\]
Equation (1) says
\[
f\circ g\circ f=g\circ f\circ g.
\]
By the hypothesis of the problem, this equality forces
\[
g=f,
\]
so
\[
f^n=f.
\tag{2}
\]

Since $n-2\ge1$, the map $f^{n-2}$ sends $T$ into $T$. For every $t\in T$, write $t=f(s)$. Then
\[
f^{n-1}(t)=f^n(s)=f(s)=t
\]
by (2). Hence, on $T$,
\[
f^{n-2}\circ f=f\circ f^{n-2}=\operatorname{id}_T.
\]
Thus $f:T\to T$ is bijective. $\square$

It remains to find an $n$ satisfying (1).

For $m\ge0$, let
\[
S_m=f^m(S).
\]
Then
\[
S=S_0\supseteq S_1\supseteq S_2\supseteq\cdots.
\]
Since $S$ is finite, this descending chain eventually stabilizes. Thus there is an integer $k\ge1$ and a set $S_\infty$ such that
\[
S_k=S_{k+1}=S_{k+2}=\cdots=S_\infty.
\]
The restriction
\[
f:S_\infty\to S_\infty
\]
is surjective, and hence bijective because $S_\infty$ is finite. Therefore it is a permutation of finite order: there exists $r\ge1$ such that
\[
f^r=\operatorname{id}
\quad\text{on }S_\infty.
\]
Consequently,
\[
f^{m+r}=f^m
\quad\text{as maps on }S
\qquad(m\ge k).
\tag{3}
\]

Choose
\[
n=2kr+1.
\]
Then
\[
n+2=2kr+3
\]
and
\[
2n+1=4kr+3.
\]
These exponents are both at least $k$ and differ by the multiple $2kr$ of $r$. Repeated use of (3) gives
\[
f^{n+2}=f^{2n+1}.
\]
The claim now implies that $f:T\to T$ is bijective. Therefore
\[
f(T)=T,
\]
as desired.
