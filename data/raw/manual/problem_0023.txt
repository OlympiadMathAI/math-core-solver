ID: 0023

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Products, Telescoping, Extremal Bounds

STATEMENT:
Find the least positive integer $n$ for which there exist $n$ distinct positive integers
\[
s_1,s_2,\ldots,s_n
\]
such that
\[
\left(1-\frac1{s_1}\right)
\left(1-\frac1{s_2}\right)
\cdots
\left(1-\frac1{s_n}\right)
=
\frac{51}{2010}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The least possible value is
\[
\boxed{39}.
\]

Suppose that such integers exist, and order them as
\[
s_1<s_2<\cdots<s_n.
\]
None of them equals $1$, because that would make the product zero. Since the $s_i$ are distinct positive integers greater than $1$,
\[
s_i\ge i+1
\qquad(i=1,\ldots,n).
\]
The function $x\mapsto1-\frac1x$ is increasing for $x>0$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{51}{2010}
&=
\prod_{i=1}^n\left(1-\frac1{s_i}\right)\\
&\ge
\prod_{i=1}^n\left(1-\frac1{i+1}\right)\\
&=
\frac12\cdot\frac23\cdots\frac{n}{n+1}\\
&=\frac1{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
n+1\ge\frac{2010}{51}=\frac{670}{17}>39,
\]
and therefore
\[
n\ge39.
\]

It remains to construct an example with $39$ integers. Take
\[
\{2,3,\ldots,33,35,36,\ldots,40,67\}.
\]
There are
\[
32+6+1=39
\]
numbers in this set, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\prod_{s=2}^{33}\left(1-\frac1s\right)
\prod_{s=35}^{40}\left(1-\frac1s\right)
\left(1-\frac1{67}\right)\\
&\qquad=
\frac12\cdot\frac23\cdots\frac{32}{33}
\cdot
\frac{34}{35}\cdot\frac{35}{36}\cdots\frac{39}{40}
\cdot\frac{66}{67}\\
&\qquad=
\frac1{33}\cdot\frac{34}{40}\cdot\frac{66}{67}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{17}{670}
=
\frac{51}{2010}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $n=39$ is attainable and is minimal.
