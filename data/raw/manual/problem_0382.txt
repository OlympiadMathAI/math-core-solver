ID: 0382

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangent-Chord Theorem, Similar Triangles, Projective Cross Ratios, Concurrency

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral. Points
\[
Q,A,B,P
\]
are collinear in this order. Assume that $AC$ is tangent to the circumcircle of triangle $ADQ$, and that $BD$ is tangent to the circumcircle of triangle $BCP$.

Let $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of $BC$ and $AD$, respectively.

Prove that the following three lines are concurrent:

\begin{itemize}
\item line $CD$;
\item the tangent at $A$ to the circumcircle of triangle $ANQ$;
\item the tangent at $B$ to the circumcircle of triangle $BMP$.
\end{itemize}

SOLUTION 1:
We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$.

First,
\[
\angle DAQ=\angle DCB
\]
because $ABCD$ is cyclic. Moreover, the tangency of $AC$ to $(ADQ)$ gives
\[
\angle AQD=\angle CAD=\angle CBD.
\]
Hence
\[
\triangle ADQ\sim\triangle CDB.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $R$ be the midpoint of $CD$. Under the similarity (1), the midpoint $N$ of $AD$ corresponds to the midpoint $R$ of $CD$. Therefore
\[
\angle QNA=\angle BRC.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $\Gamma=(ABR)$, and let $K$ be the second intersection of line $CD$ with $\Gamma$, counted with multiplicity. Thus, if $CD$ is tangent to $\Gamma$ at $R$, we set $K=R$.

Since $A,B,R,K$ are concyclic and $K,R,C,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle BAK=\angle BRK=\angle BRC.
\]
Together with (2), this gives
\[
\angle BAK=\angle QNA.
\]
Because $A,B,Q$ are collinear, the tangent--chord theorem shows that
\[
AK
\]
is tangent at $A$ to the circle $(ANQ)$.

We prove the analogous assertion at $B$. The tangency of $BD$ to $(BCP)$ and the cyclicity of $ABCD$ give
\[
\triangle PBC\sim\triangle ADC.
\]
The midpoint $M$ of $BC$ corresponds to the midpoint $R$ of $DC$, and hence
\[
\angle BMP=\angle ARD.
\tag{3}
\]
Since $A,B,R,K$ are concyclic and $R,K,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle ABK=\angle ARK=\angle ARD.
\]
By (3), and because $A,B,P$ are collinear, the tangent--chord theorem shows that
\[
BK
\]
is tangent at $B$ to the circle $(BMP)$.

Both tangents therefore meet line $CD$ at the same point $K$, proving the required concurrency.

SOLUTION 2:
We use directed projective cross ratios. This argument in fact does not require $ABCD$ itself to be cyclic; it uses only the two stated tangencies.

More generally, let $M\in BC$ and $N\in AD$ satisfy
\[
\frac{BM}{MC}=\frac{DN}{NA}.
\tag{1}
\]
The midpoint case of the problem is obtained when both ratios equal $1$.

Let
\[
T=AB\cap CD,
\]
where $T$ is understood as the common ideal point if $AB\parallel CD$.

Let the tangent at $A$ to $(ANQ)$ meet $CD$ at $K_1$, and let the tangent at $B$ to $(BMP)$ meet $CD$ at $K_2$.

Let $I$ and $J$ be the ideal points of the lines $AD$ and $BC$, respectively. The four-line pencils
\[
(AD,AC;AT,AK_1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(QA,QD;QI,QN)
\]
have equal corresponding directed angles:

\[
\angle K_1AD=\angle AQN
\]
by the tangent--chord theorem for $(ANQ)$,

\[
\angle CAD=\angle AQD
\]
by the tangency of $AC$ to $(ADQ)$, and

\[
\angle IAT=\angle IQT
\]
because $QI\parallel AD=AI$ and the lines $AT$ and $QT$ both coincide with $AB$.

Hence the two pencils have the same cross ratio. Projecting them onto $CD$ and $AD$, respectively, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(D,C;T,K_1)
&=(AD,AC;AT,AK_1)\\
&=(QA,QD;QI,QN)\\
&=(A,D;I,N)\\
&=\frac{DN}{NA}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Analogously, using the tangencies at $B$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(D,C;T,K_2)
&=(BD,BC;BT,BK_2)\\
&=(PC,PB;PJ,PM)\\
&=(C,B;J,M)\\
&=\frac{BM}{MC}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

By (1), the right-hand sides of (2) and (3) are equal. For fixed distinct points $C,D,T$, the cross ratio $(D,C;T,K)$ determines $K$ uniquely. Therefore
\[
K_1=K_2.
\]
Thus the two tangents and line $CD$ are concurrent.
