ID: 0314

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Combinatorial Games, Binary Digit Sums, Invariants, 2-adic Valuation

STATEMENT:
Players $A$ and $B$ play a game on a blackboard initially containing $2020$ copies of the number $1$. In every round, player $A$ erases two numbers $x$ and $y$, and then player $B$ writes either
\[
x+y
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
|x-y|.
\]
The game ends as soon as, after a round, either:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] one number on the board is larger than the sum of all the other numbers; or
\item[(2)] all numbers on the board are zero.
\end{enumerate}

At the end, $B$ gives $A$ as many cookies as there are numbers remaining on the board. Player $A$ tries to maximize this number, while player $B$ tries to minimize it. Determine the number of cookies received under optimal play.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{7}.
\]

We prove the more general statement that, if the initial board contains an even number $n>1$ of ones, then the value of the game is
\[
S_2(n),
\]
the number of $1$'s in the binary representation of $n$.

For
\[
n=2020=11111100100_2,
\]
we have
\[
S_2(2020)=7.
\]

\textbf{Strategy for player $A$.}

Whenever possible, $A$ chooses two equal nonzero numbers.

As long as the game has not ended, this move is always available. Indeed, by induction, every number appearing under this strategy is either $0$ or a power of $2$. If the nonzero numbers were all distinct, their largest member would be greater than the sum of all the smaller distinct powers of $2$, so the game would already have ended. If there were no nonzero numbers, it would also have ended.

Attach to every current board entry its \emph{range}: the number of original ones from which it was formed. The ranges of all current entries are disjoint and sum to $n$.

Under $A$'s strategy, induction gives:

\begin{itemize}
\item every nonzero entry $z$ has range exactly $z$;
\item every zero has a range that is a power of $2$.
\end{itemize}

Indeed, when two equal nonzero entries $z$ are combined, their ranges are both $z$. If $B$ writes $2z$, the new range is $2z$; if $B$ writes $0$, the new range is again $2z$, a power of $2$.

Thus, after every round, the ranges form a representation of $n$ as a sum of powers of $2$. Any such representation uses at least
\[
S_2(n)
\]
summands, because
\[
S_2(u+v)\le S_2(u)+S_2(v)
\]
for positive integers $u,v$. Hence the board always contains at least $S_2(n)$ numbers. Player $A$ can therefore guarantee at least $S_2(n)$ cookies.

\textbf{Strategy for player $B$.}

Put
\[
s=S_2(n).
\]
For a board position
\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_k,
\]
call a sign vector
\[
(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_k)\in\{1,-1\}^k
\]
\emph{balanced} if
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\varepsilon_i x_i=0.
\]
Call a position \emph{good} if the number of balanced sign vectors is not divisible by
\[
2^{s+1}.
\]

We need three facts.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
The initial position is good.

\textit{Proof.}
Initially, a balanced sign vector has exactly $n/2$ plus signs, so their number is
\[
\binom{n}{n/2}.
\]
Legendre's formula
\[
v_2(m!)=m-S_2(m)
\]
gives, because $n$ is even,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_2\binom{n}{n/2}
&=v_2(n!)-2v_2((n/2)!)\\
&=n-S_2(n)-2\left(\frac n2-S_2(n/2)\right)\\
&=S_2(n)=s.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the number is divisible by $2^s$ but not by $2^{s+1}$. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
After every move of $A$, player $B$ can choose an option that leaves a good position.

\textit{Proof.}
Suppose $A$ erases $x_p$ and $x_q$. Partition the balanced sign vectors of the old position into:

\begin{itemize}
\item $N_+$ vectors with $\varepsilon_p=\varepsilon_q$;
\item $N_-$ vectors with $\varepsilon_p=-\varepsilon_q$.
\end{itemize}

The number of balanced sign vectors after replacing the two entries by $x_p+x_q$ is exactly $N_+$. The number after replacing them by $|x_p-x_q|$ is exactly $N_-$. Since
\[
N_++N_-
\]
is not divisible by $2^{s+1}$, at least one of $N_+$ and $N_-$ is not divisible by $2^{s+1}$. Player $B$ chooses that option. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 3.}
If the game ends in a good position, at most $s$ numbers remain.

\textit{Proof.}
The game cannot end by condition (1), because then no balanced sign vector exists; their number would be $0$, which is divisible by $2^{s+1}$.

Hence all remaining numbers are zeros. If there are $k$ zeros, every one of the $2^k$ sign vectors is balanced. Goodness gives
\[
2^{s+1}\nmid2^k,
\]
so
\[
k\le s.
\]
$\square$

By Lemmas 1 and 2, $B$ can keep the position good throughout the game. Lemma 3 then shows that $B$ gives at most $s$ cookies.

Therefore the value is exactly
\[
S_2(n).
\]
For $n=2020$, this equals
\[
\boxed{7}.
\]
