ID: 0384

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Homothety, Spiral Similarity, Desargues' Theorem, Miquel Points, Circle Orthogonality

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle, and let $\ell_1,\ell_2$ be two parallel lines.

For $i=1,2$, let $\ell_i$ meet the lines $BC,CA,AB$ at $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$, respectively. The line through $X_i$ perpendicular to $BC$, the line through $Y_i$ perpendicular to $CA$, and the line through $Z_i$ perpendicular to $AB$ form a nondegenerate triangle $\Delta_i$.

Prove that the circumcircles of
\[
\Delta_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Delta_2
\]
are tangent to each other.

SOLUTION 1:
We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$. The argument is written for the generic position in which none of $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$ is a vertex of $ABC$; the exceptional cases follow by the same parallel-line relations, or equivalently by a limiting argument.

For $i=1,2$, denote the vertices of $\Delta_i$ by $D_i,E_i,F_i$ so that

\[
E_iF_i
\]
is the perpendicular through $X_i$ to $BC$,

\[
F_iD_i
\]
is the perpendicular through $Y_i$ to $CA$, and

\[
D_iE_i
\]
is the perpendicular through $Z_i$ to $AB$.

Let
\[
\omega_i=(D_iE_iF_i).
\]

The triangles
\[
D_1Y_1Z_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_2Y_2Z_2
\]
have pairwise parallel corresponding sides: $Y_1Z_1\parallel Y_2Z_2$, both $D_iY_i$ are perpendicular to $AC$, and both $D_iZ_i$ are perpendicular to $AB$. They are therefore homothetic. Since
\[
Y_1Y_2\cap Z_1Z_2=A,
\]
their homothety centre is $A$. Applying the same argument cyclically gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
A,D_1,D_2\ \text{are collinear},\\
B,E_1,E_2\ \text{are collinear},\\
C,F_1,F_2\ \text{are collinear}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

The corresponding sides of $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ are parallel. Thus the two triangles are either homothetic or translates. We show that they cannot be translates.

The quadrilaterals
\[
B,X_2,E_2,Z_2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C,X_2,F_2,Y_2
\]
are cyclic, because the relevant angles at $X_2,Z_2,Y_2$ are right angles. Using (1), the collinearity of $X_2,Y_2,Z_2$, and the perpendicular definitions, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(E_1E_2,F_1F_2)
&=
\measuredangle(BE_2,BX_2)
+
\measuredangle(CX_2,CF_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(Z_2E_2,Z_2X_2)
+
\measuredangle(Y_2X_2,Y_2F_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(Z_2E_2,\ell_2)
+
\measuredangle(\ell_2,Y_2F_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(Z_2E_2,Y_2F_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(AB,AC)\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Hence $E_1E_2$ and $F_1F_2$ are not parallel. Therefore $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ are homothetic. Let $H$ be their homothety centre. By (1),
\[
H\in D_1D_2\cap E_1E_2\cap F_1F_2.
\]

We now prove that
\[
H\in\omega_1\cap\omega_2.
\]
For each $i$, the quadrilateral
\[
A,Y_i,D_i,Z_i
\]
is cyclic, again because of two right angles. Therefore, using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(HE_i,HF_i)
&=
\measuredangle(E_1E_2,F_1F_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(AB,AC)\\
&=
\measuredangle(AZ_i,AY_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(D_iZ_i,D_iY_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(D_iE_i,D_iF_i).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
H,D_i,E_i,F_i
\]
are concyclic, so $H\in\omega_i$.

The homothety centred at $H$ that maps $\Delta_1$ to $\Delta_2$ also maps $\omega_1$ to $\omega_2$. Since $H$ belongs to both circles, their tangent lines at $H$ correspond to one another under a homothety centred at $H$ and therefore coincide. Hence
\[
\omega_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\omega_2
\]
are tangent at $H$.

SOLUTION 2:
We again write the proof in generic position; coincident-vertex cases follow by continuity.

For $i=1,2$, denote the vertices of $\Delta_i$ by $D_i,E_i,F_i$ so that
\[
E_iF_i\perp BC,\qquad
F_iD_i\perp CA,\qquad
D_iE_i\perp AB,
\]
with these three sides passing through $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$, respectively.

The triangles $D_1Y_1Z_1$ and $D_2Y_2Z_2$ have pairwise parallel corresponding sides and are homothetic about $A$. Thus $A,D_1,D_2$ are collinear. Applying the same argument at $B$ and $C$ gives
\[
A,D_1,D_2\ \text{collinear},\qquad
B,E_1,E_2\ \text{collinear},\qquad
C,F_1,F_2\ \text{collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]

For each $i$, the corresponding sides of triangles
\[
ABC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_iE_iF_i
\]
are perpendicular. Hence there is a direct spiral similarity with rotation angle $\pm90^\circ$ that sends
\[
A\mapsto D_i,\qquad
B\mapsto E_i,\qquad
C\mapsto F_i.
\]
Let $M_i$ be its centre. Then
\[
\angle AM_iD_i
=
\angle BM_iE_i
=
\angle CM_iF_i
=
90^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]

The circle with diameter $AD_i$ contains $M_i,Y_i,Z_i$, so
\[
M_i,A,Y_i,Z_i,D_i
\]
are concyclic. Similarly,
\[
M_i,B,X_i,Z_i,E_i
\]
and
\[
M_i,C,X_i,Y_i,F_i
\]
are cyclic.

These three circles show, by the Miquel theorem applied to the four lines
\[
AB,\ BC,\ CA,\ \ell_i,
\]
that
\[
M_i\in(ABC).
\]
Applying Miquel's theorem instead to the three sidelines of $\Delta_i$ together with $\ell_i$ gives
\[
M_i\in(D_iE_iF_i).
\tag{3}
\]

The intersections of corresponding sides of triangles $ABC$ and $D_iE_iF_i$ are
\[
Z_i,\qquad X_i,\qquad Y_i,
\]
which are collinear on $\ell_i$. By the converse of Desargues' theorem, the lines
\[
AD_i,\qquad BE_i,\qquad CF_i
\]
are concurrent. By (1), this concurrence point is the same for $i=1,2$; call it $H$.

We show that $H$ lies on both relevant circumcircles. First,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(CB,CH)
&=
\measuredangle(CX_i,CF_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(Y_iX_i,Y_iF_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(Y_iZ_i,Y_iD_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(AZ_i,AD_i)\\
&=
\measuredangle(AB,AH).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
A,B,C,H
\]
are concyclic.

Similarly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(F_iD_i,F_iH)
&=
\measuredangle(F_iY_i,F_iC)\\
&=
\measuredangle(X_iY_i,X_iC)\\
&=
\measuredangle(X_iZ_i,X_iB)\\
&=
\measuredangle(E_iZ_i,E_iB)\\
&=
\measuredangle(E_iD_i,E_iH),
\end{aligned}
\]
so
\[
D_i,E_i,F_i,H\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{4}
\]

By (3), the spiral similarity centred at $M_i$ maps two circles that both pass through $M_i$. Its rotation angle is $\pm90^\circ$, so the circles
\[
(ABC)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(D_iE_iF_i)
\]
meet orthogonally. At their other common point $H$, the tangent to $(D_iE_iF_i)$ is therefore the radius through $H$ of the fixed circle $(ABC)$.

This same radius is tangent at $H$ to both
\[
(D_1E_1F_1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(D_2E_2F_2).
\]
Hence these two circles are tangent to each other at $H$.
