ID: 0202

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Divisibility, Exponential Diophantine Equations, Cyclotomic Divisibility, Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Let $n,m,k,\ell$ be positive integers with $n\ne1$, and suppose that
\[
n^k+mn^\ell+1\mid n^{k+\ell}-1.
\]
Prove that one of the following holds:

\begin{itemize}
\item $m=1$ and $\ell=2k$;
\item $\ell\mid k$ and
\[
m=\frac{n^{k-\ell}-1}{n^\ell-1}.
\]
\end{itemize}

SOLUTION 1:
Set
\[
D=n^k+mn^\ell+1.
\]
The hypothesis is
\[
D\mid n^{k+\ell}-1.
\tag{1}
\]
Adding $D$ to the number on the right gives
\[
D\mid n^{k+\ell}+n^k+mn^\ell.
\tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
\gcd(D,n)=1.
\]

\textbf{Case 1: $\ell\ge k$.}

Factoring $n^k$ from the number in (2) and cancelling it modulo $D$, we get
\[
D\mid n^\ell+mn^{\ell-k}+1.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
n^k+mn^\ell+1
\le
n^\ell+mn^{\ell-k}+1.
\tag{4}
\]

If $m\ge2$, then
\[
(m-1)n^\ell
\ge2(m-1)n^{\ell-k}
\ge mn^{\ell-k}.
\]
After adding the positive term $n^k$, this contradicts (4). Hence
\[
m=1.
\]

Now (3) becomes
\[
n^k+n^\ell+1\mid n^\ell+n^{\ell-k}+1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
0<n^\ell+n^{\ell-k}+1
<2(n^k+n^\ell+1).
\]
Therefore the two positive numbers must be equal:
\[
n^\ell+n^{\ell-k}+1=n^k+n^\ell+1.
\]
Thus
\[
n^{\ell-k}=n^k,
\]
and hence
\[
\ell=2k.
\]

\textbf{Case 2: $\ell<k$.}

Factoring $n^\ell$ from (2) gives
\[
D\mid n^k+n^{k-\ell}+m.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence
\[
n^k+mn^\ell+1
\le
n^k+n^{k-\ell}+m,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
m\le
\frac{n^{k-\ell}-1}{n^\ell-1}.
\tag{6}
\]

Write
\[
n^{k+\ell}-1=Dt
\]
with $t$ a positive integer. Since $D>n^k$,
\[
t<n^\ell,
\]
so
\[
t\le n^\ell-1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
n^{k+\ell}-1
\le
(n^k+mn^\ell+1)(n^\ell-1).
\]
After simplification, this gives
\[
m\ge
\frac{n^{k-\ell}-1}{n^\ell-1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7),
\[
m=
\frac{n^{k-\ell}-1}{n^\ell-1}.
\]

Because this quotient is an integer,
\[
n^\ell-1\mid n^{k-\ell}-1.
\]
For $n>1$, the standard divisibility criterion
\[
n^u-1\mid n^v-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u\mid v
\]
gives
\[
\ell\mid k-\ell,
\]
and hence
\[
\ell\mid k.
\]

This proves the stated alternatives.

SOLUTION 2:
Again put
\[
D=n^k+mn^\ell+1.
\]
As in Solution 1,
\[
D\mid n^{k+\ell}+n^k+mn^\ell.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Case 1: $\ell\ge k$.}

Cancelling the factor $n^k$, which is coprime to $D$, gives
\[
D\mid R,
\qquad
R=n^\ell+mn^{\ell-k}+1.
\]
We have
\[
2D>2mn^\ell+1>R.
\]
Thus the positive multiple $R$ of $D$ must equal $D$. Hence
\[
n^k+mn^\ell+1
=
n^\ell+mn^{\ell-k}+1,
\]
or
\[
m(n^\ell-n^{\ell-k})=n^\ell-n^k.
\tag{2}
\]

If $m\ge2$, then
\[
m(n^\ell-n^{\ell-k})
\ge
2n^\ell-2n^{\ell-k}
\ge
2n^\ell-n^\ell
>
n^\ell-n^k,
\]
contradicting (2). Therefore $m=1$, and (2) yields
\[
\ell-k=k.
\]
Thus
\[
m=1,\qquad \ell=2k.
\]

\textbf{Case 2: $\ell<k$.}

Cancelling $n^\ell$ in (1) gives
\[
D\mid R,
\qquad
R=n^k+n^{k-\ell}+m.
\]
Also,
\[
2D>2n^k+m>R.
\]
Therefore $R=D$, so
\[
n^k+mn^\ell+1=n^k+n^{k-\ell}+m.
\]
This gives
\[
m=
\frac{n^{k-\ell}-1}{n^\ell-1}.
\]
The integrality of this quotient implies
\[
n^\ell-1\mid n^{k-\ell}-1,
\]
and hence
\[
\ell\mid k-\ell.
\]
Therefore $\ell\mid k$, completing the proof.
