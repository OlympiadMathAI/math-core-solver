ID: 0132

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Ceva's Theorem, Inradii, Homothety, Geometric Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle. The points $K,L,M$ lie on the segments $BC,CA,AB$, respectively, and the lines $AK,BL,CM$ are concurrent. Prove that two of the triangles
\[
ALM,\qquad BMK,\qquad CKL
\]
can be chosen so that the sum of their inradii is at least the inradius of triangle $ABC$.

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
a=\frac{BK}{KC},
\qquad
b=\frac{CL}{LA},
\qquad
c=\frac{AM}{MB}.
\]
By Ceva's theorem,
\[
abc=1.
\tag{1}
\]
After a cyclic relabelling, we may assume that $a\ge1$. At least one of $b,c$ is then at most $1$. If $b\le1$, the pair $(a,b)$ has first entry at least $1$ and second entry at most $1$. If $b>1$, then $c<1$, and the pair $(b,c)$ has the same property. Thus, after another cyclic relabelling if necessary, we may assume
\[
a\ge1,
\qquad
b\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
From (1) and (2),
\[
bc\le1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
ca\ge1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{AM}{MB}\le\frac{LA}{CL},
\qquad
\frac{MB}{AM}\le\frac{BK}{KC}.
\tag{3}
\]

Draw through $M$ the line parallel to $BC$, and let it meet $AC$ at $X$. In triangle $ABC$,
\[
\frac{AX}{XC}=\frac{AM}{MB}.
\]
The first inequality in (3) therefore implies that $X$ lies on the segment $AL$. Consequently,
\[
\triangle AMX\subseteq\triangle ALM.
\]
If $r_1$ is the inradius of triangle $AMX$, then the inradius of $ALM$ is at least $r_1$.

Similarly, draw through $M$ the line parallel to $AC$, meeting $BC$ at $Y$. We have
\[
\frac{BY}{YC}=\frac{BM}{MA}.
\]
The second inequality in (3) shows that $Y$ lies on the segment $BK$. Hence
\[
\triangle BMY\subseteq\triangle BMK.
\]
If $r_2$ is the inradius of triangle $BMY$, then the inradius of $BMK$ is at least $r_2$.

Let $r$ be the inradius of $ABC$. The homothety with centre $A$ that sends $M$ to $B$ also sends $X$ to $C$, because $MX\parallel BC$. Therefore it sends triangle $AMX$ to triangle $ABC$, and
\[
\frac{r_1}{r}=\frac{AM}{AB}.
\tag{4}
\]
Likewise, the homothety with centre $B$ that sends $M$ to $A$ sends $Y$ to $C$, so
\[
\frac{r_2}{r}=\frac{BM}{AB}.
\tag{5}
\]
Adding (4) and (5),
\[
r_1+r_2
=r\frac{AM+BM}{AB}
=r.
\]
Thus the inradii of $ALM$ and $BMK$ have sum at least $r_1+r_2=r$. These two triangles satisfy the requirement.
