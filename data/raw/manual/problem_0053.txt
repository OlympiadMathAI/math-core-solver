ID: 0053

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Divisibility, Polynomial Powers

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge1$ be an odd integer. Determine all functions
\[
f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z
\]
such that, for all integers $x,y$,
\[
f(x)-f(y)\mid x^n-y^n.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=\varepsilon x^d+c},
\]
where
\[
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},\qquad d\mid n,\qquad d\ge1,
\qquad c\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Every listed function works. Indeed, because $d\mid n$,
\[
x^d-y^d\mid x^n-y^n,
\]
and neither multiplication by $\varepsilon$ nor addition of the constant $c$ changes the required divisibility.

We now prove that there are no other solutions. Replacing $f(x)$ by
\[
f(x)-f(0)
\]
does not change any difference $f(x)-f(y)$, so we may assume
\[
f(0)=0.
\tag{1}
\]

For every prime $p$, the condition with $(x,y)=(p,0)$ gives
\[
f(p)\mid p^n.
\]
In particular, $f(p)\ne0$, and hence
\[
f(p)=\varepsilon_p p^{d_p}
\]
for some
\[
\varepsilon_p\in\{-1,1\},
\qquad
0\le d_p\le n.
\]
There are only finitely many possibilities for the pair $(\varepsilon_p,d_p)$. Therefore there exist
\[
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},
\qquad
0\le d\le n,
\]
and an infinite set $\mathcal P$ of primes such that
\[
f(p)=\varepsilon p^d
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P).
\tag{2}
\]

If $f$ satisfies the condition, then so does $-f$. Thus, after replacing $f$ by $-f$ if necessary, we may assume
\[
f(p)=p^d
\qquad(p\in\mathcal P).
\tag{3}
\]

The case $d=0$ is impossible. Indeed, then $f(p)=1$ for every $p\in\mathcal P$. For two distinct primes $p,q\in\mathcal P$, the number
\[
f(p)-f(q)=0
\]
would have to divide the nonzero integer $p^n-q^n$, a contradiction. Hence
\[
d\ge1.
\tag{4}
\]

Write
\[
n=md+r,
\qquad
m\ge1,
\qquad
0\le r<d.
\tag{5}
\]
Fix an arbitrary integer $x$. For every $p\in\mathcal P$, the condition gives
\[
p^d-f(x)=f(p)-f(x)\mid p^n-x^n.
\]
Modulo $p^d-f(x)$, we have $p^d\equiv f(x)$, and therefore
\[
p^n-x^n
=p^r(p^d)^m-x^n
\equiv p^r f(x)^m-x^n
\pmod{p^d-f(x)}.
\]
Thus
\[
p^d-f(x)\mid p^r f(x)^m-x^n.
\tag{6}
\]

Because $r<d$, for all sufficiently large $p\in\mathcal P$,
\[
\left|p^r f(x)^m-x^n\right|
<
\left|p^d-f(x)\right|.
\]
Together with (6), this forces
\[
p^r f(x)^m=x^n
\tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large $p\in\mathcal P$.

Taking $x=1$ in (7), we see that $r$ cannot be positive, since the left-hand side would then vary without bound as $p\to\infty$. Hence
\[
r=0,
\]
so
\[
d\mid n.
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (7) now becomes, for every integer $x$,
\[
f(x)^m=x^n=(x^d)^m.
\tag{9}
\]
Since $n$ is odd and $n=md$, the integer $m$ is odd. Therefore the map $t\mapsto t^m$ is injective on $\mathbb Z$, and (9) gives
\[
f(x)=x^d
\qquad(x\in\mathbb Z).
\]

Finally, restoring the possible sign and the subtracted constant from (1) and (3), we obtain exactly
\[
f(x)=\varepsilon x^d+c,
\]
with $\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$, $d\mid n$, and $c\in\mathbb Z$.
