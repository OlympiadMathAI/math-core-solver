ID: 0396

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Rounding, Fractional Parts, Optimization, Probabilistic Method

STATEMENT:
Professor Oak is feeding his $100$ Pokemon. Each Pokemon has a bowl whose capacity is a positive real number of kilograms. The total capacity of all bowls is $100$ kilograms.

Professor Oak distributes $100$ kilograms of food so that each Pokemon receives a nonnegative integer number of kilograms. The dissatisfaction of a Pokemon receiving $N$ kilograms whose bowl has capacity $C$ kilograms is
\[
|N-C|.
\]

Find the smallest real number $D$ such that, for every choice of bowl capacities, Professor Oak can distribute the food so that the total dissatisfaction is at most $D$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{D=50}.
\]

For the lower bound, take $99$ bowl capacities equal to $1/2$ and the remaining capacity equal to $101/2$. Every integer differs from each of these half-integers by at least $1/2$. Hence every distribution has total dissatisfaction at least
\[
100\cdot\frac12=50.
\]
Thus
\[
D\ge50.
\]

We now prove the matching upper bound. Let the capacities be
\[
C_1,C_2,\ldots,C_{100},
\qquad
C_1+\cdots+C_{100}=100,
\]
and write
\[
C_i=\lfloor C_i\rfloor+F_i,
\qquad
0\le F_i<1.
\]
After relabelling, assume
\[
F_1\le F_2\le\cdots\le F_{100}.
\]

Set
\[
R=100-\sum_{i=1}^{100}\lfloor C_i\rfloor
=\sum_{i=1}^{100}F_i.
\]
The number $R$ is an integer. Give $\lfloor C_i\rfloor$ kilograms to every Pokemon, and give one additional kilogram to the $R$ Pokemon with the largest fractional parts.

The resulting total dissatisfaction is
\[
\begin{aligned}
d
&=
F_1+\cdots+F_{100-R}
+(1-F_{100-R+1})+\cdots+(1-F_{100})\\
&=
2(F_1+\cdots+F_{100-R}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Because the first $100-R$ fractional parts have average at most the average of all $100$ fractional parts,
\[
F_1+\cdots+F_{100-R}
\le
\frac{100-R}{100}(F_1+\cdots+F_{100})
=
\frac{R(100-R)}{100}.
\]
Together with (1),
\[
d\le\frac{2R(100-R)}{100}\le50.
\]
Therefore $D\le50$, and hence
\[
\boxed{D=50}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again,
\[
\boxed{D=50}.
\]

The example with $99$ capacities equal to $1/2$ and one equal to $101/2$ proves
\[
D\ge50.
\]

For the upper bound, write
\[
C_i=\lfloor C_i\rfloor+F_i,
\qquad
R=\sum_{i=1}^{100}F_i.
\]
As
\[
R=100-\sum_{i=1}^{100}\lfloor C_i\rfloor,
\]
the number $R$ is an integer.

Choose uniformly at random an $R$-element subset of the $100$ Pokemon. Give one extra kilogram, in addition to $\lfloor C_i\rfloor$, precisely to the chosen Pokemon.

For each fixed $i$, the probability of receiving the extra kilogram is $R/100$. Thus its expected dissatisfaction is
\[
\frac{100-R}{100}F_i+\frac{R}{100}(1-F_i).
\]
The expected total dissatisfaction is therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{100}
\left(
\frac{100-R}{100}F_i+\frac{R}{100}(1-F_i)
\right)
&=
\frac{100-R}{100}R+\frac{R}{100}(100-R)\\
&=
\frac{2R(100-R)}{100}\\
&\le50.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence at least one of the possible distributions has total dissatisfaction at most $50$. This proves the upper bound and completes the solution.
