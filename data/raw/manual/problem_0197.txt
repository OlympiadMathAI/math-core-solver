ID: 0197

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incenter and Excenter Geometry, Inversion, Poncelet Porism, Euler's Formula, Tangential Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $I$ be the incentre of a non-equilateral triangle $ABC$. Let $I_A$ be the $A$-excentre, let $I_A'$ be the reflection of $I_A$ in $BC$, and let $\ell_A$ be the reflection of the line $AI_A'$ in $AI$. Define $I_B,I_B'$, and $\ell_B$ analogously. Let
\[
P=\ell_A\cap\ell_B.
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that $P$ lies on the line $OI$, where $O$ is the circumcentre of $\triangle ABC$.
\item[(b)] One of the tangents from $P$ to the incircle meets the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ at $X$ and $Y$. Prove that
\[
\angle XIY=120^\circ.
\]
\end{enumerate}

SOLUTION 1:
\textbf{Part (a).}
Let $A'$ be the reflection of $A$ in $BC$, and let $M$ be the second intersection of $AI$ with the circumcircle $\Gamma$ of $\triangle ABC$.

The isosceles triangles $ABA'$ and $AOC$ satisfy
\[
\angle ABA'=2\angle ABC=\angle AOC,
\]
so they are similar. Also,
\[
\triangle ABI_A\sim\triangle AIC.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{AA'}{AI_A}
=
\frac{AA'}{AB}\cdot\frac{AB}{AI_A}
=
\frac{AC}{AO}\cdot\frac{AI}{AC}
=
\frac{AI}{AO}.
\]
Together with
\[
\angle A'AI_A=\angle IAO,
\]
this gives
\[
\triangle AA'I_A\sim\triangle AIO.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
P'=AP\cap OI.
\]
Because $AP=\ell_A$ is the reflection of $AI_A'$ in $AI$, directed angles and (1) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle MAP'
&=\angle I_A'AI_A\\
&=\angle I_A'AA'-\angle I_AAA'\\
&=\angle AA'I_A-\angle(AM,OM)\\
&=\angle AIO-\angle AMO\\
&=\angle MOP'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
M,O,A,P'
\]
are concyclic.

Let $R$ and $r$ be the circumradius and inradius of $\triangle ABC$. Using a directed coordinate on the line $OI$,
\[
\overline{IP'}
=\frac{IA\cdot IM}{IO}
=\frac{IO^2-R^2}{IO}.
\tag{2}
\]
The right-hand side is independent of the chosen vertex $A$. Repeating the same argument with $B$ shows that the line $BP$ meets $OI$ at the same point $P'$. Therefore
\[
P=P'\in OI.
\]

\textbf{Part (b).}
By Poncelet's porism, the other tangents from $X$ and $Y$ to the incircle meet at a point
\[
Z\in\Gamma.
\]
Thus the triangle $XYZ$ is circumscribed about the incircle and has incentre $I$.

Let $T$ be the point where $XY$ touches the incircle, and let $D$ be the midpoint of $XY$. Taking ordinary positive lengths in part (b), equation (2) gives
\[
OI\cdot IP=R^2-OI^2=2Rr,
\tag{3}
\]
where we used Euler's formula
\[
OI^2=R^2-2Rr.
\]
The right triangles $POD$ and $PIT$ are similar, because $OD\perp XY$, $IT\perp XY$, and $O,I,P$ are collinear. In the configuration the order on the line is $P,I,O$, so, using ordinary lengths,
\[
OP=OI+IP.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
OD
&=IT\cdot\frac{OP}{IP}\\
&=r\left(1+\frac{OI}{IP}\right)\\
&=r\left(1+\frac{OI^2}{OI\cdot IP}\right)\\
&=r\left(1+\frac{R^2-2Rr}{2Rr}\right)\\
&=\frac R2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $OX=R$ and $D$ is the midpoint of the chord $XY$,
\[
\angle XZY=60^\circ.
\]
The point $I$ is the incentre of $\triangle XYZ$, so
\[
\angle XIY
=90^\circ+\frac12\angle XZY
=120^\circ.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
\textbf{Part (a).}
The triangles $\triangle AI_BC$ and $\triangle I_ABC$ are similar because their corresponding angles are equal. After reflecting the excentres in the opposite sides, the four triangles
\[
\triangle AI_B'C,\qquad
\triangle AI_BC,\qquad
\triangle I_ABC,\qquad
\triangle I_A'BC
\]
are all similar.

In particular,
\[
\triangle AI_B'C\sim\triangle I_A'BC,
\]
which yields
\[
\triangle AI_A'C\sim\triangle I_B'BC.
\]
By the definitions of $\ell_A$ and $\ell_B$,
\[
\angle ABP=\angle I_B'BC=\angle AI_A'C
\]
and
\[
\angle BAP=\angle I_A'AC.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle ABP\sim\triangle AI_A'C.
\]
In particular,
\[
AP\cdot AI_A'=AB\cdot AC.
\tag{1}
\]

Consider the composition of the inversion centred at $A$ with radius
\[
\sqrt{AB\cdot AC}
\]
and the reflection in $AI$. Under this transformation,
\[
B\leftrightarrow C,
\qquad
I\leftrightarrow I_A,
\qquad
P\leftrightarrow I_A'
\]
by (1).

The line $AO$ is mapped to the altitude from $A$. Hence $O$ is mapped to the reflection $A'$ of $A$ in $BC$. The quadrilateral
\[
AA'I_AI_A'
\]
is an isosceles trapezoid and therefore cyclic. Its inverse image is a straight line, and this line contains the inverse images
\[
O,\ I,\ P.
\]
Thus
\[
O,I,P
\]
are collinear.

\textbf{Part (b).}
Let $R$ and $r$ again denote the circumradius and inradius. The transformation above gives
\[
\triangle APO\sim\triangle AA'I_A'
\]
and
\[
\triangle AA'I_A\sim\triangle AIO.
\]
Therefore
\[
PO
=A'I_A'\cdot\frac{AO}{AI_A'}
=AI_A\cdot\frac{AO}{A'I_A}
=\frac{AO}{IO}\cdot AO.
\]
Hence
\[
PO\cdot IO=R^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus $P$ and $I$ are inverse points with respect to $\Gamma$.

Since
\[
PX\cdot PY
\]
is the power of $P$ with respect to $\Gamma$, equation (2) gives
\[
PX\cdot PY=PI\cdot PO.
\]
Therefore
\[
X,I,O,Y
\]
are concyclic.

Let $T$ be the point where $XY$ touches the incircle, and let $D$ be the midpoint of $XY$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
OD
&=IT\cdot\frac{PO}{PI}\\
&=r\cdot\frac{PO}{PO-IO}\\
&=r\cdot\frac{R^2}{R^2-IO^2}\\
&=r\cdot\frac{R^2}{2Rr}\\
&=\frac R2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle DOX=60^\circ,
\]
and hence
\[
\angle XOY=120^\circ.
\]
Since $X,I,O,Y$ are concyclic,
\[
\angle XIY=\angle XOY=120^\circ.
\]
