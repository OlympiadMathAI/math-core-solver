ID: 0207

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, AM-GM, Bernoulli Inequality, Elementary Symmetric Polynomials

STATEMENT:
Let $a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n,k$, and $M$ be positive integers such that
\[
\frac1{a_1}+\frac1{a_2}+\cdots+\frac1{a_n}=k
\]
and
\[
a_1a_2\cdots a_n=M.
\]
If $M>1$, prove that the polynomial
\[
P(x)=M(x+1)^k-(x+a_1)(x+a_2)\cdots(x+a_n)
\]
has no positive roots.

SOLUTION 1:
We prove that
\[
P(x)<0
\qquad(x>0).
\]

For every $i$ and every $x>0$,
\[
a_i(x+1)^{1/a_i}\le x+a_i,
\tag{1}
\]
with equality if and only if $a_i=1$.

Indeed, when $a_i=1$, equality is immediate. When $a_i>1$, apply AM-GM to one copy of $x+1$ and $a_i-1$ copies of $1$:
\[
\frac{(x+1)+(a_i-1)}{a_i}
\ge
\bigl((x+1)\cdot1^{a_i-1}\bigr)^{1/a_i}.
\]
Multiplying by $a_i$ gives (1), and the inequality is strict because $x+1>1$.

Multiplying (1) for $i=1,2,\ldots,n$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\prod_{i=1}^n(x+a_i)
&\ge
\prod_{i=1}^n a_i(x+1)^{1/a_i}\\
&=
M(x+1)^{\sum_{i=1}^n1/a_i}\\
&=
M(x+1)^k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $M>1$, at least one of the integers $a_i$ is greater than $1$, so at least one factor inequality is strict. Therefore
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n(x+a_i)>M(x+1)^k
\]
for every $x>0$. Hence $P(x)<0$, and $P$ has no positive roots.

SOLUTION 2:
We prove that every coefficient of $P$ is nonpositive and that at least one coefficient is negative.

Put
\[
c_i=\frac1{a_i}
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Then
\[
0<c_i\le1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c_1+\cdots+c_n=k.
\]
Let $e_r=e_r(c_1,\ldots,c_n)$ be the $r$th elementary symmetric sum, with $e_0=1$.

We claim that
\[
e_r\ge\binom kr
\qquad(0\le r\le k).
\tag{1}
\]
For $r=0$ this is clear. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(r+1)e_{r+1}
&=
\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|J|=r}}
\left(\prod_{j\in J}c_j\right)
\left(\sum_{i\notin J}c_i\right)\\
&=
\sum_{|J|=r}
\left(\prod_{j\in J}c_j\right)
\left(k-\sum_{j\in J}c_j\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since every $c_j\le1$,
\[
k-\sum_{j\in J}c_j\ge k-r.
\]
Thus
\[
(r+1)e_{r+1}\ge(k-r)e_r.
\]
Induction now gives (1).

Because $M>1$, not all $a_i$ are $1$, and hence
\[
k=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{a_i}<n.
\tag{2}
\]
For $0\le r\le k$, the coefficient of $x^r$ in the product
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n(x+a_i)
\]
is
\[
\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_{n-r}\le n}
a_{i_1}\cdots a_{i_{n-r}}
=
M e_r.
\]
Therefore the coefficient of $x^r$ in $P$ is
\[
M\binom kr-Me_r\le0
\]
by (1).

For $k<r\le n$, the term $M(x+1)^k$ contributes nothing to the coefficient of $x^r$, whereas the product contributes a positive coefficient. Hence the coefficient of $x^r$ in $P$ is negative. In particular, by (2), the leading coefficient is $-1$.

Thus all coefficients of $P$ are nonpositive and at least one is negative. Consequently
\[
P(x)<0
\qquad(x>0),
\]
so $P$ has no positive roots.
