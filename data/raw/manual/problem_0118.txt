ID: 0118

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Greedy Algorithms, Partial Sums, Extremal Inequalities

STATEMENT:
For a sequence $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n$ of real numbers, define its price to be
\[
\max_{1\le i\le n}|x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_i|.
\]

Given $n$ real numbers, Dave and George arrange them into sequences. Dave checks every possible ordering and obtains the minimum possible price $D$. George uses the following greedy procedure: at step $i$, among the unused numbers he chooses $x_i$ so that
\[
|x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_i|
\]
is as small as possible. If several choices give the same value, he may choose any of them. Let $G$ be the price of the sequence George obtains.

Find the least constant $c$ such that
\[
G\le cD
\]
for every positive integer $n$, every collection of $n$ real numbers, and every sequence that George's rule may produce.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{c=2}.
\]

First, the constant cannot be smaller than $2$. Consider the numbers
\[
1,-1,2,-2.
\]
Dave can use the ordering
\[
1,-2,2,-1,
\]
whose partial sums are $1,-1,1,0$, so $D\le1$. Conversely, in any ordering the two partial sums immediately before and after the term whose value is $2$ or $-2$ differ by $2$ in absolute value. Hence at least one of them has absolute value at least $1$, so $D=1$. George may choose
\[
1,-1,2,-2:
\]
at the first step $1$ is a permissible minimum-absolute-value choice, at the second step $-1$ makes the partial sum $0$, and at the third step either $2$ or $-2$ is permissible. This ordering has price $G=2$. Hence
\[
c\ge2.
\]

We now prove that $G\le2D$ always. Let the original numbers be $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, and define
\[
M=\max_{1\le i\le n}|x_i|,
\qquad
S=\left|x_1+\cdots+x_n\right|,
\qquad
N=\max\{M,S\}.
\]
We establish
\[
D\ge S,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
D\ge\frac M2,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
G\le N.
\tag{3}
\]

Inequality (1) is immediate because the last partial sum in every ordering has absolute value $S$.

For (2), let $d_1,\ldots,d_n$ be an ordering of price $D$, and choose $i$ with $|d_i|=M$. If
\[
p_j=d_1+\cdots+d_j
\]
and $p_0=0$, then
\[
M=|d_i|=|p_i-p_{i-1}|
\le |p_i|+|p_{i-1}|
\le2D.
\]

It remains to prove (3). Let George's ordering be $g_1,\ldots,g_n$, and put
\[
h_i=g_1+\cdots+g_i,
\qquad h_0=0.
\]
We prove by induction that
\[
|h_i|\le N
\]
for every $i$. The assertion is clear for $i=0$, and
\[
|h_n|=S\le N.
\]

Assume $|h_{i-1}|\le N$. If all unused numbers $g_i,\ldots,g_n$ have the same sign, then the remaining partial sums move monotonically from $h_{i-1}$ to $h_n$. Hence $h_i$ lies between these two numbers and
\[
|h_i|\le\max\{|h_{i-1}|,|h_n|\}\le N.
\]

Otherwise, among the unused numbers there is some $g_j$ whose sign is opposite to that of $h_{i-1}$; if $h_{i-1}=0$, choose any unused number. Then
\[
|h_{i-1}+g_j|
\le\max\{|h_{i-1}|,|g_j|\}
\le N.
\]
By George's greedy choice,
\[
|h_i|=|h_{i-1}+g_i|
\le |h_{i-1}+g_j|
\le N.
\]
This completes the induction and proves (3).

Finally,
\[
G\le N=\max\{M,S\}
\le\max\{2D,D\}=2D.
\]
Together with the example, this proves that the least possible constant is $2$.
