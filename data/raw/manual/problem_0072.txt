ID: 0072

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Graph Theory, Independent Sets, Caro-Wei Bound, Double Counting, Harmonic Series

STATEMENT:
There are $2^{500}$ points on a circle, labeled
\[
1,2,\ldots,2^{500}
\]
in some order. Prove that one can choose $100$ pairwise disjoint chords joining some of these points such that the sums of the labels at the endpoints of all $100$ chords are equal.

SOLUTION 1:
We begin with an independent-set lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $G$ be a finite graph, and let $d_v$ be the degree of a vertex $v$. Then $G$ contains an independent set of size at least
\[
F(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d_v+1}.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Proof of the lemma.}
We use induction on $|V(G)|$. Choose a vertex $v_0$ of minimum degree $d$, delete $v_0$ and all its neighbors $v_1,\ldots,v_d$, and call the remaining graph $G'$. By induction, $G'$ has an independent set $S'$ with
\[
|S'|\ge F(G').
\]
Then
\[
S=S'\cup\{v_0\}
\]
is independent in $G$.

For every vertex remaining in $G'$, its new degree is at most its old degree. Also every deleted vertex has degree at least $d$. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(G')
&\ge F(G)-\sum_{i=0}^d\frac{1}{d_{v_i}+1}\\
&\ge F(G)-\frac{d+1}{d+1}\\
&=F(G)-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
|S|=|S'|+1\ge F(G')+1\ge F(G),
\]
proving the lemma.

Now set
\[
n=2^{499},
\]
so there are $2n$ labeled points. Draw every chord determined by two of the points, and color a chord by the sum of the labels at its endpoints. The possible colors are
\[
3,4,\ldots,4n-1,
\]
so there are $4n-3$ colors. Two chords of the same color cannot share an endpoint, because their other endpoint labels would then have to be equal.

For each color $c$, form a graph $G_c$ whose vertices are the chords of color $c$, with two vertices adjacent when the corresponding chords intersect.

For a chord $\ell$, let $m(\ell)$ be the smaller of the numbers of given points lying in the two open arcs cut off by $\ell$. Thus
\[
m(\ell)\le n-1.
\]
For each
\[
i=0,1,\ldots,n-2,
\]
there are exactly $2n$ chords with $m(\ell)=i$: from each starting point, move clockwise and join it to the point having exactly $i$ intervening points. Since $i<n-1$, this clockwise arc is the uniquely shorter arc, so each such chord is counted exactly once.

A chord $\ell$ with $m(\ell)=i$ has degree at most $i$ in its graph $G_c$. Indeed, choose the open arc containing $i$ points. Every chord crossing $\ell$ has an endpoint among those $i$ points, and each such point is the endpoint of at most one chord of color $c$, because the other endpoint label is determined by the required sum $c$.

Consequently, the $2n$ chords with $m(\ell)=i$ contribute at least
\[
\frac{2n}{i+1}
\]
to the total sum of the quantities in (1) over all color graphs. Summing over $i=0,1,\ldots,n-2$, we get
\[
\sum_c F(G_c)
\ge 2n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1i.
\tag{2}
\]
Since there are $4n-3$ colors, some color $c$ satisfies
\[
F(G_c)
\ge \frac{2n}{4n-3}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1i
>\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1i.
\tag{3}
\]
By the lemma, $G_c$ has an independent set at least this large. Such an independent set consists of pairwise nonintersecting chords of the same color; and, as noted above, chords of the same color do not share endpoints. Hence these chords are pairwise disjoint and have equal endpoint sums.

It remains to show that the right-hand side of (3) exceeds $100$. Since
\[
n-1=2^{499}-1>2^{400},
\]
we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1i
&>\sum_{i=1}^{2^{400}}\frac1i\\
&=1+\sum_{k=1}^{400}\ \sum_{i=2^{k-1}+1}^{2^k}\frac1i.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each block contains $2^{k-1}$ terms, each at least $1/2^k$, and the inequality is strict in every block with $k\ge 2$. Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1i
>1+\sum_{k=1}^{400}\frac{2^{k-1}}{2^k}
=201>200.
\]
Thus (3) gives
\[
F(G_c)>100.
\]
The independent set has an integer number of vertices, so it contains at least $101$ chords. Choosing any $100$ of them completes the proof.
