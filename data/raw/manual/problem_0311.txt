ID: 0311

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Two-Colorings, Prefix Densities, Extremal Arguments, Pigeonhole Principle

STATEMENT:
Let $p$ be an odd prime, and put
\[
N=\frac{p^3-p}{4}-1.
\]
The integers
\[
1,2,\ldots,N
\]
are colored arbitrarily red or blue. For $1\le n\le N$, let $r(n)$ be the fraction of the integers
\[
1,2,\ldots,n
\]
that are red.

Prove that there exists
\[
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
\]
such that
\[
r(n)\ne\frac ap
\]
for every $1\le n\le N$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $R(n)$ and $B(n)$ denote the numbers of red and blue integers among
\[
1,2,\ldots,n.
\]
Thus
\[
R(n)+B(n)=n.
\]

Assume, for contradiction, that every fraction
\[
\frac1p,\frac2p,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{p}
\]
occurs. For each $a\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$, choose $n_a\le N$ such that
\[
R(n_a)=\frac{a n_a}{p}.
\]
Since $p\nmid a$, we have $p\mid n_a$. Write
\[
n_a=pm_a.
\]
Then
\[
R(n_a)=am_a,
\qquad
B(n_a)=(p-a)m_a.
\tag{1}
\]

The positive integers $m_1,\ldots,m_{p-1}$ are pairwise distinct: if $m_a=m_b$, then $n_a=n_b$ and (1) would give $a=b$. Moreover,
\[
m_a\le\frac Np
=
\frac{p^2-1}{4}-\frac1p
<
\frac{p^2-1}{4}.
\tag{2}
\]

Swapping the two colors replaces the sequence $(m_a)$ by $(m_{p-a})$. Hence, without loss of generality,
\[
m_1<m_{p-1}.
\tag{3}
\]

We use the following observation.

\textbf{Claim.}
If
\[
m_a<m_b,
\]
then
\[
m_b\ge\frac ab\,m_a
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
m_b\ge\frac{p-a}{p-b}\,m_a.
\tag{5}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Since $n_a<n_b$, monotonicity of the red count gives
\[
bm_b=R(n_b)\ge R(n_a)=am_a,
\]
which proves (4). Monotonicity of the blue count gives
\[
(p-b)m_b=B(n_b)\ge B(n_a)=(p-a)m_a,
\]
which proves (5). $\square$

Put
\[
q=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

\textbf{Case 1.}
Suppose
\[
m_1,m_2,\ldots,m_q<m_{p-1}.
\]
Let $m_a$ be the largest of $m_1,\ldots,m_q$. Since these are $q$ distinct positive integers,
\[
m_a\ge q,
\]
and $a\le q$. Applying (5) to $m_a<m_{p-1}$ gives
\[
m_{p-1}
\ge
(p-a)m_a
\ge
(p-q)q
=
\frac{p^2-1}{4},
\]
contradicting (2).

\textbf{Case 2.}
Suppose some $m_k$ with $k\le q$ satisfies
\[
m_k>m_{p-1}.
\]
Choose the least such $k$. By (3),
\[
2\le k\le q.
\]
Let $m_a$ be the largest of
\[
m_1,\ldots,m_{k-1}.
\]
Then
\[
a\le k-1,
\qquad
m_a\ge k-1,
\qquad
m_a<m_{p-1}<m_k.
\]
Applying (4) to $m_{p-1}<m_k$ and then (5) to $m_a<m_{p-1}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_k
&\ge
\frac{p-1}{k}m_{p-1}\\
&\ge
\frac{p-1}{k}(p-a)m_a\\
&\ge
\frac{p-1}{k}(p-k+1)(k-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{k-1}{k}\ge\frac12
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p-k+1\ge p-q=\frac{p+1}{2},
\]
we obtain
\[
m_k\ge\frac{p^2-1}{4},
\]
again contradicting (2).

Both cases are impossible. Therefore at least one value $a/p$ never occurs among the prefix red proportions.
