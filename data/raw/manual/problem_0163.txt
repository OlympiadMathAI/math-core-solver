ID: 0163

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Power of a Point, Inversion, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Midpoint Geometry

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $CA\ne CB$. Let $D,F,G$ be the midpoints of $AB,AC,BC$, respectively. A circle $\Gamma$ passing through $C$ and tangent to $AB$ at $D$ meets the segments $AF$ and $BG$ at $H$ and $I$, respectively. The points $H'$ and $I'$ are symmetric to $H$ and $I$ about $F$ and $G$, respectively. The line $H'I'$ meets $CD$ and $FG$ at $Q$ and $M$, respectively. The line $CM$ meets $\Gamma$ again at $P$. Prove that
\[
CQ=QP.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We may assume that $CA>CB$. All angle equalities are understood modulo $180^\circ$, and all products may be interpreted as directed products.

Since $H'$ is the reflection of $H$ about the midpoint $F$ of $AC$, we have $CH'=AH$. Similarly, $CI'=BI$. Using the powers of $A$ and $B$ with respect to $\Gamma$, we get
\[
CH'\cdot CA
=
AH\cdot AC
=
AD^2
=
BD^2
=
BI\cdot BC
=
CI'\cdot CB.
\]
Because $CF=CA/2$ and $CG=CB/2$, it follows that
\[
CH'\cdot CF=CI'\cdot CG.
\]
Hence the quadrilateral $H'I'GF$ is cyclic.

Let $DF$ and $DG$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $R$ and $S$, respectively. We prove that $R$ and $S$ both lie on $H'I'$.
Since $FH'=FH$ and $FA=FC$,
\[
FH'\cdot FA=FH\cdot FC=FR\cdot FD.
\]
Thus $A,D,H',R$ are concyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle RH'F
=
\angle FDA
=
\angle CGF
=
\angle I'H'C,
\]
where we used $DF\parallel BC$, $FG\parallel AB$, and the cyclicity of $H'I'GF$. Hence $R,H',I'$ are collinear. The same argument on the other side shows that $S,H',I'$ are collinear. It also gives that $B,D,I',S$ are cyclic.

We next prove that $R,S,G,F$ are cyclic. Since $R,S,H',I'$ are collinear and $S,D,G$ are collinear,
\[
\angle RSD=\angle I'SD.
\]
Using the cyclic quadrilateral $B,D,I',S$ and the parallel relations $DF\parallel BC$ and $FG\parallel AB$, we obtain
\[
\angle RSD
=
\angle I'BD
=
\angle RDA
=
\angle DFG.
\]
Because $S,D,G$ are collinear and $R,D,F$ are collinear, this is exactly the condition that $R,S,G,F$ are cyclic.

Now take powers from $M$. From the cyclic quadrilaterals $H'I'GF$ and $RSGF$, and from the circle $\Gamma$ containing $R,S,C,P$, we have
\[
MH'\cdot MI'
=
MF\cdot MG
=
MR\cdot MS
=
MC\cdot MP.
\]
Therefore $C,P,I',H'$ are concyclic. Denote their circumcircle by $\omega$.

Since $D,C,Q$ are collinear and $R,S,C,D$ lie on $\Gamma$,
\[
\angle H'CQ=\angle SDC=\angle SRC.
\]
Also, because $H',Q,R$ are collinear,
\[
\angle H'QC=\angle RQC.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle CH'Q\sim\triangle RCQ,
\]
and consequently
\[
QH'\cdot QR=QC^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\triangle CI'Q\sim\triangle SCQ,
\]
so
\[
QI'\cdot QS=QC^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Apply the inversion centered at $Q$ with radius $QC$. Equations (1) and (2) show that it interchanges
\[
R\leftrightarrow H',
\qquad
S\leftrightarrow I',
\]
and fixes $C$. Hence it maps $\Gamma$, the circumcircle of $RCS$, to $\omega$, the circumcircle of $H'CI'$.

The two circles $\Gamma$ and $\omega$ meet at $C$ and $P$. Since $C$ is fixed by the inversion, their other common point $P$ is fixed as well. Therefore
\[
QP^2=QC^2,
\]
and hence
\[
QP=QC.
\]
