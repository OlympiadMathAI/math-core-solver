ID: 0032

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Function Iteration, Induction

STATEMENT:
Determine all pairs of functions
\[
f,g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N,
\]
where $\mathbb N$ is the set of positive integers, such that
\[
f^{\,g(n)+1}(n)+g^{\,f(n)}(n)
=f(n+1)-g(n+1)+1
\tag{1}
\]
for every positive integer $n$. Here $h^k$ denotes the $k$-fold iterate of a function $h$.

SOLUTION 1:
The unique solution is
\[
\boxed{f(n)=n,\qquad g(n)=1}
\]
for every $n\in\mathbb N$.

Because all values of $f$ and $g$ are positive, equation (1) implies
\[
f^{\,g(n)+1}(n)<f(n+1)
\tag{2}
\]
for every $n$.

List the distinct values attained by $f$ in increasing order:
\[
y_1<y_2<\cdots.
\]
We prove successively that
\[
\text{(i)}_n:\quad f(x)=y_n\Longleftrightarrow x=n,
\]
and
\[
\text{(ii)}_n:\quad y_n=n.
\]

To begin, let $f(x)=y_1$. If $x>1$, then applying (2) to $x-1$ gives
\[
f^{\,g(x-1)+1}(x-1)<f(x)=y_1,
\]
contradicting the minimality of $y_1$. Hence $x=1$, so $\text{(i)}_1$ holds.

Assume now that $\text{(i)}_n$ and all preceding assertions have been established. In particular, for every $j<n$,
\[
y_j=j
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f^{-1}(j)=\{j\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The value $y_{n+1}$ exists: otherwise the image of $f$ would consist of $y_1,\ldots,y_n$, each already having its unique preimage $1,\ldots,n$, which is impossible because the domain is infinite.

Choose $x$ with
\[
f(x)=y_{n+1}.
\]
Then $x>n$. Applying (2) to $x-1$ gives
\[
f\left(f^{\,g(x-1)}(x-1)\right)<y_{n+1}.
\]
Set
\[
b=f^{\,g(x-1)}(x-1).
\]
The number $f(b)$ is one of $y_1,\ldots,y_n$. By the already proved uniqueness assertions, this implies
\[
b\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.
\tag{4}
\]
If $b<n$, then repeatedly using (3) to cancel the iterates of $f$ gives
\[
x-1=b<n,
\]
contradicting $x>n$. Therefore
\[
b=n.
\tag{5}
\]
Since $b$ itself is a value of $f$ and the preceding values are $1,\ldots,n-1$, relation (5) gives
\[
y_n=n.
\]
This proves $\text{(ii)}_n$.

Now $\text{(i)}_n$ says that $n$ has the unique preimage $n$. Repeatedly cancelling $f$ in (5) yields
\[
x-1=n,
\]
so $x=n+1$. Thus $y_{n+1}$ is attained only at $n+1$, proving $\text{(i)}_{n+1}$.

The induction shows that
\[
f(n)=n
\]
for every $n$. Substituting this into (1),
\[
n+g^n(n)=n+1-g(n+1)+1,
\]
so
\[
g^n(n)+g(n+1)=2.
\]
Both terms are positive integers, hence both equal $1$. Taking $n=1$ gives $g(1)=1$, and the relation $g(n+1)=1$ gives the same for every larger argument. Therefore
\[
g(n)=1
\]
for all $n$.

Finally, the pair $f(n)=n$, $g(n)=1$ satisfies (1), so it is the unique solution.
