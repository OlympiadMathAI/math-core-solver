ID: 0366

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Extremal Pairs, Dyadic Decomposition, Bernoulli Inequality

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge3$ be an integer, and let
\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\in[0,1].
\]
Put
\[
s=x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n,
\]
and assume that $s\ge3$. Prove that there exist integers $i,j$ with
\[
1\le i<j\le n
\]
such that
\[
2^{j-i}x_ix_j>2^{s-3}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Choose indices
\[
1\le a<b\le n
\]
for which
\[
2^{b-a}x_ax_b
\]
is maximal. Since $s\ge3$, at least two of the $x_i$ are positive, so this maximum is positive and
\[
x_a,x_b>0.
\]
Put
\[
d=b-a.
\]

Maximality gives the two estimates
\[
x_i\le2^{i-a}x_a
\qquad(1\le i<b)
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
x_i\le2^{b-i}x_b
\qquad(a<i\le n).
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, (1) follows by comparing the pair $(i,b)$ with $(a,b)$, and (2) follows by comparing $(a,i)$ with $(a,b)$.

Choose nonnegative integers $u,v$ such that
\[
2^{-u-1}<x_a\le2^{-u},
\qquad
2^{-v-1}<x_b\le2^{-v},
\]
and write
\[
x_a=2^{-\alpha},
\qquad
x_b=2^{-\beta}.
\]
Then
\[
u\le\alpha<u+1,
\qquad
v\le\beta<v+1.
\tag{3}
\]

We first note that
\[
u<d
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
v<d.
\tag{4}
\]
If $u\ge d$, then by (1) and (2),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{b-1}x_i
<2^dx_a\le1
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=b}^{n}x_i
<2x_b\le2.
\]
This would give $s<3$, a contradiction. The argument for $v\ge d$ is symmetric: in that case
\[
\sum_{i=a+1}^{n}x_i<2^dx_b\le1
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{a}x_i<2x_a\le2,
\]
again contradicting $s\ge3$. Thus (4) holds.

Using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{a+u-1}x_i
&\le
x_a\sum_{t=1-a}^{u-1}2^t\\
&<2^ux_a\\
&\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Similarly, by (2),
\[
\sum_{i=b-v+1}^{n}x_i
<2^vx_b
\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the sum of all $x_i$ whose indices lie outside the interval
\[
[a+u,b-v]
\]
is strictly smaller than $2$. Since the total sum is at least $3$ and each $x_i$ is at most $1$, this interval contains at least two integers. Hence
\[
a+u<b-v.
\tag{7}
\]

We now include the two endpoints in the geometric estimates. Equations (1), (2), and (7) give
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{a+u}x_i<2^{u+1}x_a
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=b-v}^{n}x_i<2^{v+1}x_b.
\]
Every remaining term is at most $1$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
s
&<2^{u+1}x_a+2^{v+1}x_b
  +\bigl((b-v)-(a+u)-1\bigr)\\
&=b-a+
\left(2^{u+1-\alpha}+2^{v+1-\beta}-(u+v+1)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

For $0\le t\le1$, convexity of $2^t$ on $[0,1]$ gives
\[
2^t\le1+t.
\]
Applying this to
\[
t=u+1-\alpha
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
t=v+1-\beta,
\]
which belong to $(0,1]$ by (3), we obtain
\[
2^{u+1-\alpha}+2^{v+1-\beta}-(u+v+1)
\le3-\alpha-\beta.
\]
Together with (8),
\[
s-3<b-a-\alpha-\beta.
\]
Exponentiating base $2$ gives
\[
2^{s-3}
<2^{b-a-\alpha-\beta}
=2^{b-a}x_ax_b.
\]
Thus the pair $(i,j)=(a,b)$ has the required property.
