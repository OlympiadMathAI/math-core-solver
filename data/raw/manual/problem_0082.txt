ID: 0082

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Diophantine Equations, Modular Arithmetic, 2-adic Valuation, Fermat's Little Theorem, Factorization

STATEMENT:
Find all triples $(x,y,z)$ of positive integers such that $x\le y\le z$ and
\[
x^3(y^3+z^3)=2012(xyz+2).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We prove that the only solution is
\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)=(2,251,252)}.
\]

Reducing the equation modulo $x$ gives
\[
x\mid 2\cdot 2012=2^3\cdot 503.
\tag{1}
\]
If $503\mid x$, then the left-hand side is divisible by $503^3$. Since $2012=4\cdot503$, this would imply
\[
503^2\mid xyz+2,
\]
which is impossible because $503\mid x$ gives $xyz+2\equiv2\pmod{503}$. Thus $503\nmid x$, and (1) yields
\[
x=2^m\qquad\text{for some }m\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]

If $m\ge2$, then the left-hand side is divisible by $2^{3m}$ and hence by $2^6$. On the other hand,
\[
v_2(2012)=2
\]
and
\[
xyz+2=2^m yz+2=2\bigl(2^{m-1}yz+1\bigr),
\]
where the factor in parentheses is odd. Hence the right-hand side has 2-adic valuation exactly $3$, a contradiction. Therefore
\[
x\in\{1,2\}.
\]
The original equation becomes, respectively,
\[
y^3+z^3=2012(yz+2)
\tag{2}
\]
or
\[
y^3+z^3=503(yz+1).
\tag{3}
\]

In either case,
\[
y^3+z^3\equiv0\pmod{503}.
\]
Since $\gcd(3,502)=1$, the map $u\mapsto u^3$ is a bijection on $\mathbb F_{503}$. Thus
\[
y^3\equiv(-z)^3\pmod{503}
\]
implies
\[
y\equiv-z\pmod{503}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
y+z=503k
\tag{4}
\]
for some positive integer $k$.

Using
\[
y^3+z^3=(y+z)\bigl((y-z)^2+yz\bigr),
\]
equations (2) and (3), together with (4), become
\[
k(y-z)^2+(k-4)yz=8
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
k(y-z)^2+(k-1)yz=1,
\tag{6}
\]
respectively.

We first rule out (5), which corresponds to $x=1$. Since the first term in (5) is nonnegative,
\[
(k-4)yz\le8.
\]
If $k>4$, then $1\le(k-4)yz\le8$, so $yz\le8$, hence $y,z\le8$. This contradicts $y+z=503k$. Thus $k\le4$.

Moreover, the right-hand side of (2) is even, so $y^3+z^3$, and hence $y+z$, is even. Since $503$ is odd, $k$ is even. Therefore $k=2$ or $k=4$.

For $k=4$, equation (5) gives
\[
4(y-z)^2=8,
\]
which is impossible. For $k=2$, equation (5) is equivalent to
\[
(y+z)^2-5yz=4.
\]
Since $y+z=1006$, this gives
\[
5yz=1006^2-4,
\]
but the right-hand side is congruent to $1-4\equiv2\pmod5$. This is again impossible. Hence $x\ne1$.

We now solve (6), corresponding to $x=2$. Both terms on the left are nonnegative, so
\[
0\le(k-1)yz\le1.
\]
Thus $k=1$ or $k=2$. Also,
\[
0\le k(y-z)^2\le1.
\]
If $k=2$, then $y=z$, and (6) gives $yz=1$, contradicting $y+z=1006$. Hence $k=1$. Equation (6) then gives
\[
(y-z)^2=1.
\]
Because $y\le z$ and $y+z=503$, we obtain
\[
z-y=1,
\qquad
(y,z)=(251,252).
\]
This pair satisfies (3), so the unique triple is
\[
\boxed{(2,251,252)}.
\]
