ID: 0235

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Exponential Diophantine Equations, Multiplicative Orders, Modular Arithmetic, Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Find all pairs $(p,q)$ of prime numbers with $p>q$ for which
\[
\frac{(p+q)^{p+q}(p-q)^{p-q}-1}
     {(p+q)^{p-q}(p-q)^{p+q}-1}
\]
is an integer.

SOLUTION 1:
The unique pair is
\[
\boxed{(p,q)=(3,2)}.
\]

Put
\[
M=(p+q)^{p-q}(p-q)^{p+q}-1.
\]
The assumption says that $M$ divides the numerator. Since $M$ is relatively prime to both $p+q$ and $p-q$, eliminating the $-1$ terms gives
\[
(p+q)^{2q}\equiv(p-q)^{2q}\pmod M,
\tag{1}
\]
or equivalently
\[
\left((p+q)(p-q)^{-1}\right)^{2q}\equiv1\pmod M.
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Case 1: $q\ge5$.}

Here $p$ and $q$ are odd, so $M$ is odd. Let $r$ be any prime divisor of $M$. By (2), the multiplicative order of
\[
(p+q)(p-q)^{-1}\pmod r
\]
divides $2q$, hence is one of
\[
1,2,q,2q.
\]
If the order is $q$ or $2q$, Fermat's theorem gives
\[
r\equiv1\pmod q.
\]
If the order is $1$ or $2$, then
\[
r\mid(p+q)^2-(p-q)^2=4pq.
\]
Since $r$ is odd, $r=p$ or $r=q$.

The possibility $r=p$ does not occur. Indeed,
\[
M\equiv q^{p-q}(-q)^{p+q}-1
=q^{2p}-1
\equiv q^2-1\pmod p,
\]
and neither $q-1$ nor $q+1$ is divisible by $p$, because
\[
0<q-1<q+1<p.
\]
Thus every prime divisor of $M$ is either $q$ or congruent to $1$ modulo $q$. Consequently, every positive divisor of $M$ is congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo $q$.

Now write
\[
M=(A-1)(A+1),
\qquad
A=(p+q)^{(p-q)/2}(p-q)^{(p+q)/2}.
\]
The factors $A-1$ and $A+1$ are positive divisors of $M$ differing by $2$. They cannot both be congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo a prime $q\ge5$. This contradiction eliminates the case $q\ge5$.

\textbf{Case 2: $q=2$.}

From (1),
\[
M\mid(p+2)^4-(p-2)^4.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
(p+2)^{p-2}(p-2)^{p+2}-1
&\le(p+2)^4-(p-2)^4\\
&\le(p+2)^4-1,
\end{aligned}
\]
so
\[
(p+2)^{p-6}(p-2)^{p+2}\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
If $p\ge7$, the left side of (3) is greater than $1$. For $p=5$, it equals
\[
7^{-1}3^7>1.
\]
Thus $p=3$ is the only possibility. It indeed works:
\[
\frac{5^5\cdot1-1}{5\cdot1^5-1}
=\frac{3124}{4}=781.
\]

\textbf{Case 3: $q=3$.}

Again (1) gives
\[
M\mid(p+3)^6-(p-3)^6
=64\left(
\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right)^6-
\left(\frac{p-3}{2}\right)^6
\right).
\]
Here $M$ is odd, so it divides the expression in parentheses. Therefore
\[
(p+3)^{p-3}(p-3)^{p+3}-1
\le
\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right)^6-1,
\]
and hence
\[
64(p+3)^{p-9}(p-3)^{p+3}\le1.
\tag{4}
\]
For $p\ge11$, the left side is greater than $1$. The remaining primes give
\[
p=7:\quad64\cdot10^{-2}\cdot4^{10}>1,
\]
and
\[
p=5:\quad64\cdot8^{-4}\cdot2^8=4>1.
\]
Thus there is no solution with $q=3$.

Therefore the only pair is $(3,2)$.
