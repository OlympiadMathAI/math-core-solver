ID: 0073

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Excircles, Angle Chasing, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Reflections, Tangent Lengths

STATEMENT:
In triangle $ABC$, the point $J$ is the center of the excircle opposite to $A$. This excircle is tangent to the side $BC$ at $M$, and to the lines $AB$ and $AC$ at $K$ and $L$, respectively. The lines $LM$ and $BJ$ meet at $F$, and the lines $KM$ and $CJ$ meet at $G$. Let $S$ be the intersection of $AF$ and $BC$, and let $T$ be the intersection of $AG$ and $BC$. Prove that $M$ is the midpoint of $ST$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\qquad \beta=\angle ABC,\qquad \gamma=\angle BCA.
\]
Since $AJ$ bisects $\angle BAC$ and $JK\perp AB$, $JL\perp AC$, we have
\[
\angle JAK=\angle JAL=\frac{\alpha}{2},
\qquad
\angle AKJ=\angle ALJ=90^\circ.
\]
Hence $A,K,J,L$ lie on the circle $\omega$ with diameter $AJ$.

The segments $BK$ and $BM$ are tangents from $B$ to the excircle, so
\[
BK=BM.
\]
Moreover, $BJ$ is the external angle bisector at $B$, and it is also the symmetry axis of the isosceles triangle $KBM$. Therefore
\[
\angle MBJ=90^\circ-\frac{\beta}{2},
\qquad
\angle BMK=\frac{\beta}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\angle MCJ=90^\circ-\frac{\gamma}{2},
\qquad
\angle CML=\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Because $F$ lies on both $LM$ and $BJ$, and because $B,M,C$ are collinear, the angle between $MF$ and $MB$ equals the angle between $ML$ and $MC$. Thus, using (1) and (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle LFJ
&=\angle MBJ-\angle BMF\\
&=\left(90^\circ-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)-\frac{\gamma}{2}\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{2}\\
&=\angle LAJ.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently $A,L,F,J$ are concyclic, so $F\in\omega$. By the same argument, $G\in\omega$.

Since $AJ$ is a diameter of $\omega$,
\[
AF\perp FJ,
\qquad
AG\perp GJ.
\]
Because $B,F,J$ are collinear and $C,G,J$ are collinear, this gives
\[
AF\perp BF,
\qquad
AG\perp CG.
\tag{3}
\]
Also, in the isosceles triangle $KBM$, the line $BF$ (the same line as $BJ$) is perpendicular to $KM$. Hence both $AF$ and $KM$ are perpendicular to the reflection axis $BF$.

Reflection in $BF$ interchanges the lines $AB$ and $BC$. The line $AF$ is invariant under this reflection, so the point
\[
A=AB\cap AF
\]
is sent to
\[
S=BC\cap AF.
\]
Likewise, the line $KM$ is invariant, so $K=AB\cap KM$ is sent to $M=BC\cap KM$. Therefore
\[
SM=AK.
\tag{4}
\]

Applying the analogous reflection in the line $CG$ (the same line as $CJ$), using (3), gives
\[
TM=AL.
\tag{5}
\]
Finally, $AK$ and $AL$ are tangent segments from $A$ to the same excircle, so $AK=AL$. Equations (4) and (5) yield
\[
SM=TM.
\]
Thus $M$ is the midpoint of $ST$.
