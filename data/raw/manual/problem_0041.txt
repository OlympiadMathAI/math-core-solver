ID: 0041

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Combinatorics on Words, Periodic Words, Multiplicity

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
W=\cdots x_{-1}x_0x_1x_2\cdots
\]
be an infinite periodic word over the alphabet $\{a,b\}$, and suppose its minimal period $N$ satisfies
\[
N>2^n,
\]
where $n$ is a positive integer.

A finite nonempty word $U$ \emph{appears} in $W$ if it occurs as a consecutive block. Call $U$ \emph{ubiquitous} if all four words
\[
Ua,\quad Ub,\quad aU,\quad bU
\]
appear in $W$.

Prove that there are at least $n$ ubiquitous finite nonempty words.

SOLUTION 1:
For a finite word $R$ of length $r$, define its multiplicity $\mu(R)$ to be the number of indices
\[
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
\]
for which
\[
R=x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\cdots x_{i+r},
\]
with indices interpreted periodically. Every occurrence has exactly one following letter and one preceding letter, so
\[
\mu(R)=\mu(Ra)+\mu(Rb)=\mu(aR)+\mu(bR).
\tag{1}
\]

Every word of length $N$ that appears has multiplicity exactly $1$. Indeed, if two length-$N$ blocks beginning at distinct residues $i<j$ were equal, then
\[
x_{i+t}=x_{j+t}
\]
for every integer $t$, so $j-i$ would be a positive period smaller than $N$.

Also,
\[
\mu(a)+\mu(b)=N>2^n,
\]
so one of the one-letter words has multiplicity greater than $2^{n-1}$.

For each
\[
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1,
\]
choose an appearing word $U_k$ of maximal length subject to
\[
\mu(U_k)>2^k.
\tag{2}
\]
Such a word exists by the preceding paragraph, and a maximal length exists because no length-$N$ word has multiplicity greater than $1$.

We show that $U_k$ is ubiquitous. By maximality, both longer words $U_ka$ and $U_kb$ have multiplicity at most $2^k$. If one of them did not appear, then (1) would force the other to have multiplicity
\[
\mu(U_k)>2^k,
\]
contradicting maximality. Hence both $U_ka$ and $U_kb$ appear. Applying the same argument on the left shows that both $aU_k$ and $bU_k$ appear. Thus $U_k$ is ubiquitous.

Moreover, (1) and maximality imply
\[
\mu(U_k)\le2^{k+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
For if $\mu(U_k)>2^{k+1}$, then at least one of $U_ka,U_kb$ would have multiplicity greater than $2^k$, again contradicting the maximality of the length of $U_k$.

Combining (2)--(3) for consecutive values of $k$ gives
\[
\mu(U_0)\le2<\mu(U_1)\le4<\cdots
\le2^{n-1}<\mu(U_{n-1}).
\]
Therefore the words
\[
U_0,U_1,\ldots,U_{n-1}
\]
are distinct. Each is ubiquitous, so there are at least $n$ ubiquitous words.
