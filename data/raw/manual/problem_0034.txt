ID: 0034

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Inequalities, Sign Analysis, Iteration

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
\]
satisfy
\[
f(x+y)\le y f(x)+f(f(x))
\tag{1}
\]
for all real numbers $x,y$. Prove that
\[
f(x)=0
\]
for every $x\le0$.

SOLUTION 1:
Writing $t=x+y$, inequality (1) becomes
\[
f(t)\le t f(x)-x f(x)+f(f(x))
\tag{2}
\]
for all real $t,x$.

Take arbitrary real numbers $a,b$. Apply (2) first with $(t,x)=(f(a),b)$ and then with $(t,x)=(f(b),a)$. We obtain
\[
f(f(a))-f(f(b))
\le f(a)f(b)-b f(b),
\]
\[
f(f(b))-f(f(a))
\le f(a)f(b)-a f(a).
\]
Adding,
\[
2f(a)f(b)\ge a f(a)+b f(b).
\tag{3}
\]
Set
\[
b=2f(a).
\]
The terms involving $f(b)$ cancel in (3), leaving
\[
a f(a)\le0.
\]
Hence
\[
f(a)\ge0
\qquad(a<0).
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose now that $f(x)>0$ for some real $x$. In (2), the right side is an affine function of $t$ with positive slope $f(x)$. For all sufficiently negative $t$, it is negative, so
\[
f(t)<0.
\]
This contradicts (4). Therefore
\[
f(x)\le0
\qquad\text{for every real }x.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (4) and (5),
\[
f(x)=0
\qquad(x<0).
\tag{6}
\]

Finally, in (2) choose the same negative number for $t$ and $x$. By (6),
\[
0=f(x)\le f(f(x))=f(0),
\]
so $f(0)\ge0$. Relation (5) gives $f(0)\le0$, and hence
\[
f(0)=0.
\]
Thus $f(x)=0$ for all $x\le0$.

SOLUTION 2:
We again use the equivalent form
\[
f(t)\le t f(x)-x f(x)+f(f(x)).
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Step 1: $f$ is nonpositive everywhere.}
Assume that $f(z)>0$ for some $z$. Put
\[
A=f(z)>0,
\qquad
B=-z f(z)+f(f(z)).
\]
Then (2) with $x=z$ gives
\[
f(t)\le At+B
\tag{3}
\]
for all $t$.

For a positive real number $u$, use (1) with $x=-u$ and $y=u$. Applying (3) first to $-u$ and then to $f(-u)$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(0)
&\le u f(-u)+f(f(-u))\\
&\le u(-Au+B)+A(-Au+B)+B\\
&=-Au^2-(A^2-B)u+(A+1)B.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right side tends to $-\infty$ as $u\to\infty$, a contradiction. Hence
\[
f(x)\le0
\tag{4}
\]
for all real $x$.

Since $f(f(x))\le0$, inequality (1) now implies
\[
f(x+y)\le y f(x)
\tag{5}
\]
for all real $x,y$.

\textbf{Step 2: $f$ has a zero.}
If $f(0)=0$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $f(0)<0$. Taking $x=0$ in (5),
\[
f(y)\le y f(0).
\]
Choose $a>0$ so large that
\[
f(a)^2>-f(0),
\]
and put
\[
b=f(a).
\]
Then $b<0$. Applying (5) with $x=b$ and $y=-b$,
\[
f(0)\le -b f(b).
\]
Since $-b^2<f(0)$, it follows that
\[
b<f(b).
\tag{6}
\]
Now use (2) with $x=b$ and $t=f(b)$:
\[
f(f(b))
\le (f(b)-b)f(b)+f(f(b)).
\]
By (6), the factor $f(b)-b$ is positive, so
\[
f(b)\ge0.
\]
Together with (4), this gives
\[
f(b)=0.
\]
Thus $f$ has at least one zero.

\textbf{Step 3: zeros propagate to the left.}
If $f(a)=0$ and $b<a$, apply (5) with $x=b$ and $y=a-b>0$:
\[
0=f(a)\le(a-b)f(b).
\]
Hence $f(b)\ge0$, and (4) yields
\[
f(b)=0.
\tag{7}
\]

\textbf{Step 4: $0$ is a zero.}
Choose any $r$ with $f(r)=0$. By (7),
\[
f(r-1)=0.
\]
Using (1) with $x=r$ and $y=-1$,
\[
0=f(r-1)
\le -f(r)+f(f(r))
=f(0).
\]
Together with (4), this gives $f(0)=0$. Applying (7) with $a=0$, we obtain
\[
f(x)=0
\]
for every $x<0$ as well. Therefore $f(x)=0$ for all $x\le0$.
