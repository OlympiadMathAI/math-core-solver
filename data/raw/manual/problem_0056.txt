ID: 0056

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Polynomial Congruences, Multiplicative Orders, Bezout Identity

STATEMENT:
Let $P(x),Q(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ be polynomials such that no nonconstant polynomial with rational coefficients divides both of them. Suppose that, for every positive integer $n$, the integers $P(n)$ and $Q(n)$ are positive and
\[
2^{Q(n)}-1\mid 3^{P(n)}-1.
\]
Prove that $Q(x)$ is constant.

SOLUTION 1:
Because $P$ and $Q$ are relatively prime in $\mathbb Q[x]$, there exist polynomials
\[
R_0(x),S_0(x)\in\mathbb Q[x]
\]
such that
\[
P(x)R_0(x)-Q(x)S_0(x)=1.
\]
After multiplying by a suitable positive integer $d$, we obtain polynomials
\[
R(x),S(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]
\]
satisfying
\[
P(x)R(x)-Q(x)S(x)=d.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for every positive integer $n$,
\[
\gcd(P(n),Q(n))\le d.
\tag{2}
\]

Assume, for a contradiction, that $Q$ is nonconstant. Since $Q(n)>0$ for every positive integer $n$, the sequence $Q(n)$ is unbounded. Choose a positive integer $m$ so large that
\[
M=2^{Q(m)}-1
\ge
3^{\,\max\{P(1),P(2),\ldots,P(d)\}}.
\tag{3}
\]

The number $M$ is odd. Moreover,
\[
M\mid3^{P(m)}-1,
\]
so $3\nmid M$. Hence both $2$ and $3$ are invertible modulo $M$. Let
\[
a=\operatorname{ord}_M(2),
\qquad
b=\operatorname{ord}_M(3)
\]
be their multiplicative orders.

We have
\[
a=Q(m).
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed,
\[
2^{Q(m)}\equiv1\pmod M,
\]
while for every $0<r<Q(m)$,
\[
0<2^r-1<M,
\]
so $M\nmid2^r-1$.

Also,
\[
b\mid P(m),
\tag{5}
\]
because $M\mid3^{P(m)}-1$. From (2), (4), and (5),
\[
\gcd(a,b)\le\gcd(P(m),Q(m))\le d.
\tag{6}
\]

Let
\[
g=\gcd(a,b).
\]
Choose an integer $r$ with
\[
1\le r\le g
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r\equiv m\pmod g.
\]
Then $r-m$ is an integer multiple of $g$. Since every multiple of $g$ can be written as
\[
ax-by
\]
with nonnegative integers $x,y$ after adding a sufficiently large common multiple to an integer solution, there exist $x,y\ge0$ such that
\[
r=m+ax-by.
\tag{7}
\]
By (6),
\[
1\le r\le d.
\tag{8}
\]

Because
\[
m+ax\equiv m\pmod a,
\]
integer-coefficient polynomial congruences give
\[
Q(m+ax)\equiv Q(m)\pmod a.
\]
Using (4),
\[
2^{Q(m+ax)}
\equiv2^{Q(m)}
\equiv1\pmod M.
\]
Thus
\[
M\mid2^{Q(m+ax)}-1.
\]
By the hypothesis of the problem,
\[
2^{Q(m+ax)}-1\mid3^{P(m+ax)}-1,
\]
and therefore
\[
3^{P(m+ax)}\equiv1\pmod M.
\tag{9}
\]

Next, (7) gives
\[
r\equiv m+ax\pmod b.
\]
Hence
\[
P(r)\equiv P(m+ax)\pmod b.
\]
Since $b=\operatorname{ord}_M(3)$, relation (9) implies
\[
3^{P(r)}
\equiv3^{P(m+ax)}
\equiv1\pmod M.
\]
As $P(r)>0$,
\[
M\le3^{P(r)}-1<3^{P(r)}.
\tag{10}
\]

But (8) shows that $P(r)$ is one of
\[
P(1),P(2),\ldots,P(d).
\]
Therefore, by (10),
\[
M<
3^{\,\max\{P(1),P(2),\ldots,P(d)\}},
\]
contradicting (3).

Hence $Q(x)$ must be a constant polynomial.
