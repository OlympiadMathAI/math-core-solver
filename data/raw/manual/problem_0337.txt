ID: 0337

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Recurrence Inequalities, Induction, Square Roots, AM-GM

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge1$ be an integer, and let
\[
x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}
\]
be nonnegative real numbers satisfying
\[
x_ix_{i+1}-x_{i-1}^2\ge1
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Prove that
\[
x_0+x_1+\cdots+x_{n+1}
>
\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)^{3/2}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We use two lemmas.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
If $a,b,c\ge0$ and
\[
ab-c^2\ge1,
\]
then
\[
(a+2b)^2\ge(b+2c)^2+6.
\tag{1}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
(a+2b)^2-(b+2c)^2
&=
(a-b)^2+2(b-c)^2+6(ab-c^2)\\
&\ge6.
\end{aligned}
\]
$\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
For every positive integer $n$,
\[
\sqrt1+\sqrt2+\cdots+\sqrt n
>
\frac23n^{3/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
For every $k\ge1$,
\[
(k-1)^{3/2}
=
k^{3/2}\left(1-\frac1k\right)^{3/2}
>
k^{3/2}-\frac32\sqrt k.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
k^{3/2}-(k-1)^{3/2}<\frac32\sqrt k.
\]
Summing over $k=1,\ldots,n$ gives (2). $\square$

Now define
\[
y_i=2x_i+x_{i+1}
\qquad(0\le i\le n).
\]
Apply Lemma 1 with
\[
a=x_{i+1},\qquad b=x_i,\qquad c=x_{i-1}.
\]
The hypothesis of the problem gives
\[
y_i^2\ge y_{i-1}^2+6
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Since $y_0\ge0$, induction yields
\[
y_i\ge\sqrt{6i}.
\tag{3}
\]

Furthermore,
\[
3(x_0+x_1+\cdots+x_{n+1})
\ge
y_1+y_2+\cdots+y_n.
\]
Using (3) and Lemma 2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
3\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}x_i
&\ge
\sqrt6\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt i\\
&>
\sqrt6\cdot\frac23n^{3/2}\\
&=
3\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Dividing by $3$ proves the assertion.

SOLUTION 2:
Call an index
\[
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}
\]
\emph{good} if
\[
x_i\ge\sqrt{\frac{2i}{3}},
\]
and \emph{bad} otherwise.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
No two consecutive indices are bad.

\textit{Proof.}
Suppose that $j,j+1$ are consecutive bad indices with $j$ as small as possible. Since $0$ is good, we have $j>0$, and $j-1$ is good. Therefore
\[
x_jx_{j+1}
<
\frac23\sqrt{j(j+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_jx_{j+1}
&\ge x_{j-1}^2+1\\
&\ge\frac23(j-1)+1\\
&=
\frac23\cdot\frac{j+(j+1)}2\\
&\ge
\frac23\sqrt{j(j+1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting (1). $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
If $j\le n-1$ is good, then
\[
x_{j+1}+x_{j+2}
\ge
\sqrt{\frac23}
\left(\sqrt{j+1}+\sqrt{j+2}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
The recurrence inequality at index $j+1$ gives
\[
x_{j+1}x_{j+2}
\ge
x_j^2+1
\ge
\frac{2j}{3}+1
=
\frac23\left(j+\frac32\right).
\]
Hence
\[
x_{j+1}+x_{j+2}
\ge
2\sqrt{\frac23\left(j+\frac32\right)}.
\]
By concavity of the square-root function,
\[
2\sqrt{j+\frac32}
\ge
\sqrt{j+1}+\sqrt{j+2},
\]
which proves (2). $\square$

Put
\[
S_i=x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_i
\]
and
\[
T_i=
\sqrt{\frac23}
\left(\sqrt1+\sqrt2+\cdots+\sqrt i\right),
\]
with $S_0=T_0=0$.

\textbf{Lemma 3.}
If $i$ is good, then
\[
S_i\ge T_i.
\tag{3}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
We induct over the good indices. If $i-1$ is good, then
\[
S_i=S_{i-1}+x_i
\ge
T_{i-1}+\sqrt{\frac{2i}{3}}
=T_i.
\]
If $i-1$ is bad, then $i\ge2$ and $i-2$ is good by Lemma 1. Lemma 2, applied with $j=i-2$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_i
&=
S_{i-2}+x_{i-1}+x_i\\
&\ge
T_{i-2}
+
\sqrt{\frac23}
\left(\sqrt{i-1}+\sqrt i\right)\\
&=T_i.
\end{aligned}
\]
$\square$

By Lemma 1, at least one of $n,n+1$ is good. Lemma 3 therefore implies
\[
x_1+\cdots+x_{n+1}\ge T_n.
\]
Using Lemma 2 from Solution 1,
\[
T_n
>
\sqrt{\frac23}\cdot\frac23n^{3/2}
=
\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)^{3/2}.
\]
Since $x_0\ge0$, the desired inequality follows.
