ID: 0380

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Congruent Triangles, Similar Triangles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Homothety

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDE$ be a convex pentagon such that
\[
BC=DE.
\]
Assume that there is a point $T$ inside $ABCDE$ satisfying
\[
TB=TD,\qquad TC=TE,
\qquad
\angle TBA=\angle AET.
\]
The lines $CD$ and $CT$ meet line $AB$ at $P$ and $Q$, respectively, while the lines $CD$ and $DT$ meet line $AE$ at $R$ and $S$, respectively.

Assume that
\[
P,B,A,Q
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R,E,A,S
\]
occur in these orders on their respective lines.

Prove that
\[
P,S,Q,R
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 1:
We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$ whenever convenient.

The triangles
\[
TBC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
TDE
\]
are congruent by SSS, because
\[
TB=TD,\qquad TC=TE,\qquad BC=DE.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle BTC=\angle DTE.
\tag{1}
\]

Now consider triangles $TBQ$ and $TES$. Since $B,Q,A,P$ are collinear and $E,S,A,R$ are collinear,
\[
\angle TBQ=\angle TBA=\angle AET=\angle SET.
\]
Also, because $Q,T,C$ and $S,T,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle QTB
=
180^\circ-\angle BTC
=
180^\circ-\angle DTE
=
\angle ETS.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle TBQ\sim\triangle TES.
\]
It follows that
\[
\frac{TB}{TE}=\frac{TQ}{TS}.
\]
Using $TB=TD$ and $TE=TC$, we obtain
\[
TD\cdot TS=TC\cdot TQ.
\tag{2}
\]
Since $C,T,Q$ are collinear and $D,T,S$ are collinear, equation (2) proves that
\[
C,D,Q,S
\]
are concyclic.

The similarity above also gives
\[
\angle BQT=\angle TSE.
\tag{3}
\]
Using the cyclic quadrilateral $CDQS$, the orders of the points, and (3), we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle RPQ
&=
\angle RCQ-\angle PQC\\
&=
\angle DSQ-\angle DSR\\
&=
\angle RSQ.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\angle RPQ=\angle RSQ,
\]
which is exactly the cyclicity criterion for $P,Q,R,S$.

SOLUTION 2:
Again,
\[
\triangle TBC\cong\triangle TDE,
\tag{1}
\]
so in particular
\[
\angle BTC=\angle DTE.
\]

Let
\[
V=DT\cap BA,
\qquad
W=CT\cap EA.
\]
We first prove that
\[
V,S,Q,W
\]
are concyclic.

Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle VSW
&=\angle DSE\\
&=
180^\circ-\angle SED-\angle EDS\\
&=
180^\circ-\angle AET-\angle TED-\angle EDT\\
&=
180^\circ-\angle TBA-\angle TCB-\angle CBT\\
&=
180^\circ-\angle QCB-\angle CBQ\\
&=
\angle BQC\\
&=
\angle VQW.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $V,S,Q,W$ are concyclic, and therefore
\[
\angle WVQ=\angle WSQ.
\tag{2}
\]

Next,
\[
\angle VTB
=
180^\circ-\angle BTC-\angle CTD
=
180^\circ-\angle CTD-\angle DTE
=
\angle ETW,
\]
while
\[
\angle TBV=\angle TBA=\angle AET=\angle WET.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle VTB\sim\triangle WTE.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{VT}{WT}
=
\frac{BT}{ET}
=
\frac{DT}{CT},
\]
where the last equality follows from (1). Hence triangles $TVW$ and $TDC$ are similar, so
\[
VW\parallel CD.
\tag{3}
\]

Finally, since $P,R\in CD$, $P,Q,V\in AB$, and $R,S,W\in AE$, equations (2)--(3) yield
\[
\angle RPQ
=
\angle WVQ
=
\angle WSQ
=
\angle RSQ.
\]
Therefore $P,Q,R,S$ are concyclic.
