ID: 0362

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Polynomial Iteration, Finite Rings, Chinese Remainder Theorem, Prime-Power Residues, Derivatives Modulo Primes

STATEMENT:
For a polynomial $P(x)$ with integer coefficients, define its iterates by
\[
P^1(x)=P(x)
\]
and
\[
P^{m+1}(x)=P(P^m(x))
\qquad(m\ge1).
\]

Find all positive integers $n$ for which there exists a polynomial $P(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ such that, for every integer $m\ge1$, the numbers
\[
P^m(1),P^m(2),\ldots,P^m(n)
\]
leave exactly
\[
\left\lceil\frac{n}{2^m}\right\rceil
\]
distinct remainders modulo $n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{all primes and all powers of }2}.
\]

For a positive integer $\ell$, write
\[
\mathbb Z_\ell=\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z
\]
and define
\[
f_{m,\ell}=\left|P^m(\mathbb Z_\ell)\right|,
\qquad
f_{0,\ell}=\ell.
\]
The required condition is equivalent to
\[
f_{m+1,n}
=
\left\lceil\frac{f_{m,n}}2\right\rceil
\qquad(m\ge0).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, starting with $f_{0,n}=n$, recurrence (1) gives
\[
f_{m,n}=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2^m}\right\rceil.
\]

\textbf{1. Constructions for primes and powers of $2$.}

If
\[
n=2^k,
\]
take
\[
P(x)=2x.
\]
Then
\[
P^m(x)=2^mx,
\]
and its image modulo $2^k$ has
\[
2^{\max(k-m,0)}
=
\left\lceil\frac{2^k}{2^m}\right\rceil
\]
elements.

Now let
\[
n=p
\]
be an odd prime. Every function
\[
\mathbb Z_p\to\mathbb Z_p
\]
is represented by a polynomial over $\mathbb Z_p$, and its coefficients may be lifted to integers. Choose a polynomial representing the function
\[
x\longmapsto\left\lfloor\frac x2\right\rfloor
\qquad
(x=0,1,\ldots,p-1).
\]
Its $m$th iterate sends $x$ to
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2^m}\right\rfloor.
\]
The image therefore has
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{p-1}{2^m}\right\rfloor+1
=
\left\lceil\frac p{2^m}\right\rceil
\]
elements. Thus every prime and every power of $2$ works.

\textbf{2. An integer with two distinct prime divisors cannot work.}

Suppose
\[
n=ab,
\qquad
a,b>1,
\qquad
\gcd(a,b)=1.
\]
By the Chinese remainder theorem,
\[
f_{m,ab}=f_{m,a}f_{m,b}
\qquad(m\ge0).
\tag{2}
\]

For a map on a finite set, if
\[
f_{m,\ell}=f_{m+1,\ell},
\]
then the map $P$ permutes the set $P^m(\mathbb Z_\ell)$, so
\[
f_{j,\ell}=f_{m,\ell}
\qquad(j\ge m).
\tag{3}
\]
On the other hand, (1) implies that
\[
f_{m,n}=1
\]
for all sufficiently large $m$. Equation (2) then shows that both
\[
f_{m,a}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f_{m,b}
\]
eventually equal $1$.

Choose the least $m$ such that
\[
f_{m+1,a}=1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
f_{m+1,b}=1.
\]
Assume without loss of generality that
\[
f_{m+1,a}=1.
\]
Then
\[
f_{m,a}\ge2.
\]
Also $f_{m,b}>1$ by the minimality of $m$, and therefore (3) gives
\[
f_{m+1,b}<f_{m,b}.
\]
Using (1) and (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{m+1,ab}
&=f_{m+1,b}\\
&<f_{m,b}\\
&\le\frac{f_{m,a}f_{m,b}}2\\
&=\frac{f_{m,ab}}2\\
&\le
\left\lceil\frac{f_{m,ab}}2\right\rceil
=
f_{m+1,ab},
\end{aligned}
\]
a contradiction. Hence $n$ must be a prime power.

\textbf{3. No odd composite prime power works.}

Suppose
\[
n=p^k,
\qquad
p\ge3\text{ prime},
\qquad
k\ge2.
\]
For each residue $r\in\mathbb Z_p$, let
\[
S_r=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{p^k}:x\equiv r\pmod p\}.
\]
Thus
\[
|S_r|=p^{k-1}.
\]

We first establish a local image-size lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
For every $r\in\mathbb Z_p$, either
\[
|P(S_r)|=p^{k-1}
\]
or
\[
|P(S_r)|\le p^{k-2}.
\]

\textit{Proof.}
For integers $h$,
\[
P(r+h)=P(r)+hP'(r)+h^2Q(r,h)
\]
for some integer polynomial $Q$.

If
\[
p\mid P'(r),
\]
then for $h=ps$,
\[
P(r+ps)\equiv P(r)\pmod{p^2}.
\]
All elements of $P(S_r)$ are therefore congruent modulo $p^2$, and so
\[
|P(S_r)|\le p^{k-2}.
\]

If
\[
p\nmid P'(r),
\]
take $x,y\in S_r$. We may write
\[
P(x)-P(y)=(x-y)H(x,y),
\]
where
\[
H(x,y)\equiv P'(r)\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Thus $H(x,y)$ is invertible modulo $p^k$. If
\[
P(x)\equiv P(y)\pmod{p^k},
\]
then
\[
x\equiv y\pmod{p^k}.
\]
Hence $P$ is injective on $S_r$, and its image has $p^{k-1}$ elements. $\square$

By induction on $j$, every set $P^j(S_r)$ either has at most $p^{k-2}$ elements or is a complete residue class modulo $p$. Indeed, $P^0(S_r)=S_r$. A set with at most $p^{k-2}$ elements cannot grow under $P$, while if the current set equals some $S_q$, the lemma applies; in its full-size case the image is contained in $S_{P(q)}$ and therefore equals that complete residue class.

Since $f_{j,p^k}=1$ for sufficiently large $j$, choose the smallest positive integer $m$ such that
\[
|P^m(S_q)|\le p^{k-2}
\qquad\text{for every }q\in\mathbb Z_p.
\tag{4}
\]
By minimality, there is an $r$ such that
\[
|P^{m-1}(S_r)|=p^{k-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

Put
\[
A=P^m(\mathbb Z_{p^k})\setminus P^m(S_r),
\qquad
a=|A|.
\]
The image under $P$ of
\[
P^{m-1}(\mathbb Z_{p^k})\setminus P^{m-1}(S_r)
\]
contains $A$. Hence, by (4)--(5),
\[
a+p^{k-1}
\le
f_{m-1,p^k}
\le
2f_{m,p^k}
\le
2p^{k-2}+2a.
\]
Therefore
\[
(p-2)p^{k-2}\le a.
\tag{6}
\]

Consider now the map induced by $P$ on $\mathbb Z_p$. Its iterated image is eventually a singleton. That singleton is fixed by $P$, and every fixed residue belongs to every iterated image. Hence there is a unique residue
\[
t\in\mathbb Z_p
\]
with
\[
P(t)\equiv t\pmod p.
\tag{7}
\]
If
\[
p\nmid P'(t),
\]
the lemma shows that $P$ maps $S_t$ bijectively onto itself, contradicting the eventual collapse of the image modulo $p^k$. Thus
\[
p\mid P'(t)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|P(S_t)|\le p^{k-2}.
\tag{8}
\]

For $j\ge0$, let
\[
X_j=\{x\in\mathbb Z_p:P^j(x)=t\}.
\]
The sets
\[
X_0\subseteq X_1\subseteq X_2\subseteq\cdots
\]
eventually become all of $\mathbb Z_p$. Before that happens, every inclusion is strict: if
\[
X_j=X_{j+1},
\]
then taking inverse images under $P$ shows that all later sets are equal as well. Therefore either
\[
f_{m-1,p}=1,
\tag{9}
\]
or
\[
|X_{m-1}|\ge m.
\tag{10}
\]

If (9) holds, then
\[
P^{m-1}(\mathbb Z_{p^k})\subseteq S_t.
\]
By (5), the subset $P^{m-1}(S_r)$ already has $p^{k-1}$ elements, so it equals $S_t$. Hence
\[
P^m(\mathbb Z_{p^k})=P^m(S_r),
\]
which gives $a=0$, contradicting (6).

We are left with (10). Put
\[
X=X_{m-1},
\]
let $Y$ be the union of the residue classes $S_x$ with $x\in X$, and put
\[
Z=\mathbb Z_{p^k}\setminus Y.
\]
Then
\[
P^{m-1}(Y)\subseteq S_t,
\]
so by (8),
\[
|P^m(Y)|\le p^{k-2}.
\tag{11}
\]
The set $Z$ is the union of at most $p-m$ residue classes, and (4) gives
\[
|P^m(Z)|\le(p-m)p^{k-2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Because $P^m(S_r)$ is nonempty,
\[
a<f_{m,p^k}.
\]
Combining (6), (11), and (12),
\[
(p-2)p^{k-2}
\le a
<
f_{m,p^k}
\le
(p-m+1)p^{k-2}.
\]
Thus
\[
m<3.
\tag{13}
\]

It follows from (4) and (13) that
\[
|P^2(S_q)|\le p^{k-2}
\qquad(q\in\mathbb Z_p).
\]
Therefore
\[
f_{2,p^k}\le p^{k-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
But the required condition gives
\[
f_{2,p^k}
=
\left\lceil\frac{p^k}{4}\right\rceil.
\]
For $p\ge5$, this is greater than $p^{k-1}$, contradicting (14).

It remains to exclude $p=3$. Let $t$ be the residue from (7). As above,
\[
3\mid P'(t).
\tag{15}
\]
We claim that, for every
\[
1\le j\le k-1,
\]
all elements of $P^j(S_t)$ are congruent modulo $3^{j+1}$.

For $j=1$, this follows from the Taylor expansion and (15). If it holds for $j$, then any two elements $u,v\in P^j(S_t)$ satisfy
\[
3^{j+1}\mid u-v
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
u\equiv v\equiv t\pmod3.
\]
Writing
\[
P(u)-P(v)=(u-v)H(u,v),
\]
we have
\[
H(u,v)\equiv P'(t)\equiv0\pmod3.
\]
Thus
\[
3^{j+2}\mid P(u)-P(v),
\]
which proves the induction. In particular,
\[
|P^{k-1}(S_t)|=1.
\tag{16}
\]

The image sizes of the induced map on $\mathbb Z_3$ eventually equal $1$. By the stability observation (3),
\[
f_{1,3}\le2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f_{2,3}=1.
\]
Hence $P^2(\mathbb Z_3)$ is the singleton $\{t\}$, and therefore
\[
P^2(\mathbb Z_{3^k})\subseteq S_t.
\]
Using (16),
\[
\left|P^{k+1}(\mathbb Z_{3^k})\right|
\le
\left|P^{k-1}(S_t)\right|
=
1.
\]
The required condition would therefore imply
\[
\left\lceil\frac{3^k}{2^{k+1}}\right\rceil=1,
\]
and hence
\[
3^k\le2^{k+1}.
\]
This is false for every $k\ge2$.

Thus no odd prime power other than a prime can work. Combining all three parts, the valid integers are exactly the primes and the powers of $2$.
