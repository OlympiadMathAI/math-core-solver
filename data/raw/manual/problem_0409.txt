ID: 0409

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Hamiltonian Paths, Edge Colourings, Recolouring Lemmas, Extremal Graph Theory

STATEMENT:
The Imomi archipelago consists of $n\ge2$ islands. Between every pair of distinct islands there is one ferry line running in both directions, and each ferry line is operated by one of $k$ companies.

It is known that if any one company closes all of its ferry lines, then it becomes impossible for a traveller, regardless of the starting island, to visit every island exactly once.

Determine the maximum possible value of $k$ in terms of $n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{k=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}.
\]

We regard the islands as the vertices of the complete graph $K_n$, and companies as edge colours. The condition says that every Hamiltonian path contains at least one edge of every colour. Call such a colouring \emph{good}.

\textbf{Construction.}

Put
\[
k=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor.
\]
Label the vertices
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n.
\]
Assign vertex $A_r$ the colour
\[
c_r=
\min\bigl(\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor+1,k\bigr).
\]
For $r<s$, colour edge $A_rA_s$ with colour $c_r$.

We show that every Hamiltonian path contains every colour.

There are
\[
n-2^{k-1}+1
\]
vertices of vertex-colour $k$, and only
\[
2^{k-1}-1
\]
other vertices. Since $n\ge2^k$, two vertices of colour $k$ must be adjacent in every Hamiltonian path. Their connecting edge has colour $k$.

Now fix $i<k$. There are
\[
2^{i-1}
\]
vertices of vertex-colour $i$, and
\[
2^{i-1}-1
\]
vertices of smaller colour. An edge incident with a vertex of colour $i$ avoids edge-colour $i$ only when its other endpoint has smaller vertex-colour. Therefore, if a Hamiltonian path avoided colour $i$, its colour-$i$ vertices would have to alternate with all the smaller-coloured vertices, beginning and ending with colour $i$. This already uses all those vertices and leaves no place for any vertex of colour greater than $i$, which exists because $i<k$. This is impossible.

Thus the construction is good and proves
\[
k\ge\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor.
\]

\textbf{Upper bound.}

Consider an arbitrary good colouring of $K_n$ with colours
\[
1,2,\ldots,k.
\]
For a vertex $v$, let
\[
d_i(v)
\]
be the number of incident edges of colour $i$, and put
\[
m(v)=\max_i d_i(v).
\]

We first establish three recolouring lemmas.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
Let $AB$ be an edge of colour $i$. If
\[
d_i(A)+d_i(B)\le n-1,
\tag{1}
\]
then recolouring $AB$ with any other colour preserves goodness.

\textit{Proof.}
Suppose recolouring $AB$ destroys goodness. Then, in the new colouring, there is a Hamiltonian path missing colour $i$. In the original colouring, $AB$ was therefore the unique edge of colour $i$ on this path.

Write the path as
\[
A_s-\cdots-A_1-A_0-B_0-B_1-\cdots-B_t,
\]
where
\[
A_0=A,\qquad B_0=B,\qquad s+t+2=n.
\]

The edge $B_0A_s$ must have colour $i$; otherwise
\[
A_0-A_1-\cdots-A_s-B_0-B_1-\cdots-B_t
\]
would be a Hamiltonian path with no edge of colour $i$. Similarly, $A_0B_t$ has colour $i$.

For each $0\le p<s$, at least one of
\[
B_0A_p,\qquad A_0A_{p+1}
\]
has colour $i$. Otherwise, reversing the initial portion of the path and reconnecting through these two edges produces a Hamiltonian path avoiding colour $i$.

Likewise, for each $0\le q<t$, at least one of
\[
A_0B_q,\qquad B_0B_{q+1}
\]
has colour $i$.

These requirements involve every edge incident with $A_0$ or $B_0$ exactly once, with the common edge $A_0B_0$ counted once from each endpoint. Summing them gives
\[
d_i(A)+d_i(B)\ge(s+1)+(t+1)=n,
\]
contradicting (1). $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
Let $AB$ have colour $j$. Suppose
\[
m(A)\ge m(B)
\]
and, for some colour $i\ne j$,
\[
d_i(A)=m(A).
\]
Then recolouring $AB$ with colour $i$ preserves goodness.

\textit{Proof.}
Since colour $i$ already contributes $m(A)$ edges at $A$,
\[
d_j(A)\le n-1-m(A),
\]
while
\[
d_j(B)\le m(B).
\]
Hence
\[
d_j(A)+d_j(B)
\le
n-1-m(A)+m(B)
\le n-1.
\]
Lemma 1 applies. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 3.}
Let $S$ be a nonempty set of vertices. Choose $A\in S$ with
\[
m(A)\ge m(B)
\qquad(B\in S),
\]
and choose a colour $i$ satisfying
\[
d_i(A)=m(A).
\]
One may recolour every edge $AB$ with
\[
B\in S\setminus\{A\}
\]
to colour $i$ while preserving goodness.

\textit{Proof.}
Recolour the relevant edges one at a time. Lemma 2 applies at every step. Each recolouring increases both $m(A)$ and $d_i(A)$ by $1$, while every other $m(B)$ increases by at most $1$. Thus $A$ remains a maximizer of $m$ on $S$, and colour $i$ continues to attain $m(A)$. $\square$

Apply Lemma 3 first to all vertices. We obtain a vertex $A_1$ all of whose incident edges have one colour $c_1$. Next apply the lemma to the remaining vertices, obtaining $A_2$ and a colour $c_2$, without changing edges incident with $A_1$. Continue.

At the end, the vertices are ordered
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n
\]
and assigned colours
\[
c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_n
\]
so that
\[
\text{edge }A_uA_v\text{ has colour }c_u
\qquad(u<v).
\tag{2}
\]
The colouring is still good.

Fix an edge-colour $i$. We claim that some initial segment
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_p
\]
contains more than $p/2$ vertices whose assigned colour is $i$.

Suppose not. Let
\[
A_{x_1},\ldots,A_{x_t}
\]
be the vertices assigned colour $i$, with
\[
x_1<\cdots<x_t,
\]
and let
\[
A_{y_1},\ldots,A_{y_s}
\]
be all other vertices, with
\[
y_1<\cdots<y_s.
\]
The assumption gives
\[
t\le s
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y_j<x_j
\quad(1\le j\le t).
\]
Then
\[
A_{x_1}-A_{y_1}-A_{x_2}-A_{y_2}-\cdots
-A_{x_t}-A_{y_t}-A_{y_{t+1}}-\cdots-A_{y_s}
\]
is a Hamiltonian path containing no edge of colour $i$: by (2), every edge in this path receives the assigned colour of a smaller-indexed vertex, which is never $i$. This contradicts goodness.

For each colour $i$, let $p_i$ be the smallest positive integer for which more than half of
\[
A_1,\ldots,A_{p_i}
\]
have assigned colour $i$. At the first such index, $A_{p_i}$ itself has colour $i$, so the $p_i$ are pairwise distinct. Relabel the edge-colours so that
\[
p_1<p_2<\cdots<p_k.
\tag{3}
\]

For every $j\le i$, the first $p_i$ vertices contain at least
\[
\left\lceil\frac{p_j+1}{2}\right\rceil
\]
vertices of colour $j$. These colour classes are disjoint, so
\[
p_i
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^i
\left\lceil\frac{p_j+1}{2}\right\rceil.
\tag{4}
\]
Induction on $i$ now gives
\[
p_i\ge2^i-1.
\tag{5}
\]
For $i=1$, this is clear. Assume it holds for all smaller indices. From (4),
\[
p_i-
\left\lceil\frac{p_i+1}{2}\right\rceil
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}
\left\lceil\frac{p_j+1}{2}\right\rceil
\ge
1+2+\cdots+2^{i-2}
=
2^{i-1}-1.
\]
The left-hand side is
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{p_i-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
which forces
\[
p_i\ge2^i-1.
\]

Thus
\[
n\ge p_k\ge2^k-1.
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to exclude equality
\[
n=2^k-1.
\]
Suppose equality held. Then
\[
p_k=2^k-1.
\]
In (4) with $i=k$, the final summand equals $2^{k-1}$. The lower bounds (5) give
\[
\left\lceil\frac{p_j+1}{2}\right\rceil\ge2^{j-1}
\qquad(j<k).
\]
Since these lower bounds already sum to $2^{k-1}-1$, equality must hold for every $j<k$. Together with (5), this yields
\[
p_i=2^i-1
\qquad(1\le i\le k).
\]
Moreover, equality in (4) for every prefix forces the assigned colours to occur in consecutive blocks:
\[
\begin{aligned}
& A_1 &&\text{has colour }1,\\
& A_2,A_3 &&\text{have colour }2,\\
& A_4,\ldots,A_7 &&\text{have colour }3,\\
&\ \vdots\\
& A_{2^{i-1}},\ldots,A_{2^i-1}
&&\text{have colour }i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This follows inductively because, in the prefix of length $2^i-1$, the required minimum numbers
\[
1,2,4,\ldots,2^{i-1}
\]
already sum to the full prefix length.

Let
\[
h=2^{k-1}.
\]
Using (7), the Hamiltonian path
\[
A_h-A_1-A_{h+1}-A_2-\cdots
-A_{2h-2}-A_{h-1}-A_{2h-1}
\]
alternates vertices of colour $k$ with smaller-coloured vertices. Every edge receives the colour of its smaller-indexed endpoint, so this path has no edge of colour $k$. This contradicts goodness.

Therefore equality in (6) is impossible, and
\[
n\ge2^k.
\]
Hence
\[
k\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor.
\]

Combining the upper construction and lower bound gives
\[
\boxed{k=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}.
\]
