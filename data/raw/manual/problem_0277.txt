ID: 0277

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Arrangements of Lines, Planar Graphs, Connected Components, Induction

STATEMENT:
King Arthur builds a labyrinth $L$ in the plane from $n$ infinite straight-line walls. No two walls are parallel and no three are concurrent. Merlin paints one side of every wall red and the other side blue.

At each intersection of two walls, exactly two diagonally opposite corners have one red and one blue side. A two-way door connects those two corners. Knights may pass through doors but not through walls.

Let $k(L)$ be the largest integer $k$ such that, however Merlin paints the walls, Morgana can always place at least $k$ knights in regions of the labyrinth so that no two can ever meet. Determine all possible values of $k(L)$ for labyrinths with $n$ walls.

SOLUTION 1:
For every labyrinth,
\[
\boxed{k(L)=n+1}.
\]
The $n$ lines divide the plane into
\[
1+\binom{n+1}{2}
\tag{1}
\]
regions. Indeed, when the $n$th line is added, its $n-1$ intersections divide it into $n$ pieces, each splitting one old region.

Form the graph whose vertices are regions and whose edges are doors. There are exactly $\binom n2$ doors. Every edge reduces the number of connected components by at most one, so the graph has at least
\[
1+\binom{n+1}{2}-\binom n2=n+1
\]
components. Thus Morgana can always place at least $n+1$ mutually isolated knights.

For the reverse bound, choose coordinates so that no wall is horizontal or vertical. Paint the west-facing side of every wall red and the east-facing side blue. Label each region by the number of walls whose east side contains it. The labels are $0,1,\ldots,n$.

At an intersection, the two regions connected by the door have the same label, so doors never connect different labels. We show all regions with one fixed label $i$ are connected. There is exactly one such region unbounded to the north. From any other region, move north, following a boundary wall east or west when needed. Since regions are convex and no wall is horizontal, a region bounded above has a unique northernmost vertex. The door there leads to a region of the same label farther north. Repetition reaches the unique north-unbounded region. Therefore each label is exactly one component.

This coloring has $n+1$ components, proving $k(L)=n+1$.

SOLUTION 2:
The lower bound follows from the region-and-door count in Solution 1. We give another construction attaining it.

First build a convenient arrangement. Insert the lines in order of increasing positive slope, placing each new line so far to the right that all intersections of earlier lines lie to its left. Orient each line from lower left to upper right, painting its left side blue and right side red.

Call a region right-unbounded if its $x$-coordinate is unbounded above. We claim inductively that after $m$ lines there are $m+1$ door-components and each contains exactly one right-unbounded region. The statement is clear for $m=0$. The next line crosses all earlier lines to the right of their mutual intersections, hence cuts exactly the current right-unbounded regions. Inspecting the colors at these successive crossings shows that every old component retains one right-unbounded region and one new component with one such region is created.

Now continuously translate and rotate the lines, one at a time, into the positions of the given labyrinth, never allowing two lines to become parallel. The combinatorial arrangement changes only when a moving line passes through the intersection of two others. Immediately before and after such an event, only the six local regions of those three lines are rearranged. With the chosen left-blue/right-red orientation, a direct local check shows that the number of door-components is unchanged.

Thus the final coloring of the given labyrinth still has exactly $n+1$ components. Hence again $k(L)=n+1$.
