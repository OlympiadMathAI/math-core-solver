ID: 0119

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Integer-Valued Functions, Translation Equations, Linearization

STATEMENT:
Determine all functions $f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ satisfying
\[
f(f(m)+n)+f(m)=f(n)+f(3m)+2014
\tag{1}
\]
for all integers $m,n$.

SOLUTION 1:
The only solution is
\[
\boxed{f(n)=2n+1007}.
\]

Set
\[
C=1007
\]
and define
\[
g(m)=f(3m)-f(m)+2C.
\]
Then (1) becomes
\[
f(f(m)+n)=g(m)+f(n)
\tag{2}
\]
for all integers $m,n$. Repeatedly applying (2), forward and backward, gives
\[
f(tf(m)+n)=tg(m)+f(n)
\tag{3}
\]
for all $m,n,t\in\mathbb Z$.

Apply (3) first with
\[
(m,n,t)=(r,0,f(0))
\]
and then with
\[
(m,n,t)=(0,0,f(r)).
\]
Both left-hand sides are $f(f(r)f(0))$, so
\[
f(0)g(r)=f(r)g(0).
\tag{4}
\]
Since
\[
g(0)=2C\ne0,
\]
we cannot have $f(0)=0$: otherwise (4) would imply $f(r)=0$ for every $r$, contradicting (1). Thus
\[
g(r)=\alpha f(r)
\tag{5}
\]
for all $r$, where
\[
\alpha=\frac{g(0)}{f(0)}\ne0.
\]

From the definition of $g$ and (5),
\[
f(3m)=(1+\alpha)f(m)-2C.
\]
Put
\[
\beta=\frac{2C}{\alpha}.
\]
Then
\[
f(3m)-\beta=(1+\alpha)\bigl(f(m)-\beta\bigr),
\]
and therefore
\[
f(3^km)-\beta=(1+\alpha)^k\bigl(f(m)-\beta\bigr)
\tag{6}
\]
for every $k\ge0$.

There is a value $d=f(a)$ not divisible by $3$. Indeed, if every value of $f$ were divisible by $3$, then the two sides of (1) would be incongruent modulo $3$, because $2014\equiv1\pmod3$.

Using (3), (5), and $d=f(a)$, we obtain
\[
f(n+td)=f(n)+\alpha td
\tag{7}
\]
for all integers $n,t$.

Choose a positive integer $k$ such that
\[
d\mid 3^k-1.
\]
This is possible because $\gcd(3,d)=1$; when $|d|=1$, take $k=1$, and otherwise one may use Euler's theorem modulo $|d|$. For each $m\in\mathbb Z$, equation (7) gives
\[
f(3^km)=f(m)+\alpha(3^k-1)m.
\tag{8}
\]
Comparing (6) and (8), we get
\[
\bigl((1+\alpha)^k-1\bigr)\bigl(f(m)-\beta\bigr)
=\alpha(3^k-1)m.
\tag{9}
\]
The factor $(1+\alpha)^k-1$ is nonzero, because the right-hand side of (9) is nonzero for any $m\ne0$. Hence
\[
f(m)=Am+\beta
\]
for all $m$, where
\[
A=\frac{\alpha(3^k-1)}{(1+\alpha)^k-1}.
\]

Substituting $f(m)=Am+\beta$ into (1) and comparing coefficients gives
\[
A^2=2A,
\qquad
A\beta=2C.
\]
Since $C\ne0$, we must have
\[
A=2,
\qquad
\beta=C=1007.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(n)=2n+1007.
\]

A direct substitution verifies that this function satisfies (1).
