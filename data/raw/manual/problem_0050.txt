ID: 0050

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Simson Line, Miquel's Theorem, Pascal's Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with circumcircle $\omega$, and let $t$ be a tangent line to $\omega$. Let $t_a,t_b,t_c$ be the reflections of $t$ in the lines $BC,CA,AB$, respectively. Prove that the circumcircle of the triangle determined by the three lines $t_a,t_b,t_c$ is tangent to $\omega$.

SOLUTION 1:
All angles in this solution are oriented modulo $180^\circ$. Let $T$ be the point where $t$ touches $\omega$, and put
\[
A'=t_b\cap t_c,
\qquad
B'=t_a\cap t_c,
\qquad
C'=t_a\cap t_b.
\]
Choose $A''\in\omega$ so that $A$ is the midpoint of the appropriate arc $TA''$, that is,
\[
\widehat{TA}=\widehat{AA''}.
\]
Define $B''$ and $C''$ analogously.

Because $B$ and $C$ are the midpoints of the corresponding arcs $TB''$ and $TC''$, an oriented-angle calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(t,B''C'')
&=\angle(t,TC'')+\angle(TC'',B''C'')\\
&=2\angle(t,TC)+2\angle(TC'',BC'')\\
&=2\bigl(\angle(t,TC)+\angle(TC,BC)\bigr)\\
&=2\angle(t,BC)\\
&=\angle(t,t_a).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
t_a\parallel B''C''.
\]
Similarly,
\[
t_b\parallel A''C'',
\qquad
t_c\parallel A''B''.
\tag{1}
\]
Therefore the triangles $A'B'C'$ and $A''B''C''$ have pairwise parallel corresponding sides; they are either homothetic or translates. We prove that they are homothetic with a center lying on $\omega$.

We need two claims.

\textbf{Claim 1.} The point
\[
X=B''C\cap BC''
\]
lies on $t_a$.

Indeed, because $C$ is the midpoint of the arc $TC''$, the rays $CT$ and $CB''$ are mirror images in $BC$; similarly, because $B$ is the midpoint of the arc $TB''$, the rays $BT$ and $BC''$ are mirror images in $BC$. Hence reflection in $BC$ sends the intersection $T=CT\cap BT$ to $X=CB''\cap BC''$. Since the same reflection sends $t$ to $t_a$, we get $X\in t_a$.

\textbf{Claim 2.} The point
\[
I=BB'\cap CC'
\]
lies on $\omega$.

Assume first that $t$ is not parallel to a side of $ABC$; the parallel cases follow as limiting cases. Let
\[
D=t\cap BC,
\qquad
E=t\cap CA,
\qquad
F=t\cap AB.
\]
Because $t_a$ and $t_c$ are the reflections of $t$ in $BC$ and $AB$, the point $B$ is an incenter or an excenter of triangle $B'DF$. The corresponding angle-bisector relations yield
\[
\angle(B'B,B'C')=90^\circ-\angle(BC,AB).
\tag{2}
\]
Analogously,
\[
\angle(C'C,B'C')=90^\circ-\angle(BC,AC).
\tag{3}
\]
Since $B,I,B'$ and $C,I,C'$ are collinear, (2) and (3) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(BI,CI)
&=\angle(B'B,B'C')+\angle(B'C',C'C)\\
&=\angle(BC,AC)-\angle(BC,AB)\\
&=\angle(AB,AC).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $A,B,I,C$ are cyclic, proving Claim 2.

Let $K\ne B''$ be the second intersection of the line $B'B''$ with $\omega$. Apply Pascal's theorem to the inscribed hexagon
\[
K,B'',C,I,B,C''.
\]
The intersections of its three pairs of opposite sides are
\[
KB''\cap IB=B',
\]
\[
B''C\cap BC''=X,
\]
and
\[
CI\cap C''K.
\]
Pascal's theorem says that these three points are collinear. By Claim 1, the line $B'X$ is $t_a$. Since
\[
C'=t_a\cap CI,
\]
the third intersection is $C'$. Therefore
\[
K,C',C''\text{ are collinear}.
\]
By construction,
\[
K,B',B''\text{ are collinear}.
\]
Together with (1), this shows that $K$ is the center of the homothety mapping triangle $A'B'C'$ to triangle $A''B''C''$.

The circumcircle of $A''B''C''$ is $\omega$. Under this homothety it is mapped to the circumcircle of $A'B'C'$. Since the homothety center $K$ lies on $\omega$ and is fixed, the two circles pass through $K$ and have the same tangent there. Hence they are tangent.

SOLUTION 2:
Continue to use oriented angles modulo $180^\circ$. Again let $T$ be the point of tangency of $t$ with $\omega$, and define
\[
A'=t_b\cap t_c,
\qquad
B'=t_a\cap t_c,
\qquad
C'=t_a\cap t_b.
\]
Let $X,Y,Z$ be the reflections of $T$ in $BC,CA,AB$, respectively.

The perpendicular projections of $T$ onto the three sides of $ABC$ lie on the Simson line of $T$. Reflection in a side sends $T$ to a point obtained from the corresponding projection by a homothety of ratio $2$ centered at $T$. Hence
\[
X,Y,Z\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, from the definitions of the reflected lines,
\[
X\in B'C',
\qquad
Y\in C'A',
\qquad
Z\in A'B'.
\tag{2}
\]

Put
\[
\alpha=\angle(t,TC)=\angle(BT,BC).
\]
Reflection in $BC$ and $AC$ gives
\[
\angle(BC,BX)=\alpha
\]
and
\[
\angle(XC,XC')=\angle(YC,YC')=\alpha.
\]
Thus the points
\[
X,Y,C,C'
\]
are cyclic; denote their circle by $\omega_c$. Define analogously $\omega_a$ as the circle through $Y,Z,A,A'$, and $\omega_b$ as the circle through $Z,X,B,B'$.
Let $\omega'$ be the circumcircle of $A'B'C'$.

Apply Miquel's theorem to the four lines
\[
A'B',\quad B'C',\quad C'A',\quad XYZ.
\]
Using (1) and (2), it follows that the four circles
\[
\omega',\quad\omega_a,\quad\omega_b,\quad\omega_c
\]
have a common point; call it $K$.

We first show that
\[
K\in\omega.
\tag{3}
\]
Since $X$ and $Z$ are reflections of $T$ in lines through $B$,
\[
XB=TB=ZB.
\]
Thus $B$ is the midpoint of one of the arcs $XZ$ of $\omega_b$, and consequently
\[
\angle(KB,KX)=\angle(XZ,XB).
\tag{4}
\]
Likewise, $CX=CT=CY$, because $X$ and $Y$ are reflections of $T$ in lines through $C$. Thus $C$ is the midpoint of one of the arcs $XY$ of $\omega_c$, and
\[
\angle(KX,KC)=\angle(XC,XY).
\tag{5}
\]
Adding (4) and (5), and using the collinearity of $X,Y,Z$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(KB,KC)
&=\angle(XZ,XB)+\angle(XC,XZ)\\
&=\angle(XC,XB)\\
&=\angle(TB,TC),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final equality follows from reflection in $BC$. Thus $K,B,C,T$ are cyclic. Since $B,C,T\in\omega$, relation (3) follows.

Let $k$ be the tangent to $\omega$ at $K$. We prove that $k$ is also tangent to $\omega'$ at $K$. By the tangent-chord theorem in $\omega$, and then cyclicity in $\omega_c$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(k,KC')
&=\angle(k,KC)+\angle(KC,KC')\\
&=\angle(KB,BC)+\angle(XC,XC').
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Now
\[
\angle(KB,BC)
=\angle(KB,BX)-\angle(BC,BX)
=\angle(KB,BX)-\alpha,
\]
while $\angle(XC,XC')=\alpha$. Since $K,B,B',X$ are cyclic on $\omega_b$,
\[
\angle(KB,BX)=\angle(KB',B'X).
\]
Using $B',X,C'\in t_a$, equation (6) becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(k,KC')
&=\angle(KB',B'X)-\alpha+\alpha\\
&=\angle(KB',B'C').
\end{aligned}
\]
This is precisely the tangent-chord criterion for $k$ to be tangent to $\omega'$ at $K$. Therefore the circumcircle of the triangle formed by $t_a,t_b,t_c$ is tangent to $\omega$.
