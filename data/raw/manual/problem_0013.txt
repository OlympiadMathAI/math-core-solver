ID: 0013

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Tournaments, Double Counting

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge4$ players take part in a round-robin tennis tournament with no draws. A set of four players is called \emph{bad} if one of them loses to the other three, while those three form a directed cycle among themselves. Suppose there is no bad set of four players.

For player $i$, let $w_i$ and $\ell_i$ be the numbers of wins and losses. Prove that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(w_i-\ell_i)^3\ge0.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For a tournament $T$, write
\[
S(T)=\sum_i(w_i-\ell_i)^3.
\]
We first check the result for a tournament on four vertices. Let
\[
a_1\ge a_2\ge a_3\ge a_4
\]
be the win counts. Their sum is $6$, so $a_4\le1$.

If $a_4=0$, the forbidden score sequence $(2,2,2,0)$ would be exactly a bad four-set. Thus the only possible sequence is $(3,2,1,0)$, which gives $S(T)=0$.

If $a_4=1$, the possible score sequences are $(3,1,1,1)$ and $(2,2,1,1)$. They give, respectively,
\[
3^3+3(-1)^3>0
\]
and
\[
1^3+1^3+(-1)^3+(-1)^3=0.
\]
Hence every four-player subtournament has nonnegative $S$.

Now consider the original tournament. For $i\ne j$, put
\[
\varepsilon_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if }i\text{ defeats }j,\\
-1,&\text{if }j\text{ defeats }i.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
w_i-\ell_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\varepsilon_{ij},
\]
so
\[
S(T)=\sum_{i}
\sum_{j_1,j_2,j_3\ne i}
\varepsilon_{ij_1}\varepsilon_{ij_2}\varepsilon_{ij_3}.
\tag{1}
\]
Separate the terms in which $i,j_1,j_2,j_3$ are all distinct from those in which two of $j_1,j_2,j_3$ coincide. In every repeated-index term, a factor $\varepsilon_{ij}^2=1$ disappears, so all such terms together are a fixed multiplicity times
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}\varepsilon_{ij}=0,
\]
because $\varepsilon_{ij}=-\varepsilon_{ji}$. Hence (1) is simply the sum of the terms involving four distinct vertices.

Each such term belongs to exactly one four-vertex subset. Therefore
\[
S(T)=\sum_{X\in\binom{V(T)}4}S(T[X]),
\]
where $T[X]$ is the subtournament induced by $X$. Every summand is nonnegative by the four-player case, so $S(T)\ge0$.

SOLUTION 2:
In any set of players, call a player a \emph{local champion} if that player defeats every other member, and a \emph{local loser} if that player loses to every other member. A set has at most one local champion and at most one local loser.

For a fixed $k$, player $i$ is the local champion of exactly
\[
\binom{w_i}{k-1}
\]
sets of $k$ players, and is the local loser of exactly
\[
\binom{\ell_i}{k-1}
\]
such sets.

For $k=2$, every game has one winner and one loser, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(w_i-\ell_i)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
For $k=3$, every three-player tournament is either a directed cycle, with neither a local champion nor a local loser, or is transitive, with exactly one of each. Hence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n
\left(\binom{w_i}{2}-\binom{\ell_i}{2}\right)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
For $k=4$, the hypothesis says that whenever a four-set has a local loser, it also has a local champion. Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n
\left(\binom{w_i}{3}-\binom{\ell_i}{3}\right)\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

We use the identity
\[
(x-y)^3
=24\left(\binom{x}{3}-\binom{y}{3}\right)
+24\left(\binom{x}{2}-\binom{y}{2}\right)
-\bigl(3(x+y)^2-4\bigr)(x-y).
\tag{4}
\]
For each player,
\[
w_i+\ell_i=n-1.
\]
Apply (4) with $x=w_i$ and $y=\ell_i$, then sum over $i$. The contribution from the first term is nonnegative by (3), the second contribution is zero by (2), and the last contribution is zero by (1), because its coefficient is the same for every player. Thus
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(w_i-\ell_i)^3\ge0.
\]
