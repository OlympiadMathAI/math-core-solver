TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS:  

STATEMENT: $n \geq 4$ players participated in a tennis tournament. Any two players have played exactly one game, and there was no tie game. We call a company of four players $bad$ if one player was defeated by the other three players, and each of these three players won a game and lost another game among themselves. Suppose that there is no bad company in this tournament. Let $w_i$ and $l_i$ be respectively the number of wins and losses of the $i$-th player. Prove that\[\sum^n_{i=1} \left(w_i - l_i\right)^3 \geq 0.\]

SOLUTION 1: Lemma 1: $\sum w_i = \sum l_i$
Double count. Both are equal to the number of edges

Lemma 2: $\sum w_i^2 = \sum l_i^2$
Double count. By counting the number of subgraphs size-3 that are NOT 3 cycles, we obtain $\sum \binom{w_i}{2}=\sum \binom{l_i}{2},$ and the result follows from applying lemma 1.

View the tennis tournament as a directed graph. Then $w_i,l_i$ are out and in-degree respectively.
Consider all groups of 4 players, and consider the subgraph they make. Let $S$ be the set of their out-degrees. Note, S must be one of
$\{3,2,1,0\},$
$\{3,1,1,1\}$
$\{2,2,1,1\}$
$\{2,2,2,0\}$
Let $a,b,c,d$ be the number of the first,second, third, and fourth type, respectively. Notice the condition tells us $d=0.$

Notice $a+b$ is exactly the number of times there are 3 outgoing edges from a vertex. Thus,
$\sum \binom{w_i}{3} = a+b$
Similarly,
$\sum \binom{l_i}{3} = a+d$
$\sum \binom{w_i}{2}*l_i = a+2c+3d$
$\sum \binom{l_i}{2}*w_i = a+3b+2c$
By lemmas 1 and 2,
$b=b-d=(a+b)-(a+d)=\sum\frac{w_i^3-l_i^3}{6}$
$\sum(w_i^3-l_i^3)=6b$

$3b=(a+2c+3b)-(a+2c+3d)=\sum \frac{(w_il_i^2-w_il_i)-(w_i^2l_i-w_il_i)}{2}=\sum \frac{w_il_i(l_i-w_i)}{2}$
$\sum(-3w_i^2l_i+3w_il_i^2)=18b$
Adding the two results gives us
$\sum (w_i-l_i)^3=24b \ge 0,$ as desired.
