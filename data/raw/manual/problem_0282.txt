ID: 0282

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Metric Scales, Induction, Weighted Counting, Extremal Set Theory

STATEMENT:
For two distinct real numbers $x,y$, define $D(x,y)$ to be the unique integer $d$ satisfying
\[
2^d\le |x-y|<2^{d+1}.
\]
Given a set $F$ of real numbers and an element $x\in F$, the \emph{scales of $x$ in $F$} are the values
\[
D(x,y)
\qquad(y\in F,\ y\ne x).
\]
Let $k$ be a positive integer. Suppose that every member of $F$ has at most $k$ different scales in $F$. Determine the maximum possible value of $|F|$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{2^k}.
\]

The bound is attained by
\[
F=\{0,1,2,\ldots,2^k-1\}.
\]
Every nonzero distance between two members lies between $1$ and $2^k-1$, so every scale belongs to
\[
\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}.
\]

We prove the upper bound. It is enough to consider finite sets: if an infinite set, or a finite set larger than $2^k$, satisfied the condition, then any finite subset of more than $2^k$ elements would still give each of its members at most $k$ scales.

For a finite set $S$ and $x\in S$, let $r_S(x)$ be the number of scales of $x$ in $S$. Define
\[
w(S)=\sum_{x\in S}2^{-r_S(x)}.
\]
We prove the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} For every nonempty finite set $S$ of real numbers,
\[
w(S)\le1.
\tag{1}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
We use induction on $|S|$. If $S=\{x\}$, then $r_S(x)=0$ and $w(S)=1$.

Now let
\[
x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_m
\]
be the elements of $S$, with $m\ge2$, and let $d$ be the smallest scale occurring between two members of $S$. Some adjacent pair has scale $d$. Furthermore, if $j-i>1$, then
\[
|x_j-x_i|
=(x_{i+1}-x_i)+(x_j-x_{i+1})
\ge2^d+2^d=2^{d+1},
\]
so
\[
D(x_i,x_j)>d.
\tag{2}
\]

Choose a maximal consecutive block
\[
x_p,x_{p+1},\ldots,x_q
\]
such that every adjacent gap inside the block has scale $d$. Let $E$ consist of the elements of this block with even indices, let $O$ consist of those with odd indices, and let
\[
R=S\setminus(E\cup O).
\]
Put
\[
S_E=R\cup E,
\qquad
S_O=R\cup O.
\]
Both are proper subsets of $S$, so the induction hypothesis applies to them.

For $x\in R$, deleting points cannot create a new scale, and therefore
\[
r_{S_E}(x)\le r_S(x),
\qquad
r_{S_O}(x)\le r_S(x).
\]
Thus
\[
2^{-r_S(x)}
\le
\frac12\left(2^{-r_{S_E}(x)}+2^{-r_{S_O}(x)}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

For $x\in O$, every point at scale $d$ from $x$ is an adjacent member of the chosen block, and hence belongs to $E$ by (2) and maximality. Thus scale $d$ disappears in $S_O$, so
\[
r_{S_O}(x)\le r_S(x)-1
\]
and
\[
2^{-r_S(x)}\le\frac12\,2^{-r_{S_O}(x)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Similarly, for $x\in E$,
\[
2^{-r_S(x)}\le\frac12\,2^{-r_{S_E}(x)}.
\tag{5}
\]

Summing (3)--(5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(S)
&\le
\frac12\left(
\sum_{x\in S_O}2^{-r_{S_O}(x)}
+
\sum_{x\in S_E}2^{-r_{S_E}(x)}
\right)\\
&=\frac12\bigl(w(S_O)+w(S_E)\bigr)\\
&\le1.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the lemma. $\square$

Apply the lemma to $F$. Since $r_F(x)\le k$ for every $x\in F$,
\[
1\ge w(F)
=\sum_{x\in F}2^{-r_F(x)}
\ge |F|\,2^{-k}.
\]
Therefore
\[
|F|\le2^k.
\]
Together with the construction, the maximum is $2^k$.
