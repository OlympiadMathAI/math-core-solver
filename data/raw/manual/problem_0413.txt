ID: 0413

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Homothety, Pascal's Theorem, Projective Cross Ratios, Tangent-Chord Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with
\[
AB<AC.
\]
Let $\Omega$ be its circumcircle, and let $S$ be the midpoint of arc $CAB$ of $\Omega$.

The altitude from $A$ to $BC$ meets $BS$ at $D$ and meets $\Omega$ again at $E\ne A$. The line through $D$ parallel to $BC$ meets $BE$ at $L$. Let
\[
\omega=(BDL),
\]
and let $\omega$ meet $\Omega$ again at $P\ne B$.

Prove that the tangent to $\omega$ at $P$ and the line $BS$ intersect on the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $S'$ be the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Then $S'$ is diametrically opposite $S$ on $\Omega$, and
\[
AS'
\]
is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$.

Let the tangent to $\omega$ at $P$ meet $\Omega$ again at $Q\ne P$. Since $SS'$ is a diameter,
\[
\angle SQS'=90^\circ.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove that triangles $APD$ and $S'QS$ have pairwise parallel corresponding sides.

First,
\[
AP\perp DP.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the cyclic quadrilaterals $APBE$ and $BDLP$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle PAD
&=\angle PAE\\
&=180^\circ-\angle EBP\\
&=\angle PBL\\
&=\angle PDL.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $DL\parallel BC$ and $AD\perp BC$,
\[
\angle PDL=90^\circ-\angle ADP.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle PAD+\angle ADP=90^\circ,
\]
which proves (2).

Next,
\[
AD\parallel S'S,
\tag{3}
\]
because both lines are perpendicular to $BC$.

By the tangent-chord theorem in $\omega$,
\[
\angle DPQ=\angle DBP.
\]
Since $B,D,S$ are collinear and $B,P,Q,S$ lie on $\Omega$,
\[
\angle DBP=\angle SBP=\angle SQP.
\]
Therefore
\[
PD\parallel QS.
\tag{4}
\]

Finally, (1), (2), and (4) give
\[
AP\parallel S'Q.
\tag{5}
\]

Equations (3)-(5) show that triangles $APD$ and $S'QS$ have corresponding sides parallel. They are homothetic rather than translates, because the corresponding sides $AD$ and $S'S$ have opposite directions. Hence the lines joining corresponding vertices,
\[
AS',
\qquad
PQ,
\qquad
DS,
\]
are concurrent.

Now $PQ$ is the tangent to $\omega$ at $P$, $DS=BS$, and $AS'$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$. This proves the assertion.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $S'$ be the point of $\Omega$ diametrically opposite $S$. Then
\[
AS'
\]
is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$, and $AE\parallel SS'$.

Define
\[
T=AE\cap PS',
\qquad
M=AP\cap S'B.
\]
We shall prove
\[
L,P,S\ \text{are collinear}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
T,M\in\omega.
\tag{2}
\]

For (1), the cyclic quadrilateral $BDLP$ and the geometry of $\Omega$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle LPB
&=\angle LDB\\
&=90^\circ-\angle BDE\\
&=90^\circ-\angle BSS'\\
&=\angle SS'B\\
&=180^\circ-\angle BPS.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $\angle LPB+\angle BPS=180^\circ$, proving (1).

Since $SS'$ is a diameter, $PS\perp PS'$. By (1),
\[
LP\perp PT.
\]
Also,
\[
LD\parallel BC\perp AE=DT.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle LPT=\angle LDT=90^\circ,
\]
so $L,P,D,T$ are concyclic. Since $L,P,D$ lie on $\omega$,
\[
T\in\omega.
\]

Furthermore,
\[
\angle LPM
=
\angle SPA
=
\angle SEA
=
\angle EAS'
=
\angle EBS'
=
\angle LBM.
\]
Thus $L,P,M,B$ are concyclic, and hence
\[
M\in\omega.
\]
This proves (2).

Let $X$ be the intersection of line $BDS=BS$ with the tangent to $\omega$ at $P$. Apply Pascal's theorem to the degenerate hexagon
\[
P,P,M,B,D,T
\]
inscribed in $\omega$, where the side $PP$ is the tangent at $P$.

The three intersections of opposite sides are
\[
PP\cap BD=X,
\]
\[
PM\cap DT=AP\cap AE=A,
\]
and
\[
MB\cap TP=S'B\cap S'P=S'.
\]
Pascal's theorem gives
\[
X,A,S'\ \text{collinear}.
\]
Therefore $X$ lies on the internal angle bisector $AS'$ of $\angle BAC$.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $A'$ and $S'$ be the points of $\Omega$ diametrically opposite $A$ and $S$, respectively. The points $E$ and $A'$ are reflections in the diameter $SS'$, and hence $AS'$ bisects
\[
\angle EAA'.
\tag{1}
\]

Define
\[
T=AE\cap PS'.
\]
We first record three facts.

First,
\[
\angle DPA=90^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]
The proof is the same direct angle chase:
\[
\angle PAD
=
\angle PAE
=
180^\circ-\angle EBP
=
\angle PBL
=
\angle PDL
=
90^\circ-\angle ADP.
\]

Second,
\[
L,P,S
\]
are collinear, because
\[
\angle LPB+\angle BPS=180^\circ
\]
as in the computation
\[
\angle LPB
=
\angle LDB
=
90^\circ-\angle BDE
=
90^\circ-\angle BSS'
=
\angle SS'B.
\tag{3}
\]

Third,
\[
T\in\omega.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, (3) and the diameter $SS'$ give $LP\perp PT$, while $LD\perp DT$.

By (2), the line $PDA'$ is straight. Let
\[
R=AS'\cap PDA'.
\]
An angle chase gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle ARP
&=
\angle AS'P+\angle S'PA'\\
&=
\angle AEP+\angle EPS'\\
&=
\angle ATP.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
A,P,T,R
\]
are concyclic; call their circle $\gamma$. Since
\[
\angle RPA=90^\circ,
\]
the segment $AR$ is a diameter of $\gamma$.

We next prove that $\gamma$ and $\omega$ are orthogonal. Let line $LPS$ meet $\gamma$ again at $U\ne P$. The triangles $PLT$ and $PTU$ are similar, because
\[
\angle LPT=\angle TPU=90^\circ
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle PTL
&=\angle PBL\\
&=180^\circ-\angle EBP\\
&=\angle PAE\\
&=\angle PAT\\
&=\angle PUT.
\end{aligned}
\]
The spiral similarity taking triangle $PLT$ to triangle $PTU$ has rotation angle $90^\circ$ and maps $\omega=(PLT)$ to $\gamma=(PTU)$. Thus the two circles are orthogonal.

Let
\[
X=BS\cap AS'.
\]
We prove that $X$ is the midpoint of $AR$. It will then be the centre of $\gamma$, so orthogonality will imply that $PX$ is tangent to $\omega$.

Let $t$ be the tangent to $\omega$ at $D$. From the tangent-chord theorem and (1),
\[
\angle(DT,t)
=
\angle TPD
=
\angle S'PA'
=
\angle EAS',
\]
so
\[
t\parallel AS'.
\]
Let $I$ be the ideal point common to these parallel lines, and let
\[
V=LP\cap AE.
\]

We use directed projective cross ratios. Projection from $D$ sends the quadruple
\[
(A,R;X,I)
\]
on $AS'$ to
\[
(T,P;B,D)
\]
on $\omega$. Projection from $L$ sends the latter to
\[
(T,V;E,D)
\]
on line $AE$, and projection from $P$ sends this quadruple to
\[
(S',S;E,A')
\]
on $\Omega$. Hence
\[
(A,R;X,I)
=
(T,P;B,D)
=
(T,V;E,D)
=
(S',S;E,A').
\tag{5}
\]
Because $E$ and $A'$ are reflections in the diameter $SS'$,
\[
(S',S;E,A')=-1.
\]
Thus (5) gives
\[
(A,R;X,I)=-1.
\]
With $I$ the point at infinity of $AR$, this says exactly that $X$ is the midpoint of $AR$.

Therefore $X$ is the centre of $\gamma$. Since $\gamma\perp\omega$, the line joining the centre $X$ of $\gamma$ to their common point $P$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $P$. Thus the tangent at $P$ meets $BS$ at the point $X\in AS'$, as required.
