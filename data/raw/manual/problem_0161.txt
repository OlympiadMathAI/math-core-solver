ID: 0161

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Spiral Similarity, Tangent-Chord Theorem, Angle Bisectors, Semicircles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $\angle C=90^\circ$, and let $H$ be the foot of the altitude from $C$. A point $D$ is chosen inside triangle $CBH$ so that the line $CH$ bisects the segment $AD$. Let $P=BD\cap CH$. Let $\omega$ be the semicircle with diameter $BD$ that meets the segment $CB$ at an interior point. A line through $P$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $Q$. Prove that the lines $CQ$ and $AD$ meet on $\omega$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $K$ be the projection of $D$ onto $AB$. Since $CH$ passes through the midpoint of $AD$, orthogonal projection onto $AB$ shows that $H$ is the midpoint of $AK$. Hence
\[
AH=HK.
\tag{1}
\]
Because $PH\parallel DK$, triangles $BPH$ and $BDK$ are similar. The points occur in the orders $B,D,P$ and $B,K,H$, so
\[
\frac{PD}{PB}
=
1-\frac{BD}{BP}
=
1-\frac{BK}{BH}
=
\frac{HK}{HB}
=
\frac{AH}{HB}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $L$ be the projection of $Q$ onto $DB$. Since $PQ$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $Q$, the tangent-chord theorem gives
\[
\angle PQD=\angle QBD=\angle QBP.
\]
Also, $\angle DQB=\angle BLQ=90^\circ$, so
\[
\angle DQL=\angle QBD.
\]
Thus $QD$ and $QB$ are respectively the internal and external angle bisectors of $\angle PQL$. The internal and external angle-bisector theorems yield
\[
\frac{PD}{DL}
=
\frac{PQ}{QL}
=
\frac{PB}{BL}.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3), we obtain
\[
\frac{AH}{HB}
=
\frac{PD}{PB}
=
\frac{DL}{LB}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{BD}{BA}
=
\frac{BL}{BH}.
\]
Therefore the direct spiral similarity centered at $B$ that sends $A$ to $D$ also sends $H$ to $L$.

This similarity sends the semicircle with diameter $AB$ through $C$ to the semicircle $\omega$ with diameter $DB$. Since $CH\perp AB$ and $QL\perp DB$, it sends $C$ to $Q$. Hence it sends the rays $BA,BC$ to $BD,BQ$, respectively. It follows that
\[
\angle ABD=\angle CBQ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{BA}{BD}=\frac{BC}{BQ}.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle ABD\sim\triangle CBQ,
\]
and in particular
\[
\angle ADB=\angle CQB.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
T=AD\cap CQ.
\]
By (5),
\[
\angle BDT=\angle BQT.
\]
Therefore $B,D,Q,T$ are concyclic. Since $B,D,Q$ lie on $\omega$, the point $T$ also lies on $\omega$. Hence the lines $AD$ and $CQ$ meet on $\omega$.
