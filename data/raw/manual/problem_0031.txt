ID: 0031

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Collinearity, Polynomial Functions

STATEMENT:
Determine all pairs of functions
\[
f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
\]
satisfying
\[
g(f(x+y))=f(x)+(2x+y)g(y)
\tag{1}
\]
for all real numbers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f\equiv0,\quad g\equiv0}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x^2+C,\quad g(x)=x}
\]
for an arbitrary constant $C\in\mathbb R$.

Set $y=-2x$ in (1). Then
\[
g(f(-x))=f(x).
\tag{2}
\]
Replacing $x$ in (2) by $-x-y$ gives
\[
g(f(x+y))=f(-x-y).
\]
Together with (1), this yields
\[
f(-x-y)=f(x)+(2x+y)g(y).
\tag{3}
\]

Let $a,b,c$ be arbitrary real numbers. In (3), first set
\[
x=-b,
\qquad y=a+b,
\]
to obtain
\[
f(-a)=f(-b)+(a-b)g(a+b).
\]
Cyclically,
\[
f(-b)=f(-c)+(b-c)g(b+c),
\]
\[
f(-c)=f(-a)+(c-a)g(c+a).
\]
Adding these three equalities and then writing
\[
x=b+c,
\qquad y=c+a,
\qquad z=a+b,
\]
we get
\[
(y-x)g(z)+(z-y)g(x)+(x-z)g(y)=0
\tag{4}
\]
for all real $x,y,z$.

Equation (4) says that every three points
\[
(x,g(x)),\quad(y,g(y)),\quad(z,g(z))
\]
are collinear. Hence the graph of $g$ is a line, so
\[
g(x)=Ax+B
\]
for some real constants $A,B$.

Set $x=0$ and replace $y$ by $-y$ in (3). If
\[
C=f(0),
\]
then
\[
f(y)=Ay^2-By+C.
\tag{5}
\]
Now substitute (5) and $g(x)=Ax+B$ into (2). Comparing coefficients gives
\[
A^2=A,
\qquad
AB=-B,
\qquad
AC+B=C.
\]
Thus $A\in\{0,1\}$.

If $A=0$, the last two relations force
\[
B=C=0,
\]
so $f\equiv g\equiv0$.

If $A=1$, then $B=0$, while $C$ is arbitrary. Therefore
\[
g(x)=x,
\qquad
f(x)=x^2+C.
\]
A direct substitution verifies that all the displayed pairs satisfy (1).
