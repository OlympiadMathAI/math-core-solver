ID: 0422

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Divisibility Sequences, Prime Factors, Auxiliary Quotients, Unbounded Sequences

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots
\]
be positive integers such that
\[
a_{k+1}\mid2(a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_k)
\]
for every $k\ge1$.

Suppose that infinitely many primes divide at least one term of the sequence. Prove that for every positive integer $n$, there exists an index $k$ such that
\[
n\mid a_k.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For every $k\ge2$, define
\[
b_k=\frac{2(a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{k-1})}{a_k}.
\]
By the hypothesis, every $b_k$ is a positive integer.

We first establish two properties of the sequence $(b_k)$.

\textbf{Claim 1.}
For every $k\ge2$,
\[
b_{k+1}\le b_k+1.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed,
\[
b_{k+1}a_{k+1}
=
2(a_1+\cdots+a_k)
=
b_ka_k+2a_k
=
(b_k+2)a_k.
\tag{2}
\]
Since $a_{k+1}>a_k$, equality (2) gives
\[
b_{k+1}<b_k+2.
\]
Both sides are integers, so (1) follows.

\textbf{Claim 2.}
The sequence $(b_k)$ is unbounded.

From (2),
\[
a_{k+1}
=
a_k\frac{b_k+2}{b_{k+1}},
\]
and in particular
\[
a_{k+1}\mid a_k(b_k+2).
\tag{3}
\]
If all $b_k$ were bounded by some $B$, then (3), used inductively, would show that every prime divisor of every $a_k$ belongs to the finite set consisting of the prime divisors of $a_1a_2$ together with the primes not exceeding $B+2$. This contradicts the assumption that infinitely many primes divide terms of the sequence. Hence $(b_k)$ is unbounded.

Now fix a positive integer $n_0$. Choose a positive multiple $n$ of $n_0$ such that
\[
n>b_2.
\]
It is enough to find a term divisible by this $n$, since that term will also be divisible by $n_0$.
By Claim 2, choose the smallest $k\ge2$ such that
\[
b_{k+1}\ge n.
\]
Then
\[
b_k<n.
\]
Claim 1 forces
\[
b_k=n-1,
\qquad
b_{k+1}=n.
\]
Using (2),
\[
na_{k+1}=(n+1)a_k.
\]
Since
\[
\gcd(n,n+1)=1,
\]
we conclude that
\[
n\mid a_k.
\]
Hence $n_0\mid a_k$, proving the assertion.
