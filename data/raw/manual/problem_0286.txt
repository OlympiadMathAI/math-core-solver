ID: 0286

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cevians, Circumcircles, Trigonometric Ratios, Isogonal Lines, Barycentric Coordinates, Vector Geometry

STATEMENT:
Let $P$ be a point inside triangle $ABC$. The lines $AP,BP,CP$ meet $BC,CA,AB$ at $A_1,B_1,C_1$, respectively. Let $A_2,B_2,C_2$ be defined so that $A_1,B_1,C_1$ are the midpoints of $PA_2,PB_2,PC_2$, respectively. Prove that $A_2,B_2,C_2$ cannot all lie strictly inside the circumcircle of $ABC$.

SOLUTION 1:
We have
\[
\angle APB+\angle BPC+\angle CPA=2\pi
=(\pi-C)+(\pi-A)+(\pi-B).
\]
Hence at least one of
\[
\angle APB\ge\pi-C,
\quad
\angle BPC\ge\pi-A,
\quad
\angle CPA\ge\pi-B
\]
holds. Without loss of generality,
\[
\angle BPC\ge\pi-A.
\tag{1}
\]
Because $P$ lies inside $ABC$, $\angle BPC>A$. Thus
\[
\angle BPC\ge\max\{A,\pi-A\},
\]
which implies
\[
\sin\angle BPC\le\sin A.
\tag{2}
\]

Let the rays $AP,BP,CP$ meet the circumcircle $\Omega$ of $ABC$ again at $A_3,B_3,C_3$, respectively. We show that at least one of
\[
\frac{PB_1}{B_1B_3},
\qquad
\frac{PC_1}{C_1C_3}
\]
is at least $1$.

The triangles $CB_1B_3$ and $BB_1A$ are similar, so
\[
\frac{CB_1}{B_1B_3}=\frac{BB_1}{B_1A}.
\]
Using the sine rule in the relevant triangles,
\[
\frac{PB_1}{B_1B_3}
=\frac{\sin\angle ACP}{\sin\angle BPC}
 \cdot
 \frac{\sin A}{\sin\angle PBA}.
\tag{3}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\frac{PC_1}{C_1C_3}
=\frac{\sin\angle PBA}{\sin\angle BPC}
 \cdot
 \frac{\sin A}{\sin\angle ACP}.
\tag{4}
\]
Multiplying (3) and (4), and using (2), gives
\[
\frac{PB_1}{B_1B_3}\cdot
\frac{PC_1}{C_1C_3}
=\frac{\sin^2 A}{\sin^2\angle BPC}\ge1.
\]
Thus one of the two ratios is at least $1$. Suppose, for example, that $PB_1\ge B_1B_3$. On the ray from $P$ through $B_1$, the point $B_2$ satisfies $B_1B_2=PB_1$, so $B_2$ is on or beyond $B_3$. Hence $B_2$ is not strictly inside $\Omega$. The other case is identical.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $A_3,B_3,C_3$ be as in Solution 1, and assume for contradiction that all three of $A_2,B_2,C_2$ lie strictly inside the circumcircle. Then
\[
PA_1<A_1A_3,
\qquad
PB_1<B_1B_3,
\qquad
PC_1<C_1C_3.
\tag{1}
\]

The triangles $PBC_3$ and $PCB_3$ are similar. Let $X\in PB_3$ correspond to $C_1\in PC_3$ under this similarity, so
\[
\frac{PX}{XB_3}=\frac{PC_1}{C_1C_3}.
\]
Then
\[
\angle XCP
=\angle PBC_1
=\angle B_3BA
=\angle B_3CB_1.
\]
Thus the lines $CX$ and $CB_1$ are isogonal in triangle $PCB_3$.

Let $Y$ be the point where the internal bisector of $\angle B_3CP$ meets $PB_3$. From (1),
\[
PX<XB_3.
\]
Also $PB_1<B_1B_3$, and the isogonality just obtained places $Y$ between $X$ and $B_1$. Hence
\[
PY<YB_3.
\]
By the angle-bisector theorem in triangle $PCB_3$,
\[
\frac{PY}{YB_3}=\frac{PC}{CB_3},
\]
so $PC<CB_3$. Consequently,
\[
\angle PB_3C<\angle CPB_3.
\]
But
\[
\angle PB_3C=\angle BB_3C=A,
\]
and therefore
\[
A<\angle CPB_3.
\tag{2}
\]
Cyclically applying the same argument gives
\[
B<\angle APC_3,
\qquad
C<\angle BPA_3.
\tag{3}
\]
The three angles on the right of (2)--(3) sum to $\pi$, whereas $A+B+C=\pi$. Adding the strict inequalities gives $\pi<\pi$, a contradiction.

SOLUTION 3:
Place the circumcentre of $ABC$ at the origin and normalize the circumradius to $1$. Regard points as vectors, so
\[
|A|^2=|B|^2=|C|^2=1.
\]
Because $P$ lies inside the triangle, there are positive numbers $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ with
\[
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1,
\qquad
P=\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C.
\]
We have
\[
A_1=\frac{\beta B+\gamma C}{\beta+\gamma}
=\frac{P-\alpha A}{1-\alpha},
\]
and hence
\[
A_2=2A_1-P
=\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}P
 -\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}A.
\]
It follows that
\[
\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2(1+\alpha)}|A_2|^2
=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}|P|^2
 +\frac{2\alpha^2}{1+\alpha}
 -2\alpha A\cdot P.
\tag{1}
\]
The analogous identities hold for $B_2,C_2$.

Summing the three identities, the terms involving $|P|^2$ and the scalar products cancel, because
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}\frac{1+\alpha}{2}=2
\]
and
\[
\alpha A\cdot P+\beta B\cdot P+\gamma C\cdot P=P\cdot P.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2(1+\alpha)}|A_2|^2
=
\sum_{\rm cyc}\frac{2\alpha^2}{1+\alpha}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every $\alpha$,
\[
\frac{2\alpha^2}{1+\alpha}
=\frac{3\alpha-1}{2}
 +\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2(1+\alpha)}.
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}\frac{3\alpha-1}{2}=0,
\]
equation (2) becomes
\[
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2(1+\alpha)}|A_2|^2
=
\sum_{\rm cyc}
\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{2(1+\alpha)}.
\tag{3}
\]
All three coefficients in (3) are positive. Hence at least one of
\[
|A_2|^2,
\quad |B_2|^2,
\quad |C_2|^2
\]
is at least $1$. The unit circle is the circumcircle of $ABC$, so the corresponding point is not strictly inside it.
