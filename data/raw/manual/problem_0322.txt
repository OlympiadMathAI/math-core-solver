ID: 0322

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Directed Angles, Circle Tangency, Inversion, Orthocentres, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $\Gamma$ and $I$ be the circumcircle and the incenter of an acute triangle $ABC$. Two circles
\[
\omega_B
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\omega_C,
\]
passing through $B$ and $C$, respectively, are tangent to each other at $I$.

The circle $\omega_B$ meets the shorter arc $AB$ of $\Gamma$ and the segment $AB$ again at $P$ and $M$, respectively. Similarly, $\omega_C$ meets the shorter arc $AC$ of $\Gamma$ and the segment $AC$ again at $Q$ and $N$, respectively.

The rays $PM$ and $QN$ meet at $X$, and the tangents to $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$ at $B$ and $C$, respectively, meet at $Y$.

Prove that
\[
A,X,Y
\]
are collinear.

SOLUTION 1:
Let the lines
\[
AI,\qquad BI,\qquad CI
\]
meet $\Gamma$ again at
\[
D,\qquad E,\qquad F,
\]
respectively. Let $\ell$ be the common tangent to $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$ at $I$. We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$.

\textbf{Step 1: $Y$ lies on $\Gamma$.}

For a chord of a circle, the directed angles it makes with the tangents at its endpoints are complementary. Therefore,
\[
\measuredangle(BY,BI)+\measuredangle(CI,CY)
=
\measuredangle(IB,\ell)+\measuredangle(\ell,IC)
=
\measuredangle(IB,IC).
\tag{1}
\]
Using the fact that $BI$ and $CI$ are angle bisectors,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(BY,BA)+\measuredangle(CA,CY)
&=
\measuredangle(BI,BA)
+\measuredangle(BY,BI)\\
&\quad+
\measuredangle(CI,CY)
+\measuredangle(CA,CI)\\
&=
\measuredangle(BC,BI)
+\measuredangle(IB,IC)
+\measuredangle(CI,CB)\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
Y\in\Gamma.
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Step 2: $X=\ell\cap EF$.}

Let
\[
X^*=\ell\cap EF.
\]
By symmetry, it is enough to prove that $X^*\in QN$.

We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(IX^*,IQ)
&=\measuredangle(CI,CQ)\\
&=\measuredangle(CF,CQ)\\
&=\measuredangle(EF,EQ)\\
&=\measuredangle(EX^*,EQ).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Here the first equality is the tangent--chord theorem for $\omega_C$, and the middle equalities use the fact that $A,C,E,F,Q$ all lie on $\Gamma$.

Equation (3) shows that
\[
X^*,I,Q,E
\]
are concyclic. If $Q=E$, the same relation is interpreted as tangency at $Q$.

Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(QX^*,QI)
&=\measuredangle(EX^*,EI)\\
&=\measuredangle(EF,EB)\\
&=\measuredangle(CA,CF)\\
&=\measuredangle(CN,CI)\\
&=\measuredangle(QN,QI).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
X^*\in QN.
\]
The symmetric argument gives
\[
X^*\in PM.
\]
Therefore
\[
X=X^*=\ell\cap EF.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Step 3: $A,X,Y$ are collinear.}

A standard property of the triangle formed by the second intersections of its angle bisectors with the circumcircle says that
\[
I
\]
is the orthocentre of triangle $DEF$, and that $A$ is the reflection of $I$ in the line $EF$.

Since $X\in EF$, reflection in $EF$ gives
\[
\measuredangle(AX,AE)=\measuredangle(IE,IX).
\]
Using (4), the tangency at $I$, the tangent at $B$, and (2), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(AX,AE)
&=\measuredangle(IE,IX)\\
&=\measuredangle(BI,\ell)\\
&=\measuredangle(BY,BI)\\
&=\measuredangle(BY,BE)\\
&=\measuredangle(AY,AE).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the lines $AX$ and $AY$ coincide, proving that
\[
A,X,Y
\]
are collinear.

SOLUTION 2:
Perform an inversion centred at $I$, and denote images by primes.

The circumcircle $\Gamma$ becomes a circle $\Gamma'$, and
\[
I
\]
is the orthocentre of triangle $A'B'C'$.

The circles $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$, which pass through the centre of inversion and are tangent at $I$, become parallel lines:
\[
B'P'M'\parallel C'Q'N'.
\tag{1}
\]
The points $P',Q'$ lie on $\Gamma'$.

Let
\[
\gamma_B=(A'IB'),
\qquad
\gamma_C=(A'IC').
\]
The points $M'$ and $N'$ are the second intersections of the two parallel lines in (1) with $\gamma_B$ and $\gamma_C$, respectively.

The circle $\gamma_C$ is the translate of $\gamma_B$ by the vector
\[
\overrightarrow{B'C'}.
\]
The same translation maps $B'P'$ to $C'Q'$, and hence maps $M'$ to $N'$. Thus
\[
B'M'N'C'
\]
is a parallelogram. Moreover, the chord $P'Q'$ divides it into two isosceles trapezoids.

The line $PM$ becomes the circle
\[
(IP'M'),
\]
and $QN$ becomes
\[
(IQ'N').
\]
Thus $X'$ is the second common point, other than $I$, of these two circles.

The two circles are congruent, and their centres lie on the common perpendicular bisector $p$ of the equal segments
\[
P'M'
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Q'N'.
\]
Therefore their common chord $IX'$ is perpendicular to $p$, and $X'$ is the reflection of $I$ in $p$. Since
\[
p\perp B'P',
\]
we have
\[
IX'\parallel B'P'.
\tag{2}
\]

We next locate $Y'$. The tangent--chord relations in the original figure become
\[
\measuredangle(Y'B',Y'I)
=
\measuredangle(B'P',B'I)
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\measuredangle(Y'C',Y'I)
=
\measuredangle(C'Q',C'I).
\tag{4}
\]
Using (1), (3), and (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(Y'B',Y'C')
&=
\measuredangle(B'P',B'I)
+\measuredangle(C'I,C'Q')\\
&=
\measuredangle(C'I,B'I)\\
&=
\measuredangle(A'B',A'C').
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
Y'\in\Gamma'.
\tag{5}
\]

The circles $\Gamma'$ and $\gamma_B$ are congruent. In them, the chords
\[
A'P'
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A'M'
\]
subtend the same angle
\[
\angle A'B'P'.
\]
Therefore
\[
A'P'=A'M',
\]
so $A'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector $p$ of $P'M'$. Since $X'$ is the reflection of $I$ in $p$,
\[
A'X'=A'I.
\]
Thus triangle $A'IX'$ is isosceles, and
\[
\measuredangle(IA',IX')
=
\measuredangle(X'I,X'A').
\tag{6}
\]

Finally, using (3), (5), and (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(Y'I,Y'A')
&=
\measuredangle(Y'I,Y'B')
+\measuredangle(Y'B',Y'A')\\
&=
\measuredangle(B'I,B'P')
+\measuredangle(IA',IB')\\
&=
\measuredangle(IA',B'P')\\
&=
\measuredangle(IA',IX')\\
&=
\measuredangle(X'I,X'A').
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
A',X',Y',I
\]
are concyclic.

Under inversion centred at $I$, a line not through $I$ becomes a circle through $I$. Therefore the cyclicity of $A',X',Y',I$ is equivalent to the collinearity of
\[
A,X,Y.
\]
