ID: 0033

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Construction, Induction, Obtuse Triangles

STATEMENT:
Prove that for every positive integer $n$, the set
\[
\{2,3,4,\ldots,3n+1\}
\]
can be partitioned into $n$ triples such that the three numbers in every triple are the side lengths of an obtuse triangle.

SOLUTION 1:
For integers $a\le b$, write
\[
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}.
\]
Call a triple $\{a,b,c\}$ with $a<b<c$ an \emph{obtuse triple} if its elements are the side lengths of an obtuse triangle.

We prove by induction on $n$ a slightly stronger assertion: the interval
\[
[2,3n+1]
\]
can be partitioned into triples
\[
A_i=\{i,a_i,b_i\},
\qquad 2\le i\le n+1,
\]
each of which is obtuse.

For $n=1$, take
\[
A_2=\{2,3,4\}.
\]
Indeed, $2+3>4$ and $4^2>2^2+3^2$.

We need a simple lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.} If $a<b<c$ form an obtuse triple and $x>0$, then
\[
\{a,b+x,c+x\}
\]
is also an obtuse triple.

Since $c-b<a$, we have
\[
(c+x)-(b+x)=c-b<a,
\]
so the new numbers satisfy the triangle inequality. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(c+x)^2-(b+x)^2
&=(c-b)(b+c+2x)\\
&>(c-b)(b+c)>a^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the new triangle is obtuse.

Now let $n>1$ and put
\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]
By the induction hypothesis, $[2,3t+1]$ has a partition
\[
A_i'=\{i,a_i',b_i'\},
\qquad 2\le i\le t+1,
\]
into obtuse triples. For these indices define
\[
A_i=\{i,a_i'+n-t,b_i'+n-t\}.
\]
By the lemma these triples remain obtuse. They are disjoint, and their union is
\[
[2,t+1]\cup[n+2,n+2t+1].
\tag{1}
\]

For each
\[
t+2\le i\le n+1,
\]
define
\[
A_i=\{i,n+t+i,2n+i\}.
\tag{2}
\]
These triples are disjoint, and their union is
\[
[t+2,n+1]
\cup[n+2t+2,2n+t+1]
\cup[2n+t+2,3n+1].
\tag{3}
\]
Relations (1) and (3) show that all the triples together partition $[2,3n+1]$.

It remains to verify that every triple in (2) is obtuse. First,
\[
(2n+i)-(n+t+i)=n-t<i,
\]
because $i\ge t+2$. Thus its elements satisfy the triangle inequality. Next,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(2n+i)^2-(n+t+i)^2
&=(n-t)(3n+t+2i)\\
&\ge \frac n2\bigl(3n+3(t+1)+1\bigr)\\
&>\frac{9n^2}{4}\\
&\ge(n+1)^2\\
&\ge i^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $n\ge2$ and $i\le n+1$. Therefore
\[
(2n+i)^2>(n+t+i)^2+i^2,
\]
so the triangle is obtuse. This completes the induction.
