ID: 0317

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Tangent Circles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Directed Angles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral such that
\[
\angle ABC>90^\circ,
\qquad
\angle CDA>90^\circ,
\qquad
\angle DAB=\angle BCD.
\]
Let $E$ and $F$ be the reflections of $A$ in the lines $BC$ and $CD$, respectively. Suppose that the segments $AE$ and $AF$ meet the line $BD$ at $K$ and $L$, respectively.

Prove that the circumcircles of triangles $BEK$ and $DFL$ are tangent to each other.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $A'$ be the reflection of $A$ in $BD$. We show that the two given circles both pass through $A'$ and are tangent there.

Reflection in $BC$ interchanges $A$ and $E$, while the line $AE$ is invariant. Hence
\[
\angle BEK=\angle BAK.
\]
Reflection in $BD$ interchanges $A$ and $A'$ and fixes $B$ and $K$, so
\[
\angle BAK=\angle BA'K.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle BEK=\angle BA'K,
\]
and thus
\[
A',B,K,E
\]
are concyclic.

The same argument, using the reflections in $CD$ and $BD$, shows that
\[
A',D,L,F
\]
are concyclic.

For the two circles through $A'$ to be tangent at $A'$, the tangent--chord theorem shows that it is enough to prove
\[
\angle A'KB+\angle A'LD=\angle BA'D.
\tag{1}
\]
Now
\[
AK\perp BC,
\qquad
AL\perp CD.
\]
Reflection in $BD$ preserves the angles made with $BD$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle A'KB+\angle A'LD
&=
180^\circ-\angle KA'L\\
&=
180^\circ-\angle KAL\\
&=
\angle BCD\\
&=
\angle BAD\\
&=
\angle BA'D.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (1), and hence the two circumcircles are tangent at $A'$.

SOLUTION 2:
Reflect both given circles in the line $BD$.

The reflection of the circle $(BEK)$ is the circle $(KAB)$. Indeed,
\[
\angle KEB=\angle KAB,
\]
because $E$ is the reflection of $A$ in $BC$. Similarly, the reflection of the circle $(DFL)$ is the circle $(LAD)$.

Thus it is enough to prove that the circles
\[
(KAB)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(LAD)
\]
are tangent at $A$.

Since
\[
AK\perp BC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AL\perp CD,
\]
we have
\[
\angle KAL=180^\circ-\angle BCD.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle AKB+\angle ALD
=
180^\circ-\angle KAL
=
\angle BCD
=
\angle BAD.
\tag{1}
\]

Choose a ray $AT$ inside $\angle BAD$ such that
\[
\angle BAT=\angle AKB.
\]
Equation (1) then gives
\[
\angle DAT=\angle DLA.
\]
By the tangent--chord theorem, $AT$ is tangent at $A$ to both circles $(KAB)$ and $(LAD)$. Hence these two reflected circles are tangent, and therefore so are the original circles $(BEK)$ and $(DFL)$.
