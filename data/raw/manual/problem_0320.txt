ID: 0320

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incenter and Excenter Geometry, Miquel Point, Circle Tangency, Inversion, Spiral Similarity

STATEMENT:
Let $I$ and $I_A$ be the incenter and the $A$-excenter of an acute triangle $ABC$ with
\[
AB<AC.
\]
The incircle touches $BC$ at $D$. The line $AD$ meets the lines $BI_A$ and $CI_A$ at $E$ and $F$, respectively.

Prove that the circumcircles of triangles
\[
AID
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_AEF
\]
are tangent to each other.

SOLUTION 1:
Use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$. Let
\[
\Gamma=(BICI_A),
\qquad
\omega=(I_AEF),
\qquad
\Omega=(AID).
\]
The circle $\Gamma$ has diameter $II_A$.

Let $T\ne I_A$ be the second intersection of $\omega$ and $\Gamma$. The four lines
\[
BC,\qquad BI_A,\qquad CI_A,\qquad DEF
\]
form a complete quadrilateral. The point $T$ is its Miquel point, so
\[
T\in(BDE)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
T\in(CDF).
\tag{1}
\]

We first show that $T\in\Omega$. Using (1), the cyclic quadrilateral $BII_AT$, and the collinearities
\[
A,D,E,F\ \text{collinear},
\qquad
B,E,I_A\ \text{collinear},
\qquad
A,I,I_A\ \text{collinear},
\]
we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(DT,DA)
&=\measuredangle(DT,DE)\\
&=\measuredangle(BT,BE)\\
&=\measuredangle(BT,BI_A)\\
&=\measuredangle(IT,II_A)\\
&=\measuredangle(IT,I_AA).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly the cyclicity condition for
\[
A,I,D,T,
\]
so $T\in\Omega$.

Let $\ell$ be the tangent to $\omega$ at $T$. By the tangent--chord theorem,
\[
\measuredangle(TI_A,\ell)=\measuredangle(EI_A,ET).
\]
Using the cyclic quadrilaterals in (1) and $BICI_A$, we obtain
\[
\measuredangle(EI_A,ET)
=
\measuredangle(EB,ET)
=
\measuredangle(DB,DT).
\]
Since
\[
DI\perp DB
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
TI\perp TI_A,
\]
it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(TI,\ell)
&=
90^\circ+\measuredangle(TI_A,\ell)\\
&=
90^\circ+\measuredangle(DB,DT)\\
&=
\measuredangle(DI,DT).
\end{aligned}
\]
By the tangent--chord theorem, $\ell$ is also tangent to $\Omega$ at $T$. Hence $\omega$ and $\Omega$ are tangent.

SOLUTION 2:
Keep the notation
\[
\Gamma=(BICI_A),
\qquad
\omega=(I_AEF),
\qquad
\Omega=(AID).
\]

Let $L$ be the point diametrically opposite $I$ on $\Omega$. Then
\[
\angle IAL=\angle IDL=90^\circ.
\]
Thus $AL$ is the external angle bisector at $A$, and
\[
DL\parallel BC.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the line $LI$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $M$. Let $T$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $I$ to $I_AL$.

Because
\[
IT\perp I_AT,
\]
we have $T\in\Gamma$. Since $T,L,I_A$ are collinear, we also have
\[
IT\perp LT,
\]
so $T\in\Omega$, whose diameter is $IL$. Thus $T$ is the second common point of $\Gamma$ and $\Omega$.

We prove that $T\in\omega$. Directed-angle calculations give
\[
\measuredangle(LT,LM)=\measuredangle(AT,AI)
\]
and
\[
\measuredangle(MT,ML)
=
\measuredangle(MT,MI)
=
\measuredangle(I_AT,I_AI).
\]
Hence
\[
\triangle TML\sim\triangle TI_AA
\]
by a direct similarity. Let $\tau$ be the corresponding spiral similarity centred at $T$, so that
\[
\tau(M)=I_A,
\qquad
\tau(L)=A.
\]

From (1),
\[
\measuredangle(ML,LD)=\measuredangle(AI,AD),
\]
and therefore
\[
\tau(BC)=AD.
\tag{2}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(MB,ML)
&=\measuredangle(MB,MI)\\
&=\measuredangle(I_AB,I_AI)\\
&=\measuredangle(I_AE,I_AA).
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with (2), this gives
\[
\tau(B)=E.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\tau(C)=F.
\]

The points $M,B,C,T$ lie on $\Gamma$. Their images
\[
I_A,E,F,T
\]
are therefore concyclic, so
\[
T\in\omega.
\]

Finally, since $\tau(L)=A$ and $\tau(B)=E$, the corresponding triangles give
\[
\measuredangle(AT,AL)
=
\measuredangle(ET,EB)
=
\measuredangle(EI_A,ET).
\]
By the tangent--chord theorem, the tangents to $\Omega$ and $\omega$ at $T$ make the same angle with the common line
\[
I_A,T,L.
\]
Hence the two circles are tangent at $T$.

SOLUTION 3:
Perform an inversion centred at $D$, and denote the image of every point by a prime.

The circle
\[
\Omega=(AID)
\]
passes through the centre of inversion, so it becomes the line
\[
\Omega'=A'I'.
\]
The other circle becomes
\[
\omega'=(E'F'I_A').
\]
It is enough to prove that the line $A'I'$ is tangent to $\omega'$.

Put
\[
\beta=\angle BID,
\qquad
\gamma=\angle CID,
\qquad
\alpha=180^\circ-\beta-\gamma
       =90^\circ-\angle BAI.
\]

A direct angle chase after inversion gives
\[
\angle DB'I'=\beta,
\qquad
\angle DC'I'=\gamma,
\qquad
\angle B'I'C'=\alpha,
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\angle E'I_A'F'=180^\circ-\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]

It also gives
\[
\angle A'E'B'
=
\beta
=
90^\circ-\frac12\angle E'A'B'.
\]
Thus triangle $A'B'E'$ is isosceles and
\[
A'B'=A'E'.
\tag{3}
\]
Similarly,
\[
A'C'=A'F'.
\tag{4}
\]

A further directed-angle calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle A'B'I'
&=\angle I'B'D-\angle A'B'D\\
&=\beta-\angle BAD\\
&=\beta-(90^\circ-\alpha)+\angle IAD\\
&=\angle ICD+\angle IAD\\
&=\angle C'I'D+\angle A'I'D\\
&=\angle C'I'A'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\angle A'C'I'=\angle A'I'B'.
\]
Hence there is a direct similarity
\[
\triangle A'B'I'\sim\triangle A'I'C'.
\]
Consequently,
\[
A'I'^2=A'B'\cdot A'C'.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (3)--(5),
\[
A'E'\cdot A'F'=A'I'^2.
\tag{6}
\]

Let $T'$ be the reflection of $I'$ in $A'$. Then
\[
A'T'=A'I',
\]
so (6) becomes
\[
A'T'^2=A'E'\cdot A'F'.
\tag{7}
\]

We now show that $T'\in\omega'$. The similarity
\[
\triangle A'B'I'\sim\triangle A'I'C'
\]
and the fact that $A'$ is the midpoint of $I'T'$ imply that
\[
B',I',C',T'
\]
form a harmonic cyclic quadrilateral. We include the geometric verification from the official solution.

Let $C^*$ be the reflection of $C'$ in the perpendicular bisector of $I'T'$. Since $A'$ is the midpoint of $I'T'$ and
\[
\angle B'A'I'=\angle A'I'C'=\angle T'I'C^*,
\]
the point $C^*$ lies on $B'A'$. Furthermore,
\[
A'B'\cdot A'C^*
=
A'B'\cdot A'C'
=
A'I'^2
=
A'I'\cdot A'T'.
\]
Thus $C^*$ lies on the circle $(I'B'T')$. Reflection in the perpendicular bisector of $I'T'$ preserves this circle and maps $C^*$ to $C'$, so
\[
B',I',C',T'
\]
are concyclic.

The line $B'C^*$ passes through the midpoint $A'$ of $I'T'$, and hence is a median of triangle $B'I'T'$. In the cyclic configuration above, the line $B'C'$ is the corresponding symmedian; this establishes the harmonicity and gives
\[
\angle A'B'T'=\angle I'B'C'.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\angle A'C'T'=\angle I'C'B'.
\tag{8}
\]

By (1) and the isosceles triangles from (3)--(4),
\[
\angle I'B'C'=\beta=\angle A'B'E',
\]
so (8) gives
\[
E'\in B'T'.
\]
Similarly,
\[
F'\in C'T'.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\angle E'T'F'
=
\angle B'T'C'
=
\angle B'I'C'
=
\alpha.
\]
Together with (2), this proves that
\[
E',T',F',I_A'
\]
are concyclic; hence $T'\in\omega'$.

Finally, (7) says that the power of $A'$ with respect to $\omega'$ equals $A'T'^2$. Thus the line $A'T'$ is tangent to $\omega'$ at $T'$. Since $A',I',T'$ are collinear, this is precisely the line
\[
\Omega'=A'I'.
\]
Inverting back proves that $\Omega$ and $\omega$ are tangent.
