ID: 0156

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Injective Sequences, Directed Graphs, Pigeonhole Principle, Bounded Discrepancy

STATEMENT:
Let $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ be an infinite sequence of positive integers satisfying
\[
a_i\le2015
\]
for every $i\ge1$. Suppose that
\[
i+a_i\ne j+a_j
\]
whenever $i\ne j$.

Prove that there exist positive integers $b$ and $N$ such that
\[
\left|\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}(a_i-b)\right|\le1007^2
\]
whenever $n>m\ge N$.

SOLUTION 1:
Draw a directed arrow from each positive integer $i$ to $i+a_i$. Every arrow points to the right and has length at most $2015$. The hypothesis says that every positive integer receives at most one arrow, while every positive integer emits exactly one arrow.

Call a positive integer a \emph{starting point} if it receives no arrow. Following arrows from a starting point produces an infinite strictly increasing path, which we call a ray. Every positive integer belongs to exactly one ray: tracing predecessors must terminate because all arrows point from a smaller positive integer to a larger one.

A ray whose starting point is at most $x$ meets every interval
\[
[x,x+2014].
\]
Indeed, take the first point of the ray not smaller than $x$; its predecessor is less than $x$, and the intervening arrow has length at most $2015$.

There are at most $2015$ starting points. Otherwise, choose $x$ larger than $2016$ starting points. The interval $[x,x+2014]$ contains only $2015$ integers but would have to meet the corresponding $2016$ disjoint rays, a contradiction. Let
\[
b=\text{the number of starting points},
\]
so
\[
1\le b\le2015,
\]
and choose $N$ larger than every starting point.

Fix $n>m\ge N$. On each of the $b$ rays, let $x_j$ be the first point greater than $m$, and let $y_j$ be the first point greater than $n$, with the same ray indexed by $j$. The arrows with tails in
\[
\{m+1,m+2,\ldots,n\}
\]
form, on each ray, the path from $x_j$ to $y_j$; this path may be empty, in which case $x_j=y_j$. Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}a_i
=
\sum_{j=1}^{b}(y_j-x_j).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}(a_i-b)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{b}(y_j-n)
-
\sum_{j=1}^{b}(x_j-m).
\tag{1}
\]

The numbers
\[
x_1-m,x_2-m,\ldots,x_b-m
\]
are distinct members of $\{1,2,\ldots,2015\}$. Moreover, $m+1$ is not a starting point, so it belongs to one of the rays and the offset $1$ occurs. Thus their sum lies between
\[
1+2+\cdots+b
\]
and
\[
1+(2017-b)+(2018-b)+\cdots+2015.
\]
The same bounds hold for the sum of the offsets $y_j-n$. The difference between the upper and lower bounds is
\[
(b-1)(2015-b).
\]
Using (1), we obtain
\[
\left|\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}(a_i-b)\right|
\le(b-1)(2015-b).
\]
Finally,
\[
(b-1)(2015-b)
\le
\left(\frac{(b-1)+(2015-b)}2\right)^2
=1007^2.
\]
This proves the assertion.

SOLUTION 2:
Set
\[
s_i=i+a_i.
\]
Then
\[
i+1\le s_i\le i+2015,
\]
and the numbers $s_1,s_2,\ldots$ are distinct. Let
\[
M=\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus\{s_1,s_2,\ldots\}
\]
be the set of positive integers omitted by the sequence $(s_i)$.

\textbf{Claim.} The set $M$ has at most $2015$ elements.

\textit{Proof.}
If $m_1<\cdots<m_{2016}$ were elements of $M$, put $r=m_{2016}$. Then
\[
\{s_1,\ldots,s_r\}\sqcup\{m_1,\ldots,m_{2016}\}
\subseteq
\{1,2,\ldots,r+2015\}.
\]
The left side has $r+2016$ elements, whereas the right side has only $r+2015$, a contradiction. $\square$

The set $M$ is nonempty because $1\notin\{s_i\}$. Put
\[
b=|M|,
\qquad
N=\max M.
\]
Then $1\le b\le2015$.

For an integer $r\ge N$, define
\[
B_r=M\cup\{s_1,s_2,\ldots,s_r\}.
\]
This is a set of $b+r$ integers contained in $\{1,2,\ldots,r+2015\}$. Moreover,
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,r+1\}\subseteq B_r.
\]
Indeed, if $t\le r+1$ is not in $M$, then $t=s_i$ for some $i$, and $s_i\ge i+1$ gives $i\le r$.

Hence there is a set
\[
C_r\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,2014\},
\qquad
|C_r|=b-1,
\]
such that
\[
B_r=
\{1,2,\ldots,r+1\}
\cup
\{r+1+x:x\in C_r\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a finite set $X$ of integers, write $\sum X$ for the sum of its elements. Computing $\sum B_r$ from its two descriptions gives
\[
\sum M+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(i+a_i)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{r}i+b(r+1)+\sum C_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum M+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_i-b)
=b+\sum C_r.
\tag{3}
\]

Take $n>m\ge N$ and subtract (3) for $r=m$ from (3) for $r=n$. We obtain
\[
\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}(a_i-b)
=
\sum C_n-
\sum C_m.
\tag{4}
\]
Both $C_n$ and $C_m$ are $(b-1)$-element subsets of $\{1,2,\ldots,2014\}$. The greatest possible difference between their sums is attained by taking one to be
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,b-1\}
\]
and the other to be
\[
\{2016-b,2017-b,\ldots,2014\}.
\]
Thus (4) yields
\[
\left|\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}(a_i-b)\right|
\le(b-1)(2015-b)
\le1007^2.
\]
