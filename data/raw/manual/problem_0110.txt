ID: 0110

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Product Decompositions, Induction, Binary Expansions, Telescoping Products

STATEMENT:
Prove that for every pair of positive integers $k,n$ there exist positive integers
\[
m_1,m_2,\ldots,m_k
\]
such that
\[
1+\frac{2^k-1}{n}
=
\left(1+\frac1{m_1}\right)
\left(1+\frac1{m_2}\right)
\cdots
\left(1+\frac1{m_k}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We use induction on $k$.

For $k=1$, equation (1) is immediate by taking $m_1=n$.

Assume that the assertion is true for $k=j-1$, and let us prove it for $k=j$.

\textbf{Case 1: $n$ is odd.} Write $n=2t-1$ with $t\ge1$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\frac{2^j-1}{2t-1}
&=\frac{2(t+2^{j-1}-1)}{2t}\cdot\frac{2t}{2t-1}\\
&=\left(1+\frac{2^{j-1}-1}{t}\right)
  \left(1+\frac1{2t-1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
By the induction hypothesis, the first factor is a product of $j-1$ factors of the required form. Taking $m_j=2t-1$ completes this case.

\textbf{Case 2: $n$ is even.} Write $n=2t$ with $t\ge1$. We have
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\frac{2^j-1}{2t}
&=\frac{2t+2^j-1}{2t+2^j-2}
  \cdot
  \frac{2t+2^j-2}{2t}\\
&=\left(1+\frac1{2t+2^j-2}\right)
  \left(1+\frac{2^{j-1}-1}{t}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Again the second factor is, by induction, a product of $j-1$ factors of the required form, and we may take
\[
m_j=2t+2^j-2>0.
\]
This completes the induction.

SOLUTION 2:
We construct the $m_i$ directly from binary expansions modulo $2^k$.

Let $A$ be the least nonnegative residue of $n-1$ modulo $2^k$, and let $B$ be the least nonnegative residue of $-n$ modulo $2^k$. Write
\[
A=2^{a_1}+2^{a_2}+\cdots+2^{a_r},
\qquad
0\le a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_r\le k-1,
\]
and
\[
B=2^{b_1}+2^{b_2}+\cdots+2^{b_s},
\qquad
0\le b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_s\le k-1.
\]
Since $A+B=2^k-1$, the sets of exponents are complementary:
\[
\{a_1,\ldots,a_r\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{b_1,\ldots,b_s\}
=
\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\},
\]
and hence
\[
r+s=k.
\tag{2}
\]

Define
\[
S_p=2^{a_p}+2^{a_{p+1}}+\cdots+2^{a_r}
\qquad(1\le p\le r),
\]
\[
T_q=2^{b_1}+2^{b_2}+\cdots+2^{b_q}
\qquad(1\le q\le s),
\]
and put
\[
S_{r+1}=0,
\qquad
T_0=0.
\]
Then
\[
S_1+T_s=2^k-1
\]
(with the natural empty-sum interpretation if $r=0$ or $s=0$), and because $T_s\equiv-n\pmod{2^k}$,
\[
n+T_s\equiv0\pmod{2^k}.
\tag{3}
\]

Now telescope:
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\frac{2^k-1}{n}
&=\frac{n+S_1+T_s}{n}\\
&=
\left(\prod_{p=1}^{r}
\frac{n+S_p+T_s}{n+S_{p+1}+T_s}\right)
\left(\prod_{q=1}^{s}
\frac{n+T_q}{n+T_{q-1}}\right)\\
&=
\left(\prod_{p=1}^{r}
\left(1+\frac{2^{a_p}}{n+S_{p+1}+T_s}\right)\right)
\left(\prod_{q=1}^{s}
\left(1+\frac{2^{b_q}}{n+T_{q-1}}\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

For $1\le p\le r$, define
\[
m_p=\frac{n+S_{p+1}+T_s}{2^{a_p}},
\]
and for $1\le q\le s$, define
\[
m_{r+q}=\frac{n+T_{q-1}}{2^{b_q}}.
\]
All these numbers are positive integers. Indeed, by (3),
\[
n+S_{p+1}+T_s\equiv n+T_s\equiv0\pmod{2^{a_p}},
\]
because every term of $S_{p+1}$ is divisible by $2^{a_p}$. Similarly,
\[
n+T_{q-1}\equiv n+T_s\equiv0\pmod{2^{b_q}},
\]
because $T_s-T_{q-1}$ is divisible by $2^{b_q}$.

By (2), exactly $k$ integers have been constructed, and (4) is the desired identity.
