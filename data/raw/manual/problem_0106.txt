ID: 0106

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangency, Similar Triangles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Isogonal Geometry, Projective Transformations

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with
\[
\angle B>\angle C.
\]
Let $P$ and $Q$ be two distinct points on the line $AC$ such that
\[
\angle PBA=\angle QBA=\angle ACB,
\]
and suppose that $A$ lies between $P$ and $C$. Assume that there is an interior point $D$ of segment $BQ$ such that
\[
PD=PB.
\]
The ray $AD$ meets the circumcircle of $ABC$ again at $R\ne A$. Prove that
\[
QB=QR.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$, and put
\[
\gamma=\angle ACB.
\]
Since $\angle ABC>\gamma$, we have $\gamma<90^\circ$. By the tangent-chord theorem, the equality
\[
\angle PBA=\angle BCA=\gamma
\]
shows that $PB$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $B$. Hence
\[
PA\cdot PC=PB^2=PD^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Because $\gamma<\angle ABC$, the ray $BQ$ lies inside $\angle ABC$ and meets the segment $AC$, while the tangent ray $BP$ meets the extension of $AC$ beyond $A$. Thus the points occur on the line $AC$ in the order
\[
P,A,Q,C.
\]
From (1),
\[
\frac{PA}{PD}=\frac{PD}{PC}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle APD=\angle DPC.
\]
Therefore, by SAS similarity,
\[
\triangle PAD\sim\triangle PDC,
\]
so
\[
\angle ADP=\angle DCP.
\tag{2}
\]

Since
\[
\angle ABQ=\angle ACB,
\]
the triangles $ABC$ and $AQB$ are similar. Thus
\[
\angle AQB=\angle ABC=\angle ARC.
\]
Because $A,Q,C$ are collinear and $B,D,Q$ are collinear,
\[
\angle DQC=180^\circ-\angle AQB.
\]
Also $A,D,R$ are collinear, so
\[
\angle DRC=\angle ARC.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle DQC+\angle DRC=180^\circ,
\]
and therefore $D,R,C,Q$ are concyclic. Consequently,
\[
\angle DRQ=\angle DCQ=\angle DCP=\angle ADP,
\tag{3}
\]
where the last equality is (2).

The triangle $PBD$ is isosceles, because $PB=PD$. Also,
\[
\angle PBD
 =\angle PBA+\angle ABQ
 =2\gamma.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle PDB=\angle PBD=2\gamma.
\tag{4}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\angle ARB=\angle ACB=\gamma.
\tag{5}
\]
Using that $D$ lies on both $AR$ and $BQ$, equations (3)--(5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle QBR
&=\angle ADB-\angle ARB\\
&=\angle ADP+\angle PDB-\angle ARB\\
&=\angle DRQ+2\gamma-\gamma\\
&=\angle DRQ+\gamma\\
&=\angle QRB.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus triangle $QBR$ is isosceles, and therefore
\[
QB=QR.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$ and put
\[
\gamma=\angle ACB.
\]
As in Solution 1, $PB$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $B$.

Let $E\ne B$ be the second intersection of $BQ$ with $\omega$. Let the ray $CE$ beyond $E$ meet the ray $BA$ at $V$. To justify this construction and record two similarities, observe that
\[
\angle BEV
 =180^\circ-\angle BEC
 =180^\circ-\angle BAC
 =\angle PAB,
\]
while
\[
\angle VBE=
\angle ABQ=
\angle PBA.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle VEB\sim\triangle PAB.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\angle BVE=\angle BPA.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle AEV
 =\angle BEV-\gamma
 =\angle PAB-\angle ABQ
 =\angle AQB.
\]
Together with (2), this gives
\[
\triangle VAE\sim\triangle PBQ.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $PH$ be the altitude of the isosceles triangle $PBD$. Since $PB=PD$, the point $H$ is the midpoint of $BD$. Let
\[
G=PH\cap AB.
\]
Reflection in $PH$ interchanges $B$ and $D$, so
\[
\angle BDG=\angle DBG=\gamma.
\]
Also,
\[
\angle BEA=\angle BCA=
\gamma.
\]
Hence
\[
DG\parallel AE.
\]
In triangle $BAE$, this gives
\[
\frac{BG}{GA}=\frac{BD}{DE}.
\tag{4}
\]
Under the similarity (1), the sides $BA$ and $BE$ correspond. Relation (4) therefore shows that $G$ and $D$ are corresponding points. Hence
\[
\angle DVB=\angle GPB.
\]
Since $PG\perp BQ$ and, by (3), $\angle PBQ=\angle VAE$, we obtain
\[
\angle DVB=90^\circ-\angle PBQ
             =90^\circ-\angle VAE.
\]
Thus
\[
VD\perp AE.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
T=VD\cap AE,
\]
and let $S$ be the foot of the altitude from $D$ to $BR$. The triangles $ADE$ and $BDR$ are similar. Indeed, because $A,D,R$ are collinear and $B,D,E$ are collinear,
\[
\angle ADE=\angle BDR.
\]
Also, $A,B,E,R$ lie on $\omega$, and hence
\[
\angle AED=\angle AEB=\angle ARB=\angle DRB.
\]
Thus the correspondence is
\[
A\leftrightarrow B,
\qquad
D\leftrightarrow D,
\qquad
E\leftrightarrow R.
\]
By (5), $T$ and $S$ are the feet of corresponding altitudes. Therefore
\[
\frac{BS}{SR}=\frac{AT}{TE}.
\tag{6}
\]

Likewise, in the similar triangles $VAE$ and $PBQ$ from (3), the points $T$ and $H$ are the feet of corresponding altitudes. Thus
\[
\frac{AT}{TE}=\frac{BH}{HQ}.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7),
\[
\frac{BS}{SR}=\frac{BH}{HQ}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{BS}{BR}=\frac{BH}{BQ}.
\]
Since the included angle at $B$ is common, this proves
\[
\triangle BHS\sim\triangle BQR.
\tag{8}
\]

The triangle $SBD$ is right-angled at $S$, and $H$ is the midpoint of its hypotenuse $BD$. Hence
\[
HS=HB.
\]
Using (8), we conclude that
\[
QR=QB.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
Let $\omega$ and $O$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$ and its center. As before, $BP$ is tangent to $\omega$ at $B$.

Let $E\ne B$ be the second intersection of $BD$ with $\omega$. Since $PB=PD$, the tangent to $\omega$ at $E$ is parallel to $DP$; let this tangent meet $BP$ at $L$. Thus
\[
DP\parallel LE.
\tag{1}
\]
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BE$, and let $H$ be the midpoint of $BR$.

Because
\[
\angle AEB=\angle ACB=\angle ABQ=\angle ABE,
\]
we have $AE=AB$, so $A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BE$. The points $L$ and $O$ also lie on this perpendicular bisector: $LB=LE$ because they are tangent lengths, and $OB=OE$. Hence
\[
L,A,M,O
\]
are collinear.

Let $\omega_1$ be the circle with diameter $BO$, and define
\[
Q'=HO\cap BE.
\]
Since $H$ is the midpoint of $BR$ and $OB=OR$, the line $HO$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BR$. Therefore the desired equality $QB=QR$ is equivalent to
\[
Q'=Q.
\tag{2}
\]

Consider the following five projective maps.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Project the line $BE$ to the line $LB$ from the center $A$. Under this map, $Q\mapsto P$.
\item Project the line $LB$ to the line $BE$ in the direction parallel to $LE$. By (1), $P\mapsto D$.
\item Project the line $BE$ to the circle $\omega$ from the point $A$, taking a point to the second intersection of its line through $A$ with $\omega$. Thus $D\mapsto R$.
\item Apply the homothety with center $B$ and ratio $1/2$. It sends $\omega$ to $\omega_1$ and $R\mapsto H$.
\item Project $\omega_1$ to the line $BE$ from the point $O$. Thus $H\mapsto Q'$.
\end{enumerate}

Their composition is a projectivity from the line $BE$ to itself, and it sends $Q$ to $Q'$. We prove that this projectivity is the identity by finding three fixed points.

First, $B$ is fixed by every map. Second, $M$ is fixed, since its successive images are
\[
M\mapsto L\mapsto E\mapsto E\mapsto M\mapsto M.
\]

For a third fixed point, draw through $A$ the line parallel to $LE$. Let it meet $BE$, $LB$, and $\omega$ at $X,Y,Z\ne A$, respectively. Then the first three maps send
\[
X\mapsto Y\mapsto X\mapsto Z.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle XBZ
 =\angle EAZ
 =\angle AEL
 =\angle LBA
 =\angle BZX.
\]
Thus triangle $XBZ$ is isosceles with
\[
XB=XZ.
\]
Let $U$ be the midpoint of $BZ$. Both $O$ and $X$ lie on the perpendicular bisector of $BZ$, so $O,U,X$ are collinear. The last two maps therefore send
\[
Z\mapsto U\mapsto X.
\]
Hence $X$ is fixed.

A projectivity of a line fixing three distinct points is the identity. Consequently the image $Q'$ of $Q$ equals $Q$. By (2),
\[
QB=QR.
\]
