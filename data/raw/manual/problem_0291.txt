ID: 0291

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Factorials, Prime Valuations, Legendre Formula, LTE, Size Estimates

STATEMENT:
Find all pairs $(m,n)$ of positive integers satisfying
\[
(2^n-1)(2^n-2)(2^n-2^2)\cdots(2^n-2^{n-1})=m!.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{(m,n)=(1,1)\ \text{or}\ (3,2)}.
\]

Let
\[
L_n=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(2^n-2^i).
\]
Factoring $2^i$ from the $i$th factor gives
\[
L_n
=
2^{1+2+\cdots+(n-1)}
(2^n-1)(2^{n-1}-1)\cdots(2-1).
\]
Hence
\[
v_2(L_n)=1+2+\cdots+(n-1)=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
\tag{1}
\]

If $L_n=m!$, Legendre's formula gives
\[
\frac{n(n-1)}2
=v_2(m!)
=\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2^j}\right\rfloor
<
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{m}{2^j}
=m.
\tag{2}
\]

We also have
\[
L_n<(2^n)^n=2^{n^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
We claim that
\[
2^{n^2}
<
\left(\frac{n(n-1)}2\right)!
\qquad(n\ge6).
\tag{4}
\]
For $n=6$, this is
\[
2^{36}<15!,
\]
which is immediate numerically. If $n\ge7$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{n(n-1)}2\right)!
&=15!\cdot16\cdot17\cdots\frac{n(n-1)}2\\
&>2^{36}\cdot16^{\,\frac{n(n-1)}2-15}\\
&=2^{\,2n(n-1)-24}\\
&=2^{\,n^2+(n-6)(n+4)}
>2^{n^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (4) holds.

For $n\ge6$, equations (2)--(4) imply
\[
L_n
<
2^{n^2}
<
\left(\frac{n(n-1)}2\right)!
<
m!
=
L_n,
\]
a contradiction. Hence $n\le5$.

Direct calculation gives
\[
L_1=1=1!,
\qquad
L_2=6=3!,
\]
and
\[
5!<L_3=168<6!,
\]
\[
7!<L_4=20160<8!,
\]
\[
10!<L_5=9999360<11!.
\]
Therefore the only solutions are
\[
\boxed{(1,1)\ \text{and}\ (3,2)}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again write
\[
L_n=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(2^n-2^i).
\]
The cases $n=1,2,3,4$ can be checked directly. We prove that no solution exists for $n\ge5$ by comparing the exponents of $3$ and $31$.

For an odd prime $p$, the lifting-the-exponent lemma gives
\[
v_p(u^r-v^r)=v_p(u-v)+v_p(r)
\]
whenever $p\mid u-v$ and $p\nmid uv$.

The number $3$ divides $2^j-1$ exactly when $j$ is even. Moreover,
\[
v_3(2^{2r}-1)
=v_3(4^r-1)
=1+v_3(r)
=v_3(3r).
\]
Therefore, if
\[
s=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,
\]
then
\[
v_3(L_n)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{s}v_3(2^{2r}-1)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{s}v_3(3r)
=
v_3((3s)!).
\tag{1}
\]
If $L_n=m!$, equality of the $3$-adic valuations in (1) implies
\[
3s\le m\le3s+2.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the values of $v_3(t!)$ are equal for
\[
t=3s,\ 3s+1,\ 3s+2,
\]
but strictly smaller before $3s$ and strictly larger at $3s+3$.

Now $31=2^5-1$. Among the exponents
\[
1,2,\ldots,n,
\]
there are $\lfloor n/5\rfloor$ multiples of $5$, so
\[
v_{31}(L_n)\ge\left\lfloor\frac n5\right\rfloor.
\]
For $n\ge5$,
\[
\frac n{10}
\le
\left\lfloor\frac n5\right\rfloor
\le
v_{31}(m!)
<
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{m}{31^j}
=\frac m{30}.
\]
Thus
\[
m>3n.
\tag{3}
\]

On the other hand, (2) gives
\[
m\le3\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+2
\le\frac{3n}{2}+2,
\]
which is incompatible with (3) for $n\ge5$.

Hence $n\le4$. Direct checking yields only
\[
L_1=1!
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L_2=3!,
\]
so the solutions are again
\[
\boxed{(m,n)=(1,1),(3,2)}.
\]
