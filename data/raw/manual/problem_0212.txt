ID: 0212

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Injectivity, Integer Translations, Symmetry

STATEMENT:
Find all functions
\[
f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
\]
such that
\[
f\bigl(f(x)f(y)\bigr)+f(x+y)=f(xy)
\tag{1}
\]
for all real numbers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=0,\qquad f(x)=x-1,\qquad f(x)=1-x.}
\]

A direct substitution verifies all three functions.

If $f$ is a solution, then so is $-f$. We may therefore assume without loss of generality that
\[
f(0)\le0.
\tag{2}
\]

For every $x\ne1$, choose
\[
y=\frac{x}{x-1}.
\]
Then $x+y=xy$, so (1) gives
\[
f\left(f(x)f\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right)\right)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, taking $x=0$,
\[
f\bigl(f(0)^2\bigr)=0.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Case 1: $f(0)=0$.}

Put $y=0$ in (1):
\[
f(f(x)f(0))+f(x)=f(0).
\]
Thus
\[
f(x)=0
\]
for every $x$.

\textbf{Case 2: $f(0)<0$.}

Equation (4) shows that $f$ has a zero. We claim that its only zero is $1$. Indeed, if
\[
f(a)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a\ne1,
\]
then applying (3) with $x=a$ gives
\[
f(0)=0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence
\[
f(1)=0,
\qquad
f(a)=0\Longrightarrow a=1.
\tag{5}
\]
From (4),
\[
f(0)^2=1.
\]
Together with (2),
\[
f(0)=-1.
\tag{6}
\]

Putting $y=1$ in (1), and using (5)--(6),
\[
f(0)+f(x+1)=f(x),
\]
so
\[
f(x+1)=f(x)+1.
\]
By induction in both directions,
\[
f(x+m)=f(x)+m
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R,\ m\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{7}
\]

We next prove that $f$ is injective. Suppose
\[
f(a)=f(b)
\qquad\text{with }a\ne b.
\]
By (7), for every integer $N$,
\[
f(a+N+1)=f(b+N)+1.
\tag{8}
\]
Choose a sufficiently negative integer $N$ so that there exist real numbers $u,v$ satisfying
\[
u+v=a+N+1,
\qquad
uv=b+N.
\]
Such $u,v$ exist because the discriminant is positive for sufficiently negative $N$. Neither is equal to $1$, since otherwise the two displayed equations would give $a=b$.

Applying (1) to $u,v$ and using (8),
\[
f(f(u)f(v))+1=0.
\]
By (7),
\[
f(f(u)f(v)+1)=0.
\]
The uniqueness of the zero in (5) gives
\[
f(u)f(v)=0.
\]
Thus $u=1$ or $v=1$, a contradiction. Hence $f$ is injective.

Now take $(x,y)=(t,-t)$ in (1):
\[
f(f(t)f(-t))+f(0)=f(-t^2).
\]
Using (6)--(7),
\[
f(f(t)f(-t))=f(1-t^2).
\]
Injectivity gives
\[
f(t)f(-t)=1-t^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Next take $(x,y)=(t,1-t)$. Since $f(1)=0$,
\[
f(f(t)f(1-t))=f(t(1-t)).
\]
Injectivity gives
\[
f(t)f(1-t)=t(1-t).
\tag{10}
\]
By (7),
\[
f(1-t)=f(-t)+1.
\]
Substituting this and (9) into (10),
\[
1-t^2+f(t)=t-t^2,
\]
so
\[
f(t)=t-1
\]
for every real $t$.

Under the normalization $f(0)\le0$, the only solutions are $0$ and $x-1$. Negating them adds the solution $1-x$, completing the classification.
