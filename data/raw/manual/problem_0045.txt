ID: 0045

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Orthogonal Projections, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Radical Axis, Parallelograms

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral whose sides $AD$ and $BC$ are not parallel. The circles with diameters $AB$ and $CD$ meet at two points $E$ and $F$ inside the quadrilateral. Let $\omega_E$ be the circle through the feet of the perpendiculars from $E$ to the lines $AB,BC,CD$, and let $\omega_F$ be the circle through the feet of the perpendiculars from $F$ to the lines $CD,DA,AB$. Prove that the midpoint of $EF$ lies on the line through the two intersection points of $\omega_E$ and $\omega_F$.

SOLUTION 1:
We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$ whenever points lie on extensions. Let $P,Q,R,S$ be the perpendicular projections of $E$ onto the lines
\[
DA,\quad AB,\quad BC,\quad CD,
\]
respectively. We first show that all four points lie on $\omega_E$.

The points $P,Q,E,A$ are cyclic, since the angles at $P$ and $Q$ are right angles. Hence
\[
\angle QPE=\angle QAE.
\]
Similarly, $Q,R,E,B$ are cyclic, and therefore
\[
\angle QRE=\angle QBE.
\]
Because $E$ lies on the circle with diameter $AB$,
\[
\angle AEB=90^\circ,
\]
so
\[
\angle QPE+\angle QRE=90^\circ.
\tag{1}
\]
In the same way, using the circle with diameter $CD$, we get
\[
\angle SPE+\angle SRE=90^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]
Adding (1) and (2) gives
\[
\angle QPS+\angle QRS=180^\circ.
\]
Thus $P,Q,R,S$ are concyclic. In particular, the fourth projection $P$ also lies on the circle $\omega_E$ specified in the statement. Analogously, all four perpendicular projections of $F$ onto the four side-lines lie on $\omega_F$.

Let
\[
K=AD\cap BC.
\]
After interchanging the names of $A,D$ if necessary, assume that $A$ lies on the segment $DK$. The angle between the lines $AD$ and $BC$ at $K$ is acute. Indeed, if it were at least $90^\circ$, the closed disk with diameter $CD$ would contain the whole convex quadrilateral, so its boundary circle could not pass through the interior points $E$ and $F$.

Consequently, the line $EP$, which is perpendicular to $AD$, meets $BC$ at a point $P'$, and the line $ER$, which is perpendicular to $BC$, meets $AD$ at a point $R'$. We claim that
\[
P',R'\in\omega_E.
\]
Since $Q,R,E,B$ are cyclic and $R,B,K,P'$ are collinear,
\[
\angle QRP'
=\angle QRK
=\angle QEB
=90^\circ-\angle QBE
=\angle QAE
=\angle QPE
=\angle QPP'.
\]
Hence $P,Q,R,P'$ are cyclic, proving $P'\in\omega_E$. The proof for $R'$ is analogous.

Now let $M,N$ be the projections of $F$ onto $AD$ and $BC$, respectively, and define
\[
M'=FM\cap BC,
\qquad
N'=FN\cap AD.
\]
The same argument gives
\[
M',N'\in\omega_F.
\]

Set
\[
U=NN'\cap PP',
\qquad
V=MM'\cap RR'.
\]
The points $N,N',P,P'$ are cyclic because the angles at $N$ and $P$ are right. Therefore
\[
UN\cdot UN'=UP\cdot UP'.
\]
The left side is the power of $U$ with respect to $\omega_F$, while the right side is its power with respect to $\omega_E$. Hence $U$ lies on the radical axis of $\omega_E$ and $\omega_F$. Similarly, $V$ lies on that radical axis.

Finally,
\[
EU\perp AD,
\qquad
FV\perp AD,
\]
so $EU\parallel FV$, while
\[
EV\perp BC,
\qquad
FU\perp BC,
\]
so $EV\parallel FU$. Thus $EUFV$ is a parallelogram. Its diagonals $EF$ and $UV$ bisect one another. Since $UV$ is the radical axis of $\omega_E$ and $\omega_F$, it passes through the midpoint of $EF$, as required.
