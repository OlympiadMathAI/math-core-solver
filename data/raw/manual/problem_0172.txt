ID: 0172

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Iterated Functions, Divisibility, Arithmetic Progressions, Periodicity

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb Z_{>0}$ denote the set of positive integers, and let
\[
f:\mathbb Z_{>0}\to\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
For positive integers $m,n$, write $f^n(m)$ for the $n$-fold iterate of $f$ at $m$. Suppose that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every $m,n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$,
\[
\frac{f^n(m)-m}{n}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
\item[(ii)] The set
\[
\mathbb Z_{>0}\setminus\{f(n):n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}
\]
is finite.
\end{enumerate}

Prove that the sequence
\[
f(1)-1,\ f(2)-2,\ f(3)-3,\ldots
\]
is periodic.

SOLUTION 1:
We divide the proof into three steps.

\textbf{Step 1: decomposition into finitely many forward orbits.}

First, $f$ is injective. Suppose
\[
f(m)=f(k).
\]
Then $f^n(m)=f^n(k)$ for every $n\ge1$, and condition (i) gives
\[
\frac{k-m}{n}
=
\frac{f^n(m)-m}{n}
-
\frac{f^n(k)-k}{n}
\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Taking $n=|k-m|+1$ forces $k=m$.

By condition (ii), there are finitely many positive integers
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_r
\]
which are not in the image of $f$. Also, condition (i) with $n=1$ gives
\[
f(m)>m
\qquad(m\ge1).
\tag{1}
\]

Every positive integer belongs to exactly one of the forward orbits
\[
a_i,\ f(a_i),\ f^2(a_i),\ldots.
\]
Uniqueness follows from injectivity. For existence, use induction on the integer in question: an integer not among the $a_i$ has a preimage, and by (1) that preimage is smaller.

Thus the positive integers occur exactly once in the following table:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
a_1&f(a_1)&f^2(a_1)&f^3(a_1)&\cdots\\
a_2&f(a_2)&f^2(a_2)&f^3(a_2)&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\\
a_r&f(a_r)&f^2(a_r)&f^3(a_r)&\cdots
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Step 2: every row of the table is an arithmetic progression.}

Suppose, for contradiction, that not every row is an arithmetic progression. Relabel the rows so that exactly the first $t<r$ rows are arithmetic progressions, with respective common differences
\[
T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_t.
\]
If $t>0$, put
\[
T=\operatorname{lcm}(T_1,\ldots,T_t),
\qquad
A=\max(a_1,\ldots,a_t).
\]
If $t=0$, put $T=1$ and $A=0$.

For every $n\ge A$, each interval of $T$ consecutive integers
\[
[n+1,n+T]
\]
contains exactly $T/T_i$ terms from the $i$th arithmetic row. Hence the number of terms in this interval belonging to the remaining $r-t$ rows is independent of $n$. It cannot be zero, because those rows contain infinitely many integers. Therefore every such interval contains at least one term from the last $r-t$ rows.

Fix a positive integer $d$. The interval
\[
[A+1,\ A+(d+1)(r-t)T]
\]
contains at least $(d+1)(r-t)$ terms from the last $r-t$ rows. Hence one of those rows, say row $x$, contributes at least $d+1$ terms, and therefore
\[
f^d(a_x)\le A+(d+1)(r-t)T.
\tag{3}
\]
There are only finitely many possible rows $x$, so some fixed $x>t$ satisfies (3) for infinitely many $d$. Let $X$ be the infinite set of such $d$.

For $d\in X$, condition (i) shows that
\[
\beta_d=\frac{f^d(a_x)-a_x}{d}
\]
is a positive integer. By (3), the numbers $\beta_d$ are bounded independently of $d$. Thus there is a positive integer $T_x$ and an infinite subset $Y\subseteq X$ such that
\[
f^d(a_x)=a_x+dT_x
\qquad(d\in Y).
\tag{4}
\]

We now prove that (4) holds for every positive integer $d$. Fix $j\ge1$. Choose $y\in Y$ with $y>j$ and
\[
y-j>
\left|f^j(a_x)-(a_x+jT_x)\right|.
\tag{5}
\]
Condition (i), applied with initial value $f^j(a_x)$ and exponent $y-j$, gives
\[
y-j\mid f^y(a_x)-f^j(a_x).
\]
By (4),
\[
f^y(a_x)-(a_x+jT_x)=(y-j)T_x,
\]
which is also divisible by $y-j$. Hence
\[
y-j\mid f^j(a_x)-(a_x+jT_x).
\]
Together with (5), this forces
\[
f^j(a_x)=a_x+jT_x.
\]
Thus row $x$ is an arithmetic progression, contradicting the definition of $t$.

Therefore every row in (2) is an arithmetic progression. Let its common differences be
\[
T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_r.
\]

\textbf{Step 3: periodicity.}

Put
\[
T=\operatorname{lcm}(T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_r).
\]
Take any positive integer $n$, and suppose it lies in row $i$. Then
\[
f^j(n)=n+jT_i
\qquad(j\ge0).
\]
In particular,
\[
n+T=f^{T/T_i}(n),
\]
and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(n+T)-(n+T)
&=f^{1+T/T_i}(n)-(n+T)\\
&=(n+T+T_i)-(n+T)\\
&=T_i\\
&=f(n)-n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the sequence $f(n)-n$ has period $T$, as required.
