ID: 0321

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Radical Axes, Simson's Theorem, Reflections, Orthocentre, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $P$ be a point on the circumcircle of an acute triangle $ABC$. Let $D,E,F$ be the reflections of $P$ in the midlines of triangle $ABC$ parallel to $BC,CA,AB$, respectively.

Let
\[
\omega_A=(ADP),\qquad
\omega_B=(BEP),\qquad
\omega_C=(CFP).
\]
The perpendicular bisectors of $AD,BE,CF$ form a triangle. Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of that triangle.

Prove that
\[
\omega_A,\omega_B,\omega_C,\omega
\]
have a common point.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
AA_1,\qquad BB_1,\qquad CC_1
\]
be the altitudes of triangle $ABC$, and let
\[
m_A,\qquad m_B,\qquad m_C
\]
be its midlines parallel to $BC,CA,AB$, respectively. We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$.

\textbf{Step 1: The circles $\omega_A,\omega_B,\omega_C$ have a second common point $Q$.}

The line $m_A$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PD$. Reflection in $m_A$ also sends $A$ to $A_1$. Since $\omega_A$ is symmetric about $m_A$,
\[
A_1\in\omega_A.
\]
Similarly,
\[
B_1\in\omega_B,
\qquad
C_1\in\omega_C.
\]

Let $H$ be the orthocentre of triangle $ABC$. The quadrilaterals
\[
A,B,A_1,B_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B,C,B_1,C_1
\]
are cyclic. Using directed lengths along the three altitudes,
\[
HA\cdot HA_1
=
HB\cdot HB_1
=
HC\cdot HC_1.
\tag{1}
\]
The three directed products in (1) are the powers of $H$ with respect to
\[
\omega_A,\qquad\omega_B,\qquad\omega_C.
\]
Thus $H$ lies on every pairwise radical axis. The point $P$ also lies on all three circles, so the common radical axis is
\[
PH.
\]

Because $ABC$ is acute, $H$ lies inside each of the three circles, so $PH$ is not tangent to them at $P$. Let $Q\ne P$ be the second intersection of $PH$ with $\omega_A$. Equation (1) implies that $Q$ also lies on $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$. Hence
\[
Q\in\omega_A\cap\omega_B\cap\omega_C.
\tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Step 2: The point $Q$ lies on $\omega$.}

Let
\[
p_A,\qquad p_B,\qquad p_C
\]
be the perpendicular bisectors of $AD,BE,CF$, respectively, and let $\Delta$ be the triangle formed by these three lines.

By Simson's theorem, it is enough to show that the perpendicular projections of $Q$ onto the three sidelines
\[
p_A,p_B,p_C
\]
are collinear. Equivalently, it is enough to show that the reflections
\[
Q_A,\qquad Q_B,\qquad Q_C
\]
of $Q$ in $p_A,p_B,p_C$, respectively, are collinear.

In fact, we prove the stronger statement that
\[
P,Q_A,Q_B,Q_C
\]
are collinear.

The line $p_A$ is the common perpendicular bisector of $AD$ and $QQ_A$. Hence $Q_A\in\omega_A$. Using the cyclic quadrilateral
\[
P,A,D,Q_A
\]
and the fact that $D$ is the reflection of $P$ in the line parallel to $BC$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(PA,PQ_A)
&=\measuredangle(DA,DQ_A)\\
&=\measuredangle(AQ,AD)\\
&=\measuredangle(PQ,PD)\\
&=\measuredangle(PQ,BC)+90^\circ.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\measuredangle(PB,PQ_B)
=
\measuredangle(PQ,CA)+90^\circ.
\tag{4}
\]

Using (3)--(4) and the cyclicity of $A,B,C,P$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(PQ_A,PQ_B)
&=
\measuredangle(PQ_A,PA)
+\measuredangle(PA,PB)
+\measuredangle(PB,PQ_B)\\
&=
\measuredangle(BC,PQ)+90^\circ
+\measuredangle(CA,CB)
+\measuredangle(PQ,CA)+90^\circ\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
P,Q_A,Q_B
\]
are collinear. The same calculation gives
\[
P,Q_A,Q_C
\]
collinear as well.

Therefore the projections of $Q$ onto $p_A,p_B,p_C$ are collinear. By Simson's theorem,
\[
Q\in\omega.
\]
Together with (2), this proves that all four circles have the common point $Q$.
