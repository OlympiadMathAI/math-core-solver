ID: 0012

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Constructive Processes, Recursion

STATEMENT:
Six stacks $S_1,\ldots,S_6$ of coins stand in a row, initially with one coin in each stack. The following moves are allowed:

1. If $S_k$ is nonempty for some $1\le k\le5$, remove one coin from $S_k$ and add two coins to $S_{k+1}$.

2. If $S_k$ is nonempty for some $1\le k\le4$, remove one coin from $S_k$ and exchange the contents of $S_{k+1}$ and $S_{k+2}$.

Is it possible to reach a position in which $S_1,\ldots,S_5$ are empty and
\[
S_6=2010^{2010^{2010}}?
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Yes, it is possible.

For consecutive stacks, write
\[
(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\longrightarrow(a'_1,\ldots,a'_m)
\]
when a finite sequence of allowed moves changes those stack sizes as indicated while leaving all other stacks unchanged.

Let
\[
A=2010^{2010^{2010}}.
\]
We will reach
\[
(1,1,1,1,1,1)\longrightarrow(0,0,0,0,0,A).
\]

\textbf{Lemma 1.} For every integer $a\ge1$,
\[
(a,0,0)\longrightarrow(0,2^a,0).
\]
More generally, by induction on $k$,
\[
(a,0,0)\longrightarrow(a-k,2^k,0)
\qquad(1\le k\le a).
\]
For $k=1$, use Move 1:
\[
(a,0,0)\longrightarrow(a-1,2,0).
\]
If $(a-k,2^k,0)$ has been reached, apply Move 1 to the middle stack $2^k$ times, obtaining
\[
(a-k,0,2^{k+1}),
\]
and then apply Move 2 to the first stack:
\[
(a-k,0,2^{k+1})\longrightarrow(a-k-1,2^{k+1},0).
\]
Taking $k=a$ proves the lemma.

For $n\ge1$, define the tower
\[
P_1=2,
\qquad
P_{n+1}=2^{P_n}.
\]
Thus, for example, $P_3=2^{2^2}=16$.

\textbf{Lemma 2.} For every integer $a\ge1$,
\[
(a,0,0,0)\longrightarrow(0,P_a,0,0).
\]
We prove more generally that
\[
(a,0,0,0)\longrightarrow(a-k,P_k,0,0)
\qquad(1\le k\le a).
\]
The case $k=1$ follows from Move 1. If $(a-k,P_k,0,0)$ has been reached, apply Lemma 1 to the final three stacks:
\[
(a-k,P_k,0,0)
\longrightarrow(a-k,0,2^{P_k},0)
=(a-k,0,P_{k+1},0).
\]
Then apply Move 2 to the first stack to get
\[
(a-k-1,P_{k+1},0,0).
\]
This proves Lemma 2.

We now begin from the initial position. Apply Move 1 to $S_5$, and then Move 2 at $S_4,S_3,S_2,S_1$ in that order:
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1,1,1,1,1,1)
&\longrightarrow(1,1,1,1,0,3)\\
&\longrightarrow(1,1,1,0,3,0)\\
&\longrightarrow(1,1,0,3,0,0)\\
&\longrightarrow(1,0,3,0,0,0)\\
&\longrightarrow(0,3,0,0,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply Lemma 2 twice:
\[
(0,3,0,0,0,0)
\longrightarrow(0,0,P_3,0,0,0)
=(0,0,16,0,0,0)
\longrightarrow(0,0,0,P_{16},0,0).
\]
The number $P_{16}$ exceeds $A$. One convenient chain of estimates is
\[
\begin{aligned}
A
&< (2^{11})^{2010^{2010}}
=2^{11\cdot2010^{2010}}\\
&<2^{2010^{2011}}
<2^{(2^{11})^{2011}}\\
&=2^{2^{11\cdot2011}}
<2^{2^{2^{15}}}
<P_{16}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $A$ is divisible by $4$, repeatedly apply Move 2 at $S_4$. At each application one coin is removed from $S_4$, while the two empty stacks $S_5,S_6$ are merely exchanged. Thus
\[
(0,0,0,P_{16},0,0)
\longrightarrow(0,0,0,A/4,0,0).
\]
Finally, repeatedly use Move 1 first at $S_4$ and then at $S_5$:
\[
(0,0,0,A/4,0,0)
\longrightarrow(0,0,0,0,A/2,0)
\longrightarrow(0,0,0,0,0,A).
\]
Hence the required position is reachable.
