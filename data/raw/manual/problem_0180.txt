ID: 0180

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Polynomials, Real Roots, Sign Analysis, Modular Classes

STATEMENT:
The equation
\[
(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-2016)
=
(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-2016)
\]
is written on a board. One tries to erase some linear factors from both sides so that each side still has at least one factor and the resulting equation has no real roots.

Find the least number of linear factors that must be erased.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{2016}.
\]

For every $k\in\{1,2,\ldots,2016\}$, at least one of the two copies of the factor $x-k$ must be erased. Otherwise $x=k$ remains a root of the resulting equation. Hence at least $2016$ factors must be erased.

We now show that $2016$ erasures suffice. On the left erase all factors $x-k$ with
\[
k\equiv2,3\pmod4,
\]
and on the right erase all factors $x-k$ with
\[
k\equiv0,1\pmod4.
\]
The remaining equation is
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{503}(x-4j-1)(x-4j-4)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{503}(x-4j-2)(x-4j-3).
\tag{1}
\]
Exactly $2016$ factors have been erased. We prove that (1) has no real solution.

If $x$ is one of the integers $1,2,\ldots,2016$, exactly one side of (1) is zero.

Suppose next that, for some $0\le k\le503$,
\[
4k+1<x<4k+2
\quad\text{or}\quad
4k+3<x<4k+4.
\]
On the left, the factor pair corresponding to $j=k$ is negative, while every other pair is positive. Every pair on the right is positive. Thus the two sides have opposite signs.

Now suppose that
\[
x<1,
\quad x>2016,
\quad\text{or}\quad
4k<x<4k+1
\]
for some $1\le k\le503$. Dividing the two sides of (1) gives
\[
1=
\prod_{j=0}^{503}
\left(
1-
\frac{2}{(x-4j-2)(x-4j-3)}
\right).
\tag{2}
\]
In these ranges,
\[
(x-4j-2)(x-4j-3)>2
\]
for every $j$, so every factor on the right of (2) lies strictly between $0$ and $1$. Their product cannot equal $1$.

The only remaining possibility is
\[
4k+2<x<4k+3
\qquad(0\le k\le503).
\]
Equation (1) may then be rearranged as
\[
\begin{aligned}
1={}&
\frac{x-1}{x-2}\cdot
\frac{x-2016}{x-2015}\\
&\cdot
\prod_{j=1}^{503}
\left(
1+
\frac{2}{(x-4j+1)(x-4j-2)}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Both initial fractions and every factor in the product are greater than $1$, so the right side of (3) is greater than $1$, again impossible.

All real numbers have been covered. Hence the constructed equation has no real roots, and the minimum number of erasures is $2016$.
