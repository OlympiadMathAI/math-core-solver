ID: 0388

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Divisors, Unit Fractions, Diophantine Equations, Divisibility

STATEMENT:
A positive integer is called \emph{Norwegian} if it has three distinct positive divisors whose sum is equal to $2022$.

Determine the smallest Norwegian number.

The total number of positive divisors of a Norwegian number is allowed to be larger than $3$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{1344}.
\]

Indeed,
\[
6+672+1344=2022,
\]
and all three summands are distinct positive divisors of $1344$. Thus $1344$ is Norwegian.

Suppose, for contradiction, that a Norwegian number
\[
N<1344
\]
exists. Write three of its distinct divisors as
\[
\frac Na,\qquad \frac Nb,\qquad \frac Nc,
\]
where
\[
a<b<c
\]
are positive integers. Then
\[
2022
=
N\left(\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c\right),
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c
>
\frac{2022}{1344}
=
\frac32+\frac1{224}.
\tag{1}
\]

If $a>1$, then
\[
a\ge2,\qquad b\ge3,\qquad c\ge4,
\]
so
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c
\le
\frac12+\frac13+\frac14
=
\frac{13}{12}
<
\frac32,
\]
contrary to (1). Hence
\[
a=1.
\]

If $b\ge4$, then $c\ge5$ and
\[
1+\frac1b+\frac1c
\le
1+\frac14+\frac15
<
\frac32,
\]
again contradicting (1). Thus
\[
b\in\{2,3\}.
\]

Suppose first that $b=3$. From (1),
\[
\frac1c
>
\frac32+\frac1{224}-1-\frac13
>
\frac16.
\]
Since $c>3$, this leaves only
\[
c=4\qquad\text{or}\qquad c=5.
\]
If $c=4$, then
\[
2022
=
N\left(1+\frac13+\frac14\right)
=
\frac{19}{12}N,
\]
which is impossible because $19\nmid2022$. If $c=5$, then
\[
2022
=
N\left(1+\frac13+\frac15\right)
=
\frac{23}{15}N,
\]
which is impossible because $23\nmid2022$.

It remains to consider $b=2$. Inequality (1) gives
\[
\frac1c>\frac1{224},
\]
so
\[
c<224.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
2022
=
N\left(1+\frac12+\frac1c\right)
=
\frac{3c+2}{2c}N,
\]
and hence
\[
(3c+2)N=4044c.
\tag{3}
\]
Since
\[
\gcd(c,3c+2)=\gcd(c,2)\in\{1,2\},
\]
equation (3) implies
\[
3c+2\mid8088=2^3\cdot3\cdot337.
\]
But
\[
3c+2\not\equiv0\pmod3,
\]
so in fact
\[
3c+2\mid2^3\cdot337.
\]
Now $c\ge3$, so $3c+2>8$, and
\[
3c+2\ne337
\]
because the two numbers have different residues modulo $3$. The next possible divisor of
\[
2^3\cdot337
\]
is at least
\[
2\cdot337=674.
\]
Thus
\[
3c+2\ge674,
\]
which gives $c\ge224$, contradicting (2).

Therefore no Norwegian number smaller than $1344$ exists, and the minimum is
\[
\boxed{1344}.
\]
