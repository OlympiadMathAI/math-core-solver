ID: 0343

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Circular Binary Sequences, Pigeonhole Principle, Periodic Sums, Extremal Arguments

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ and $k$ be integers with
\[
n>k\ge1.
\]
There are $2n+1$ students standing in a circle. Each student has $2k$ neighbours: the $k$ closest students on the right and the $k$ closest students on the left.

Suppose that $n+1$ of the students are girls and the other $n$ are boys. Prove that some girl has at least $k$ girls among her neighbours.

SOLUTION 1:
Represent girls by $1$ and boys by $0$. This gives a circular binary sequence
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_{2n+1},
\]
which we extend periodically to all integer indices.

For every integer $i$, define
\[
b_i=a_i+a_{i-k-1}-1.
\]
Then
\[
b_i\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
Since every period of the sequence $(a_i)$ contains $n+1$ ones,
\[
\sum_{i=t+1}^{t+2n+1}b_i
=
2(n+1)-(2n+1)
=
1
\tag{1}
\]
for every integer $t$. In particular, some $b_i$ equals $1$.

We claim that there is an index $i$ such that
\[
b_i=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b_{i+1}+b_{i+2}+\cdots+b_{i+k}\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, (2) gives
\[
a_i=a_{i-k-1}=1
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le
\sum_{j=1}^k b_{i+j}\\
&=
\sum_{j=1}^k a_{i+j}
+
\sum_{j=1}^k a_{i+j-k-1}
-k\\
&=
\left(a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{i+k}\right)
+
\left(a_{i-k}+\cdots+a_{i-1}\right)
-k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the girl at position $i$ has at least $k$ girls among her $2k$ neighbours.

It remains to prove the claim. Suppose, for contradiction, that whenever $b_i=1$,
\[
b_{i+1}+\cdots+b_{i+k}<0.
\tag{3}
\]
Choose an index $i_0$ with $b_{i_0}=1$. Recursively, let $i_{j+1}$ be the smallest index satisfying
\[
i_{j+1}>i_j+k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
b_{i_{j+1}}=1.
\]
Such an index always exists because $(b_i)$ is periodic and has sum $1$ over every period.

Among the $2n+2$ indices
\[
i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_{2n+1},
\]
two are congruent modulo $2n+1$. After discarding an initial part of the sequence and relabelling, we may assume that
\[
i_T\equiv i_0\pmod{2n+1}
\]
for some $T>0$.

For $0\le j<T$, put
\[
S_j=\sum_{r=i_j}^{i_{j+1}-1}b_r.
\]
By the minimality of $i_{j+1}$, all terms
\[
b_{i_j+k+1},\ldots,b_{i_{j+1}-1}
\]
are nonpositive. Using (3),
\[
S_j
\le
b_{i_j}+b_{i_j+1}+\cdots+b_{i_j+k}
\le0.
\tag{4}
\]

On the other hand,
\[
2n+1\mid i_T-i_0.
\]
Equation (1) therefore gives
\[
S_0+\cdots+S_{T-1}
=
\sum_{r=i_0}^{i_T-1}b_r
=
\frac{i_T-i_0}{2n+1}
>0,
\]
contradicting (4). Hence an index satisfying (2) exists, and the proof is complete.
