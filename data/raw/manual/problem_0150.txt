ID: 0150

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Finite Differences, Quasi-Periodicity, Integer-Valued Functions

STATEMENT:
Let $2\mathbb Z+1$ denote the set of odd integers. Find all functions
\[
f:\mathbb Z\to 2\mathbb Z+1
\]
satisfying
\[
f\bigl(x+f(x)+y\bigr)+f\bigl(x-f(x)-y\bigr)
=f(x+y)+f(x-y)
\tag{1}
\]
for every pair of integers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The complete family of solutions is as follows. Choose an odd positive integer $d$, an integer $k$, and odd integers
\[
\ell_0,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{d-1}.
\]
Then define
\[
\boxed{
f(md+i)=2kmd+\ell_i d
}
\tag{2}
\]
for all $m\in\mathbb Z$ and $i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}$. Every such function satisfies (1), and there are no other solutions.

For a function $g:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ and a nonzero integer $t$, write
\[
\Delta_tg(x)=g(x+t)-g(x).
\]
Difference operators commute:
\[
\Delta_a\Delta_bg=\Delta_b\Delta_ag.
\]
We call $t$ a \emph{quasi-period} of $g$ if $\Delta_tg$ is constant. Equivalently, $t$ is a period of $\Delta_1g$. Consequently, if a function is quasi-periodic, then its least positive quasi-period divides all of its quasi-periods.

Put
\[
a=x+y
\]
in (1). Then (1) is equivalent to
\[
\Delta_{f(x)}f(a)
=
\Delta_{f(x)}f\bigl(2x-a-f(x)\bigr)
\tag{3}
\]
for all integers $x,a$.

Let $b\in\mathbb Z$, and let $q$ be a positive integer. Apply (3) successively with
\[
a=b,b+f(x),\ldots,b+(q-1)f(x)
\]
and add the resulting equations. Both sides telescope, giving
\[
\Delta_{qf(x)}f(b)
=
\Delta_{qf(x)}f\bigl(2x-b-qf(x)\bigr).
\]
The same identity holds for negative $q$. Thus
\[
\Delta_Mf(b)=\Delta_Mf(2x-b-M)
\tag{4}
\]
whenever $M\ne0$ is divisible by $f(x)$.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
For distinct integers $x,y$, the function
\[
\Delta_Lf,
\qquad
L=\operatorname{lcm}(|f(x)|,|f(y)|),
\]
is $2(y-x)$-periodic.

\textit{Proof.}
Applying (4) first with $x$ and then with $y$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_Lf(b)
&=\Delta_Lf(2x-b-L)\\
&=\Delta_Lf\bigl(2y-(2x-b-L)-L\bigr)\\
&=\Delta_Lf\bigl(b+2(y-x)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the lemma. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
Let $g:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$, and let $t,s$ be nonzero integers. If
\[
\Delta_{ts}g=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Delta_t\Delta_tg=0,
\]
then
\[
\Delta_tg=0.
\]

\textit{Proof.}
We may assume $s>0$. Since $\Delta_t\Delta_tg=0$, the values
\[
\Delta_tg(a),\Delta_tg(a+t),\ldots,\Delta_tg(a+(s-1)t)
\]
are equal. Their sum is
\[
\Delta_{ts}g(a)=0,
\]
so each of them is zero. $\square$

\textbf{Step 1: $f$ is quasi-periodic.}
Let
\[
Q=\operatorname{lcm}(|f(0)|,|f(1)|).
\]
By Lemma 1, the function
\[
g=\Delta_Qf
\]
is $2$-periodic. Setting $M=Q$ and $x=b=0$ in (4) gives
\[
g(0)=g(-Q).
\]
Because $Q$ is odd, $0$ and $-Q$ have opposite parity. A $2$-periodic function is constant separately on the even and odd integers, so the last equality shows that these two constants coincide. Hence $g$ is constant, and $Q$ is a quasi-period of $f$.

\textbf{Step 2: the least positive quasi-period divides every value of $f$.}
Let $T$ be the least positive quasi-period of $f$. Since the odd number $Q$ is a quasi-period, $T$ is odd.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that there are an odd prime $p$, an integer $\alpha\ge1$, and $u\in\mathbb Z$ such that
\[
p^\alpha\mid T
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
p^\alpha\nmid f(u).
\tag{5}
\]
Putting $x=u$ and $y=0$ in (1), we get
\[
f(u+f(u))+f(u-f(u))=2f(u).
\]
Since $p$ is odd, (5) implies that at least one of the two values on the left is not divisible by $p^\alpha$. Let $v$ be the corresponding argument, so
\[
v\in\{u+f(u),u-f(u)\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p^\alpha\nmid f(v).
\]
Set
\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}(|f(u)|,|f(v)|).
\]
Then $p^\alpha\nmid L$.

By Lemma 1, $\Delta_Lf$ has period $2f(u)$. Since $T$ is a quasi-period of $f$, the function $\Delta_Lf$ also has period $T$. Therefore it has period
\[
A=\gcd(T,2f(u)),
\]
so
\[
\Delta_A\Delta_Lf=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Similarly, $\Delta_Af$ has periods $L$ and $T$, hence it has period
\[
B=\gcd(T,L),
\]
and therefore
\[
\Delta_B\Delta_Af=0.
\tag{7}
\]
Both $A$ and $B$ divide $T/p$. Put $q=T/p$, and write $q=rA=sB$ with positive integers $r,s$. By (7), the function $\Delta_Af$ is $B$-periodic, hence also $q$-periodic. Therefore
\[
\Delta_q\Delta_Af=0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\Delta_qf(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\Delta_Af(x+jA).
\]
Applying $\Delta_q$ to this identity shows that
\[
\Delta_q\Delta_qf=0,
\]
i.e.
\[
\Delta_{T/p}\Delta_{T/p}f=0.
\]
Taking one more first difference,
\[
\Delta_{T/p}\Delta_{T/p}\Delta_1f=0.
\tag{8}
\]
On the other hand, because $T$ is a quasi-period,
\[
\Delta_T\Delta_1f=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Apply Lemma 2 to $g=\Delta_1f$, with $t=T/p$ and $s=p$. From (8) and (9), we obtain
\[
\Delta_{T/p}\Delta_1f=0.
\]
Thus $T/p$ is a positive quasi-period of $f$, contradicting the minimality of $T$. Hence
\[
T\mid f(x)
\]
for every integer $x$.

\textbf{Step 3: description of all solutions.}
Let $d$ be the positive greatest common divisor of all values of $f$. Since all values are odd, $d$ is odd. From Step 2, $T\mid d$; therefore $d$ is also a quasi-period. Hence
\[
\Delta_df
\]
is constant. This constant is divisible by $d$, and it is even because it is a difference of two odd integers. Thus there is an integer $k$ such that
\[
\Delta_df=2dk.
\]
For $i=0,1,\ldots,d-1$, set
\[
\ell_i=\frac{f(i)}d.
\]
Each $\ell_i$ is odd. Therefore, for every $m\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
f(md+i)=f(i)+m\Delta_df
=\ell_i d+2kmd,
\]
which is exactly (2).

It remains to verify the construction. For a function of the form (2), every value $f(x)$ is divisible by $d$, and $\Delta_{qd}f$ is constant for every integer $q$. In particular, $\Delta_{f(x)}f$ is constant, so the two sides of (3) are equal. Hence (1) holds. This completes the classification.
