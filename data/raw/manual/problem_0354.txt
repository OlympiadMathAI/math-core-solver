ID: 0354

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Excircles, Brianchon's Theorem, Inversion, Spiral Similarity, Circle Tangency

STATEMENT:
Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$, and let $\Omega_A$ be its $A$-excircle, which is tangent to segment $BC$. Let $X$ and $Y$ be the intersection points of $\omega$ and $\Omega_A$.

Let $P$ and $Q$ be the projections of $A$ onto the tangent lines to $\Omega_A$ at $X$ and $Y$, respectively. The tangent at $P$ to the circumcircle of triangle $APX$ meets the tangent at $Q$ to the circumcircle of triangle $AQY$ at $R$.

Prove that
\[
AR\perp BC.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $D$ be the point where $\Omega_A$ touches $BC$, and let $D_1$ be the point diametrically opposite $D$ on $\Omega_A$.

Let $R_1$ be the intersection of the line through $A$ perpendicular to $BC$ and the line through $D_1$ parallel to $BC$. We prove that
\[
R_1=R.
\]

Let the tangent line $PX$ meet $AB$ at $S$ and $D_1R_1$ at $T$. Let $U$ be the point at infinity in the common direction of
\[
BC\parallel D_1R_1.
\]

The degenerate hexagon
\[
A,S,X,T,U,C
\]
is circumscribed about $\Omega_A$: its sides lie successively on the tangent lines $AB$, the tangent at $X$ twice, the tangent at $D_1$, the tangent $BC$, and the tangent $CA$.

By Brianchon's theorem, the three main diagonals
\[
AT,\qquad SU,\qquad XC
\]
are concurrent. Let their common point be $V$. Since $U$ is the point at infinity in the direction of $BC$,
\[
SV\parallel BC.
\tag{1}
\]

The points $A,B,C,X$ lie on $\omega$. Hence
\[
\measuredangle(SV,VX)
=
\measuredangle(BC,CX)
=
\measuredangle(BA,AX).
\]
Thus
\[
A,X,S,V
\]
are concyclic. Consequently,
\[
\measuredangle(PX,XA)
=
\measuredangle(SV,VA)
=
\measuredangle(R_1T,TA).
\tag{2}
\]

We have
\[
\angle APT=90^\circ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle AR_1T=90^\circ,
\]
so
\[
A,P,R_1,T
\]
are concyclic. Using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(XA,AP)
&=
90^\circ-\measuredangle(PX,XA)\\
&=
90^\circ-\measuredangle(R_1T,TA)\\
&=
\measuredangle(TA,AR_1)\\
&=
\measuredangle(TP,PR_1).
\end{aligned}
\]
By the tangent--chord theorem,
\[
PR_1
\]
is tangent at $P$ to the circle $(APX)$.

The same argument with $Y,Q$ in place of $X,P$ shows that
\[
QR_1
\]
is tangent at $Q$ to the circle $(AQY)$. Thus the two tangents meet at $R_1$, so
\[
R=R_1.
\]
By the definition of $R_1$,
\[
AR\perp BC.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let $J$ and $r$ be the centre and radius of the $A$-excircle $\Omega_A$. Let $O$ be the centre of $\omega$, and let
\[
d=2R_\omega
\]
be the diameter of $\omega$.

Euler's formula for the distance between the circumcentre and the $A$-excentre gives
\[
OJ^2=\left(\frac d2\right)^2+dr.
\]
Hence the power of $J$ with respect to $\omega$ is
\[
dr.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the line $JX$ meet $\omega$ again at $L$. Since
\[
JX=r,
\]
equation (1) gives
\[
JL=d.
\tag{2}
\]
Let $LK$ be a diameter of $\omega$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $JK$. From (2),
\[
JL=LK.
\]
Thus the median $LM$ in triangle $JLK$ is perpendicular to $JK$, so
\[
\angle LMK=90^\circ.
\]
Since $LK$ is a diameter of $\omega$,
\[
M\in\omega.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $R'$ be the intersection of the tangent at $P$ to $(APX)$ with the line through $A$ perpendicular to $BC$. The line $AR'$ is the reflection of $AO$ in the angle-bisector line $AJ$.

We need the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $JK$ in a triangle $AJK$. Suppose
\[
X\in(AMK)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle JXK=90^\circ.
\]
Then there is a point $T\in KX$ such that
\[
\triangle AKJ\sim\triangle AJT
\]
with the same orientation.

\textit{Proof.}
Since $M$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $JK$ of the right triangle $JXK$,
\[
MX=MJ=MK.
\]
Construct the parallelogram $AJNK$. Choose $T\in KX$ so that
\[
\measuredangle(NJ,JA)=\measuredangle(KJ,JT).
\]
Because $A,M,N$ are collinear and $A,K,M,X$ are concyclic,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(JN,NA)
&=\measuredangle(KA,AM)\\
&=\measuredangle(KX,XM)\\
&=\measuredangle(MK,KX)\\
&=\measuredangle(JK,KT).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence a spiral similarity centred at $J$ maps triangle $AJN$ to triangle $TJK$. Since $AJNK$ is a parallelogram, this gives
\[
\triangle AKJ\sim\triangle AJT
\]
with the same orientation. $\square$

In our configuration, (3) gives
\[
A,M,K,X\in\omega.
\]
Also, $J,X,L$ are collinear and $LK$ is a diameter, so
\[
\angle JXK=\angle LXK=90^\circ.
\]
Apply the lemma and obtain $T\in KX$.

Let $\varphi$ be the composition of the inversion centred at $A$ with radius $AJ$ and reflection in the line $AJ$. For any points $E,F$, the triangles
\[
AEF
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A\varphi(F)\varphi(E)
\]
are directly similar. The similarity supplied by the lemma therefore gives
\[
\varphi(K)=T,
\qquad
\varphi(T)=K.
\tag{4}
\]

Put
\[
P^*=\varphi(P),
\qquad
R^*=\varphi(R').
\]
The line
\[
TK=KX=PX
\]
is mapped by $\varphi$ to the circle with diameter $AP^*$, because $AP\perp PX$.

Let $AA'$ be a diameter of $\omega$. Since $K$ lies on the circle with diameter $AP^*$,
\[
P^*K\perp AK.
\]
Also,
\[
A'K\perp AK.
\]
Therefore
\[
P^*,K,A'
\]
are collinear.

The direct similarity
\[
\triangle AR'P\sim\triangle AP^*R^*
\]
and the tangent--chord theorem at $P$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(AA',A'P^*)
&=\measuredangle(AA',A'K)\\
&=\measuredangle(AX,XP)\\
&=\measuredangle(AP,PR')\\
&=\measuredangle(AR^*,R^*P^*).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
A,A',R^*,P^*
\]
are concyclic.

The point $R'$ lies on the altitude from $A$, which is the reflection of $AO$ in $AJ$. Hence $R^*$ lies on $AO$. The line $AO$ meets the circle $(AA'R^*P^*)$ at $A$ and $A'$, so
\[
R^*=A'.
\]
Therefore
\[
R'=\varphi(A').
\tag{5}
\]

Repeating the same argument with $Y,Q$ in place of $X,P$ shows that the tangent at $Q$ to $(AQY)$ also passes through the same point $\varphi(A')$. By (5), this point is $R'$. Hence
\[
R=R'.
\]
Since $AR'$ is the altitude from $A$,
\[
AR\perp BC.
\]
