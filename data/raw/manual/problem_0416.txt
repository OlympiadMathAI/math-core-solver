ID: 0416

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Direct and Opposite Similarity, Apollonius Circles, Orthocentre, Circumcentre

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute scalene triangle with orthocentre $H$.

Let $\ell_a$ be the line through the reflection of $B$ in $CH$ and the reflection of $C$ in $BH$. Define $\ell_b$ and $\ell_c$ cyclically. Suppose that
\[
\ell_a,\ell_b,\ell_c
\]
form a nondegenerate triangle $\mathcal T$.

Prove that the orthocentre of $\mathcal T$, the circumcentre of $\mathcal T$, and $H$ are collinear.

SOLUTION 1:
Let

\[
A_b,\ A_c
\]
be the reflections of $A$ in $BH$ and $CH$, respectively;

\[
B_c,\ B_a
\]
the reflections of $B$ in $CH$ and $AH$; and

\[
C_a,\ C_b
\]
the reflections of $C$ in $AH$ and $BH$.

Then
\[
\ell_a=B_cC_b,
\qquad
\ell_b=C_aA_c,
\qquad
\ell_c=A_bB_a.
\]
Put
\[
A_1=\ell_b\cap\ell_c,\qquad
B_1=\ell_c\cap\ell_a,\qquad
C_1=\ell_a\cap\ell_b.
\]
Let $H_1$ and $O_1$ be the orthocentre and circumcentre of triangle
\[
A_1B_1C_1.
\]

We use directed angles. Write
\[
\triangle U_1U_2U_3\sim_+\triangle V_1V_2V_3
\]
for direct similarity and
\[
\triangle U_1U_2U_3\sim_-\triangle V_1V_2V_3
\]
for opposite similarity.

\textbf{Claim 1.}
\[
\triangle AA_bA_c\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
P=BH\cap AC,
\qquad
Q=CH\cap AB.
\]
The standard altitude-angle relations give
\[
\triangle APQ\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\]
Since $P$ and $Q$ are the feet of the perpendiculars from $A$ to $BH$ and $CH$, respectively, the dilation of ratio $2$ centred at $A$ sends
\[
P\longmapsto A_b,
\qquad
Q\longmapsto A_c.
\]
This proves (1).

\textbf{Claim 2.}
The triangles
\[
AA_bA_c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AB_aC_a
\]
are directly similar, and
\[
A_1\in(AA_bA_c).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, the circle in (2) has centre $H$.

Indeed, triangle $AB_aC_a$ is the reflection of triangle $ABC$ in $AH$, so
\[
\triangle AB_aC_a\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\]
Together with Claim 1, this gives the direct similarity
\[
\triangle AA_bA_c\sim_+\triangle AB_aC_a,
\]
with $A$ as its spiral-similarity centre. Since
\[
A_1=A_cC_a\cap A_bB_a,
\]
the spiral-similarity angle gives
\[
\measuredangle A_cA_1A_b
=
\measuredangle A_cAA_b.
\]
Thus $A,A_b,A_c,A_1$ are cyclic.

Reflection fixes $H$, so
\[
HA_b=HA=HA_c.
\]
Hence $H$ is the centre of this circle. In particular,
\[
HA_1=HA.
\tag{3}
\]
Cyclically,
\[
HB_1=HB,
\qquad
HC_1=HC.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Claim 3.}
\[
\triangle A_1B_1C_1\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\tag{5}
\]

Using Claim 2 and the definitions of the three sidelines,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle C_1A_1B_1
&=
\measuredangle A_cA_1A_b\\
&=
\measuredangle A_cAA_b\\
&=
-\measuredangle CAB.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two cyclic analogues give
\[
\measuredangle A_1B_1C_1=-\measuredangle ABC,
\qquad
\measuredangle B_1C_1A_1=-\measuredangle BCA.
\]
This proves (5).

Let $\lambda>0$ be the similarity ratio in (5). Opposite similarity sends orthocentres to orthocentres, so
\[
\lambda
=
\frac{H_1A_1}{HA}
=
\frac{H_1B_1}{HB}
=
\frac{H_1C_1}{HC}.
\]
Using (3)-(4),
\[
\frac{H_1A_1}{HA_1}
=
\frac{H_1B_1}{HB_1}
=
\frac{H_1C_1}{HC_1}
=
\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]

If $H_1=H$, the conclusion is immediate. Assume $H_1\ne H$. If $\lambda=1$, equation (6) would place the three noncollinear points $A_1,B_1,C_1$ on the perpendicular bisector of $HH_1$, which is impossible. Hence $\lambda\ne1$.

Equation (6) now says that $A_1,B_1,C_1$ lie on the Apollonius circle of segment $HH_1$ with ratio $\lambda$. Therefore their circumcircle is that Apollonius circle, whose centre lies on
\[
HH_1.
\]
Thus
\[
H,\ O_1,\ H_1
\]
are collinear.

SOLUTION 2:
Use the reflection notation
\[
A_b,A_c,B_c,B_a,C_a,C_b
\]
defined as follows:
\[
A_b=\operatorname{refl}_{BH}(A),\quad
A_c=\operatorname{refl}_{CH}(A),
\]
and cyclically. Put
\[
\ell_a=B_cC_b,\qquad
\ell_b=C_aA_c,\qquad
\ell_c=A_bB_a,
\]
and
\[
A_1=\ell_b\cap\ell_c,\qquad
B_1=\ell_c\cap\ell_a,\qquad
C_1=\ell_a\cap\ell_b.
\]
Let $O_1,H_1$ be the circumcentre and orthocentre of $A_1B_1C_1$, and let $O$ be the circumcentre of $ABC$.

We first establish three elementary facts.

\textbf{Fact 1.}
\[
\triangle AA_bA_c\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\]
Indeed, if
\[
P=BH\cap AC,\qquad Q=CH\cap AB,
\]
then $\triangle APQ\sim_-\triangle ABC$, while the dilation of ratio $2$ centred at $A$ sends $P,Q$ to $A_b,A_c$.

\textbf{Fact 2.}
The point $A_1$ lies on the circle
\[
(AA_bA_c),
\]
and this circle has centre $H$.

The triangle $AB_aC_a$ is the reflection of $ABC$ in $AH$, so it is oppositely similar to $ABC$. Combining this with Fact 1 gives a direct spiral similarity centred at $A$ from $AA_bA_c$ to $AB_aC_a$. Since
\[
A_1=A_cC_a\cap A_bB_a,
\]
we obtain
\[
\measuredangle A_cA_1A_b=\measuredangle A_cAA_b,
\]
and hence the cyclicity. The equalities
\[
HA_b=HA=HA_c
\]
show that the centre is $H$.

Cyclically,
\[
HA_1=HA,\qquad HB_1=HB,\qquad HC_1=HC.
\tag{1}
\]

\textbf{Fact 3.}
\[
\triangle A_1B_1C_1\sim_-\triangle ABC.
\tag{2}
\]
For example,
\[
\measuredangle C_1A_1B_1
=
\measuredangle A_cA_1A_b
=
\measuredangle A_cAA_b
=
-\measuredangle CAB,
\]
and the other two angles follow cyclically.

Now let $A_2$ be the reflection of $A_1$ in $AH$. Since the circle $(AA_bA_c)$ is centred at $H$, it contains $A_2$ as well.

The direct similarity used in Fact 2 also gives
\[
\measuredangle B_aA_1C_a
=
\measuredangle A_bA_1A_c
=
\measuredangle A_bAA_c
=
\measuredangle B_aAC_a.
\]
Hence
\[
A_1\in(AB_aC_a).
\]
Reflecting this circle in $AH$ shows that
\[
A_2\in(ABC).
\]
Thus $A$ and $A_2$ are the two common points of a circle centred at $H$ and the circumcircle centred at $O$. Their common chord $AA_2$ is perpendicular to $OH$, so $A$ and $A_2$ are reflections in line $OH$.

Define $B_2,C_2$ cyclically. Then
\[
\triangle A_2B_2C_2
\]
is the reflection of $\triangle ABC$ in $OH$, and
\[
(A_2B_2C_2)=(ABC).
\tag{3}
\]

\textbf{Claim 1.}
The lines
\[
A_1A_2,\qquad B_1B_2,\qquad C_1C_2
\]
are concurrent at a point $T\in(ABC)$.

Because $A_2$ is the reflection of $A_1$ in $AH$,
\[
A_1A_2\parallel BC.
\]
Similarly,
\[
B_1B_2\parallel CA,
\qquad
C_1C_2\parallel AB.
\]
Let
\[
T=A_1A_2\cap B_1B_2.
\]
Using (3),
\[
\measuredangle A_2TB_2
=
\measuredangle BCA
=
\measuredangle A_2C_2B_2.
\]
Hence $T\in(A_2B_2C_2)=(ABC)$. The same argument with $A_1A_2$ and $C_1C_2$ shows that $C_1C_2$ also passes through $T$.

\textbf{Claim 2.}
The point $T$ lies on $(A_1B_1C_1)$, and the opposite similarity in (2) fixes $T$.

From Claim 1 and (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle B_1TC_1
&=
\measuredangle B_2TC_2\\
&=
\measuredangle B_2A_2C_2\\
&=
-\measuredangle BAC\\
&=
\measuredangle B_1A_1C_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
T\in(A_1B_1C_1).
\]
To identify its image under the similarity, use
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle A_1B_1T
&=
\measuredangle A_1B_1B_2\\
&=
\measuredangle A_1A_bA\\
&=
\measuredangle A_1A_2A\\
&=
-\measuredangle AA_2T\\
&=
-\measuredangle ABT.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with the corresponding cyclic-angle relation, this shows that the similarity maps $T$ to itself.

\textbf{Claim 3.}
The circumradius of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ is
\[
HO.
\tag{4}
\]

The two circles centred at $H$ which cut line $\ell_c=A_bB_a$ in
\[
A_1,A_b
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B_1,B_a
\]
have the same perpendicular from $H$ to $\ell_c$. Therefore the two chords have the same midpoint, and
\[
A_1B_1=A_bB_a.
\tag{5}
\]

Let $O_a$ be the circumcentre of triangle $AB_aC_a$. It is the reflection of $O$ in $AH$, so
\[
HO_a=HO.
\]
The direct spiral similarity centred at $A$ from $AA_bA_c$ to $AB_aC_a$ sends their circumcentres
\[
H\longmapsto O_a.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{AA_b}{B_aA_b}
=
\frac{AH}{HO_a}
=
\frac{AH}{HO},
\]
or
\[
\frac{AH}{AA_b}
=
\frac{HO}{A_bB_a}.
\tag{6}
\]

Furthermore, triangles $A_1O_1B_1$ and $AHA_b$ are directly similar, since each is oppositely similar to $AOB$. Hence
\[
\frac{A_1O_1}{A_1B_1}
=
\frac{AH}{AA_b}.
\]
Using (5)-(6),
\[
A_1O_1=HO.
\]
This proves (4).

Since $T$ lies on $(A_1B_1C_1)$,
\[
O_1T=HO.
\tag{7}
\]

The opposite similarity from Claim 2 sends
\[
A_1\longmapsto A,\qquad O_1\longmapsto O,\qquad T\longmapsto T.
\]
Using $T,A,A_2\in(ABC)$ and $OH\perp AA_2$, the corresponding directed angles give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(TA_1,TO_1)
&=
\measuredangle(TO,TA)\\
&=
90^\circ+\measuredangle(TA_1,AA_2)\\
&=
\measuredangle(TA_1,OH).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
O_1T\parallel HO.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)-(8) show that $O_1TOH$ is a parallelogram.

Finally, the same opposite similarity sends $H_1$ to $H$. Hence
\[
\measuredangle H_1O_1T=\measuredangle TOH.
\]
The parallelogram gives
\[
\measuredangle TOH=\measuredangle HO_1T.
\]
Thus
\[
H_1,O_1,H
\]
are collinear, as required.
