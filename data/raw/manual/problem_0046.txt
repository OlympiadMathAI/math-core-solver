ID: 0046

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Homothety, Radical Axis, Tangent Circles, Centroid

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with circumcircle $\Omega$. Let $B_0$ and $C_0$ be the midpoints of $AC$ and $AB$, respectively. Let $D$ be the foot of the altitude from $A$, and let $G$ be the centroid of triangle $ABC$. A circle $\omega$ through $B_0$ and $C_0$ is tangent to $\Omega$ at a point $X\ne A$. Prove that $D,G,X$ are collinear.

SOLUTION 1:
If $AB=AC$, the whole configuration is symmetric about the altitude from $A$, and the assertion follows immediately. Assume from now on that
\[
AB<AC.
\]
Let $a$ and $x$ be the tangents to $\Omega$ at $A$ and $X$, respectively. Let $\Omega_1$ be the circumcircle of triangle $AB_0C_0$.

The homothety with center $A$ and ratio $1/2$ maps $\Omega$ to $\Omega_1$. Hence the two circles are tangent at $A$, and their radical axis is the line $a$. The radical axis of $\Omega$ and $\omega$ is the common tangent $x$, while the radical axis of $\Omega_1$ and $\omega$ is $B_0C_0$. By the radical-axis theorem, the three lines
\[
a,\quad x,\quad B_0C_0
\]
meet at one point; call it $W$.

The line $B_0C_0$ is the midline parallel to $BC$, and reflection in it maps $A$ to the altitude foot $D$. Thus
\[
WA=WD.
\]
Also $WA$ and $WX$ are tangent segments from $W$ to $\Omega$, so
\[
WA=WX.
\]
Therefore
\[
WA=WD=WX,
\]
and $W$ is the center of the circle $\gamma$ through $A,D,X$.

Let $O$ be the center of $\Omega$. Since
\[
WA\perp OA,
\qquad
WX\perp OX,
\]
the points $A,W,X,O$ are cyclic, and hence
\[
\angle AWX+\angle AOX=180^\circ.
\tag{1}
\]
Let $T\ne X$ be the second intersection of the line $DX$ with $\Omega$. In the circle $\gamma$,
\[
\angle ADX=\frac12\bigl(360^\circ-\angle AWX\bigr),
\]
while in $\Omega$,
\[
\angle ATX=\frac12\angle AOX.
\]
Using directed angles along the line $D,X,T$ (equivalently, the exterior-angle relation in the displayed configuration), we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DAT
&=\angle ADX-\angle ATX\\
&=180^\circ-\frac12\bigl(\angle AWX+\angle AOX\bigr)\\
&=90^\circ
\end{aligned}
\]
by (1). Thus
\[
AT\parallel BC.
\tag{2}
\]
The cyclic trapezoid $ATCB$ is therefore isosceles, so
\[
\angle TCB=\angle CBA.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $A_0$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Consider the homothety $h$ with center $G$ and ratio $-1/2$. It maps
\[
A\mapsto A_0,
\qquad
B\mapsto B_0,
\qquad
C\mapsto C_0.
\]
We show that $h(T)=D$. By (2), the image of the line $AT$ is the line through $A_0$ parallel to $BC$, namely $BC$ itself. Thus $h(T)\in BC$.

Moreover, reflection in $B_0C_0$ maps $A$ to $D$, so
\[
\angle B_0C_0A=\angle DC_0B_0.
\]
Since $B_0C_0\parallel BC$, relation (3) implies
\[
CT\parallel C_0D.
\]
The image of $CT$ under $h$ is therefore the line $C_0D$. Hence $h(T)$ lies on both $BC$ and $C_0D$, and so
\[
h(T)=D.
\]
A point and its image under a homothety are collinear with the center; therefore $D,G,T$ are collinear. Since $D,X,T$ are collinear as well, the points $D,G,X$ are collinear.

SOLUTION 2:
Use the notation $A_0,O,W$ from Solution 1, and again assume $AB<AC$. Let $Q$ be the perpendicular projection of $A_0$ onto $B_0C_0$. Since $O,A_0,Q$ are collinear and $B_0C_0\parallel BC$, we have
\[
WQ\perp OQ.
\]
Together with
\[
WA\perp OA,
\qquad
WX\perp OX,
\]
this shows that the five points
\[
A,W,X,O,Q
\]
lie on the circle with diameter $WO$.

Reflection in $B_0C_0$ maps $A$ to $D$ and fixes $Q,W$. Also, because
\[
OA=OX
\quad\text{and}\quad
WA=WX,
\]
the line $OW$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AX$, so reflection in $OW$ maps $A$ to $X$. These symmetries and the cyclicity above give
\[
\angle WQD
=\angle AQW
=\angle AXW
=\angle WAX
=\angle WQX.
\]
Hence the rays $QD$ and $QX$ lie on the same line, so
\[
Q,D,X\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]

Now consider the homothety with center $G$ and ratio $-1/2$ that maps triangle $ABC$ to triangle $A_0B_0C_0$. The altitude $AD$ is mapped to the altitude from $A_0$ to $B_0C_0$, namely $A_0Q$. The line $BC$ is mapped to $B_0C_0$. Therefore their intersection $D$ is mapped to
\[
A_0Q\cap B_0C_0=Q.
\]
Thus $D,G,Q$ are collinear. Combining this with (1), we conclude that $D,G,X$ are collinear.
