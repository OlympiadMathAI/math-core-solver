ID: 0131

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Similar Triangles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Reflections, Pascal's Theorem

STATEMENT:
The points $P$ and $Q$ are chosen on the side $BC$ of an acute-angled triangle $ABC$ so that
\[
\angle PAB=\angle ACB
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle QAC=\angle CBA.
\]
The points $M$ and $N$ are taken on the rays $AP$ and $AQ$, respectively, so that
\[
AP=PM
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AQ=QN.
\]
Prove that the lines $BM$ and $CN$ intersect on the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
S=BM\cap CN,
\qquad
\beta=\angle QAC=\angle CBA,
\qquad
\gamma=\angle PAB=\angle ACB.
\]
The triangles $ABP$ and $CAQ$ are similar: indeed,
\[
\angle BAP=\gamma=\angle ACQ
\quad\text{and}\quad
\angle ABP=\beta=\angle CAQ.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{BP}{PA}=\frac{AQ}{QC}.
\]
Using $PA=PM$ and $AQ=QN$, we obtain
\[
\frac{BP}{PM}=\frac{NQ}{QC}.
\tag{1}
\]
Since $M$ lies beyond $P$ on the ray $AP$, while $N$ lies beyond $Q$ on the ray $AQ$,
\[
\angle BPM=\beta+\gamma=\angle CQN.
\]
Together with (1), this proves
\[
\triangle BPM\sim\triangle NQC.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle BMP=\angle NCQ.
\tag{2}
\]

Now $B,P,C$ are collinear, $B,M,S$ are collinear, and $C,N,S$ are collinear. Thus
\[
\angle PBM=\angle CBS,
\]
and (2) gives
\[
\angle BMP=\angle BCS.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle BPM\sim\triangle BSC.
\]
It follows that
\[
\angle BSC=\angle BPM=\beta+\gamma.
\]
But
\[
\beta+\gamma=180^\circ-\angle BAC.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle BSC+\angle BAC=180^\circ,
\]
so the points $A,B,C,S$ are concyclic. Since $S=BM\cap CN$, the desired result follows.

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
S=BM\cap CN.
\]
Let the circumcircle of $ABC$ meet $AP$ again at $K\ne A$ and meet $AQ$ again at $L\ne A$.

Because $A,B,C,L$ are concyclic and $A,Q,L$ are collinear,
\[
\angle LBC=\angle LAC=\angle QAC=\angle CBA.
\]
Thus $BL$ is the reflection of $BA$ across $BC$. Similarly,
\[
\angle KCB=\angle KAB=\angle PAB=\angle ACB,
\]
so $CK$ is the reflection of $CA$ across $BC$. Hence, if
\[
X=BL\cap CK,
\]
then $X$ is the reflection of $A$ across the line $BC$.

We next show that $M,N,X$ are collinear. Choose coordinates in which $BC$ is the $x$-axis and write
\[
A=(a,h),\qquad P=(p,0),\qquad Q=(q,0).
\]
Since $P$ and $Q$ are the midpoints of $AM$ and $AN$, respectively,
\[
M=(2p-a,-h),
\qquad
N=(2q-a,-h).
\]
The reflection of $A$ across $BC$ is $X=(a,-h)$, so $M,N,X$ indeed lie on one line.

Consider the hexagon
\[
A,L,B,S,C,K.
\]
The intersections of its three pairs of opposite sides are
\[
AL\cap SC=N,
\qquad
LB\cap CK=X,
\qquad
BS\cap KA=M.
\]
These three points are collinear. By the converse of Pascal's theorem, the six vertices of the hexagon lie on one conic. Five of them, namely $A,L,B,C,K$, already lie on the circumcircle of $ABC$. Hence the conic is that circle, and therefore $S$ lies on the circumcircle of $ABC$.
