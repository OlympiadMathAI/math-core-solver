SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Fibres of Functions, Arithmetic Progressions, 2-adic Valuation

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb Z$ be the set of integers. Consider functions
\[
f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z
\]
satisfying
\[
f(f(x+y)+y)=f(f(x)+y)
\tag{1}
\]
for all integers $x,y$.

For such a function, call an integer $v$ \emph{$f$-rare} if the set
\[
X_v=\{x\in\mathbb Z:f(x)=v\}
\]
is finite and nonempty.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that there is a function satisfying (1) for which an $f$-rare integer exists.
\item[(b)] Prove that no function satisfying (1) can have more than one $f$-rare integer.
\end{enumerate}

SOLUTION 1:
\textbf{Part (a).}
Define
\[
f(0)=0,
\]
and, for $x\ne0$, let $f(x)$ be the largest power of $2$ dividing $2x$. Then
\[
f(2x)=2f(x)
\tag{2}
\]
for every integer $x$, and every value of $f$ is even. Also,
\[
X_0=\{0\},
\]
so $0$ is $f$-rare.

It remains to verify (1). By repeatedly dividing both $x$ and $y$ by $2$ and using (2), it is enough to consider the case in which at least one of $x,y$ is odd, together with the trivial case $x=y=0$.

If $y$ is odd, then both
\[
f(x+y)+y
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(x)+y
\]
are odd, so applying $f$ to either gives $2$. If $x$ is odd and $y$ is even, then
\[
f(x+y)=2=f(x),
\]
so the two arguments of the outer $f$ in (1) are equal. Thus (1) holds.

\textbf{Part (b).}
Repeatedly applying (1), with $x$ replaced by $x+ky$, gives
\[
f(f(x+ky)+y)=f(f(x)+y)
\tag{3}
\]
for all integers $x,y,k$.

Let $v$ be $f$-rare, and let
\[
a=\min X_v,
\qquad
b=\max X_v.
\]
In (3), put
\[
y=a-f(x).
\]
The right-hand side becomes $f(a)=v$, so
\[
f(x+k(a-f(x)))-f(x)+a\in X_v.
\]
By the minimality of $a$,
\[
f(x+k(a-f(x)))\ge f(x)
\tag{4}
\]
for all $x,k$. Similarly, using $y=b-f(x)$ and the maximality of $b$ gives
\[
f(x+k(b-f(x)))\le f(x).
\tag{5}
\]
Apply (4) with $k$ replaced by $k(b-f(x))$, and (5) with $k$ replaced by $k(a-f(x))$. We obtain
\[
f\bigl(x+k(a-f(x))(b-f(x))\bigr)=f(x)
\tag{6}
\]
for all integers $x,k$.

If $f(x)\notin\{a,b\}$, then the common difference in (6) is nonzero, so the fibre $X_{f(x)}$ contains a nondegenerate infinite arithmetic progression. Therefore the only possible $f$-rare values are $a$ and $b$.

In particular, the rare value $v$ must equal $a$ or $b$. Since
\[
f(a)=f(b)=v,
\]
we get
\[
f(v)=v.
\tag{7}
\]
If $v'$ were another rare value, the same argument would give $v'\in\{a,b\}$ and $f(v')=v'$. But $f(a)=f(b)=v$, so (7) forces $v'=v$. Hence there is at most one $f$-rare integer.

SOLUTION 2:
\textbf{Part (a).}
Define
\[
f(0)=0,
\]
and, for $x\ne0$, let $f(x)$ be the largest power of $2$ dividing $2x$. Then $f(2x)=2f(x)$, every value of $f$ is even, and $X_0=\{0\}$.

If $x$ and $y$ are both even, equation (1) reduces, after using $f(2t)=2f(t)$, to the same equation with $x/2$ and $y/2$. We may therefore reduce to the case in which at least one of $x,y$ is odd. If $y$ is odd, both inner arguments in (1) are odd and both outer values are $2$. If $x$ is odd and $y$ is even, then $f(x+y)=f(x)=2$. Thus (1) holds, and $0$ is rare.

\textbf{Part (b).}
Let $v$ be $f$-rare, and let
\[
a=\min X_v,
\qquad
b=\max X_v.
\]
Substitute $(x,y)=(v,a-v)$ into (1). Since $f(a)=v$, the left side is $f(a)=v$, and therefore
\[
f(v)-v+a\in X_v.
\]
Thus $f(v)\ge v$. The substitution $(x,y)=(v,b-v)$ similarly gives
\[
f(v)-v+b\in X_v,
\]
so $f(v)\le v$. Hence
\[
f(v)=v.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose that a second rare value $v'$ exists. If $f$ satisfies (1), then for every integer $c$ the translated conjugate
\[
g(x)=f(x+c)-c
\]
also satisfies (1), and rare values are translated in the same way. We may therefore assume that
\[
v=0.
\]
By (2),
\[
f(0)=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(v')=v'.
\]
We claim that
\[
f(kv')=v'
\qquad(k=1,2,3,\ldots).
\tag{3}
\]
The case $k=1$ is known. If (3) holds for $k$, substitute
\[
x=0,
\qquad
y=kv'
\]
into (1). Then
\[
f(f(kv')+kv')=f(f(0)+kv'),
\]
so
\[
f((k+1)v')=f(kv')=v'.
\]
This proves (3) by induction.

If $v'\ne0$, equation (3) makes the fibre $X_{v'}$ infinite, contradicting that $v'$ is rare. Therefore $v'=0=v$, and no second rare value exists.
