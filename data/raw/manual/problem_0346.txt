ID: 0346

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Permutations, Coding Theory, Parity, Pigeonhole Principle

STATEMENT:
Determine the largest integer $N$ for which there exists a table $T$ with $N$ rows and $100$ columns satisfying:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] every row contains the numbers
\[
1,2,\ldots,100
\]
in some order;
\item[(ii)] for every two distinct rows $r,s$, there is a column $c$ such that
\[
|T(r,c)-T(s,c)|\ge2.
\]
\end{enumerate}

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\frac{100!}{2^{50}}}.
\]

\textbf{Upper bound.}

In any row, replace the two numbers
\[
2k-1,\ 2k
\]
by one symbol $x_k$, for each $k=1,\ldots,50$. The resulting \emph{pattern} is an arrangement of the multiset
\[
\{x_1,x_1,x_2,x_2,\ldots,x_{50},x_{50}\}.
\]
There are
\[
\frac{100!}{2^{50}}
\tag{1}
\]
different patterns.

If two rows have the same pattern, then in every column their entries belong to the same pair
\[
\{2k-1,2k\},
\]
and hence differ by at most $1$. This violates condition (ii). Therefore all rows have distinct patterns, and (1) is an upper bound.

\textbf{Construction.}

For every pattern, construct one row by replacing its symbols with numbers. Process
\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{50}
\]
in this order.

For $x_1$, put $1$ in its left occurrence and $2$ in its right occurrence.

Suppose $k\ge2$. The number $2k-2$ has already been placed. Insert $2k-1$ and $2k$ into the two positions occupied by $x_k$ so that, from left to right, the three numbers
\[
2k-2,\ 2k-1,\ 2k
\]
occur in one of the three cyclic orders
\[
\begin{aligned}
&2k-2,\ 2k-1,\ 2k,\\
&2k,\ 2k-2,\ 2k-1,\\
&2k-1,\ 2k,\ 2k-2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
This is always possible, according as the already placed number $2k-2$ lies before, between, or after the two occurrences of $x_k$.

We prove that the resulting rows satisfy condition (ii). Take two distinct rows $r,s$, arising from patterns $P_r,P_s$. Call $x_i$ \emph{aligned} if its two occurrences occupy the same two columns in both patterns. Let $k$ be the largest index for which $x_k$ is not aligned. Necessarily
\[
k\ge2.
\]

Let $c_r$ be the column containing $2k$ in row $r$, and let $c_s$ be the column containing $2k$ in row $s$. Because every $x_i$ with $i>k$ is aligned,
\[
T(r,c_s)\le2k,
\qquad
T(s,c_r)\le2k.
\tag{3}
\]

If
\[
T(r,c_s)\le2k-2
\]
or
\[
T(s,c_r)\le2k-2,
\]
condition (ii) already holds. If
\[
T(r,c_s)=T(s,c_r)=2k-1,
\]
then the two positions occupied by $x_k$ are the same in both patterns, contrary to the choice of $k$. The only remaining possibility is
\[
c_r=c_s=:c,
\qquad
T(r,c)=T(s,c)=2k.
\tag{4}
\]

Let $d_r$ and $d_s$ be the columns containing $2k-1$ in rows $r$ and $s$, respectively. Since $x_k$ is not aligned,
\[
d_r\ne d_s.
\]
As in (3),
\[
T(r,d_s)\le2k-2,
\qquad
T(s,d_r)\le2k-2.
\tag{5}
\]
If either value in (5) is at most $2k-3$, condition (ii) holds. Hence the only remaining case is
\[
T(r,d_s)=T(s,d_r)=2k-2.
\tag{6}
\]

In row $r$, the locations of
\[
2k-2,\ 2k-1,\ 2k
\]
are respectively
\[
d_s,\ d_r,\ c,
\]
whereas in row $s$ they are
\[
d_r,\ d_s,\ c.
\]
These two orders have opposite parity, so they cannot both belong to the three cyclic orders in (2). This contradiction proves condition (ii).

Thus the construction supplies one valid row for every pattern, giving exactly
\[
\frac{100!}{2^{50}}
\]
rows. Together with the upper bound, this proves the answer.

SOLUTION 2:
The upper bound is again
\[
\frac{100!}{2^{50}},
\]
by replacing each pair $\{2k-1,2k\}$ with a symbol $x_k$.

We construct one row for every pattern in a parity form equivalent to the construction in Solution 1. Require that each row induce an even permutation on each of the following sets:
\[
\{1,2\},\quad
\{2,3,4\},\quad
\{4,5,6\},\quad\ldots,\quad
\{98,99,100\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here the induced permutation on a subset is obtained by deleting all entries not belonging to that subset.

There is exactly one way to orient the pairs in any fixed pattern so that all conditions in (1) hold. First, the condition on $\{1,2\}$ uniquely decides which occurrence of $x_1$ is $1$. Inductively, after the number $2k-2$ has been placed, exactly one of the two assignments of
\[
2k-1,\ 2k
\]
to the occurrences of $x_k$ makes the induced order on
\[
\{2k-2,2k-1,2k\}
\]
even. Hence this construction gives
\[
100!/2^{50}
\]
rows.

We show that any two distinct constructed rows satisfy condition (ii).

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $\pi_1,\pi_2$ be permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that
\[
|\pi_1(i)-\pi_2(i)|\le1
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Then $\pi_2$ is obtained from $\pi_1$ by swapping some pairwise disjoint pairs of consecutive values
\[
(j,j+1).
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Reorder the columns so that $\pi_1(i)=i$. We prove the result by induction on $n$.

If $\pi_2(n)=n$, apply the induction hypothesis to the first $n-1$ positions. Otherwise the hypothesis forces
\[
\pi_2(n)=n-1.
\]
The value $n$ can then occur only in position $n-1$, so $\pi_2$ swaps $n-1$ and $n$. Remove those two positions and apply induction. $\square$

Suppose two distinct rows $\pi_1,\pi_2$ differed by at most $1$ in every column. By the lemma, there is a nonempty set
\[
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,99\}
\]
whose elements differ pairwise by at least $2$, such that $\pi_2$ is obtained from $\pi_1$ by swapping every pair
\[
(j,j+1),
\qquad j\in S.
\]
Let
\[
r=\min S.
\]

If $r=2k-1$ is odd, then the induced orders on
\[
\{2k-2,2k-1,2k\}
\]
have opposite parity; for $r=1$, use the set $\{1,2\}$. This contradicts (1). Hence
\[
r=2k
\]
is even.

The swap $(2k,2k+1)$ reverses the parity of the induced order on
\[
\{2k,2k+1,2k+2\}.
\]
Because $2k$ is the smallest element of $S$ and the transpositions are disjoint, every other transposition leaves all three of these values fixed, except possibly
\[
(2k+2,2k+3).
\]
Therefore, to restore the parity required in (1), the set $S$ must also contain
\[
2k+2.
\]
Repeating the same argument gives
\[
2k,2k+2,2k+4,\ldots,98\in S.
\]
But then the swap $(98,99)$ reverses the parity of the induced order on
\[
\{98,99,100\},
\]
and there is no further swap available to restore it. This is a contradiction.

Therefore two distinct constructed rows cannot differ by at most $1$ in every column. The table is valid and has
\[
\boxed{\frac{100!}{2^{50}}}
\]
rows.
