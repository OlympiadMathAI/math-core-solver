ID: 0288

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incircle Contact Triangle, Spiral Similarity, Harmonic Division, Polar Lines, Inversion, Projective Geometry

STATEMENT:
Let $I$ be the incentre of an acute triangle $ABC$. The incircle touches $BC,CA,AB$ at $D,E,F$, respectively. The line $EF$ meets the circumcircle of $ABC$ at $P$ and $Q$, labelled so that $F$ lies between $E$ and $P$. Prove that
\[
\angle DPA+\angle AQD=\angle QIP.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $N$ and $M$ be the midpoints of the arcs $BC$ of the circumcircle which contain and do not contain $A$, respectively. The right-angled kites
\[
AFIE
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
NBMC
\]
are similar, because
\[
\angle FAE=\angle BAC=\angle BNC.
\]
Let $\varphi$ be the corresponding spiral similarity, with
\[
A\mapsto N,
\quad F\mapsto B,
\quad I\mapsto M,
\quad E\mapsto C.
\]
The change of direction under $\varphi$ is the same as
\[
\angle(AF,NB)=\angle(AP,NP)=\angle(AQ,NQ).
\]
Thus $\varphi$ sends $AP,AQ,EF$ to $NP,NQ,BC$, respectively. Put
\[
P'=NP\cap BC,
\qquad
Q'=NQ\cap BC.
\]
Then $\varphi(P)=P'$ and $\varphi(Q)=Q'$.

Let $L$ be the midpoint of $BC$. We claim that $P,Q,D,L$ are concyclic. Put
\[
Z=PQ\cap BC.
\]
Menelaus' theorem in triangle $ABC$, applied to the line $EFZ$, together with the tangent-length equalities
\[
BF=BD,
\qquad
CE=CD,
\]
gives
\[
\frac{BD}{DC}
=\frac{BF}{FA}\cdot\frac{AE}{EC}
=-\frac{BZ}{ZC}.
\]
Hence $(B,C;D,Z)$ is a harmonic quadruple. A standard metric property of a harmonic division with $L$ the midpoint of $BC$ is
\[
ZB\cdot ZC=ZD\cdot ZL.
\tag{1}
\]
On the other hand, by the power of $Z$ with respect to the circumcircle of $ABC$,
\[
ZB\cdot ZC=ZP\cdot ZQ.
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) prove the claim.

The points $M$ and $N$ are antipodal on the circumcircle, so
\[
\angle MPP'=\angle MQQ'=90^\circ.
\]
Also $ML\perp BC$, and therefore
\[
\angle MLP'=\angle MLQ'=90^\circ.
\]
Thus $M,L,P,P'$ and $M,L,Q,Q'$ are cyclic.

Using directed angles, the desired identity follows as
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DPA+\angle AQD
&=360^\circ-\angle PAQ-\angle QDP\\
&=360^\circ-\angle PNQ-\angle QLP\\
&=\angle LPN+\angle NQL\\
&=\angle P'ML+\angle LMQ'\\
&=\angle P'MQ'\\
&=\angle PIQ.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality follows because $\varphi$ sends $P,I,Q$ to $P',M,Q'$; the ordinary angles $\angle PIQ$ and $\angle QIP$ are equal as unsigned angles.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $M$ and the spiral similarity $\varphi$ be as in Solution 1. The centre of the spiral similarity sending $F,E$ to $B,C$ is the Miquel point of the four lines $EF,BC,BF,CE$; equivalently, it is the second intersection $S$ of the circumcircles of $ABC$ and $AEF$.

The similarities
\[
\triangle SBF\sim\triangle SCE
\]
give
\[
\frac{SB}{SC}=\frac{BF}{CE}=\frac{BD}{CD}.
\]
By the converse of the angle-bisector theorem, $SD$ bisects $\angle BSC$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ opposite $A$, the same angle bisector passes through $M$; hence $S,D,M$ are collinear.

Let
\[
K=EF\cap SI.
\]
The map $\varphi$ sends
\[
S,F,E,I,K
\quad\text{to}\quad
S,B,C,M,D.
\]
In particular,
\[
KD\parallel IM.
\tag{1}
\]

We claim that
\[
\triangle SPI\sim\triangle SDQ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\triangle SPD\sim\triangle SIQ.
\tag{2}
\]
Let the ray $SI$ meet the circumcircle of $ABC$ again at $L$. Since $EF$ is perpendicular to the angle bisector $AM$, one obtains
\[
\angle AML=\angle ASL=\angle ASI=90^\circ,
\]
and hence
\[
ML\parallel PQ.
\tag{3}
\]
The parallelism (3) implies equality of the arcs $PL$ and $MQ$, so
\[
\angle PSL=\angle MSQ=\angle DSQ.
\tag{4}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\angle QPS=\angle QMS,
\]
so triangles $SPK$ and $SMQ$ are similar. Using this similarity and the scale factor of $\varphi$,
\[
\frac{SP}{SI}
=\frac{SP}{SK}\cdot\frac{SK}{SI}
=\frac{SM}{SQ}\cdot\frac{SD}{SM}
=\frac{SD}{SQ}.
\]
Together with (4), this proves the first similarity in (2). The second is obtained analogously.

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DPA+\angle AQD
&=\angle DPS+\angle SQD\\
&=\angle QIS+\angle SIP\\
&=\angle QIP,
\end{aligned}
\]
as required.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of $ABC$. Let the rays $PD$ and $QD$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $V$ and $U$, respectively. We prove
\[
AU\perp IP,
\qquad
AV\perp IQ.
\tag{1}
\]
These perpendicularities immediately imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DPA+\angle AQD
&=\angle VUA+\angle AVU\\
&=180^\circ-\angle UAV\\
&=\angle QIP.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ containing $U,V$, and let $N$ be the midpoint of the arc $CAB$. The lines $AIM$ and $AN$ are the internal and external angle bisectors of $\angle BAC$, so
\[
AM\perp AN.
\tag{2}
\]
Let the tangents to $\Gamma$ at $B,C$ meet at $R$. Let $PQ$ meet $AU,AI,AV,BC$ at $X,T,Y,Z$, respectively.

As in Solution 1, the pairs $(B,C)$ and $(D,Z)$ are harmonic. Projecting from $Q$ onto $\Gamma$ shows that $(B,C;U,P)$ is harmonic. Similarly, $(B,C;V,Q)$ is harmonic.

Invert about the circle centred at $R$ through $B,C$. On $\Gamma$, this inversion exchanges each point with its harmonic conjugate relative to $B,C$. Therefore it maps
\[
B,C,N,U,V
\quad\text{to}\quad
B,C,M,P,Q.
\]
Now project from $A$ onto the line $PQ$. The points $B,C,M,P,Q$ go to $F,E,T,P,Q$, respectively. Hence the composition gives a projectivity between the two pencils
\[
AB,AC,AN,AU,AV
\]
and
\[
IF,IE,IT,IP,IQ,
\]
respectively.

The first three pairs of corresponding lines are perpendicular:
\[
AB\perp IF,
\qquad
AC\perp IE,
\qquad
AN\perp IT
\]
(the last one follows from (2), since $T\in AI$). A projectivity between two pencils is determined by three corresponding lines; therefore this projectivity is precisely the perpendicularity correspondence. Consequently the remaining corresponding pairs are also perpendicular, proving (1).
