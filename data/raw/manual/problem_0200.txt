ID: 0200

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Divisor Functions, Congruences, Prime Factorization, Parity Counting

STATEMENT:
Let $\tau(n)$ denote the number of positive divisors of $n$, and let $\tau_1(n)$ denote the number of positive divisors of $n$ that are congruent to $1$ modulo $3$. Determine all possible integer values of
\[
\frac{\tau(10n)}{\tau_1(10n)}
\]
as $n$ ranges over the positive integers.

SOLUTION 1:
The possible values are
\[
\boxed{2\ \text{and all composite positive integers}.}
\]

We first compute $\tau_1(m)$. Write
\[
m
=
3^x
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{a_i}
\prod_{j=1}^{t}q_j^{b_j},
\]
where
\[
p_i\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad
q_j\equiv2\pmod3.
\]
Empty products are allowed.

A divisor of $m$ that is congruent to $1$ modulo $3$ cannot contain the prime $3$. The exponents of the primes $p_i$ may be chosen arbitrarily. For the primes $q_j$, the sum of the chosen exponents must be even. The number of exponent choices of even total parity is
\[
\left\lceil
\frac12\prod_{j=1}^{t}(b_j+1)
\right\rceil.
\]
Indeed, if one of the numbers $b_j+1$ is even, exactly half of all choices have even total parity. If all $b_j$ are even, an elementary parity count gives one more even choice than odd choices.

Consequently,
\[
\tau_1(m)
=
\prod_{i=1}^{s}(a_i+1)
\left\lceil
\frac12\prod_{j=1}^{t}(b_j+1)
\right\rceil.
\tag{1}
\]

Now write
\[
n
=
3^x2^y5^z
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{a_i}
\prod_{j=1}^{t}q_j^{b_j},
\]
where the primes $q_j\equiv2\pmod3$ are taken different from $2$ and $5$. Put
\[
A=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(a_i+1),
\qquad
B=\prod_{j=1}^{t}(b_j+1),
\qquad
C=(y+2)(z+2)B.
\]
Then
\[
\tau(10n)
=(x+1)AC
\tag{2}
\]
and, by (1),
\[
\tau_1(10n)
=A\left\lceil\frac C2\right\rceil.
\tag{3}
\]

If $C$ is even, equations (2) and (3) give
\[
\frac{\tau(10n)}{\tau_1(10n)}
=2(x+1).
\]
As $x$ ranges over the nonnegative integers, this gives every positive even integer.

Suppose now that $C$ is odd. Then $y,z$ are odd and every $b_j$ is even. In particular,
\[
C\ge3\cdot3=9.
\]
Equations (2) and (3) give
\[
\frac{\tau(10n)}{\tau_1(10n)}
=
\frac{2(x+1)C}{C+1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since $\gcd(C,C+1)=1$, expression (4) is an integer exactly when
\[
C+1\mid2(x+1).
\]
Write
\[
2(x+1)=h(C+1).
\]
Then the value in (4) is
\[
hC.
\]
Because $C\ge9$ is itself composite, every integer obtained in this odd case is composite.

Conversely, let $N$ be any odd composite positive integer. Write
\[
N=uv
\]
with odd integers $u,v\ge3$. Choose
\[
n
=
3^{(N-1)/2}\,2^{u-2}\,5^{v-2}.
\]
Here
\[
C=(u-2+2)(v-2+2)=uv=N
\]
and
\[
2(x+1)=N+1.
\]
Thus (4) equals $N$.

We have therefore obtained every positive even integer and every odd composite integer. Equivalently, the possible values are $2$ and all composite positive integers.
