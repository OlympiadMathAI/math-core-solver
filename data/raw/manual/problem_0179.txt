ID: 0179

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Rational Approximation, Squares, Parity, Diophantine Inequalities

STATEMENT:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Prove that for every positive integer $n$, there exists a fraction $a/b$, where $a,b$ are integers, satisfying
\[
0<b\le\sqrt{n}+1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sqrt{n}\le\frac ab\le\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
\item[(b)] Prove that there are infinitely many positive integers $n$ for which there is no fraction $a/b$, where $a,b$ are integers, satisfying
\[
0<b\le\sqrt{n}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sqrt{n}\le\frac ab\le\sqrt{n+1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

SOLUTION 1:
\textbf{Part (a).}
Let $r$ be the unique positive integer satisfying
\[
r^2\le n<(r+1)^2.
\]
Write
\[
n=r^2+s,
\qquad
0\le s\le2r.
\]

If $s$ is even, consider
\[
r+\frac{s}{2r}=\frac{r^2+s/2}{r}.
\]
Its denominator is $r\le\sqrt{n}$, and
\[
\left(r+\frac{s}{2r}\right)^2
=r^2+s+\left(\frac{s}{2r}\right)^2.
\]
Because $0\le s/(2r)\le1$,
\[
n\le\left(r+\frac{s}{2r}\right)^2\le n+1.
\]
Thus this fraction has the required properties.

If $s$ is odd, put
\[
t=2r+1-s
\]
and consider
\[
r+1-\frac{t}{2(r+1)}
=
\frac{(r+1)^2-r+(s-1)/2}{r+1}.
\]
This is a fraction with integer numerator and denominator $r+1\le\sqrt{n}+1$. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(r+1-\frac{t}{2(r+1)}\right)^2
&=(r+1)^2-t+\left(\frac{t}{2(r+1)}\right)^2\\
&=n+\left(\frac{t}{2(r+1)}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $0<t<2(r+1)$, so the last square is at most $1$. Hence this fraction also lies between $\sqrt{n}$ and $\sqrt{n+1}$.

\textbf{Part (b).}
We show that every integer
\[
n=r^2+1
\qquad(r\ge1)
\]
has the required nonexistence property. Suppose, to the contrary, that integers $a,b$ satisfy
\[
0<b\le\sqrt{r^2+1}
\]
and
\[
\sqrt{r^2+1}\le\frac ab\le\sqrt{r^2+2}.
\]
Since $b<r+1$ and $b$ is an integer, $b\le r$. Squaring gives
\[
(br)^2<b^2(r^2+1)\le a^2\le b^2(r^2+2).
\]
Using $b\le r$,
\[
b^2(r^2+2)=b^2r^2+2b^2
\le b^2r^2+2br
<(br+1)^2.
\]
Thus the square $a^2$ lies strictly between the consecutive squares $(br)^2$ and $(br+1)^2$, an impossibility. Since there are infinitely many choices of $r$, part (b) follows.
