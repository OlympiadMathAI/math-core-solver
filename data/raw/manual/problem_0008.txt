ID: 0008

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Polynomials

STATEMENT:
Let $a,b,c,d,e,f$ be positive real numbers satisfying
\[
a<b<c<d<e<f.
\]
Set
\[
S=a+c+e,
\qquad
T=b+d+f.
\]
Prove that
\[
2ST>
\sqrt{3(S+T)\left(S(bd+bf+df)+T(ac+ae+ce)\right)}.
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
\sigma=ac+ce+ae,
\qquad
\tau=bd+bf+df.
\]
Consider the cubic polynomial
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(x)
&=T(x-a)(x-c)(x-e)+S(x-b)(x-d)(x-f)\\
&=T(x^3-Sx^2+\sigma x-ace)
 +S(x^3-Tx^2+\tau x-bdf).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Its leading coefficient is $S+T>0$. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(a)&=S(a-b)(a-d)(a-f)<0,\\
P(c)&=S(c-b)(c-d)(c-f)>0,\\
P(e)&=S(e-b)(e-d)(e-f)<0,\\
P(f)&=T(f-a)(f-c)(f-e)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $P$ has one root in each of the disjoint intervals $(a,c)$, $(c,e)$ and $(e,f)$. Denote them by $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$. They are distinct, and
\[
P(x)=(S+T)(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)(x-\gamma).
\tag{3}
\]
Comparing coefficients in (2) and (3), we find
\[
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\frac{2ST}{S+T},
\qquad
\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\beta\gamma
=\frac{S\tau+T\sigma}{S+T}.
\]
Since the roots are distinct,
\[
0<(\alpha-\beta)^2+(\alpha-\gamma)^2+(\beta-\gamma)^2,
\]
so
\[
(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^2
>3(\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\beta\gamma).
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\frac{4S^2T^2}{(S+T)^2}
>\frac{3(S\tau+T\sigma)}{S+T},
\]
or
\[
4S^2T^2>3(S+T)(S\tau+T\sigma).
\]
Taking positive square roots yields (1).

SOLUTION 2:
Define
\[
U=\frac12\left((e-a)^2+(c-a)^2+(e-c)^2\right)
=S^2-3(ac+ae+ce)
\]
and
\[
V=\frac12\left((f-b)^2+(f-d)^2+(d-b)^2\right)
=T^2-3(bd+bf+df).
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(2ST)^2
-3(S+T)\left(S(bd+bf+df)+T(ac+ae+ce)\right)\\
&\qquad=(S+T)(SV+TU)-ST(T-S)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore it is enough to prove
\[
(S+T)(SV+TU)>ST(T-S)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
By Cauchy-Schwarz,
\[
(S+T)(TU+SV)
\ge \left(\sqrt{S\cdot TU}+\sqrt{T\cdot SV}\right)^2
=ST(\sqrt U+\sqrt V)^2.
\tag{3}
\]
We now estimate $\sqrt U+\sqrt V$. Omitting the positive terms $(e-c)^2$ and $(d-b)^2$ and then using the quadratic-mean inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt U+\sqrt V
&>\sqrt{\frac{(e-a)^2+(c-a)^2}{2}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{(f-b)^2+(f-d)^2}{2}}\\
&\ge \frac{(e-a)+(c-a)}{2}
 +\frac{(f-b)+(f-d)}{2}\\
&=(T-S)+\frac32(e-d)+\frac32(c-b)\\
&>T-S.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3) and (4) proves (2), and hence the desired inequality.

SOLUTION 3:
Keep the notation
\[
\sigma=ac+ce+ae,
\qquad
\tau=bd+bf+df,
\]
and put
\[
s=c+e.
\]
Each term in
\[
(c-b)(c-d)+(e-f)(e-d)+(e-f)(c-b)
\]
is negative. Expanding therefore gives
\[
\tau-\sigma<s(T-S).
\tag{2}
\]
Using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
S\tau+T\sigma
&=S(\tau-\sigma)+(S+T)\sigma\\
&<Ss(T-S)+(S+T)(ce+as).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $c+e=s$, we have $ce\le s^2/4$, and since $a=S-s$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S\tau+T\sigma
&\le Ss(T-S)+(S+T)
\left(\frac{s^2}{4}+(S-s)s\right)\\
&=s\left(2ST-\frac34(S+T)s\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, by AM-GM,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{\frac34(S+T)(S\tau+T\sigma)}
&<\sqrt{\frac34(S+T)s
\left(2ST-\frac34(S+T)s\right)}\\
&\le \frac{\frac34(S+T)s+
2ST-\frac34(S+T)s}{2}\\
&=ST.
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying the expression under the square root by $4$ gives exactly (1).

SOLUTION 4:
Again write
\[
\sigma=ac+ce+ae,
\qquad
\tau=bd+bf+df.
\]
We will modify the variables while preserving the order
\[
a\le b\le c\le d\le e\le f
\]
and the sums $S,T$, but increasing $\sigma$ and $\tau$. Since the left-hand side of (1) then stays fixed while the right-hand side increases, it is enough to prove the non-strict inequality for the final modified variables.

We use the following elementary smoothing lemma. For positive $x,y,z$, set
\[
U(x,y,z)=x+y+z,
\qquad
v(x,y,z)=xy+xz+yz.
\]
If
\[
x'+y'=x+y
\quad\text{and}\quad
|x-y|\ge |x'-y'|,
\]
then
\[
U(x',y',z)=U(x,y,z),
\qquad
v(x',y',z)\ge v(x,y,z).
\]
Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v(x',y',z)
&=z(x'+y')+x'y'\\
&=z(x+y)+\frac{(x+y)^2-(x'-y')^2}{4}\\
&\ge z(x+y)+\frac{(x+y)^2-(x-y)^2}{4}\\
&=v(x,y,z).
\end{aligned}
\]

Apply this lemma in three stages, writing the new variables with the same letters.

First, let
\[
k=\frac{d-c}{2}
\]
and replace
\[
(b,c,d,e)
\quad\text{by}\quad
(b+k,c+k,d-k,e-k).
\]
Then $c=d$, while $S,T$ are unchanged and both $\sigma,\tau$ increase.

Second, let
\[
\lambda=\frac{e-d}{2}
\]
and replace
\[
(c,d,e,f)
\quad\text{by}\quad
(c+\lambda,d+\lambda,e-\lambda,f-\lambda).
\]
Now $c=d=e$, again with $S,T$ fixed and $\sigma,\tau$ increased.

Finally, let
\[
m=\frac{c-b}{3}
\]
and replace
\[
(a,b,c,d,e,f)
\]
by
\[
(a+2m,b+2m,c-m,d-m,e-m,f-m).
\]
This last transformation is the composition of two applications of the lemma, and it leads to
\[
a<b=c=d=e<f.
\]

Thus it remains to prove the inequality when $b=c=d=e$. In this case
\[
S=a+2b,
\qquad
T=2b+f,
\]
and the desired non-strict inequality becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
2(a+2b)(2b+f)
\ge\sqrt{3b(a+4b+f)
\left((a+2b)(b+2f)+(2b+f)(2a+b)\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
But
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\cdot 2(a+2b)(2b+f)
&=3b(a+4b+f)\\
&\quad +(a+2b)(b+2f)+(2a+b)(2b+f).
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying AM-GM to the two positive quantities
\[
3b(a+4b+f)
\]
and
\[
(a+2b)(b+2f)+(2a+b)(2b+f)
\]
gives (2). Since at least one of the smoothing steps is strict for the original strict ordering, the original inequality is strict as required.
