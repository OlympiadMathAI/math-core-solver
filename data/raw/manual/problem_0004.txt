ID: 0004

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Sequences, Induction

STATEMENT:
A sequence $x_1,x_2,\ldots$ is defined by $x_1=1$ and
\[
x_{2k}=-x_k,
\qquad
x_{2k-1}=(-1)^{k+1}x_k
\]
for every $k\ge 1$. Prove that
\[
x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n\ge 0
\]
for every $n\ge 1$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i.
\]
From the recurrence, for every positive integer $k$,
\[
x_{4k-3}=x_{2k-1}=-x_{4k-2},
\qquad
x_{4k-1}=x_{4k}=-x_{2k}=x_k.
\tag{1}
\]
Therefore
\[
S_{4k}=2S_k,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
S_{4k+2}=S_{4k}.
\tag{3}
\]
Also every $x_i$ is either $1$ or $-1$, so
\[
S_n\equiv n\pmod 2.
\tag{4}
\]

We prove by induction on $k$ that $S_i\ge 0$ for all $i\le 4k$. The assertion is immediate for $k=1$. Assume it is true up to $4k$. Relations (2) and (3) give
\[
S_{4k+4}=2S_{k+1}\ge 0,
\qquad
S_{4k+2}=S_{4k}\ge 0.
\]
Since $x_{4k+3}=x_{4k+4}$, we also have
\[
S_{4k+3}=\frac{S_{4k+2}+S_{4k+4}}{2}\ge 0.
\]
It remains to consider $S_{4k+1}$.

If $k$ is odd, then $S_k$ is a nonnegative odd integer by (4), hence $S_k\ge 1$. Thus
\[
S_{4k}=2S_k\ge 2,
\]
and since $x_{4k+1}\ge -1$, we get $S_{4k+1}\ge 1$.

If $k$ is even, then
\[
x_{4k+1}=x_{2k+1}=x_{k+1},
\]
so
\[
S_{4k+1}=2S_k+x_{k+1}=S_k+S_{k+1}\ge 0.
\]
This completes the induction.

SOLUTION 2:
Retain the notation
\[
S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i
\]
and the relations
\[
S_{4k}=2S_k,
\qquad
S_{4k+2}=S_{4k}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume, for a contradiction, that some partial sum is negative, and choose the least $n$ such that
\[
S_{n+1}<0.
\]
Then $S_n\ge 0$. Since $x_{n+1}\in\{-1,1\}$, minimality forces
\[
S_n=0,
\qquad
x_{n+1}=-1.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
M=\{m\ge 1:S_m=0\}.
\]
We record three properties:

1. Every member of $M$ is even, because $S_m\equiv m\pmod 2$.

2. We have $2\in M$.

3. For every even $m\ge 4$,
\[
m\in M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor\in M.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if $m=4q$ or $m=4q+2$, then (1) gives $S_m=2S_q$.

We now prove by induction on $m\in M$ that
\[
x_{m+1}=1.
\tag{4}
\]
The smallest element of $M$ is $2$, and $x_3=1$. Now let $m\ge 4$ belong to $M$, and put
\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor.
\]
By (3), $q\in M$, so $q$ is even and the induction hypothesis gives $x_{q+1}=1$.

If $m=4q$, then
\[
x_{m+1}=x_{4q+1}=x_{2q+1}=x_{q+1}=1.
\]
If $m=4q+2$, then
\[
x_{m+1}=x_{4q+3}=-x_{2q+2}=x_{q+1}=1.
\]
Thus (4) holds for every $m\in M$. Applying it to the $n$ from (2) gives $x_{n+1}=1$, contradicting (2). Therefore all partial sums are nonnegative.
