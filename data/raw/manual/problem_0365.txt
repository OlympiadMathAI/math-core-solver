ID: 0365

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Uniqueness, AM-GM Inequality, Monotonicity, Convexity

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb R_{>0}$ be the set of positive real numbers. Determine all functions
\[
f:\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}
\]
such that, for every $x\in\mathbb R_{>0}$, there exists a unique $y\in\mathbb R_{>0}$ satisfying
\[
xf(y)+yf(x)\le2.
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The unique solution is
\[
\boxed{f(x)=\frac1x}.
\]
Indeed, for this function, (1) becomes
\[
\frac{x}{y}+\frac{y}{x}\le2.
\]
By AM-GM, the left-hand side is at least $2$, with equality exactly when $x=y$. Thus the required $y$ exists and is unique.

Now let $f$ satisfy the condition. Call a pair $(x,y)$ \emph{good} if
\[
xf(y)+yf(x)\le2.
\]
Goodness is symmetric in $x$ and $y$.

\textbf{Lemma.} Every good pair satisfies $x=y$.

Suppose that $(x,y)$ is good with $x\ne y$. Since $y$ is the unique good partner of $x$, the pair $(x,x)$ is not good, so
\[
xf(x)>1.
\]
Similarly,
\[
yf(y)>1.
\]
Therefore AM-GM gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
xf(y)+yf(x)
&\ge2\sqrt{xyf(x)f(y)}\\
&=2\sqrt{\bigl(xf(x)\bigr)\bigl(yf(y)\bigr)}\\
&>2,
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting the goodness of $(x,y)$. The lemma follows.

For every $x$, a good partner exists, and the lemma says it must be $x$. Hence
\[
2xf(x)\le2,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
f(x)\le\frac1x.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix $t>0$ and apply (2) to $1/f(t)$:
\[
\frac1{f(t)}f\left(\frac1{f(t)}\right)\le1.
\]
Multiplying by $tf(t)$ and using (2) once more gives
\[
tf\left(\frac1{f(t)}\right)
\le tf(t)
\le1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
tf\left(\frac1{f(t)}\right)
+\frac1{f(t)}f(t)
\le2.
\]
Thus
\[
\left(t,\frac1{f(t)}\right)
\]
is a good pair. By the lemma,
\[
t=\frac1{f(t)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
f(t)=\frac1t
\]
for every $t>0$.

SOLUTION 2:
We again verify first that $f(x)=1/x$ works. Let $f$ be any solution, and for each $x>0$ let $g(x)$ denote its unique good partner.

We first prove that $f$ is strictly decreasing. Take $0<x<y$. The pair
\[
(g(y),y)
\]
is good. Since $y$ is its unique second component and $x\ne y$, the pair
\[
(g(y),x)
\]
is not good. Hence
\[
x f(g(y))+g(y)f(x)>2
\]
and
\[
y f(g(y))+g(y)f(y)\le2.
\]
Subtracting,
\[
f(g(y))(x-y)>g(y)(f(y)-f(x)).
\]
The left-hand side is negative, while $g(y)>0$, so
\[
f(x)>f(y).
\]
Thus $f$ is strictly decreasing.

We now show that every good pair is diagonal. If $(x,y)$ were good with $x\ne y$, uniqueness would imply that neither $(x,x)$ nor $(y,y)$ is good. Hence
\[
xf(x)>1,
\qquad
yf(y)>1.
\]
It follows that
\[
xf(x)+yf(y)>2\ge xf(y)+yf(x),
\]
or
\[
(x-y)(f(x)-f(y))>0,
\]
contradicting the fact that $f$ is decreasing. Therefore the unique good partner of $x$ is $x$, and
\[
f(x)\le\frac1x.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix $x>0$ and take $y>x$. Since $(x,y)$ is not good,
\[
xf(y)+yf(x)>2.
\]
Using $f(x)>f(y)$, we obtain
\[
(x+y)f(x)>2,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
f(x)>\frac2{x+y}.
\]
Letting $y$ decrease to $x$ gives
\[
f(x)\ge\frac1x.
\]
Together with (2), this yields
\[
f(x)=\frac1x.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
For $f(x)=1/x$, the condition follows from AM-GM, with equality only when $y=x$. We prove that no other solution exists.

For each $x>0$, let $g(x)$ be the unique positive number for which
\[
xf(g(x))+g(x)f(x)\le2.
\]
We prove that this inequality is always an equality.

Suppose, to the contrary, that for some $x>0$, with
\[
y=g(x),
\]
we have
\[
A:=2-xf(y)-yf(x)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<\frac{A}{2f(x)}.
\]
Since $y+\varepsilon\ne y$, uniqueness gives
\[
xf(y+\varepsilon)+(y+\varepsilon)f(x)>2,
\]
and hence
\[
f(y+\varepsilon)
>
 f(y)+\frac{A}{2x}.
\tag{3}
\]

Put
\[
h=g(y+\varepsilon).
\]
The pair $(h,y+\varepsilon)$ is good, while $(h,y)$ is not good. Therefore
\[
hf(y+\varepsilon)+(y+\varepsilon)f(h)\le2
\]
and
\[
hf(y)+yf(h)>2.
\]
Eliminating $f(h)$ gives
\[
2\varepsilon
<
h\bigl((y+\varepsilon)f(y)-y f(y+\varepsilon)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]
By (3), the expression in parentheses is smaller than
\[
\varepsilon f(y)-\frac{yA}{2x},
\]
which is negative for all sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. This contradicts (4). Hence
\[
xf(g(x))+g(x)f(x)=2
\tag{5}
\]
for every $x>0$.

By uniqueness, every other pair has value strictly greater than $2$. Thus, for all positive $x,y$,
\[
xf(y)+yf(x)\ge2.
\]
Taking $y=x$ gives
\[
f(x)\ge\frac1x.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (6) in (5),
\[
2
\ge
\frac{x}{g(x)}+\frac{g(x)}{x}
\ge2.
\]
Equality in AM-GM forces
\[
g(x)=x.
\]
Equation (5) then gives
\[
2xf(x)=2,
\]
so
\[
f(x)=\frac1x.
\]

SOLUTION 4:
The function $f(x)=1/x$ works by AM-GM. Let $f$ be any solution, and let $g(x)$ denote the unique good partner of $x$.

We first prove that $f$ is strictly convex. For a real number $s$, define
\[
q_s(t)=f(t)+st.
\]
Suppose that strict convexity fails. Then there are
\[
0<u<v,
\qquad
0<\theta<1,
\qquad
w=\theta u+(1-\theta)v
\]
such that
\[
f(w)\ge\theta f(u)+(1-\theta)f(v).
\tag{2}
\]
Choose
\[
s=\frac{f(g(w))}{g(w)}>0.
\]
The goodness of $(w,g(w))$ gives
\[
q_s(w)\le\frac2{g(w)}.
\]
For every $t\ne w$, the pair $(g(w),t)$ is not good, so
\[
q_s(t)>\frac2{g(w)}.
\]
Thus $q_s$ has a unique minimum at $w$.

On the other hand, (2) implies
\[
q_s(w)\ge\theta q_s(u)+(1-\theta)q_s(v),
\]
so at least one of $q_s(u),q_s(v)$ is no larger than $q_s(w)$. This contradicts the uniqueness of the minimum. Hence $f$ is strictly convex and therefore continuous on $(0,\infty)$.

We next show that every good pair is diagonal. Suppose $(x,y)$ is good with $x\ne y$. Uniqueness implies that $(x,x)$ and $(y,y)$ are not good, so
\[
xf(x)>1,
\qquad
yf(y)>1.
\]
AM-GM then gives
\[
xf(y)+yf(x)
\ge2\sqrt{\bigl(xf(x)\bigr)\bigl(yf(y)\bigr)}
>2,
\]
a contradiction. Thus the unique good partner of $x$ is $x$, and consequently
\[
f(x)\le\frac1x.
\tag{3}
\]

If strict inequality held in (3) for some $x$, then the continuous function
\[
h(y)=xf(y)+yf(x)
\]
would satisfy
\[
h(x)=2xf(x)<2.
\]
It would therefore be smaller than $2$ for every $y$ in a nontrivial interval around $x$, producing more than one good partner of $x$. This is impossible. Hence
\[
f(x)=\frac1x
\]
for all $x>0$.
