ID: 0083

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Binomial Coefficients, Prime Characterization, Divisibility, p-adic Valuation

STATEMENT:
Determine all integers $m\ge2$ such that every integer $n$ satisfying
\[
\frac m3\le n\le\frac m2
\]
divides the binomial coefficient
\[
\binom{n}{m-2n}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The required integers are exactly the primes.

First let $m=p$ be prime. For $p=2$, the only integer in the required interval is $n=1$, and
\[
1\mid\binom10.
\]
Now suppose that $p$ is odd and
\[
\frac p3\le n\le\frac p2.
\]
Put $r=p-2n$. Then
\[
1\le r\le n,
\]
so the standard binomial identity
\[
r\binom nr=n\binom{n-1}{r-1}
\tag{1}
\]
applies. Moreover,
\[
\gcd(r,n)=\gcd(p-2n,n)=\gcd(p,n)=1,
\]
because $1\le n<p$. Equation (1) shows that $n$ divides $r\binom nr$; coprimality therefore gives
\[
n\mid\binom nr=\binom{n}{p-2n}.
\]
Thus every prime has the required property.

We next show that no composite $m$ works.

If $m=2k$ is even and composite, then $k>1$. Choose $n=k$. It lies in the required interval, but
\[
\binom{n}{m-2n}=\binom{k}{0}=1,
\]
which is not divisible by $k$.

Now let $m$ be odd and composite. Choose a proper prime divisor $p$ of $m$. Since both $m$ and $p$ are odd, we may write
\[
m=p(2k+1)
\]
with an integer $k\ge1$. Put
\[
n=pk.
\]
Then
\[
\frac m3\le n\le\frac m2
\]
and
\[
m-2n=p.
\]
If $n$ divided $\binom np$, then
\[
\frac1n\binom np
=\frac1{pk}\binom{pk}{p}
=\frac{(pk-1)(pk-2)\cdots(pk-p+1)}{p!}
\tag{2}
\]
would be an integer. However, the denominator in (2) is divisible by $p$, while none of the factors
\[
pk-1,pk-2,\ldots,pk-(p-1)
\]
is divisible by $p$. Thus the right-hand side of (2) has $p$-adic valuation $-1$ and is not an integer. Hence $n\nmid\binom np$.

Therefore no composite integer works, and the answer is
\[
\boxed{m\text{ is prime}}.
\]
