ID: 0275

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Subset Sums, Induction, Ordered Weights, Gap Arguments

STATEMENT:
A set of $n$ blocks is given. Every block weighs at least $1$, and their total weight is $2n$. Prove that for every real number $r$ satisfying
\[
0\le r\le2n-2,
\]
there is a subset of the blocks whose total weight lies in the interval
\[
[r,r+2].
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We prove a slightly stronger assertion.

\textbf{Claim.} Suppose $n$ blocks have weights at least $1$ and total weight $s\le2n$. Then, for every
\[
-2\le r\le s,
\]
some subset has total weight between $r$ and $r+2$.

We use induction on $n$. The assertion is immediate for $n=1$: the empty subset and the full subset cover the relevant values of $r$.

Let $x$ be a largest block weight. Since $x\ge s/n$,
\[
s-x\le\frac{n-1}{n}s\le2(n-1),
\]
so the induction hypothesis applies to the remaining $n-1$ blocks. It supplies the desired subset for every
\[
-2\le r\le s-x.
\tag{1}
\]
Adding the removed block of weight $x$ to the subsets supplied by induction gives the desired result for every
\[
x-2\le r\le s.
\tag{2}
\]
It remains to check that the two intervals overlap. Since all other $n-1$ blocks weigh at least $1$,
\[
x\le s-(n-1).
\]
Also $s\le2n$, so $x\le n+1$. Adding these two bounds gives
\[
2x\le s+2,
\]
or equivalently
\[
x-2\le s-x.
\]
Thus (1) and (2) cover the whole interval $[-2,s]$.

Applying the claim with $s=2n$ proves the problem.

SOLUTION 2:
Let the block weights be
\[
x_1\le x_2\le\cdots\le x_n.
\]
For $0\le k\le n$, let $S_k$ be the set of all subset sums formed from
\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_k.
\]
We show inductively that the distance between any two consecutive elements of $S_k$ is at most $2$.

This is trivial for $k=0$, since $S_0=\{0\}$. For $k\ge1$,
\[
S_k=S_{k-1}\cup(x_k+S_{k-1}).
\]
The two copies individually have gaps at most $2$, so it is enough to prove that they do not leave a gap larger than $2$ between them. Since the largest element of $S_{k-1}$ is
\[
x_1+\cdots+x_{k-1},
\]
it suffices to show
\[
x_k\le x_1+\cdots+x_{k-1}+2.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume that (1) fails. Since every $x_j\ge1$,
\[
x_k>x_1+\cdots+x_{k-1}+2\ge k+1.
\]
The sequence is nondecreasing, so $x_j>k+1$ for every $j\ge k$. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
2n=\sum_{j=1}^n x_j
&>(k-1)+(n-k+1)(k+1)\\
&=n+k(n+1-k).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $n>k(n+1-k)$, impossible for $1\le k\le n$, because the right-hand side is at least $n$.

Therefore every gap between consecutive elements of $S_n$ is at most $2$. Since $S_n$ contains both $0$ and $2n$, every interval $[r,r+2]$ with $0\le r\le2n-2$ contains an element of $S_n$.
