ID: 0215

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Checkerboard Coloring, Parity, Rectangle Tilings, Invariants

STATEMENT:
A rectangle $R$ with odd integer side lengths is divided into smaller rectangles with integer side lengths. Prove that at least one of the smaller rectangles has distances from the four sides of $R$ that are either all odd or all even.

SOLUTION 1:
Let the side lengths of $R$ be the odd integers $a$ and $b$. Divide $R$ into $ab$ unit squares and color them green and yellow in a checkerboard pattern. Since both side lengths are odd, the four corner unit squares of $R$ have the same color; call it green.

Call an integer-sided rectangle \emph{green} if all four of its corner unit squares are green, \emph{yellow} if all four are yellow, and \emph{mixed} otherwise. For any such rectangle:

\begin{itemize}
\item a mixed rectangle contains equally many green and yellow unit squares;
\item a green rectangle contains one more green square than yellow square;
\item a yellow rectangle contains one more yellow square than green square.
\end{itemize}

The large rectangle $R$ is green, so it contains more green unit squares than yellow ones. Therefore at least one smaller rectangle, say $S$, is green.

Let the distances from $S$ to the top, bottom, left, and right sides of $R$ be
\[
x,\qquad y,\qquad u,\qquad v,
\]
respectively. Comparing the top-left corner squares of $R$ and $S$, which have the same color, gives
\[
x+u\equiv0\pmod2.
\]
The other three pairs of corresponding green corners similarly give
\[
x+v\equiv0,\qquad y+u\equiv0,\qquad y+v\equiv0\pmod2.
\]
Hence
\[
x\equiv y\equiv u\equiv v\pmod2.
\]
Thus the four distances are either all even or all odd.
