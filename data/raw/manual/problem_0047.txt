ID: 0047

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Radical Axis, Pascal's Theorem, Homothety, Incenter

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with incenter $I$ and circumcircle $\omega$. Let $D$ and $E$ be the second intersection points of $\omega$ with the lines $AI$ and $BI$, respectively. The chord $DE$ meets $AC$ at $F$ and $BC$ at $G$. Let $P$ be the intersection of the line through $F$ parallel to $AD$ and the line through $G$ parallel to $BE$. The tangents to $\omega$ at $A$ and $B$ meet at $K$. Prove that the three lines $AE,BD,KP$ are either parallel or concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
We use oriented angles modulo $180^\circ$. Since $A,I,D$ are collinear, $B,I,E$ are collinear, and $D,E,F,G$ are collinear,
\[
\angle IAF
=\angle DAC
=\angle BAD
=\angle BED
=\angle IEF.
\]
Hence $A,I,F,E$ are cyclic; denote their circle by $\omega_1$. Similarly, $B,D,G,I$ are cyclic; denote their circle by $\omega_2$.

The line $AE$ is the radical axis of $\omega$ and $\omega_1$, while $BD$ is the radical axis of $\omega$ and $\omega_2$. Let $t$ be the radical axis of $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. By the radical-axis theorem, the three lines
\[
AE,\quad BD,\quad t
\]
are concurrent, or all three are parallel. It remains to prove that
\[
t=KP.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $L$ be the second common point of $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, so $t=IL$. If the two circles are tangent at $I$, interpret $L=I$ and $t$ as their common tangent. Let $K'\ne L$ be the second intersection of $t$ with the circumcircle of $ABL$, and let $P'\ne L$ be the second intersection of $t$ with the circumcircle of $FGL$.

We first prove $K'=K$. Since $A,B,K',L$ are cyclic and $A,E,I,L$ are cyclic,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(AB,BK')
&=\angle(AL,LK')\\
&=\angle(AL,LI)\\
&=\angle(AE,EI)\\
&=\angle(AE,EB).
\end{aligned}
\]
By the tangent-chord theorem at $B$ for the circumcircle $\omega$,
\[
\angle(AE,EB)=\angle(AB,BK).
\]
Thus
\[
BK'\parallel BK.
\]
The same argument at $A$ gives
\[
AK'\parallel AK.
\]
Therefore $K'=K$.

We next prove $P'=P$. Since $F,G,P',L$ are cyclic and $B,D,G,I,L$ lie on the relevant circles and lines,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle(P'F,FG)
&=\angle(P'L,LG)\\
&=\angle(IL,LG)\\
&=\angle(ID,DG)\\
&=\angle(AD,DE)\\
&=\angle(PF,FG),
\end{aligned}
\]
because $PF\parallel AD$ and $FG$ lies on $DE$. Hence
\[
P'F\parallel PF.
\]
Analogously,
\[
P'G\parallel PG,
\]
because $PG\parallel BE$. Thus $P'=P$.

We have shown that both $K$ and $P$ lie on $t$, proving (1). Therefore $KP$ is the third radical axis, and the lines $AE,BD,KP$ are concurrent or parallel.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $M$ be the intersection of the tangents to $\omega$ at $D$ and $E$, and let
\[
T=AE\cap BD.
\]
If $AE\parallel BD$, regard $T$ as their common point at infinity. Pascal's theorem applied to the degenerate inscribed hexagons
\[
A,A,D,B,B,E
\quad\text{and}\quad
A,D,D,B,E,E
\]
shows that
\[
T,I,K,M\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]

Since $AD$ and $BE$ are angle bisectors, $D$ and $E$ are the midpoints of the arcs $BC$ and $CA$, respectively. Consequently, the tangent at $D$ is parallel to $BC$, and the tangent at $E$ is parallel to $AC$:
\[
DM\parallel BC,
\qquad
EM\parallel AC.
\tag{2}
\]
Apply Pascal's theorem to the degenerate hexagon
\[
C,A,D,D,E,B.
\]
The three intersections of opposite sides are
\[
CA\cap DE=F,
\qquad
AD\cap EB=I,
\]
and the point at infinity common to the parallel lines $DM$ and $BC$. Hence
\[
FI\parallel BC\parallel DM.
\tag{3}
\]
Analogously,
\[
GI\parallel AC\parallel EM.
\tag{4}
\]

Now consider the homothety that sends
\[
E\mapsto G,
\qquad
D\mapsto F.
\]
Its ratio is
\[
-\frac{FG}{ED}.
\]
By (2)--(4), the triangles $EDM$ and $GFI$ have pairwise parallel sides, so this homothety sends
\[
M\mapsto I.
\]
Also, by the definition of $P$,
\[
FP\parallel AD=DI,
\qquad
GP\parallel BE=EI.
\]
Thus the triangles $DEI$ and $FGP$ have pairwise parallel sides, and the same homothety sends
\[
I\mapsto P.
\]
The homothety center therefore lies on both $MI$ and $IP$, so
\[
M,I,P\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (1) and (5), the point $P$ lies on the line $TK$. Hence $KP$ passes through $T=AE\cap BD$. In the case when $T$ is an ideal point, the same statement means that all three lines are parallel. This proves the result.
