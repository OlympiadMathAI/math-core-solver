ID: 0395

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Divisibility, Binary Powers, Quadratic Forms, Infinite Descent, Jacobi Symbols

STATEMENT:
Prove that
\[
2^n+65
\]
does not divide
\[
5^n-3^n
\]
for any positive integer $n$.

SOLUTION 1:
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
m=2^n+65
\]
divides
\[
5^n-3^n.
\tag{1}
\]

If $n$ is even, then
\[
3\mid m,
\]
whereas
\[
3\nmid5^n-3^n,
\]
contradicting (1). Thus $n$ is odd. The case $n=1$ is immediate, so assume
\[
n=2h+1\ge3.
\]
Then
\[
\gcd(m,30)=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $m_1$ be the smallest positive multiple of $m$ which can be written in at least one of the forms
\[
|5a^2-3b^2|
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
|a^2-15b^2|
\tag{3}
\]
with integers $a,b$.

Such a multiple exists, because
\[
5^n-3^n
=
5(5^h)^2-3(3^h)^2
\]
has the first form in (3) and is divisible by $m$.

\textbf{Step 1: $m_1\le5m$.}

Consider all residues modulo $m$ of the numbers
\[
5^{h+1}x+3^{h+1}y,
\qquad
0\le x,y\le\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor.
\]
There are
\[
\bigl(\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor+1\bigr)^2>m
\]
pairs $(x,y)$, so two distinct pairs give the same residue. Subtracting them, we obtain integers $a,b$, not both zero, such that
\[
|a|,|b|\le\sqrt m
\]
and
\[
5^{h+1}a+3^{h+1}b\equiv0\pmod m.
\]
Squaring and using
\[
5^n\equiv3^n\pmod m
\]
from (1), we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\equiv
5^{n+1}a^2-3^{n+1}b^2\\
&\equiv
3^n(5a^2-3b^2)
\pmod m.
\end{aligned}
\]
By (2), $3$ is invertible modulo $m$, so
\[
m\mid5a^2-3b^2.
\]
The expression is nonzero, because
\[
5a^2=3b^2
\]
has no nonzero integer solution. Therefore
\[
0<|5a^2-3b^2|
\le
\max(5a^2,3b^2)
\le5m.
\]
By the minimality of $m_1$,
\[
m_1\le5m.
\tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Step 2: $m_1$ is divisible by none of $2,3,5$.}

Each possible divisibility would produce a smaller positive multiple of $m$ of one of the forms in (3).

If
\[
m_1=|5a^2-3b^2|,
\]
then:

\[
5\mid m_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
5\mid b
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left|a^2-15\left(\frac b5\right)^2\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{5};
\]

\[
3\mid m_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3\mid a
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left|b^2-15\left(\frac a3\right)^2\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{3};
\]

and, if $2\mid m_1$, then $a$ and $b$ have the same parity, and
\[
\left|
\left(\frac{5a-3b}{2}\right)^2
-
15\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)^2
\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{2}.
\]

If
\[
m_1=|a^2-15b^2|,
\]
then:

\[
5\mid m_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
5\mid a
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left|
5\left(\frac a5\right)^2-3b^2
\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{5};
\]

\[
3\mid m_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3\mid a
\quad\text{and}\quad
\left|
5b^2-3\left(\frac a3\right)^2
\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{3};
\]

and, if $2\mid m_1$, then $a$ and $b$ have the same parity, and
\[
\left|
5\left(\frac{a-3b}{2}\right)^2
-
3\left(\frac{a-5b}{2}\right)^2
\right|
=
\frac{m_1}{2}.
\]

Because of (2), each quotient above remains a positive multiple of $m$. This contradicts the minimality of $m_1$. Hence
\[
\gcd(m_1,30)=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Since $m_1$ is a positive multiple of $m$, equations (4)--(5) imply
\[
m_1=m.
\tag{6}
\]

\textbf{Step 3: neither form in (3) can equal $m$.}

For odd $n\ge3$,
\[
m\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad
m\equiv1\pmod4,
\qquad
m\equiv\pm2\pmod5.
\tag{7}
\]

The equality
\[
m=5a^2-3b^2
\]
is impossible modulo $3$, because its right-hand side is congruent to
\[
2a^2\in\{0,2\}\pmod3.
\]

The equality
\[
m=3b^2-5a^2
\]
is impossible modulo $4$, because an expression of the form
\[
3b^2-a^2
\]
is never congruent to $1$ modulo $4$.

The equality
\[
m=a^2-15b^2
\]
is impossible modulo $5$, because its right-hand side is a quadratic residue modulo $5$, whereas $m\equiv\pm2\pmod5$.

Finally,
\[
m=15b^2-a^2
\]
is impossible modulo $4$, because
\[
3b^2-a^2
\]
is never congruent to $1$ modulo $4$.

All possibilities contradict (6). Therefore
\[
2^n+65\nmid5^n-3^n
\]
for every positive integer $n$.

SOLUTION 2:
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
m=2^n+65
\mid
5^n-3^n.
\tag{1}
\]
The integer $n$ cannot be even, because then
\[
3\mid m
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
3\nmid5^n-3^n.
\]
The case $n=1$ is immediate, so
\[
n\ge3
\]
is odd. In particular,
\[
m\equiv1\pmod4,
\qquad
m\equiv1\pmod3,
\qquad
\gcd(m,15)=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Using Jacobi symbols and quadratic reciprocity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
-1
&=
\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac{5}{m}\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac{5^n}{m}\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac{3^n}{m}\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac{3}{m}\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac{m}{3}\right)\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here:

\begin{itemize}
\item the first equality follows because, for odd $n$, the residue
\[
m\equiv2^n\pmod5
\]
is either $2$ or $3$, both quadratic nonresidues modulo $5$;
\item the second and sixth equalities follow from quadratic reciprocity, using
\[
5\equiv1\pmod4
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
m\equiv1\pmod4;
\]
\item the fourth equality follows from the congruence in (1);
\item the last equality follows from
\[
m\equiv1\pmod3.
\]
\end{itemize}

The contradiction proves that the divisibility in (1) is impossible.
