ID: 0305

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities, Dyadic Decomposition, Cauchy-Schwarz, Induction

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ and $k$ be positive integers. Prove that, for
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n\in[1,2^k],
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\frac{a_i}{\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2+\cdots+a_i^2}}
\le4\sqrt{kn}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Partition the index set $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ into disjoint sets
\[
M_1,M_2,\ldots,M_k
\]
so that
\[
2^{j-1}\le a_\ell\le2^j
\qquad(\ell\in M_j).
\]
At common endpoints of two intervals, assign the index to either one of them.

Fix $j$, and list the elements of $M_j$ in increasing order:
\[
\ell_1<\ell_2<\cdots<\ell_{p_j},
\qquad
p_j=|M_j|.
\]
For the $r$th such index, the denominator contains at least $r$ terms from $M_j$, each of square at least $2^{2j-2}$. Therefore
\[
\frac{a_{\ell_r}}
{\sqrt{a_1^2+\cdots+a_{\ell_r}^2}}
\le
\frac{2^j}{2^{j-1}\sqrt r}
=\frac2{\sqrt r}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{\ell\in M_j}
\frac{a_\ell}{\sqrt{a_1^2+\cdots+a_\ell^2}}
\le
2\sum_{r=1}^{p_j}\frac1{\sqrt r}.
\]
Since
\[
\sqrt r-\sqrt{r-1}
=\frac1{\sqrt r+\sqrt{r-1}}
\ge\frac1{2\sqrt r},
\]
we have
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{p_j}\frac1{\sqrt r}
\le2\sqrt{p_j}.
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_{\ell\in M_j}
\frac{a_\ell}{\sqrt{a_1^2+\cdots+a_\ell^2}}
\le4\sqrt{p_j}.
\]
Summing over $j$ and applying Cauchy-Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\frac{a_i}{\sqrt{a_1^2+\cdots+a_i^2}}
&\le4\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{p_j}\\
&\le4\sqrt{k\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_j}\\
&=4\sqrt{kn}.
\end{aligned}
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Put
\[
S_i=a_1^2+\cdots+a_i^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
x_i=\frac{a_i}{\sqrt{S_i}}.
\]
We prove the result by strong induction on $n$.

For $n\le16$,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i\le n\le4\sqrt n\le4\sqrt{kn}.
\]

Assume now that $n\ge17$ and that the result holds for all smaller positive integers.

\textbf{Case 1: $n=2t$.}

For $i=t+1,\ldots,2t$,
\[
1-x_i^2=\frac{S_{i-1}}{S_i}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\prod_{i=t+1}^{2t}(1-x_i^2)
=\frac{S_t}{S_{2t}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Since
\[
a_{t+r}\le2^k a_r
\qquad(1\le r\le t),
\]
we have
\[
S_{2t}\le(1+4^k)S_t.
\]
Thus the product in (1) is at least $1/(1+4^k)$.

Using $1-u\le e^{-u}$ for $0\le u<1$,
\[
e^{-\sum_{i=t+1}^{2t}x_i^2}
\ge
\prod_{i=t+1}^{2t}(1-x_i^2)
\ge\frac1{1+4^k}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{i=t+1}^{2t}x_i^2
\le\log(1+4^k)
\le2k.
\]
By Cauchy-Schwarz,
\[
\sum_{i=t+1}^{2t}x_i
\le\sqrt{2kt}.
\]
The induction hypothesis applied to the first $t$ terms gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2t}x_i
\le4\sqrt{kt}+\sqrt{2kt}
\le4\sqrt{2kt}.
\]

\textbf{Case 2: $n=2t+1$.}

The same telescoping argument, now for the indices $t+2,\ldots,2t+1$, gives
\[
\sum_{i=t+2}^{2t+1}x_i\le\sqrt{2kt}.
\]
The first $t+1$ terms satisfy the induction hypothesis, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2t+1}x_i
\le4\sqrt{k(t+1)}+\sqrt{2kt}.
\]
For $t\ge8$,
\[
4\sqrt{t+1}+\sqrt{2t}\le4\sqrt{2t+1};
\]
after two squarings, this reduces to
\[
17t^2-32t\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2t+1}x_i
\le4\sqrt{k(2t+1)}.
\]

The induction is complete.
