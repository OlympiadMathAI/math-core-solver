ID: 0350

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangent-Chord Theorem, Spiral Similarity, Homothety, Radical Axes

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle $\Omega$. The tangent to $\Omega$ at $D$ meets the rays $BA$ and $BC$ at $E$ and $F$, respectively.

A point $T$ is chosen inside triangle $ABC$ so that
\[
TE\parallel CD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
TF\parallel AD.
\]
Let $K\ne D$ be a point on segment $DF$ such that
\[
TD=TK.
\]

Prove that the lines
\[
AC,\qquad DT,\qquad BK
\]
are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
P=TE\cap AC,
\qquad
Q=TF\cap AC.
\]

Because $PE\parallel CD$ and $ED$ is tangent to $\Omega$,
\[
\angle EPA=\angle DCA=\angle EDA.
\]
Thus
\[
A,P,D,E
\]
are concyclic; denote their circle by $\alpha$. Similarly,
\[
C,Q,D,F
\]
are concyclic; denote their circle by $\gamma$.

The equality
\[
\angle FCD+\angle EAD=180^\circ
\]
follows from the cyclicity of $ABCD$ and the positions of $E$ and $F$. It implies that $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are tangent to each other at $D$.

We next show that $TD$ is their common tangent. Since
\[
\angle QFE=\angle ADE=\angle APE,
\]
the quadrilateral $PEFQ$ is cyclic. Therefore
\[
TP\cdot TE=TQ\cdot TF.
\]
These are the powers of $T$ with respect to $\alpha$ and $\gamma$, so $T$ lies on their radical axis. Because the circles are tangent at $D$, their radical axis is their common tangent at $D$. Hence
\[
TD
\]
is tangent to both $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ at $D$.

Since $TD=TK$,
\[
\angle TKD=\angle TDK.
\]
Using the tangent--chord theorem first in $\alpha$ and then in $\Omega$,
\[
\angle TKD
=
\angle TDK
=
\angle EAD
=
\angle BDE.
\]
Thus
\[
TK\parallel BD.
\tag{1}
\]

We now show that
\[
T,P,Q,D,K
\]
are concyclic. Since $TD$ is tangent to $\alpha$,
\[
\angle EPD=\angle TDF=\angle TKD,
\]
so $P$ lies on the circle $(TDK)$. The same argument with $\gamma$ gives
\[
Q\in(TDK).
\]
Denote this circle by $\tau$.

Using $\tau$ and $\gamma$,
\[
\angle PKD
=
\angle PQD
=
\angle DFC.
\]
Therefore
\[
PK\parallel BC.
\tag{2}
\]
We also have
\[
TP\parallel DC.
\tag{3}
\]

By (1)--(3), the triangles
\[
TPK
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
DCB
\]
have pairwise parallel corresponding sides. Hence they are homothetic, and the lines joining corresponding vertices,
\[
TD,\qquad PC,\qquad KB,
\]
are concurrent. Since $P,C\in AC$, this is the desired result.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\varphi$ be the direct spiral similarity centred at $D$ that sends
\[
B\longmapsto F.
\]
For any points $U,V$, the triangles
\[
DU\varphi(U)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
DV\varphi(V)
\]
are similar and similarly oriented.

Put
\[
T'=\varphi(E).
\]
Using the tangent--chord theorem,
\[
\angle CDF
=
\angle FBD
=
\angle T'ED,
\]
so
\[
T'E\parallel CD.
\]
Likewise,
\[
\angle ADE
=
\angle ABD
=
\angle T'FD,
\]
and therefore
\[
T'F\parallel AD.
\]
The two parallel conditions defining $T$ determine a unique point, so
\[
T'=T.
\]

It follows that
\[
\angle BDE=\angle FDT=\angle DKT,
\]
and hence
\[
TK\parallel BD.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
X=BK\cap TD,
\qquad
Y=AC\cap TD,
\qquad
Q=AC\cap TF.
\]
From (1), the similar triangles determined by $X$ give
\[
\frac{TX}{XD}
=
\frac{TK}{BD}
=
\frac{TD}{BD}.
\tag{2}
\]

We claim that
\[
\varphi(A)=Q.
\]
Indeed,
\[
\angle DA\varphi(A)
=
\angle DBF
=
\angle DAC,
\]
so $\varphi(A)\in AC$. Also, since $\varphi(E)=T$ and $\varphi(B)=F$, the image of line $EB$ is line $TF$. Thus
\[
\varphi(A)=AC\cap TF=Q.
\]
The spiral similarity therefore gives
\[
\frac{TQ}{AE}=\frac{TD}{ED}.
\tag{3}
\]

Because $TQ\parallel AD$, triangles $YTQ$ and $YDA$ are similar:
\[
\frac{TY}{YD}=\frac{TQ}{AD}.
\tag{4}
\]
The tangent--chord theorem shows that
\[
\triangle EAD\sim\triangle EDB,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{AE}{AD}=\frac{ED}{BD}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining (3)--(5),
\[
\frac{TY}{YD}
=
\frac{TQ}{AE}\cdot\frac{AE}{AD}
=
\frac{TD}{ED}\cdot\frac{ED}{BD}
=
\frac{TD}{BD}.
\]
Together with (2), this gives
\[
\frac{TY}{YD}=\frac{TX}{XD}.
\]
Since both $X$ and $Y$ lie on line $TD$, we obtain
\[
X=Y.
\]
Thus
\[
BK\cap DT=AC\cap DT,
\]
which proves the concurrency.
