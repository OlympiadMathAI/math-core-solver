ID: 0076

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Reflections, Power of a Point, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Symmetry

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $AB\ne AC$ and circumcenter $O$. The bisector of $\angle BAC$ intersects $BC$ at $D$. Let $E$ be the reflection of $D$ in the midpoint of $BC$. The line through $D$ perpendicular to $BC$ intersects $AO$ at $X$, and the line through $E$ perpendicular to $BC$ intersects $AD$ at $Y$. Prove that the quadrilateral $BXCY$ is cyclic.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $P$ be the second intersection of the angle bisector $AD$ with the circumcircle of $ABC$. Then $P$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$. Hence $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$.

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Then
\[
O,P,M
\]
are collinear and
\[
OP\perp BC.
\]
The assumption $AB\ne AC$ ensures that the angle bisector $AD$ and the perpendicular bisector $OP$ are distinct.

Let $Y'$ be the reflection of $Y$ in the line $OP$. We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$. Reflection in $OP$ reverses orientation and interchanges $B$ and $C$, so the two effects cancel and give
\[
\measuredangle(YB,YC)=\measuredangle(Y'B,Y'C).
\tag{1}
\]
It is therefore enough to prove that $B,X,C,Y'$ are concyclic.

Since $X$ lies on $AO$, we have
\[
\angle XAP=\angle OAP.
\]
The points $A$ and $P$ lie on the circumcircle centered at $O$, so $OA=OP$. Therefore triangle $AOP$ is isosceles and
\[
\angle OAP=\angle OPA.
\tag{2}
\]
Also, $EY\perp BC$ and $OP\perp BC$, so $EY\parallel OP$. Since $Y,P,A$ are collinear,
\[
\angle EYP=\angle OPA.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3),
\[
\angle XAP=\angle EYP.
\tag{4}
\]

The points $D$ and $E$ are symmetric with respect to $OP$, because $M$ is their midpoint and $DM\parallel BC\perp OP$. The points $Y$ and $Y'$ are also symmetric with respect to $OP$. Hence reflection gives
\[
\angle EYP=\angle DY'P.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, the reflection of the line through $E$ perpendicular to $BC$ is the line through $D$ perpendicular to $BC$. Thus $D,X,Y'$ are collinear, and
\[
\angle DY'P=\angle XY'P.
\tag{6}
\]
From (4), (5), and (6),
\[
\angle XAP=\angle XY'P.
\]
Therefore $X,A,Y',P$ are concyclic.

Taking powers of $D$ with respect to the circles $(XAY'P)$ and $(ABCP)$ gives
\[
DX\cdot DY'=DA\cdot DP=DB\cdot DC.
\tag{7}
\]
Because $D,X,Y'$ are collinear and $D,B,C$ are collinear, equation (7) implies that
\[
B,X,C,Y'
\]
are concyclic. Hence
\[
\measuredangle(XB,XC)=\measuredangle(Y'B,Y'C).
\]
Together with (1), this gives
\[
\measuredangle(XB,XC)=\measuredangle(YB,YC),
\]
which is precisely the directed-angle criterion for $B,X,C,Y$ to be concyclic.
