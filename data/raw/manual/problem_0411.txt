ID: 0411

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Reflections, Congruent Triangles, Circumcentres

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with
\[
AC>BC.
\]
Let $\omega$ be its circumcircle and let $r$ be the radius of $\omega$.

Choose $P$ on segment $AC$ so that
\[
CP=CB,
\]
and let $S$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to line $AB$. The ray $BP$ meets $\omega$ again at $D$. Choose $Q$ on line $SP$ so that
\[
PQ=r
\]
and
\[
S,P,Q
\]
occur in this order.

The line through $A$ perpendicular to $CQ$ and the line through $B$ perpendicular to $DQ$ meet at $E$.

Prove that
\[
E\in\omega.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Because $A,P,C$ are collinear and $B,P,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle DPA=\angle BPC.
\]
The equality $CP=CB$ gives
\[
\angle BPC=\angle CBP.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\angle CBP=\angle CBD=\angle CAD=\angle PAD,
\]
where the middle equality follows from the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$. Hence
\[
\angle DPA=\angle PAD,
\]
and therefore
\[
DP=DA.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $E_0$ be the reflection of $P$ in line $CD$. Since reflection fixes $C$ and $D$,
\[
\angle CE_0D=\angle CPD.
\]
Now
\[
\angle CPD
=
180^\circ-\angle CPB
=
180^\circ-\angle PBC
=
180^\circ-\angle DBC.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle CE_0D+\angle DBC=180^\circ,
\]
so
\[
B,C,D,E_0
\]
are concyclic. As $A,B,C,D$ already lie on $\omega$, we obtain
\[
E_0\in\omega.
\tag{2}
\]

Let $O$ be the centre of $\omega$. We prove that
\[
BE_0\perp DQ.
\tag{3}
\]
We have
\[
AO=PQ=r
\]
and, by (1),
\[
AD=DP.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle DAO
=
90^\circ-\angle ABD
=
\angle DPQ,
\]
because $PQ\perp AB$ and $B,P,D$ are collinear. Hence
\[
\triangle AOD\cong\triangle PQD
\]
by SAS. In particular,
\[
\angle ODA=\angle QDP=\angle QDB.
\tag{4}
\]

Since $E_0$ is the reflection of $P$ in $CD$,
\[
DE_0=DP=DA.
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle DAE_0=\angle AE_0D.
\tag{5}
\]
Also, from the cyclic quadrilateral $ABDE_0$,
\[
\angle DBE_0=\angle DAE_0.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (4)-(6) and the isosceles triangle $AOD$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle QDB+\angle DBE_0
&=
\angle ODA+\angle DAE_0\\
&=
\angle DAO+\angle AE_0D\\
&=
\left(90^\circ-\angle ABD\right)+\angle AE_0D\\
&=
\left(90^\circ-\angle AE_0D\right)+\angle AE_0D\\
&=90^\circ.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (3).

The entire configuration is symmetric under the interchange
\[
(A,D)\longleftrightarrow(B,C):
\]
the point $P$, line $CD$, point $E_0$, line $AB$, and point $Q$ remain fixed. The same argument therefore gives
\[
AE_0\perp CQ.
\]
Thus $E_0$ is exactly the intersection point $E$ defined in the statement. By (2),
\[
E\in\omega.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
As in the angle chain below,
\[
\angle DPA
=
\angle BPC
=
\angle CBP
=
\angle CBD
=
\angle CAD
=
\angle PAD.
\]
Hence
\[
DA=DP.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of triangle $PCD$. Since
\[
DA=DP
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle ACD=\angle PCD,
\]
the sine rule in triangles $ACD$ and $PCD$ shows that $\Gamma$ and $\omega$ have the same circumradius $r$.

Let $O_\Gamma$ be the centre of $\Gamma$. The radius $PO_\Gamma$ is perpendicular to the tangent at $P$, so the tangent-chord theorem gives
\[
\angle DPO_\Gamma=90^\circ-\angle PCD.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DPQ
&=\angle BPS\\
&=90^\circ-\angle ABD\\
&=90^\circ-\angle PCD.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The order $S,P,Q$ selects the same side of chord $PD$ as $O_\Gamma$. Hence $P,Q,O_\Gamma$ are collinear. Since
\[
PQ=r=PO_\Gamma,
\]
we obtain
\[
Q=O_\Gamma.
\]
Consequently,
\[
QP=QC=QD.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $E$ be the intersection of the two perpendiculars in the statement. From $AE\perp CQ$ and (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle EAC
&=
90^\circ-\angle ACQ\\
&=
90^\circ-\angle QPC\\
&=
90^\circ-\angle SPA\\
&=
\angle CAB.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\angle EAB=2\angle PAB.
\tag{4}
\]

Similarly, using $BE\perp DQ$ and $QD=QP$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DBE
&=
90^\circ-\angle QDP\\
&=
90^\circ-\angle DPQ\\
&=
90^\circ-\angle BPS\\
&=
\angle ABD.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $B,P,D$ are collinear,
\[
\angle ABE=2\angle ABP.
\tag{5}
\]

Equations (4)-(5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BEA
&=
180^\circ-2\bigl(\angle PAB+\angle ABP\bigr)\\
&=
180^\circ-2\angle APD.
\end{aligned}
\]
By (1), triangle $ADP$ is isosceles, so
\[
\angle BDA
=
\angle PDA
=
180^\circ-2\angle APD.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle BEA=\angle BDA,
\]
which proves that
\[
A,B,D,E
\]
are concyclic. Hence $E$ lies on $\omega$.
