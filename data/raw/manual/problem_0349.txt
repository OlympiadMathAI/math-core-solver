ID: 0349

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Lattice Geometry, Polygon Areas, Central Symmetry, Cauchy-Schwarz

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ be a fixed positive integer, and let
\[
S=\{(x,y):x,y\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le x,y<2n\}.
\]
Thus
\[
|S|=4n^2.
\]

\textbf{Version 1.}
Suppose that $\mathcal F$ consists of $n^2$ quadrilaterals whose vertices lie in $S$, and every point of $S$ is a vertex of exactly one quadrilateral in $\mathcal F$. Determine the largest possible sum of the areas of the quadrilaterals.

\textbf{Version 2.}
Suppose that $\mathcal F$ is a collection of polygons whose vertices lie in $S$, and every point of $S$ is a vertex of exactly one polygon in $\mathcal F$. Determine the largest possible sum of the areas of the polygons.

SOLUTION 1:
For both versions, the maximum is
\[
\boxed{
\Sigma(n)=\frac13n^2(2n+1)(2n-1)
}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
O=\left(n-\frac12,n-\frac12\right),
\]
the centre of the grid.

For a polygon
\[
P=A_1A_2\cdots A_m,
\qquad A_{m+1}=A_1,
\]
we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
[P]
&\le
\sum_{i=1}^m[OA_iA_{i+1}]\\
&\le
\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m OA_i\cdot OA_{i+1}\\
&\le
\frac14\sum_{i=1}^m
\left(OA_i^2+OA_{i+1}^2\right)\\
&=
\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m OA_i^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
If $P$ is a square centred at $O$, equality holds throughout (2).

Rotation through $90^\circ$ about $O$ maps $S$ to itself. Every orbit has four points, and those four points are the vertices of a square centred at $O$. Hence the points of $S$ are partitioned into
\[
n^2
\]
such central squares. Let $\mathcal G$ be this collection.

For any admissible collection $\mathcal F$, summing (2) over all its polygons gives
\[
\sum_{P\in\mathcal F}[P]
\le
\frac12\sum_{A\in S}OA^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For $\mathcal G$, equality holds in (3). Thus it remains only to compute the right-hand side.

Put
\[
c=n-\frac12.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\sum_{(x,y)\in S}
\left((x-c)^2+(y-c)^2\right)
&=
2n\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}(j-c)^2\\
&=
n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(2j+1)^2\\
&=
n\cdot\frac{n(2n-1)(2n+1)}3\\
&=
\frac13n^2(2n-1)(2n+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly (1), and the central squares attain it. The same construction is admissible in both versions.

SOLUTION 2:
This solution concerns Version 1.

The answer is again
\[
\boxed{\frac13n^2(2n-1)(2n+1)}.
\]

For a quadrilateral $ABCD$, let $\phi$ be the angle between its diagonals. Then
\[
[ABCD]
=
\frac12 AC\cdot BD\,|\sin\phi|
\le
\frac14\left(AC^2+BD^2\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Across all $n^2$ quadrilaterals, their two diagonals pair the $4n^2$ grid points into
\[
2n^2
\]
pairs. Write these pairs as
\[
A_i=(p_i,q_i),
\qquad
B_i=(r_i,s_i),
\qquad
1\le i\le2n^2.
\]
Every point of $S$ occurs exactly once among all the $A_i$ and $B_i$.

We first bound the contribution of the first coordinates:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i-r_i)^2
=
2\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i^2+r_i^2)
-
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i+r_i)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Each integer $0,1,\ldots,2n-1$ occurs exactly $2n$ times among the first coordinates of all endpoints. Hence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i^2+r_i^2)
=
2n\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}j^2
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i+r_i)
=
2n\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}j
=
2n^2(2n-1).
\tag{4}
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i+r_i)^2
\ge
\frac{\left(2n^2(2n-1)\right)^2}{2n^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Substituting (3)--(5) into (2) gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}(p_i-r_i)^2
\le
\frac{2n^2(2n-1)(2n+1)}3.
\tag{6}
\]
The same estimate holds for the second coordinates. Therefore
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2n^2}A_iB_i^2
\le
\frac{4n^2(4n^2-1)}3.
\tag{7}
\]

Summing (1) over all quadrilaterals and using (7),
\[
\sum_{P\in\mathcal F}[P]
\le
\frac14\cdot
\frac{4n^2(4n^2-1)}3
=
\frac13n^2(2n-1)(2n+1).
\]

Equality is attained by partitioning $S$ into the orbits of the rotation through $90^\circ$ about
\[
O=\left(n-\frac12,n-\frac12\right).
\]
Each orbit is a square centred at $O$; its two diagonals join centrally symmetric points. These $n^2$ squares therefore attain the bound.
