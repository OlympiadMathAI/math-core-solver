ID: 0196

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Angle Bisectors, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Harmonic Division, Power of a Point, Directed Angles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral with
\[
\angle ABC=\angle ADC<90^\circ.
\]
The internal angle bisectors of $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ADC$ meet $AC$ at $E$ and $F$, respectively, and meet one another at $P$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AC$, and let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of triangle $BPD$. The segments $BM$ and $DM$ meet $\omega$ again at $X$ and $Y$, respectively. Let
\[
Q=XE\cap YF.
\]
Prove that
\[
PQ\perp AC.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\omega_1$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$.

We first prove that
\[
Y\in\omega_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose $Y'$ on the ray $MD$ so that
\[
MY'\cdot MD=MA^2.
\tag{2}
\]
The triangles $MAY'$ and $MDA$ are oppositely similar. Since
\[
MC^2=MA^2=MY'\cdot MD,
\]
the triangles $MCY'$ and $MDC$ are also oppositely similar. Thus, in directed angles,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle AY'C
&=\angle AY'M+\angle MY'C\\
&=\angle MAD+\angle DCM\\
&=\angle CDA\\
&=\angle ABC.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $Y'\in\omega_1$.

Let
\[
Z=AD\cap BC.
\]
Since
\[
\angle PDZ=\angle PBC=\angle PBZ,
\]
we have $Z\in\omega$. Also,
\[
\angle Y'BZ
=\angle Y'BC
=\angle Y'AC
=\angle Y'AM
=\angle Y'DZ,
\]
so $Y'\in\omega$ as well. The hypothesis $\angle ADC<90^\circ$ implies $MA\ne MD$, and hence $Y'\ne D$. Therefore $Y'$ is the second intersection of $DM$ with $\omega$, so
\[
Y'=Y.
\]
This proves (1).

Using the angle-bisector theorem and the two opposite similarities above,
\[
\frac{FA}{FC}
=\frac{AD}{CD}
=\frac{AD}{AM}\cdot\frac{CM}{CD}
=\frac{YA}{YM}\cdot\frac{YM}{YC}
=\frac{YA}{YC}.
\]
Therefore $FY$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle AYC$.

Let $B'$ be the second intersection of the internal angle bisector of $\angle CBA$ with $\omega_1$. Then $B'$ is the midpoint of the arc $AC$ not containing $B$, so $YB'$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle AYC$. Consequently,
\[
YB'\perp YF.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $\ell$ be the line through $P$ parallel to $AC$, and let
\[
S=\ell\cap B'Y.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle PSY
&=\angle(AC,B'Y)\\
&=\angle ACY+\angle CYB'\\
&=\angle ACY+\angle CAB'\\
&=\angle ACY+\angle B'CA\\
&=\angle B'CY\\
&=\angle B'BY\\
&=\angle PBY.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $S\in\omega$.

The symmetric argument, starting from the line $XE$, shows that the line through $X$ perpendicular to $XE$ also passes through the second intersection of $\ell$ with $\omega$, namely $S$. Thus
\[
XS\perp XE
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
YS\perp YF.
\]
Since $Q=XE\cap YF$, the angles $QXS$ and $QYS$ are right angles, so $Q\in\omega$ and $QS$ is a diameter of $\omega$.

Because $P,S\in\omega$,
\[
PQ\perp PS.
\]
Finally,
\[
PS\parallel AC,
\]
and therefore
\[
PQ\perp AC.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ be the circumcircles of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ADC$, respectively. Since
\[
\angle ABC=\angle ADC,
\]
the two circles are reflections of one another in the midpoint $M$ of $AC$.

We first show that
\[
X\in\omega_2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let $X_1$ be the second intersection of the ray $MB$ with $\omega_2$, and let $X_0$ be its reflection in $M$. Then $X_0\in\omega_1$, and $X_0AX_1C$ is a parallelogram. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DX_1B
&=\angle DX_1A+\angle AX_1B\\
&=\angle DCA+\angle AX_1X_0\\
&=\angle DCA+\angle CX_0X_1\\
&=\angle DCA+\angle CAB\\
&=\angle(CD,AB).
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\angle DPB
=\angle PDC+\angle(CD,AB)+\angle ABP
=\angle(CD,AB).
\]
Thus $X_1\in\omega$. Since $X_1$ is the second intersection of $BM$ with $\omega$,
\[
X_1=X.
\]
This proves (1). By symmetry,
\[
Y\in\omega_1.
\tag{2}
\]

Let the perpendicular bisector of $AC$ meet $\omega_1$ at $B'$ and $M_1$, and meet $\omega_2$ at $D'$ and $M_2$, with $B,M_1,D'$ on the same side of $AC$. The point $B'$ lies on the angle bisector of $\angle ABC$, and $D'$ lies on $DP$.

We show that $XE$ is the angle bisector of $\angle AXC$. Since $X_0$ is the reflection of $X$ in $M$,
\[
\frac{BA\cdot X_0A}{BC\cdot X_0C}
=
\frac{[BAX_0]}{[BCX_0]}
=
\frac{MA}{MC}
=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{EA}{EC}
=\frac{BA}{BC}
=\frac{X_0C}{X_0A}
=\frac{XA}{XC}.
\]
The angle-bisector theorem gives that $XE$ bisects $\angle AXC$. Hence
\[
X,E,M_2\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{3}
\]
Similarly,
\[
Y,F,M_1\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{4}
\]

Using (3) and (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle XQY
&=\angle M_2QM_1\\
&=\angle QM_2M_1+\angle M_2M_1Q\\
&=\angle XM_2D'+\angle B'M_1Y\\
&=\angle XDD'+\angle B'BY\\
&=\angle XDP+\angle PBY\\
&=\angle XBP+\angle PBY\\
&=\angle XBY.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
Q\in\omega.
\tag{5}
\]

The quadrilateral $X_0M_2XM_1$ is a parallelogram, because $M_1$ and $M_2$ are reflections in $M$. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle XQP
&=\angle XBP\\
&=\angle X_0BB'\\
&=\angle X_0M_1B'\\
&=\angle XM_2M_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
QP\parallel M_2M_1.
\]
Since the line $M_1M_2$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$,
\[
PQ\perp AC.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
We use two lemmas.

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
In a triangle $X_0Y_0Z_0$ with $X_0Y_0\ne X_0Z_0$, let $N_0$ be the midpoint of $Y_0Z_0$, and let $W_0$ be the foot of the internal angle bisector from $X_0$. Then, with directed angles,
\[
\tan^2\angle W_0X_0Z_0
=
\tan\angle N_0X_0W_0\,
\tan\angle Z_0W_0X_0.
\tag{1}
\]

To prove this, assume $X_0Y_0>X_0Z_0$, so $W_0$ lies between $N_0$ and $Z_0$. Set
\[
\alpha=\angle W_0X_0Z_0,\qquad
\beta=\angle N_0X_0W_0,\qquad
\gamma=\angle Z_0W_0X_0.
\]
The sine rule in the two triangles around $N_0$ gives
\[
\frac{\sin(\gamma-\alpha)}{\sin(\alpha-\beta)}
=
\frac{N_0X_0}{N_0Y_0}
=
\frac{N_0X_0}{N_0Z_0}
=
\frac{\sin(\gamma+\alpha)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}.
\]
Rewriting in tangent form,
\[
\frac{\tan\gamma-\tan\alpha}{\tan\gamma+\tan\alpha}
=
\frac{\tan\alpha-\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta},
\]
which simplifies to (1).

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
Let $A_0B_0C_0D_0$ be cyclic. Suppose its diagonals $A_0C_0$ and $B_0D_0$ meet at $E_0$, while the lines $A_0B_0$ and $C_0D_0$ meet at $F_0$. If $M_0$ is the midpoint of $E_0F_0$, then
\[
\operatorname{Pow}_\Gamma(M_0)=M_0E_0^2,
\tag{2}
\]
where $\Gamma$ is the circumcircle.

Let $O_0$ and $R_0$ be the centre and radius of $\Gamma$. A standard complete-quadrilateral theorem says that $E_0$ and $F_0$ are conjugate with respect to $\Gamma$; in vector form,
\[
\overrightarrow{O_0E_0}\cdot\overrightarrow{O_0F_0}=R_0^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
e=\overrightarrow{O_0E_0},
\qquad
f=\overrightarrow{O_0F_0}.
\]
Since $M_0$ is the midpoint of $E_0F_0$,
\[
\overrightarrow{O_0M_0}=\frac{e+f}{2}.
\]
Using (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Pow}_\Gamma(M_0)
&=\left|\frac{e+f}{2}\right|^2-R_0^2\\
&=\frac{|e|^2+|f|^2-2R_0^2}{4}\\
&=\left|\frac{f-e}{2}\right|^2\\
&=M_0E_0^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the lemma.

We now return to the problem. Let
\[
Z=AD\cap BC,
\qquad
W=AB\cap CD.
\]
As in Solution 1,
\[
Z,W\in\omega.
\]
Apply Lemma 2 to the cyclic quadrilateral $ZBDW$. Its diagonal intersection is $A$, the intersection of the opposite side lines is $C$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Pow}_\omega(M)=MA^2.
\]
Since $B,M,X$ are collinear,
\[
MX\cdot MB=MA^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Let the line through $B$ perpendicular to $BE$ meet $AC$ at $T$. The lines $BE$ and $BT$ are the internal and external angle bisectors of $\angle CBA$, so
\[
(T,E;A,C)
\]
is harmonic. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AC$,
\[
ME\cdot MT=MA^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Combining (3) and (4), the points
\[
E,T,B,X
\]
are concyclic.

If $AD=CD$, then $P$ and $Q$ both lie on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$, and the result is immediate. The case $AB=CB$ is analogous. We now assume neither equality.

Let $P_0,Q_0$ be the orthogonal projections of $P,Q$ onto $AC$. Then
\[
PQ\perp AC
\]
is equivalent to
\[
P_0=Q_0,
\]
which in directed lengths is equivalent to
\[
\frac{EP_0}{FP_0}
=
\frac{EQ_0}{FQ_0}.
\tag{5}
\]

We have
\[
\frac{EP_0}{FP_0}
=
\frac{\tan\angle EFP}{\tan\angle FEP}
=
\frac{\tan\angle AFD}{\tan\angle AEB}.
\tag{6}
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{EQ_0}{FQ_0}
=
\frac{\tan\angle EFQ}{\tan\angle FEQ}.
\]
Because $E,T,B,X$ are cyclic,
\[
\angle FEQ=\angle TEX=\angle TBX
=\frac\pi2+\angle EBM.
\]
By symmetry,
\[
\angle EFQ=\frac\pi2+\angle FDM.
\]
Thus (5) reduces to
\[
\frac{\tan\angle AFD}{\tan\angle AEB}
=
\frac{\tan\angle MBE}{\tan\angle MDF}.
\tag{7}
\]

Applying Lemma 1 in the two relevant angle-bisector configurations,
\[
\tan\angle AFD\,\tan\angle MDF
=
\tan^2\angle FDC
=
\tan^2\angle EBA
=
\tan\angle MBE\,\tan\angle AEB.
\]
This is precisely (7), and therefore $PQ\perp AC$.
