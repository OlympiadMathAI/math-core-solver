ID: 0192

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incenter Geometry, Directed Angles, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Mixtilinear Incircle, Butterfly Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with circumcircle $\Gamma$ and incentre $I$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$, and let $D$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $I$ to $BC$. The line through $I$ perpendicular to $AI$ meets $AB$ and $AC$ at $F$ and $E$, respectively. Suppose that the circumcircle of $AEF$ meets $\Gamma$ again at $X\ne A$. Prove that the lines $XD$ and $AM$ meet on $\Gamma$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $AM$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $Y$, and put
\[
D'=XY\cap BC.
\]
It is enough to prove that $D'=D$. All ratios on the line $BC$ below are directed.

We first use the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
If $PQRS$ is cyclic and its diagonals meet at $T$, then
\[
\frac{QT}{TS}=\frac{PQ\cdot QR}{PS\cdot SR}.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed,
\[
\frac{QT}{TS}
=\frac{[PQR]}{[PSR]}
=\frac{PQ\cdot QR\sin\angle PQR}
       {PS\cdot SR\sin\angle PSR},
\]
and the two sines are equal because $PQRS$ is cyclic.

Applying (1) to the cyclic quadrilaterals $ABYC$ and $XBYC$ gives
\[
1=\frac{BM}{MC}
=\frac{AB\cdot BY}{AC\cdot CY}
\]
and
\[
\frac{BD'}{D'C}
=\frac{XB\cdot BY}{XC\cdot CY}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{BD'}{D'C}
=\frac{XB}{XC}\cdot\frac{AC}{AB}.
\tag{2}
\]

Using directed angles,
\[
\angle XBF=\angle XBA=\angle XCA=\angle XCE
\]
and
\[
\angle XFB=\angle XFA=\angle XEA=\angle XEC.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle XBF\sim\triangle XCE,
\]
so
\[
\frac{XB}{XC}=\frac{BF}{CE}.
\tag{3}
\]

We now calculate the last ratio. Since
\[
\angle FIB=\angle AIB-90^\circ=\frac12\angle ACB=\angle ICB
\]
and
\[
\angle FBI=\angle IBC,
\]
the triangles $FBI$ and $IBC$ are similar. Analogously, $EIC\sim IBC$. Hence
\[
\frac{BF}{IB}=\frac{IB}{BC},
\qquad
\frac{CE}{IC}=\frac{IC}{BC},
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{BF}{CE}=\frac{IB^2}{IC^2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Draw the other common internal tangent to the incircle parallel to $BC$, meeting $AB$ and $AC$ at $B_1$ and $C_1$. Let the incircle touch $AB$ and $AC$ at $B_2$ and $C_2$. By homothety,
\[
B_1I
\]
is parallel to the external angle bisector at $B$, so
\[
\angle B_1IB=90^\circ.
\]
Together with $\angle BB_2I=90^\circ$, this gives
\[
BB_2\cdot BB_1=BI^2.
\]
Similarly,
\[
CC_2\cdot CC_1=CI^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{BI^2}{CI^2}
=\frac{BB_2\cdot BB_1}{CC_2\cdot CC_1}
=\frac{AB}{AC}\cdot\frac{BD}{CD}.
\tag{5}
\]

Combining (2), (3), (4), and (5),
\[
\frac{BD'}{D'C}
=\frac{BF}{CE}\cdot\frac{AC}{AB}
=\frac{BI^2}{CI^2}\cdot\frac{AC}{AB}
=\frac{BD}{DC}.
\]
Thus $D'=D$. Hence $X,D,Y$ are collinear, and the lines $XD$ and $AM$ meet at the point $Y\in\Gamma$.

For completeness, the ratio in (4) may also be obtained trigonometrically. If
\[
\beta=\frac12\angle ABC,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac12\angle ACB,
\]
then
\[
\frac{BF}{CE}
=\left(\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\beta}\right)^2.
\]
Using the sine law in $\triangle ABC$,
\[
\frac{AC}{AB}=\frac{\sin2\beta}{\sin2\gamma},
\]
so (2) becomes
\[
\frac{BD'}{D'C}
=\frac{\tan\gamma}{\tan\beta}
=\frac{BD}{DC}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\omega_A$ be the $A$-mixtilinear incircle of $\triangle ABC$. Its points of tangency with $AB$ and $AC$ are precisely $F$ and $E$. Let $\omega_A$ touch $\Gamma$ at $T$.

Let $AM$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $Y$. Reflect $D$ and $T$ in the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, obtaining $D_1$ and $T_1$. A standard property of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle gives
\[
\angle BAT=\angle D_1AC,
\]
hence
\[
A,D_1,T_1\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove that $X,M,T_1$ are collinear. Let $R$ be the radical centre of $\omega_A$, $\Gamma$, and the circumcircle of $AEF$. Then
\[
R\in AX,\qquad R\in EF,
\]
and $R$ also lies on the tangent to $\Gamma$ at $T$.

Let $AT$ meet $\omega_A$ again at $S$ and meet $EF$ at $P$. The quadrilateral $SFTE$ is harmonic. Projecting from $T$, the pencil
\[
(R,P;F,E)
\]
is harmonic. Projecting this pencil from $A$ onto $\Gamma$ shows that
\[
(X,B;T,C)
\]
is harmonic.

Since
\[
TT_1\parallel BC,
\]
projection from $T_1$ to $BC$ sends $T$ to the point at infinity. It therefore sends $X$ to the midpoint $M$ of $BC$. Hence
\[
X,M,T_1\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{2}
\]

Now both chords $AY$ and $XT_1$ of $\Gamma$ pass through the midpoint $M$ of the chord $BC$. By the butterfly theorem, the intersections of $XY$ and $AT_1$ with $BC$ are symmetric about $M$. In view of (1), the latter intersection is $D_1$, whose reflection about $M$ is $D$. Thus
\[
X,D,Y\ \text{are collinear}.
\]
Since $Y=AM\cap\Gamma$, the conclusion follows.

One can avoid the butterfly theorem as follows. The quadrilateral $DTT_1D_1$ is an isosceles trapezoid, and directed-angle calculations give
\[
\angle YTD=\angle YMD,
\]
so $D,T,Y,M$ are concyclic. Using (2),
\[
\angle AYD
=\angle MTD
=\angle D_1T_1M
=\angle AT_1X
=\angle AYX.
\]
Therefore $X,D,Y$ are collinear.
