ID: 0067

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Extremal Combinatorics, Pairings, Constructive Combinatorics, Double Counting

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge 1$ be an integer. What is the maximum number of disjoint pairs of elements of the set
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
\]
such that the sums of the different pairs are different integers not exceeding $n$?

SOLUTION 1:
The maximum is
\[
\boxed{\left\lfloor\frac{2n-1}{5}\right\rfloor}.
\]

Suppose that $x$ disjoint pairs satisfy the conditions. Let $S$ be the sum of all $2x$ elements used in the pairs. Since these elements are distinct,
\[
S\ge 1+2+\cdots+2x=\frac{2x(2x+1)}2.
\tag{1}
\]
On the other hand, $S$ is also the sum of the $x$ pair-sums. Those sums are distinct positive integers not exceeding $n$, so
\[
S\le n+(n-1)+\cdots+(n-x+1)
=nx-\frac{x(x-1)}2.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) gives
\[
\frac{2x(2x+1)}2\le nx-\frac{x(x-1)}2.
\]
For $x>0$, division by $x$ and simplification yield
\[
5x\le 2n-1.
\]
The same conclusion is trivial for $x=0$. Therefore
\[
x\le \left\lfloor\frac{2n-1}{5}\right\rfloor.
\tag{3}
\]

It remains to attain this bound. First let
\[
n=5k+3
\qquad(k\ge 0).
\]
For $j=1,2,\ldots,k$, take the pairs
\[
P_j=(3k+2-j,\,2j)
\]
and
\[
Q_j=(4k+3-j,\,2j-1),
\]
and also take
\[
R=(3k+2,\,2k+1).
\]
The sums are
\[
\sum P_j=3k+2+j
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,k),
\]
\[
\sum Q_j=4k+2+j
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,k),
\]
and
\[
\sum R=5k+3.
\]
Thus the pair-sums are exactly
\[
3k+3,3k+4,\ldots,5k+3,
\]
so they are distinct and do not exceed $n$. Moreover, the $2k+1$ pairs use every integer from $1$ to $4k+2$ exactly once. Hence this is a valid construction of $2k+1$ pairs.

The same construction works without change for
\[
n=5k+4\quad\text{and}\quad n=5k+5,
\]
because all elements and all sums are at most $5k+3$. In each of these three cases,
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{2n-1}{5}\right\rfloor=2k+1.
\]

For
\[
n=5k+2,
\]
omit the last pair $R$. This gives $2k$ valid pairs, which equals the value in (3).

Finally, for
\[
n=5k+1,
\]
omit $R$ and subtract $1$ from the first entry of every remaining pair. The first entries then lie in
\[
\{2k+1,\ldots,3k\}\cup\{3k+2,\ldots,4k+1\},
\]
while the second entries are $1,2,\ldots,2k$, so all used elements are distinct and belong to $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The pair-sums become the distinct integers
\[
3k+2,3k+3,\ldots,5k+1.
\]
Thus we again obtain $2k$ valid pairs, equal to the upper bound.

The constructions also cover $k=0$, with the indicated families of pairs empty when appropriate. Hence the maximum is exactly
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{2n-1}{5}\right\rfloor.
\]
