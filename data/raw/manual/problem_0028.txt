ID: 0028

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Fibonacci Numbers, Modular Periods, Directed Graphs, Two-Adic Valuation

STATEMENT:
The rows and columns of a
\[
2^n\times2^n
\]
table are numbered
\[
0,1,\ldots,2^n-1.
\]
The cells are colored so that, for every $0\le i,j<2^n$, the cell in row $i$, column $j$ has the same color as the cell in row $j$, column $i+j$, where column indices are taken modulo $2^n$.

Prove that the maximum possible number of colors is
\[
2^n.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We use the convention $v_2(0)=+\infty$.
Represent a cell by its coordinate pair $(i,j)$. Consider the directed graph whose vertices are the cells and whose edges are
\[
(i,j)\longrightarrow(j,i+j\pmod{2^n}).
\tag{1}
\]
Every vertex has exactly one outgoing edge. It also has exactly one incoming edge, because the unique predecessor of $(j,k)$ is
\[
(k-j,j)
\pmod{2^n}.
\]
Therefore the graph is a disjoint union of directed cycles.

The coloring condition says that all cells in one cycle must have the same color. Conversely, assigning a distinct color to every cycle satisfies the condition. Thus the maximum number of colors equals the number of cycles of the map in (1).

Take a cycle beginning at $(a_0,a_1)$ and define
\[
a_{r+1}=a_r+a_{r-1}
\qquad(r\ge1).
\]
Then the successive cells are
\[
(a_0,a_1),\ (a_1,a_2),\ (a_2,a_3),\ldots
\pmod{2^n}.
\]
We must therefore study Fibonacci-type sequences modulo $2^n$.

Let
\[
F_0=0,\qquad F_1=1,\qquad F_{r+2}=F_{r+1}+F_r,
\]
and set $F_{-1}=1$. For a positive integer $m$, let
\[
\nu(m)=v_2(m).
\]

\textbf{Lemma 1.} Every Fibonacci-type sequence satisfies
\[
a_r=F_{r-1}a_0+F_ra_1
\qquad(r\ge0).
\tag{2}
\]

This follows immediately by induction.

\textbf{Lemma 2.} For every integer $m\ge3$:

\[
\text{(a)}\qquad
v_2\left(F_{3\cdot2^{m-2}}\right)=m;
\]

\[
\text{(b)}\qquad
3\cdot2^{m-2}
\]
is the least positive index $d$ for which
\[
2^m\mid F_d;
\]

\[
\text{(c)}\qquad
F_{3\cdot2^{m-2}+1}
\equiv1+2^{m-1}\pmod{2^m}.
\]

We prove the three assertions simultaneously by induction on $m$. For $m=3$,
\[
F_6=8,
\qquad
F_7=13\equiv1+4\pmod8,
\]
and none of $F_1,\ldots,F_5$ is divisible by $8$.

Now let $m>3$ and put
\[
k=3\cdot2^{m-3}.
\]
The standard identities
\[
F_{2k}=2F_{k+1}F_k-F_k^2,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
F_{2k+1}=F_k^2+F_{k+1}^2
\tag{4}
\]
follow from Lemma 1. By induction,
\[
v_2(F_k)=m-1,
\]
while $F_{k+1}$ is odd. In (3), the first term has valuation $m$ and the second has larger valuation, so
\[
v_2(F_{2k})=m.
\]
This proves (a).

By the induction hypothesis, for some integer $c$,
\[
F_{k+1}=1+2^{m-2}+c2^{m-1}.
\]
Using (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{2k+1}
&=F_k^2+F_{k+1}^2\\
&\equiv
\left(1+2^{m-2}+c2^{m-1}\right)^2\\
&\equiv1+2^{m-1}\pmod{2^m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (c).

It remains to prove minimality in (b). Suppose
\[
0<\ell<2k
\quad\text{and}\quad
2^m\mid F_\ell.
\]
Then $2^{m-1}\mid F_\ell$, so the induction hypothesis gives $\ell>k$. By the addition formula,
\[
F_\ell
=
F_{k-1}F_{\ell-k}
+
F_kF_{\ell-k+1}.
\]
The second term is divisible by $2^{m-1}$. The first is not: $F_{k-1}$ is odd and
\[
0<\ell-k<k,
\]
so the induction hypothesis says that $F_{\ell-k}$ is not divisible by $2^{m-1}$. Hence $F_\ell$ is not even divisible by $2^{m-1}$, a contradiction. Lemma 2 follows.

For a pair $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, define
\[
\mu(a,b)=\min\{v_2(a),v_2(b)\}.
\]
The value of $\mu$ is unchanged under
\[
(a,b)\mapsto(b,a+b),
\]
so it is constant along a Fibonacci-type sequence. Denote it by $\mu(A)$.

Let $p_n(A)$ be the least positive period of a Fibonacci-type sequence $A$ modulo $2^n$.

\textbf{Lemma 3.} If
\[
\mu(A)=k<n,
\]
then
\[
p_n(A)=3\cdot2^{n-1-k}.
\tag{5}
\]

Divide all terms by $2^k$. This reduces the assertion to the case $k=0$, with modulus $2^{n-k}$. Thus assume $\mu(A)=0$.

For $n=1$, every such sequence has period $3$ modulo $2$; for $n=2$, it has period $6$ modulo $4$. Now take $n\ge3$ and put
\[
s=3\cdot2^{n-2}.
\]
By induction, $s$ is the period modulo $2^{n-1}$, so
\[
s\mid p_n(A).
\tag{6}
\]

After shifting the sequence if necessary, we may suppose that $a_0$ is even and $a_1$ is odd. Write
\[
a_0=2b_0,
\qquad
a_1=2b_1+1,
\]
and let $(b_r)$ be the Fibonacci-type sequence beginning with $b_0,b_1$. By induction and Lemma 1,
\[
a_r=2b_r+F_r.
\tag{7}
\]

The sequence $(b_r)$ has period dividing $s$ modulo $2^{n-1}$, so $(2b_r)$ is $s$-periodic modulo $2^n$. By Lemma 2,
\[
F_{s+1}\equiv1+2^{n-1}\pmod{2^n},
\]
and
\[
F_{2s}\equiv0,\qquad
F_{2s+1}\equiv1\pmod{2^n}.
\]
Using (7),
\[
a_{s+1}
\equiv a_1+2^{n-1}
\not\equiv a_1\pmod{2^n},
\]
so $s$ is not a period. On the other hand,
\[
a_{2s}\equiv a_0,
\qquad
a_{2s+1}\equiv a_1
\pmod{2^n},
\]
so $2s$ is a period. Together with (6), this gives
\[
p_n(A)=2s=3\cdot2^{n-1}.
\]
Restoring the factor $2^k$ proves (5).

We now count the cycles. Fix
\[
0\le k\le n-1.
\]
The number of pairs $(i,j)$ modulo $2^n$ satisfying
\[
\mu(i,j)=k
\]
is
\[
2^{2(n-k)}-2^{2(n-k-1)}
=
3\cdot2^{2n-2k-2}.
\]
By Lemma 3, every corresponding cycle has length
\[
3\cdot2^{n-1-k}.
\]
Hence the number of cycles in this class is
\[
\frac{3\cdot2^{2n-2k-2}}
     {3\cdot2^{n-1-k}}
=
2^{n-k-1}.
\]
The remaining cell $(0,0)$ forms one cycle by itself. Therefore the total number of cycles is
\[
1+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{n-k-1}
=
1+(1+2+\cdots+2^{n-1})
=
2^n.
\]
Thus the maximal possible number of colors is
\[
\boxed{2^n}.
\]
