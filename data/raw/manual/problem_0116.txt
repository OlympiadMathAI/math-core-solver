ID: 0116

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Sequences, Monovariants, Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
z_0<z_1<z_2<\cdots
\]
be an infinite sequence of positive integers. Prove that there exists a unique integer $n\ge1$ such that
\[
z_n<\frac{z_0+z_1+\cdots+z_n}{n}\le z_{n+1}.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For every $n\ge1$, define
\[
d_n=(z_0+z_1+\cdots+z_n)-nz_n.
\]
The first inequality in the desired conclusion is equivalent to
\[
d_n>0.
\]
Also,
\[
d_{n+1}
=z_0+\cdots+z_n+z_{n+1}-(n+1)z_{n+1}
=z_0+\cdots+z_n-nz_{n+1},
\]
so the second inequality is equivalent to
\[
d_{n+1}\le0.
\]
Thus it is enough to prove that there is a unique $n\ge1$ such that
\[
d_n>0\ge d_{n+1}.
\tag{1}
\]

We have
\[
d_1=z_0>0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
d_{n+1}-d_n
=n(z_n-z_{n+1})<0.
\]
Hence $(d_n)_{n\ge1}$ is strictly decreasing. Since every $d_n$ is an integer, in fact
\[
d_{n+1}\le d_n-1,
\]
and therefore $d_n\to-\infty$. Consequently the sequence has a last positive term. If this term is $d_n$, then (1) holds.

The strict decrease also shows that there can be only one such index $n$. This proves both existence and uniqueness.
