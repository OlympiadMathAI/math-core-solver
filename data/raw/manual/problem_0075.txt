ID: 0075

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2012

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Orthic Triangle, Incenters, Circumcenters, Radical Axis, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Similarity

STATEMENT:
In an acute triangle $ABC$, the points $D,E,F$ are the feet of the altitudes through $A,B,C$, respectively. The incenters of triangles $AEF$ and $BDF$ are $I_1$ and $I_2$, respectively. The circumcenters of triangles $ACI_1$ and $BCI_2$ are $O_1$ and $O_2$, respectively. Prove that $I_1I_2$ is parallel to $O_1O_2$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
\alpha=\angle BAC,\qquad \beta=\angle ABC,\qquad \gamma=\angle BCA,
\]
and let $I$ be the incenter of $ABC$.

The line $AI_1$ is the bisector of $\angle EAF=\angle BAC$, so $A,I_1,I$ are collinear. Similarly, $B,I_2,I$ are collinear.

Because $BE\perp AC$ and $CF\perp AB$, the points $B,C,E,F$ lie on the circle with diameter $BC$. Hence
\[
\angle AEF=\angle ABC,
\qquad
\angle AFE=\angle ACB.
\]
Thus triangles $AEF$ and $ABC$ are similar. Their similarity ratio is
\[
\frac{AE}{AB}=\cos\alpha.
\]
The similarity sends the incenter $I$ of $ABC$ to the incenter $I_1$ of $AEF$, and therefore
\[
AI_1=AI\cos\alpha.
\]
Since the triangle is acute, $I_1$ lies between $A$ and $I$, so
\[
II_1=AI-AI_1=AI(1-\cos\alpha)=2AI\sin^2\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
By symmetry,
\[
II_2=2BI\sin^2\frac{\beta}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Applying the sine rule in triangle $ABI$ gives
\[
AI\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}=BI\sin\frac{\beta}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Using (1), (2), and (3),
\[
II_1\cdot IA
=2\left(AI\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^2
=2\left(BI\sin\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^2
=II_2\cdot IB.
\tag{4}
\]
Since $A,I_1,I$ are collinear and $B,I_2,I$ are collinear, equation (4) is the converse of the intersecting-chords theorem. Hence
\[
A,B,I_1,I_2
\]
are concyclic.

Now consider the circles
\[
\Gamma_1=(ACI_1),
\qquad
\Gamma_2=(BCI_2).
\]
The power of $I$ with respect to $\Gamma_1$ is
\[
IA\cdot II_1,
\]
while its power with respect to $\Gamma_2$ is
\[
IB\cdot II_2.
\]
These are equal by (4). Since both circles pass through $C$, their radical axis is the line $CI$. Therefore
\[
O_1O_2\perp CI.
\tag{5}
\]

It remains to prove that $I_1I_2\perp CI$. Let
\[
Q=I_1I_2\cap CI.
\]
From the cyclic quadrilateral $ABI_1I_2$ and the collinearity of $A,I_1,I$, we get
\[
\angle II_1I_2=\frac{\beta}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
In the configuration, $\angle I_1IQ$ is the exterior angle at $I$ of triangle $ACI$, so
\[
\angle I_1IQ
=\angle CAI+\angle ACI
=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Using (6) and (7),
\[
\angle II_1Q+\angle I_1IQ
=\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}
=90^\circ.
\]
Thus $\angle I_1QI=90^\circ$, and hence
\[
I_1I_2\perp CI.
\tag{8}
\]
From (5) and (8), both $I_1I_2$ and $O_1O_2$ are perpendicular to $CI$. Therefore
\[
I_1I_2\parallel O_1O_2.
\]
