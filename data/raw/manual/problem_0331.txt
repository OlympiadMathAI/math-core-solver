ID: 0331

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Extremal Sets, Pigeonhole Principle, Geometric Progressions, Contradiction

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let
\[
A\subseteq\{0,1,2,\ldots,5^n\}
\]
contain $4n+2$ numbers. Prove that there exist
\[
a,b,c\in A
\]
such that
\[
a<b<c
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c+2a>3b.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Assume, for contradiction, that no such triple exists. Write the elements of $A$ in increasing order:
\[
x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_{4n+1}.
\]
For every $i=0,1,\ldots,4n-1$, the triple
\[
x_i<x_{i+1}<x_{4n+1}
\]
must violate the desired inequality. Hence
\[
x_{4n+1}+2x_i\le3x_{i+1},
\]
or equivalently,
\[
x_{4n+1}-x_i
\ge
\frac32\bigl(x_{4n+1}-x_{i+1}\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

Iterating (1) gives
\[
x_{4n+1}-x_i
\ge
\left(\frac32\right)^{4n-i}
\bigl(x_{4n+1}-x_{4n}\bigr).
\]
Taking $i=0$ and using the fact that two distinct integers differ by at least $1$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{4n+1}-x_0
&\ge
\left(\frac32\right)^{4n}
\bigl(x_{4n+1}-x_{4n}\bigr)\\
&\ge
\left(\frac{81}{16}\right)^n
>
5^n.
\end{aligned}
\]
However, both $x_0$ and $x_{4n+1}$ belong to
\[
\{0,1,\ldots,5^n\},
\]
so
\[
x_{4n+1}-x_0\le5^n,
\]
a contradiction.

Therefore the required three elements exist.

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
c=\max A.
\]
For each
\[
k=0,1,\ldots,4n-1,
\]
define
\[
A_k=
\left\{
x\in A:
\left(1-\left(\frac23\right)^k\right)c
\le x<
\left(1-\left(\frac23\right)^{k+1}\right)c
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

We claim that these $4n$ sets cover $A\setminus\{c\}$. Their intervals begin at $0$, and the last upper endpoint is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(1-\left(\frac23\right)^{4n}\right)c
&=
c-\left(\frac{16}{81}\right)^n c\\
&>
c-\left(\frac15\right)^n c\\
&\ge c-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every integer in $A$ smaller than $c$ is at most $c-1$, so it belongs to one of the sets in (1).

Now $A\setminus\{c\}$ has $4n+1$ elements distributed among only $4n$ sets. By the pigeonhole principle, some $A_k$ contains two elements
\[
a<b.
\]
Then $a<b<c$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
c+2a
&\ge
c+2\left(1-\left(\frac23\right)^k\right)c\\
&=
3\left(1-\left(\frac23\right)^{k+1}\right)c\\
&>
3b.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $a,b,c$ have the required properties.
