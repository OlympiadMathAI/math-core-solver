ID: 0104

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Reflections, Circumcircles, Arc Midpoints, Perpendicular Bisectors, Isosceles Trapezoids

STATEMENT:
Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of a triangle $ABC$. Let $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, respectively, and let $T$ be the midpoint of the arc $BC$ of $\omega$ not containing $A$.

The circumcircles of $AMT$ and $ANT$ intersect the perpendicular bisectors of $AC$ and $AB$ at points $X$ and $Y$, respectively; assume that $X$ and $Y$ lie inside triangle $ABC$. The lines $MN$ and $XY$ intersect at $K$. Prove that
\[
KA=KT.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $O$ be the center of $\omega$. Since $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $AC$, respectively, the perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $AC$ are $OM$ and $ON$. Thus
\[
O=M Y\cap N X.
\]

Let $\ell$ be the perpendicular bisector of $AT$. The point $O$ lies on $\ell$, because $A,T\in\omega$. Let $r$ denote reflection in $\ell$.

Since $T$ is the midpoint of the arc $BC$ not containing $A$, the line $AT$ bisects $\angle BAC$. It follows that the reflection of the line $AB$ in $\ell$ is parallel to $AC$. As
\[
OM\perp AB
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
ON\perp AC,
\]
the reflected line $r(OM)$ is the line through $O$ parallel to $ON$. Therefore
\[
r(OM)=ON.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $\gamma$ be the circumcircle of $AMT$. Any circle through $A$ and $T$ has its center on the perpendicular bisector of $AT$, so $\gamma$ is invariant under $r$. Hence $r(M)$ belongs both to $ON$ and to $\gamma$. It is precisely the specified interior intersection point $X$, and therefore
\[
r(M)=X.
\tag{2}
\]
Analogously,
\[
r(N)=Y.
\tag{3}
\]

Equations (2) and (3) show that reflection in $\ell$ sends the line $MN$ to the line $XY$. Their intersection $K$ is therefore fixed by this reflection, so
\[
K\in\ell.
\]
Since $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AT$, we conclude that
\[
KA=KT.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let $\gamma$ be the circumcircle of $AMT$, and let $L\ne A$ be the second intersection of $\gamma$ with the line $AC$.

The quadrilaterals $ABTC$ and $AMTL$ are cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle BTC=180^\circ-\angle BAC=\angle MTL,
\]
which gives
\[
\angle BTM=\angle CTL.
\]
Also, $BT=TC$ because $T$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$. Since $AT$ bisects $\angle BAC$,
\[
\angle MAT=\angle TAL.
\]
These are inscribed angles of the circle $AMTL$, so they subtend equal chords and
\[
MT=TL.
\]
Hence
\[
\triangle BTM\cong\triangle CTL,
\]
and so
\[
CL=BM=AM.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $X'$ be the intersection of $NX$ with the external bisector of $\angle BAC$. The point $X'$ lies outside triangle $ABC$. Since $X'$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$,
\[
X'A=X'C,
\]
and, because the internal and external angle bisectors at $A$ are perpendicular,
\[
\angle TAX'=90^\circ.
\]
Using (1), we obtain
\[
\angle X'AM
 =90^\circ+\frac{\angle BAC}{2}
 =180^\circ-\angle X'AC
 =180^\circ-\angle X'CA
 =\angle X'CL.
\]
Together with $X'A=X'C$ and $AM=CL$, this yields
\[
\triangle X'AM\cong\triangle X'CL.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle MX'L
&=\angle AX'C+\bigl(\angle CX'L-\angle AX'M\bigr)\\
&=\angle AX'C\\
&=180^\circ-2\angle X'AC\\
&=\angle BAC\\
&=\angle MAL.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $A,M,L,X'$ are concyclic, so $X'\in\gamma$.

Because $X,X',N$ are collinear and $A,M,T,X$ are concyclic,
\[
\angle TXN=\angle TXX'=\angle TAX'=90^\circ.
\]
Since $XN\perp AC$, it follows that
\[
TX\parallel AC.
\tag{2}
\]
Now
\[
\angle XTA=\angle TAC=\angle TAM.
\]
In the cyclic quadrilateral $MATX$, the equality
\[
\angle XTA=\angle TAM
\]
shows that the diagonals $XA$ and $TM$ are equal. A cyclic quadrilateral with equal diagonals is an isosceles trapezoid; here its bases are $AT$ and $MX$. Its axis of symmetry is therefore the perpendicular bisector of $AT$, and that reflection exchanges $M$ and $X$.

The same argument applied symmetrically shows that the same reflection exchanges $N$ and $Y$. Hence it sends the line $MN$ to the line $XY$. Their intersection $K$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AT$, and therefore
\[
KA=KT.
\]
