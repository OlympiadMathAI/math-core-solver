ID: 0402

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Permutations, Approximation of Square Roots, Multiset Induction, Lattice Rankings

STATEMENT:
Let $N$ be a positive integer. Prove that there exist three permutations
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_N,
\qquad
b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_N,
\qquad
c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_N
\]
of $1,2,\ldots,N$ such that
\[
\left|
\sqrt{a_k}+\sqrt{b_k}+\sqrt{c_k}-2\sqrt N
\right|
<2023
\]
for every
\[
k=1,2,\ldots,N.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For each positive integer $j$, round $\sqrt j$ to the integer $r$ determined by
\[
r^2-r+1\le j\le r^2+r.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
|\sqrt j-r|<\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]

We first treat
\[
N=m(m+1).
\]
By (1), the rounded values of
\[
\sqrt1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt N
\]
form the multiset
\[
\{1^{\times2},2^{\times4},\ldots,m^{\times2m}\}.
\]
Let
\[
T_m=\{1^{\times1},2^{\times2},\ldots,m^{\times m}\}.
\]

We claim that there are three permutations
\[
(u_k),\qquad(v_k),\qquad(w_k)
\]
of $T_m$ such that
\[
u_k+v_k+w_k=2m+1
\tag{3}
\]
for every $k$.

For $m=1$, use
\[
1+1+1=3.
\]
Assume the assertion for $T_{m-1}$. The multiset identities
\[
T_m=T_{m-1}\sqcup\{m^{\times m}\}
\]
and
\[
T_m=(T_{m-1}+1)\sqcup\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
\tag{4}
\]
give the following construction. On the old columns, use
\[
u_k,\qquad v_k+1,\qquad w_k+1.
\]
On the $m$ new columns indexed by $r=1,\ldots,m$, use
\[
m,\qquad r,\qquad m+1-r.
\]
Each row is a permutation of $T_m$ by (4), and every column sum is $2m+1$. This proves the claim.

Use two copies of these three arrangements to arrange the full rounded multiset. In each row, replace the occurrences of every rounded value $r$ by the distinct integers $j$ satisfying (1), in arbitrary order. This gives three permutations of $1,\ldots,N$. By (2)-(3),
\[
2m+1-\frac32
<
\sqrt{a_k}+\sqrt{b_k}+\sqrt{c_k}
<
2m+1+\frac32.
\tag{5}
\]

We now pass to arbitrary $N$. If $N\le5$, take all three permutations to be
\[
1,2,\ldots,N.
\]
Then the required absolute value is certainly smaller than $5\sqrt5<2023$. Hence assume $N\ge6$. Choose $m\ge2$ such that
\[
m(m+1)\le N<(m+1)(m+2),
\]
and put
\[
M=m(m+1),
\qquad
t=N-M,
\qquad
L=\left\lfloor\frac{4N}{9}\right\rfloor.
\]
For $m\ge2$,
\[
4N<4(m+1)(m+2)<9m(m+1)=9M,
\]
and hence
\[
L<M.
\]

Start with the three permutations of $1,\ldots,M$ obtained above. In each permutation, replace every entry $x$ by
\[
\phi(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\le L,\\
x+t,&x>L,
\end{cases}
\]
and append
\[
L+1,L+2,\ldots,L+t.
\]
Each resulting row is a permutation of $1,\ldots,N$.

For an old entry,
\[
0\le\sqrt{\phi(x)}-\sqrt x
\le
\frac{t}{2\sqrt{L+1}}
<
2,
\tag{6}
\]
because
\[
t\le2m+1,\qquad L+1>\frac{4N}{9},\qquad\sqrt N>m.
\]
Combining (5) and (6), the square-root sum in every old column lies between
\[
2m-\frac12
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2m+\frac{17}{2}.
\]
Since
\[
m<\sqrt N<m+2,
\]
its distance from $2\sqrt N$ is smaller than $17/2$.

In a new column, all three entries equal $L+r$ for some $1\le r\le t$. Its square-root sum is
\[
3\sqrt{L+r}.
\]
We have
\[
3\sqrt{L+r}>3\sqrt{L+1}>2\sqrt N,
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
3\sqrt{L+r}
&\le3\sqrt{L+t}\\
&\le\sqrt{4N+9t}\\
&<
2\sqrt N+\frac{17}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the last inequality, note that
\[
\sqrt{4N+9t}-2\sqrt N
=
\frac{9t}{\sqrt{4N+9t}+2\sqrt N}
<
\frac{9(2m+1)}{4m}
<
\frac{17}{2}.
\]

Thus in every column
\[
\left|
\sqrt{a_k}+\sqrt{b_k}+\sqrt{c_k}-2\sqrt N
\right|
<
\frac{17}{2}
<2023.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Again round $\sqrt j$ according to
\[
r^2-r+1\le j\le r^2+r,
\]
so that
\[
|\sqrt j-r|<\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]

For integers
\[
m\ge1,
\qquad
0\le n\le m+1,
\]
define the multiset
\[
T_{m,n}
=
\{1^{\times1},2^{\times2},\ldots,m^{\times m},(m+1)^{\times n}\}.
\]

We need the following lemma.

\textbf{Lemma.}
There are three permutations $(u_k),(v_k),(w_k)$ of $T_{m,n}$ such that every column sum belongs to
\[
\begin{cases}
\{2m+1\},&n=0,\\
\{2m+1,2m+2,2m+3\},&1\le n\le m,\\
\{2m+3\},&n=m+1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
First prove the case $n=0$ by induction on $m$. For $m=1$, use $1+1+1=3$. Given three arrangements of
\[
T_{m-1,0},
\]
place on the old columns
\[
u_k,\qquad v_k+1,\qquad w_k+1,
\]
and on the $m$ new columns place
\[
m,\qquad r,\qquad m+1-r
\qquad(r=1,\ldots,m).
\]
All column sums are $2m+1$, and each row is a permutation of $T_{m,0}$.

The case $n=m+1$ is the already proved case $T_{m+1,0}$.

For the remaining case $1\le n\le m$, the base case $m=1,n=1$ is given by the three rows
\[
(1,2),\qquad(1,2),\qquad(2,1),
\]
whose column sums are $4$ and $5$. We now use induction on $m$. We have
\[
T_{m,n}
=
T_{m-1,n}
\sqcup
\{m^{\times(m-n)},(m+1)^{\times n}\}
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
T_{m,n}
=
(T_{m-1,n}+1)\sqcup\{1,2,\ldots,m\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Take three arrangements of $T_{m-1,n}$ supplied by induction. On their old columns use
\[
u_k,\qquad v_k+1,\qquad w_k+1.
\]
On the $m$ new columns indexed by $r=1,\ldots,m$, use in the first row
\[
\underbrace{m,\ldots,m}_{m-n\text{ times}},
\underbrace{m+1,\ldots,m+1}_{n\text{ times}},
\]
and in the second and third rows use
\[
r,\qquad m+1-r.
\]
Equations (3)-(4) show that all three rows are permutations of $T_{m,n}$. The old column sums increase by $2$, while every new column has sum $2m+1$ or $2m+2$. Thus all sums lie in the set stated in (2). $\square$

For $N=1$, take the three one-term permutations $(1)$. Now assume $N\ge2$ and choose $m\ge1$ such that
\[
m(m+1)\le N<(m+1)(m+2).
\]
and write
\[
N=m(m+1)+t,
\qquad
0\le t\le2m+1.
\]
The rounded values of
\[
\sqrt1,\sqrt2,\ldots,\sqrt N
\]
form the multiset
\[
\{1^{\times2},2^{\times4},\ldots,m^{\times2m},(m+1)^{\times t}\}.
\]
Put
\[
n_1=\left\lfloor\frac t2\right\rfloor,
\qquad
n_2=\left\lceil\frac t2\right\rceil.
\]
Then this multiset is
\[
T_{m,n_1}\sqcup T_{m,n_2}.
\]

Apply the lemma to both parts and concatenate the corresponding rows. Every rounded column sum lies between
\[
2m+1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2m+3.
\tag{5}
\]
Replace rounded labels by the actual integers $1,\ldots,N$ in each row. The rows become permutations, and (1) changes each column sum by less than $3/2$. Therefore
\[
2m-\frac12
<
\sqrt{a_k}+\sqrt{b_k}+\sqrt{c_k}
<
2m+\frac92.
\]
Since
\[
m<\sqrt N<m+2,
\]
we obtain
\[
\left|
\sqrt{a_k}+\sqrt{b_k}+\sqrt{c_k}-2\sqrt N
\right|
<
\frac92
<2023.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
We give a lattice-ranking construction.

Choose the positive integer $m$ such that
\[
\frac{m(m-1)}2+1
\le
N
\le
\frac{m(m+1)}2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
X_m=
\left\{
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3:
x+y+z=m-1
\right\}.
\]
This set has
\[
\frac{m(m+1)}2
\]
elements. Delete
\[
\frac{m(m+1)}2-N
\]
arbitrary elements having $x=0$, and call the remaining $N$-element set $X$.

Order $X$ in three ways:

\begin{itemize}
\item decreasing lexicographic order of $(x,y,z)$;
\item decreasing lexicographic order of $(y,z,x)$;
\item decreasing lexicographic order of $(z,x,y)$.
\end{itemize}

For $P\in X$, let
\[
W_A(P),\qquad W_B(P),\qquad W_C(P)
\]
be its ranks in these three orders. Each of the three rank functions is a bijection from $X$ to
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}.
\]

For
\[
P=(x,y,z),
\]
put
\[
\ell_A=m-x,\qquad
\ell_B=m-y,\qquad
\ell_C=m-z.
\]
Then
\[
\ell_A+\ell_B+\ell_C=2m+1.
\tag{2}
\]

No element was deleted from any row preceding the $\ell_A$th row in the first ordering. Hence
\[
\frac{\ell_A(\ell_A-1)}2+1
\le
W_A(P)
\le
\frac{\ell_A(\ell_A+1)}2.
\tag{3}
\]
The deleted points have $x=0$ and therefore have pairwise distinct $y$-coordinates and pairwise distinct $z$-coordinates. Thus at most one deleted point came from each row of either of the other two orderings. Consequently,
\[
\frac{(\ell_B-1)(\ell_B-2)}2+1
\le
W_B(P)
\le
\frac{\ell_B(\ell_B+1)}2,
\tag{4}
\]
and the analogous bounds hold for $W_C(P)$.

Equations (3)-(4) imply
\[
\ell_A-\frac12
<
\sqrt{2W_A(P)}
<
\ell_A+\frac12,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\ell_B-\frac32
<
\sqrt{2W_B(P)}
<
\ell_B+\frac12,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\ell_C-\frac32
<
\sqrt{2W_C(P)}
<
\ell_C+\frac12.
\tag{7}
\]
Adding and using (2),
\[
2m-\frac52
<
\sqrt{2W_A(P)}
+
\sqrt{2W_B(P)}
+
\sqrt{2W_C(P)}
<
2m+\frac52.
\tag{8}
\]

From (1),
\[
2m-1<2\sqrt{2N}<2m+1.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)-(9),
\[
\left|
\sqrt{2W_A(P)}
+
\sqrt{2W_B(P)}
+
\sqrt{2W_C(P)}
-
2\sqrt{2N}
\right|
<
\frac72.
\]
After division by $\sqrt2$,
\[
\left|
\sqrt{W_A(P)}
+
\sqrt{W_B(P)}
+
\sqrt{W_C(P)}
-
2\sqrt N
\right|
<
\frac{7\sqrt2}{4}
<
2023.
\]

Enumerate the points of $X$ as
\[
P_1,P_2,\ldots,P_N
\]
and set
\[
a_k=W_A(P_k),\qquad
b_k=W_B(P_k),\qquad
c_k=W_C(P_k).
\]
Each row is a permutation of $1,\ldots,N$, and the required inequality holds for every $k$.
