ID: 0386

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Orthocentre, Nine-Point Circle, Pole and Polar, Brocard's Theorem, Coaxial Circles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ be two triangles with the same circumcircle $\omega$ and the same orthocentre $H$.

The three lines
\[
AA',\qquad BB',\qquad CC'
\]
form a nondegenerate triangle. Let $\Omega$ be its circumcircle.

Prove that
\[
H,
\]
the centre of $\omega$, and the centre of $\Omega$ are collinear.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $O$ and $R$ be the centre and radius of $\omega$.

In every triangle, the homothety of ratio $-1/2$ centred at the centroid sends the orthocentre to the circumcentre and the vertices to the midpoints of the opposite sides. Since the two triangles have the same $O$ and $H$, they have the same centroid $G$. Their six side-midpoints therefore lie on one common nine-point circle $\rho$, whose centre lies on line $OH$.

We prove that
\[
\omega,\qquad\Omega,\qquad\rho
\]
are coaxial.

Let
\[
D=BB'\cap CC',\qquad
E=CC'\cap AA',\qquad
F=AA'\cap BB'.
\]
Thus
\[
\Omega=(DEF).
\]
Also define
\[
S=BC'\cap B'C,
\qquad
T=BC\cap B'C'.
\]

The points $D,S,T$ are the three diagonal points of the complete quadrilateral formed by the four chords
\[
BB',\quad CC',\quad BC',\quad B'C
\]
of $\omega$. By Brocard's theorem, triangle $DST$ is self-polar with respect to $\omega$.

Because $D$ is the pole of $ST$, the inverse $D^*$ of $D$ with respect to $\omega$ is the perpendicular projection of $D$ onto $ST$. Let $N$ be the midpoint of $SD$. Since $D^*$ lies on the circle with diameter $SD$,
\[
ON^2-ND^2=OD\cdot OD^*=R^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Pow}_{\omega}(N)=ON^2-R^2=ND^2.
\tag{1}
\]

We next show that $SD$ is tangent to $\Omega$ at $D$.

Since $T$ is the pole of $SD$,
\[
OT\perp SD.
\tag{2}
\]
Let $M$ and $M'$ be the midpoints of $BC$ and $B'C'$, respectively. Then
\[
OM\perp BC,\qquad OM'\perp B'C',
\]
so the four points
\[
O,M,M',T
\]
are concyclic. Consequently,
\[
\measuredangle(SD,BC)
=
\measuredangle(OT,OM)
=
\measuredangle(B'C',MM').
\tag{3}
\]

The cyclicity of $B,B',C,C'$ gives
\[
\measuredangle(BC,BB')
=
\measuredangle(CC',B'C').
\tag{4}
\]
Adding (3)--(4),
\[
\measuredangle(SD,BB')
=
\measuredangle(CC',MM').
\tag{5}
\]

Because the two triangles have the same centroid,
\[
MM'\parallel AA'.
\tag{6}
\]
Now
\[
DF=BB',\qquad DE=CC',\qquad EF=AA',
\]
so equations (5)--(6) become
\[
\measuredangle(SD,DF)=\measuredangle(DE,EF).
\]
By the tangent--chord theorem, $SD$ is tangent to $\Omega$ at $D$. Since $N\in SD$,
\[
\operatorname{Pow}_{\Omega}(N)=ND^2.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to compute the power of $N$ with respect to $\rho$. The cyclic quadrilateral $BB'CC'$ makes triangles $DBC$ and $DC'B'$ inversely similar, from which
\[
\measuredangle(BB',DM')
=
\measuredangle(DM,CC').
\tag{8}
\]
Using (5), (8), and (6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(SD,DM')
&=
\measuredangle(SD,BB')
+
\measuredangle(BB',DM')\\
&=
\measuredangle(CC',MM')
+
\measuredangle(DM,CC')\\
&=
\measuredangle(DM,MM').
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the circle $(DMM')$ is tangent to $SD$ at $D$.

The points $N,M,M'$ are collinear on the Newton--Gauss line of the same complete quadrilateral. Taking powers from $N$ to $(DMM')$ gives
\[
ND^2=NM\cdot NM'.
\tag{9}
\]
Since $M,M'\in\rho$, the right-hand side of (9) is
\[
\operatorname{Pow}_{\rho}(N).
\]
Combining (1), (7), and (9), the point $N$ has the same power with respect to
\[
\omega,\qquad\Omega,\qquad\rho.
\tag{10}
\]

Repeating the same construction cyclically at $E$ and $F$ gives two further points, lying respectively on the tangents to $\Omega$ at $E$ and $F$, that satisfy (10). The three tangents to a nondegenerate circle at three distinct points cannot be concurrent. Hence at least two of these equal-power points are distinct.

Therefore the pairwise radical axes of $\omega,\Omega,\rho$ contain the same two distinct points, so the three circles are coaxial. Their centres are consequently collinear. Since the centres of $\omega$ and $\rho$ lie on $OH$, the centre of $\Omega$ also lies on $OH$.

Thus $H$, $O$, and the centre of $\Omega$ are collinear.
