ID: 0415

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Homothety, Menelaus' Theorem, Spiral Similarity, Inversion, Harmonic Cross Ratios

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with circumcircle $\omega$. A circle $\Gamma$ is internally tangent to $\omega$ at $A$ and tangent to $BC$ at $D$.

The lines $AB$ and $AC$ meet $\Gamma$ again at $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Choose $M,N$ on line $BC$ so that $B$ is the midpoint of $DM$ and $C$ is the midpoint of $DN$.

The lines $MP$ and $NQ$ meet at $K$ and meet $\Gamma$ again at $I$ and $J$, respectively. The ray $KA$ meets the circumcircle of triangle $IJK$ again at $X\ne K$.

Prove that
\[
\angle BXP=\angle CXQ.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
K_1=MP\cap AD,
\qquad
K_2=NQ\cap AD.
\]
Applying Menelaus' theorem to triangle $ABD$ and the transversal $M,P,K_1$ gives
\[
\frac{AK_1}{K_1D}
=
\frac{AP}{PB}\cdot\frac{BM}{MD}
=
\frac{AP}{2PB},
\tag{1}
\]
because $B$ is the midpoint of $DM$. Similarly,
\[
\frac{AK_2}{K_2D}
=
\frac{AQ}{2QC}.
\tag{2}
\]

The homothety centred at $A$ which sends $\Gamma$ to $\omega$ sends
\[
P\longmapsto B,
\qquad
Q\longmapsto C.
\]
It also sends $D$ to the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$: the tangent at the image of $D$ is parallel to the tangent $BC$ at $D$. Consequently,
\[
PQ\parallel BC
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
AD
\]
is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$.

If the homothety ratio is $\lambda$, then
\[
AB=\lambda AP,
\qquad
AC=\lambda AQ.
\]
Hence
\[
PB=(\lambda-1)AP,
\qquad
QC=(\lambda-1)AQ,
\]
so
\[
\frac{AP}{PB}=\frac{AQ}{QC}.
\]
Equations (1)-(2) yield
\[
K_1=K_2.
\]
Therefore
\[
K\in AD.
\tag{4}
\]

Since $K,X,A,D$ are collinear and $I,J,K,X$ are cyclic,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle JXD
&=\angle JXK\\
&=\angle JIK\\
&=\angle JIP\\
&=\angle JQP\\
&=\angle JND.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Here $I,K,P$ are collinear, $J,K,Q,N$ are collinear, the points $P,Q,I,J$ lie on $\Gamma$, and the last equality uses (3).

Equation (5) gives
\[
J,X,N,D
\]
cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle DXN
=
\angle DJN
=
\angle DJQ
=
\angle DAQ
=
\angle DAC.
\]
Thus
\[
XN\parallel AC.
\tag{6}
\]
In triangle $DXN$, the point $C$ is the midpoint of $DN$. The line through $C$ parallel to $XN$ meets $DX$ at $A$, so the midpoint theorem and (6) show that
\[
A
\]
is the midpoint of $DX$. In particular,
\[
XA=AD.
\tag{7}
\]

The homothety above makes triangles $APQ$ and $ABC$ similar. Hence
\[
\angle AQP=\angle ACB.
\]
Because $A,P,Q,D$ lie on $\Gamma$,
\[
\angle ADP=\angle AQP=\angle ACB=\angle ACD.
\]
Together with
\[
\angle PAD=\angle DAC,
\]
this gives
\[
\triangle APD\sim\triangle ADC.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{CD}{DP}
=
\frac{AD}{AP}
=
\frac{XA}{AP},
\tag{8}
\]
where (7) was used.

Since $BC$ is tangent to $\Gamma$ at $D$, the tangent-chord theorem gives
\[
\angle PDB=\angle PAD.
\]
The rays $DB$ and $DC$ are opposite, as are $AD$ and $AX$. Thus
\[
\angle CDP
=
180^\circ-\angle PDB
=
180^\circ-\angle PAD
=
\angle XAP.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)-(9) imply
\[
\triangle PDC\sim\triangle PAX.
\]
Hence $P$ is the centre of a spiral similarity sending
\[
CD\longmapsto XA.
\]
A standard consequence is
\[
\triangle PXC\sim\triangle PAD,
\]
and therefore
\[
\angle PXC=\angle PAD=\frac12\angle BAC.
\tag{10}
\]

It follows that
\[
\angle BXP
=
\angle BXC-\frac12\angle BAC.
\tag{11}
\]
The whole construction and the argument are symmetric under
\[
(B,P,M,I)\longleftrightarrow(C,Q,N,J),
\]
while $A,D,K,X$ remain fixed. Thus the same reasoning gives
\[
\angle CXQ
=
\angle BXC-\frac12\angle BAC.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (11)-(12) prove the result.

SOLUTION 2:
The homothety centred at $A$ taking $\Gamma$ to $\omega$ gives
\[
PQ\parallel BC
\]
and shows that $AD$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BAC$. Applying Menelaus' theorem exactly as follows,
\[
\frac{A(MP\cap AD)}{(MP\cap AD)D}
=
\frac{AP}{2PB},
\qquad
\frac{A(NQ\cap AD)}{(NQ\cap AD)D}
=
\frac{AQ}{2QC},
\]
and using
\[
\frac{AP}{PB}=\frac{AQ}{QC},
\]
we obtain
\[
K\in AD.
\tag{1}
\]

Put
\[
A^*=PQ\cap AD.
\]
Because $C$ is the midpoint of $DN$, the quadruple on line $BC$ is harmonic:
\[
(C,\infty_{BC};N,D)=-1.
\]
Projecting from $Q$ first to line $AD$ and then to the circle $\Gamma$ gives
\[
-1
=
(A,A^*;K,D)
=
(A,P;J,D).
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed,
\[
C\mapsto A,\quad
\infty_{BC}\mapsto A^*,\quad
N\mapsto K,\quad
D\mapsto D,
\]
and then
\[
A\mapsto A,\quad
A^*\mapsto P,\quad
K\mapsto J,\quad
D\mapsto D.
\]
Similarly, using that $B$ is the midpoint of $DM$ and projecting from $P$,
\[
(A,Q;I,D)=-1.
\tag{3}
\]

The tangent $BC$ at $D$ is parallel to chord $PQ$, so $D$ is the midpoint of an arc $PQ$ of $\Gamma$. Hence
\[
DP=DQ.
\tag{4}
\]
Invert about $D$ with radius $DP$, and write $U^*$ for the inverse of $U$. The points $P$ and $Q$ are fixed. The circle $\Gamma=(DAPQ)$ and line $PQ$ are interchanged, so the inverse of $A$ is precisely the point $A^*$ defined above.

Apply inversion to the harmonic relations (2)-(3). Since $D$ goes to the point at infinity,
\[
I^*,\ J^*,\ K^*
\]
are respectively the midpoints of
\[
A^*Q,\qquad A^*P,\qquad A^*A.
\tag{5}
\]

The circle $(IJKX)$ inverts to the circle $(I^*J^*K^*X^*)$. The homothety of ratio $2$ centred at $A^*$ sends
\[
I^*\longmapsto Q,\qquad
J^*\longmapsto P,\qquad
K^*\longmapsto A.
\]
It therefore sends the circle $(I^*J^*K^*)$ to $\Gamma$. Its two intersections with $AD$ correspond to the two intersections $A,D$ of $\Gamma$ with $AD$. Since $K^*$ maps to $A$, the other intersection $X^*$ maps to $D$. Thus
\[
X^*\ \text{is the midpoint of }A^*D.
\tag{6}
\]

Standard angle relations under inversion, together with the tangent-chord theorem, give
\[
\angle PDB
=
\angle BAD
=
\angle DAC
=
\angle DC^*A^*.
\]
Hence
\[
DP\parallel C^*A^*.
\]
Also,
\[
A^*P\subset PQ\parallel BC=DC^*.
\]
Therefore
\[
PA^*C^*D
\]
is a parallelogram. Similarly,
\[
QA^*B^*D
\]
is a parallelogram.

By (6), $X^*$ is the midpoint of the diagonal $A^*D$ of each parallelogram. Consequently,
\[
B^*,X^*,Q\ \text{are collinear},
\qquad
C^*,X^*,P\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{7}
\]

We now compare the desired angles. The standard formula for angles under inversion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BXP-\angle CXQ
&=
(\angle BXD-\angle PXD)
-
(\angle DXC-\angle DXQ)\\
&=
(\angle DB^*X^*-\angle DPX^*)
-
(\angle X^*C^*D-\angle X^*QD).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Because $DP=DQ$, triangle $DPQ$ is isosceles. Using this and (7), expression (8) becomes
\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\bigl(\angle DB^*X^*+\angle X^*PQ\bigr)
-
\bigl(\angle X^*C^*D+\angle PQX^*\bigr)\\
&=
\bigl(\angle DB^*Q-\angle PQB^*\bigr)
-
\bigl(\angle PC^*D-\angle C^*PQ\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Now
\[
DB^*\parallel PQ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
DC^*\parallel PQ,
\]
because $B^*,C^*,D$ lie on $BC$. Both parentheses are therefore $0$. Hence
\[
\angle BXP-\angle CXQ=0,
\]
which is the required equality.
