ID: 0194

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Isosceles Triangles, Incenter and Excenter Geometry, Reflections, Cyclic Quadrilaterals, Similar Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with
\[
AB=AC\ne BC,
\]
and let $I$ be its incentre. The line $BI$ meets $AC$ at $D$, and the line through $D$ perpendicular to $AC$ meets $AI$ at $E$. Prove that the reflection $I'$ of $I$ in $AC$ lies on the circumcircle of triangle $BDE$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\Gamma$ be the circle with centre $E$ through $B$ and $C$. Since $AB=AC$, the line $AI$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, so indeed
\[
EB=EC.
\]

Let $F$ be the reflection of $C$ in $D$. Because
\[
ED\perp AC
\]
and $D$ is the midpoint of $CF$, we have
\[
EF=EC,
\]
so $F\in\Gamma$.

Now
\[
\angle DCI=\angle ICB=\angle CBI,
\]
so $DC$ is tangent to the circumcircle of $\triangle IBC$ at $C$. Let $J$ be the reflection of $I$ in $D$. In directed lengths,
\[
DC\cdot DF=-DC^2=-DI\cdot DB=DJ\cdot DB.
\]
Since $B,C,F\in\Gamma$, this equality of powers shows that
\[
J\in\Gamma.
\]

The diagonals $IJ$ and $CF$ bisect one another at $D$, so $CJFI$ is a parallelogram. Reflecting $I$ in $AC$ gives $I'$, and
\[
\angle FI'C=\angle CIF=\angle FJC.
\]
Thus $I'\in\Gamma$, and hence
\[
EI'=EB.
\tag{1}
\]

The line $AC$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle BDI'$, because $I'$ is the reflection of $I$ in $AC$. Since $DE\perp AC$, the line $DE$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle BDI'$. Together with (1), the standard external-angle-bisector criterion for a cyclic quadrilateral gives
\[
B,D,E,I'\ \text{concyclic}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
S=I'C\cap AI
\]
and put
\[
\theta=\angle ACI=\angle ICB=\angle CBI.
\]
All angles below are directed modulo $\pi$.

We have
\[
\angle I'SE
=\angle I'CA+\angle CAI
=\theta+\left(\frac\pi2+2\theta\right)
=3\theta+\frac\pi2.
\]
Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle I'DE
&=\angle I'DC+\frac\pi2\\
&=\angle CDI+\frac\pi2\\
&=\angle DCB+\angle CBD+\frac\pi2\\
&=3\theta+\frac\pi2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
I',D,E,S
\]
are concyclic.

Furthermore,
\[
\angle I'SB=2\angle I'SE=6\theta,
\]
while
\[
\angle I'DB=2\angle CDI=6\theta.
\]
Therefore
\[
I',D,B,S
\]
are concyclic as well. The two circles share the three points $I',D,S$, so they coincide, and consequently
\[
B,D,E,I'
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $D'$ be the second intersection of $AI$ with the circumcircle of $\triangle ABD$. Since $AD'$ bisects $\angle BAD$, the point $D'$ is the midpoint of the arc $BD$ not containing $A$. Thus
\[
DD'=BD'=CD'.
\tag{1}
\]
The points $A,E,D'$ lie on $AI$ in this order.

We have
\[
\angle ED'D=\angle AD'D=\angle ABD=\angle IBC=\angle ICB.
\]
Since $D'$ is the circumcentre of $\triangle BCD$ by (1),
\[
\angle EDD'
=90^\circ-\angle D'DC
=\angle CBD
=\angle IBC.
\]
Hence
\[
\triangle ED'D\sim\triangle ICB.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{BC}{CI'}
=\frac{BC}{CI}
=\frac{DD'}{D'E}
=\frac{BD'}{D'E}.
\tag{2}
\]

Also,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle BCI'
&=\angle BCA+\angle ACI'\\
&=\angle BCA+\angle ICA\\
&=\angle BCA+\angle DBC\\
&=\angle BDA\\
&=\angle BD'E.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with (2), this proves
\[
\triangle BCI'\sim\triangle BD'E.
\]
It follows that
\[
\triangle BCD'\sim\triangle BI'E.
\]
Since $BCD'$ is isosceles,
\[
BE=EI'.
\tag{3}
\]

As in Solution 1, $DE$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle BDI'$. The equality (3) therefore implies that
\[
B,D,E,I'
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 4:
Let $AI$ and $BI$ meet the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ again at $A'$ and $B'$, respectively. Let $E'$ be the reflection of $E$ in $AC$.

A directed-angle calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle B'AE'
&=\angle B'AD-\angle E'AD\\
&=\frac12\angle ABC-\frac12\angle BAC\\
&=90^\circ-\angle BAC-\frac12\angle ABC\\
&=90^\circ-\angle B'DA\\
&=\angle B'DE'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $B',A,D,E'$ are concyclic.

Then
\[
\angle DB'E'
=\angle DAE'
=\frac12\angle BAC
=\angle BAA'
=\angle DB'A',
\]
so
\[
B',E',A'\ \text{are collinear}.
\]

It is a standard fact that $A'B'$ is the perpendicular bisector of $CI$. Therefore
\[
CE'=IE'.
\]
Reflecting in $AC$ gives
\[
BE=CE=I'E.
\]
Since $DE$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle BDI'$, it follows that
\[
B,D,E,I'
\]
are concyclic.

SOLUTION 5:
Let
\[
F=CI\cap AB,
\]
and let $O_1$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle BIF$. The points $F$ and $D$ are symmetric with respect to $AI$. Let $I'$ be the reflection of $I$ in $AC$.

From
\[
\angle BFO_1=90^\circ-\angle FIB
=\frac12\angle BAC
=\angle BAI,
\]
we obtain
\[
EI\parallel FO_1.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
\angle O_1IB
=90^\circ-\angle BFI
=90^\circ-\angle CDI
=\angle I'ID,
\]
so
\[
O_1,I,I'\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{2}
\]

Both $ED$ and $O_1I$ are perpendicular to $AC$, hence
\[
ED\parallel O_1I.
\]
Using (1),
\[
\angle FEI=\angle DEI=\angle O_1IE,
\]
so $EFO_1I$ is an isosceles trapezoid. Therefore
\[
\angle DIE=\angle FIE=\angle O_1EI,
\]
and consequently
\[
EO_1\parallel ID.
\]
Thus $DEO_1I$ is a parallelogram.

Hence
\[
DI'=DI=EO_1,
\]
so $DEO_1I'$ is an isosceles trapezoid and therefore cyclic. Moreover,
\[
ED=O_1I=O_1B
\]
and $EO_1\parallel BD$, so $EO_1BD$ is another isosceles trapezoid and is cyclic. These two cyclic quadrilaterals share $D,E,O_1$, so
\[
B,D,E,I'
\]
lie on one circle.

SOLUTION 6:
Let $T$ and $M$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from $I$ to $AB$ and $BC$, respectively. Since $BI$ is the perpendicular bisector of $TM$,
\[
DT=DM.
\tag{1}
\]

Because
\[
\angle ADE=\angle ATI=90^\circ
\]
and
\[
\angle DAE=\angle TAI,
\]
we have
\[
\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ATI.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{AD}{AE}=\frac{AT}{AI}=\frac{AT}{AI'}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\angle DAT=2\angle DAE=\angle EAI',
\]
so
\[
\triangle DAT\sim\triangle EAI'.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{DT}{EI'}=\frac{AT}{AI}.
\tag{2}
\]

The right triangles $AMB$ and $ATI$ are similar, hence
\[
\frac{AM}{AB}=\frac{AT}{AI}.
\tag{3}
\]
Also,
\[
\triangle AMB\sim\triangle ATI\sim\triangle ADE,
\]
so
\[
\frac{AM}{AB}=\frac{AD}{AE}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\triangle ADM\sim\triangle AEB,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{DM}{EB}=\frac{AM}{AB}.
\tag{4}
\]

Combining (1)--(4),
\[
EB=EI'.
\]
As $DE$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle BDI'$, the points
\[
B,D,E,I'
\]
are concyclic.
