ID: 0107

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2013

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Parallelograms, Directed Angles, Vectors, Complex Numbers, Concurrency

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCDEF$ be a convex hexagon such that
\[
AB=DE,
\qquad
BC=EF,
\qquad
CD=FA,
\]
and
\[
\angle A-\angle D
=
\angle C-\angle F
=
\angle E-\angle B.
\]
Prove that the diagonals $AD,BE,CF$ are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Put
\[
\theta=
\angle A-\angle D
=
\angle C-\angle F
=
\angle E-\angle B.
\]
After reversing the order of the vertices if necessary, assume
\[
\theta\ge0.
\]
Also set
\[
x=AB=DE,
\qquad
y=CD=FA,
\qquad
z=EF=BC.
\]

Choose points $P,Q,R$ so that $CDEP$, $EFAQ$, and $ABCR$ are parallelograms. We first compute
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle PEQ
&=\angle FEQ+\angle DEP-\angle E\\
&=(180^\circ-\angle F)+(180^\circ-\angle D)-\angle E\\
&=360^\circ-\angle D-\angle E-\angle F.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the interior angles of a hexagon sum to $720^\circ$,
\[
360^\circ-\angle D-\angle E-\angle F
=
\frac12\bigl(
\angle A+\angle B+\angle C
-
\angle D-\angle E-\angle F
\bigr)
=\frac\theta2.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle PEQ=\frac\theta2.
\]
Cyclically,
\[
\angle QAR=\angle RCP=\frac\theta2.
\tag{1}
\]

If $\theta=0$, then triangle $RCP$ is isosceles with zero apex angle, so $R=P$. From the defining parallelograms,
\[
AB\parallel RC,
\qquad
PC\parallel DE.
\]
Since $R=P$, it follows that $AB\parallel DE$; together with $AB=DE$, this makes $ABDE$ a parallelogram. Similarly, $BCEF$ and $CDFA$ are parallelograms. Their diagonals $AD,BE,CF$ pass through the same midpoint, so the result follows.

Assume now that $\theta>0$. The triangles
\[
PEQ,
\qquad QAR,
\qquad RCP
\]
are isosceles, have the same apex angle $\theta/2$, and have equal-side lengths $y,z,x$, respectively. Therefore they are similar in the ratio
\[
y:z:x.
\]
In particular, the three side lengths of triangle $PQR$ are proportional to
\[
y,z,x.
\tag{2}
\]

Next,
\[
\frac{RQ}{QP}=\frac zy=\frac{RA}{AF}.
\tag{3}
\]
Using directed angles between rays, the equal base angles of the three isosceles triangles and the parallelogram relations give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(RQ,QP)
&=\measuredangle(RQ,QE)+\measuredangle(QE,QP)\\
&=\measuredangle(RQ,QE)+\measuredangle(RA,RQ)\\
&=\measuredangle(RA,QE)\\
&=\measuredangle(RA,AF),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
because $QE\parallel AF$. From (3) and (4),
\[
\triangle PQR\sim\triangle FAR.
\]
Now $FA=y$ and $AR=z$, while (2) says that the side $PR$ corresponding to $FR$ is proportional to $x$. The similarity therefore yields
\[
FR=x.
\]
By the same cyclic argument,
\[
FP=x.
\]
Since the parallelograms give
\[
CR=CP=x,
\]
the quadrilateral $CRFP$ is a rhombus.

Thus $CF$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PR$. Cyclically, $BE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$, and $AD$ is the perpendicular bisector of $QR$. Hence the three diagonals meet at the circumcenter of triangle $PQR$.

SOLUTION 2:
Let
\[
\theta=
\angle A-\angle D
=
\angle C-\angle F
=
\angle E-\angle B,
\]
and, after reversing the order of the vertices if necessary, assume $\theta\ge0$. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=CD\cap EF,&
Y&=EF\cap AB,&
Z&=AB\cap CD,\\
X'&=FA\cap BC,&
Y'&=BC\cap DE,&
Z'&=DE\cap FA.
\end{aligned}
\]
All intersections are taken on the corresponding extended side-lines.

From the angle conditions,
\[
\angle B+
\angle C
=
\angle E+
\angle F,
\]
and similarly for the other cyclic pairs. Hence
\[
\angle YZX
=
\angle B+
\angle C-180^\circ
=
\angle E+
\angle F-180^\circ
=
\angle Y'Z'X'.
\]
The other two angles are equal in the same way, so
\[
\triangle XYZ\sim\triangle X'Y'Z'.
\tag{1}
\]

Let $\mathcal R$ be the rotation that sends the directions of the sides of $XYZ$ to the corresponding directions of $X'Y'Z'$. The orientation in the convex configuration and the equalities of opposite side lengths give
\[
\mathcal R(\overrightarrow{AB})=\overrightarrow{DE},
\qquad
\mathcal R(\overrightarrow{CD})=\overrightarrow{FA},
\qquad
\mathcal R(\overrightarrow{EF})=\overrightarrow{BC}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since the six side vectors of the hexagon sum to zero, (2) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\vec0
&=
\overrightarrow{AB}+
\overrightarrow{BC}+
\overrightarrow{CD}+
\overrightarrow{DE}+
\overrightarrow{EF}+
\overrightarrow{FA}\\
&=
\bigl(
\overrightarrow{AB}+
\overrightarrow{CD}+
\overrightarrow{EF}
\bigr)
+
\mathcal R\bigl(
\overrightarrow{AB}+
\overrightarrow{CD}+
\overrightarrow{EF}
\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

If $\mathcal R$ is a rotation through $180^\circ$, then each pair of opposite sides of the hexagon is equal and oppositely parallel. The hexagon is centrally symmetric, and $AD,BE,CF$ pass through its center.

Otherwise, a nonzero vector cannot be sent by $\mathcal R$ to its negative. Equation (3) therefore implies
\[
\overrightarrow{AB}+
\overrightarrow{CD}+
\overrightarrow{EF}=
\vec0.
\tag{4}
\]

Using (4), construct a triangle $LMN$ such that
\[
\overrightarrow{LM}=
\overrightarrow{EF},
\qquad
\overrightarrow{MN}=
\overrightarrow{AB},
\qquad
\overrightarrow{NL}=
\overrightarrow{CD}.
\]
Let $O$ be its circumcenter. Choose $O_1,O_2,O_3$ so that
\[
\triangle AO_1B,
\qquad
\triangle CO_2D,
\qquad
\triangle EO_3F
\]
are translations of
\[
\triangle MON,
\qquad
\triangle NOL,
\qquad
\triangle LOM,
\]
respectively, with the displayed vertex correspondences.

The vectors $FO_3$ and $AO_1$ are both translates of $MO$, so $AFO_3O_1$ is a parallelogram. Consequently,
\[
O_3O_1=FA=CD=NL.
\]
Similarly,
\[
O_1O_2=LM,
\qquad
O_2O_3=MN.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle O_1O_2O_3\cong\triangle LMN.
\tag{5}
\]
The rotation $\mathcal R$ shows that these congruent triangles have the same orientation.

Let $T$ be the circumcenter of $O_1O_2O_3$. We prove that $C,T,F$ are collinear. By construction and (5),
\[
CO_2=O_2T=TO_3=O_3F,
\]
all four lengths being equal to the circumradius of $LMN$. Hence triangles $TO_3F$ and $CO_2T$ are isosceles, and directed angles between rays give
\[
\measuredangle(TF,TO_3)=\measuredangle(FO_3,FT),
\tag{6}
\]
\[
\measuredangle(TO_2,TC)=\measuredangle(CT,CO_2).
\tag{7}
\]
Because $T$ and $O$ are the circumcenters of the equally oriented congruent triangles in (5),
\[
\measuredangle(TO_3,TO_2)=\measuredangle(ON,OM).
\]
The segments $CO_2$ and $FO_3$ are translates of $NO$ and $MO$, respectively, so
\[
\measuredangle(TO_3,TO_2)=\measuredangle(CO_2,FO_3).
\tag{8}
\]
Adding (6), (7), and (8), we obtain
\[
\measuredangle(TF,TC)=\measuredangle(CT,FT).
\]
The two angles are negatives of one another, so the rays $TF$ and $TC$ are collinear. Therefore
\[
T\in CF.
\]
The same cyclic argument gives
\[
T\in AD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
T\in BE.
\]
Thus $AD,BE,CF$ are concurrent.

SOLUTION 3:
Let
\[
\theta=
\angle A-\angle D
=
\angle C-\angle F
=
\angle E-\angle B.
\]
Place the hexagon in the complex plane, with $A=0$ and the vertices labelled clockwise. Define the complex side vectors
\[
a=B-A,
\qquad
b=D-C,
\qquad
c=F-E,
\]
and
\[
a'=E-D,
\qquad
b'=A-F,
\qquad
c'=C-B.
\]
The length conditions give
\[
|a|=|a'|,
\qquad
|b|=|b'|,
\qquad
|c|=|c'|.
\tag{1}
\]

The angle-difference conditions imply
\[
\frac{a'}a\frac{b'}b=e^{-i\theta},
\qquad
\frac{b'}b\frac{c'}c=e^{-i\theta},
\qquad
\frac{c'}c\frac{a'}a=e^{-i\theta}.
\]
All six ratios in these equations are nonzero. Comparing the equations shows that
\[
\frac{a'}a=\frac{b'}b=\frac{c'}c=r
\]
for some complex number $r$. By (1),
\[
|r|=1,
\]
and therefore
\[
a'=ar,
\qquad
b'=br,
\qquad
c'=cr.
\tag{2}
\]

The sum of the side vectors is zero, so by (2),
\[
0=a+cr+b+ar+c+br=(a+b+c)(1+r).
\tag{3}
\]
If $r=-1$, then opposite side vectors are negatives of each other; the hexagon is centrally symmetric, and the three diagonals meet at its center. Hence assume
\[
r\ne-1.
\]
Equation (3) gives
\[
a+b+c=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The vertices then have coordinates
\[
A=0,
\qquad
B=a,
\qquad
C=a+cr,
\qquad
D=c(r-1),
\qquad
E=-br-c,
\qquad
F=-br.
\tag{5}
\]

The diagonals $AD$ and $BE$ of a convex hexagon meet at an interior point $W$. Write
\[
W=c(r-1)\lambda,
\qquad
0<\lambda<1.
\]
Since $D\ne A$, we have $r\ne1$. Put
\[
s=\frac1{r-1}.
\]
Using $r\overline r=1$, we obtain
\[
1+s
=
\frac r{r-1}
=
\frac r{r-r\overline r}
=
\frac1{1-\overline r}
=
-\overline s.
\tag{6}
\]
Here and below, $u\parallel v$ for nonzero complex numbers means that $u/v$ is real.

From (4)--(6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
W\in BE
&\iff c(r-1)\lambda-a\parallel a-(-br-c)=b(r-1)\\
&\iff c\lambda-as\parallel b\\
&\iff -a\lambda-b\lambda-as\parallel b\\
&\iff a(\lambda+s)\parallel b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Because $r\ne\pm1$, the number $s$ is nonreal, and hence $\lambda+s\ne0$.

Similarly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
W\in CF
&\iff c(r-1)\lambda+br\parallel -br-(a+cr)=a(r-1)\\
&\iff c\lambda+b(1+s)\parallel a\\
&\iff -a\lambda-b\lambda-b\overline s\parallel a\\
&\iff b(\lambda+\overline s)\parallel a\\
&\iff b\parallel a(\lambda+s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last equivalence uses
\[
(\lambda+s)(\lambda+\overline s)=|\lambda+s|^2>0.
\]
Conditions (7) and (8) are the same. Since $W$ was chosen on $BE$, it follows that $W\in CF$. Thus
\[
W=AD\cap BE\cap CF,
\]
and the three diagonals are concurrent.
