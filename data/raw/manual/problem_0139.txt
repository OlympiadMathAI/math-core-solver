ID: 0139

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Diophantine Equations, Perfect Squares, Parametrization, Polynomial Factorization

STATEMENT:
Determine all pairs $(x,y)$ of positive integers satisfying
\[
\sqrt[3]{7x^2-13xy+7y^2}=|x-y|+1.
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The solutions are
\[
(x,y)=(1,1)
\]
and, for every integer $m\ge2$,
\[
\{x,y\}
=
\{m^3+m^2-2m-1,\ m^3+2m^2-m-1\}.
\tag{2}
\]

If $x=y$, equation (1) gives $\sqrt[3]{x^2}=1$, hence $x=y=1$.

Now suppose $x\ne y$. By symmetry, assume $x>y$ and put
\[
n=x-y>0.
\]
Cubing (1) and substituting $x=y+n$, we obtain
\[
7(y+n)^2-13(y+n)y+7y^2=(n+1)^3,
\]
which simplifies to
\[
y^2+ny=n^3-4n^2+3n+1.
\tag{3}
\]
Multiplying by $4$ and adding $n^2$ gives
\[
(2y+n)^2
=4n^3-15n^2+12n+4
=(n-2)^2(4n+1).
\tag{4}
\]
The cases $n=1$ and $n=2$ are impossible: for $n=1$, the right-hand side of (4) is $5$, while for $n=2$ it is $0$ although $2y+n>0$. Thus $n>2$.

Equation (4) shows that $4n+1$ is the square of a positive rational number. Since it is an integer, it must in fact be a perfect square. It is odd, so for some integer $m\ge0$,
\[
4n+1=(2m+1)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
n=m^2+m.
\tag{5}
\]
Because $n>2$, we have $m\ge2$.

Substituting (5) into (4), and taking the positive square root, yields
\[
2y+m^2+m
=(m^2+m-2)(2m+1)
=2m^3+3m^2-3m-2.
\]
Hence
\[
y=m^3+m^2-2m-1,
\]
and then
\[
x=y+n=m^3+2m^2-m-1.
\]
For $m\ge2$, both numbers are positive.

Conversely, for the two numbers in (2),
\[
x-y=m^2+m
\]
and a direct factorization gives
\[
7x^2-13xy+7y^2=(m^2+m+1)^3=(|x-y|+1)^3.
\]
Thus all the listed pairs satisfy (1), and the classification is complete.
