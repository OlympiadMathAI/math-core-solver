ID: 0404

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: 2-Adic Valuation, Signed Sums, Partial Sums, Pigeonhole Principle

STATEMENT:
Determine the maximum possible length $L$ of a sequence
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_L
\]
of positive integers satisfying both conditions:

\begin{itemize}
\item every term is at most $2^{2023}$;
\item there is no consecutive subsequence
\[
a_i,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_j
\]
and no choice of signs
\[
\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_{i+1},\ldots,\varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}
\]
for which
\[
\varepsilon_i a_i+\varepsilon_{i+1}a_{i+1}
+\cdots+\varepsilon_j a_j=0.
\]
\end{itemize}

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{L=2^{2024}-1}.
\]

We prove the more general statement in which the terms are at most
\[
n=2^k.
\]
The maximum length is then
\[
2n-1=2^{k+1}-1.
\]

For the construction, define
\[
a_i=2^{k-v_2(i)}
\qquad(1\le i\le2n-1),
\tag{1}
\]
where $v_2(i)$ is the exponent of $2$ in $i$. Since
\[
1\le i\le2^{k+1}-1,
\]
we have $0\le v_2(i)\le k$, and therefore every $a_i$ is a positive integer at most $2^k=n$.

Consider any interval of indices
\[
r,r+1,\ldots,s.
\]
Among these integers, there is a unique index $x$ at which $v_2$ is maximal. Indeed, if two indices in the interval had the same maximal valuation $v$, then between two consecutive such multiples of $2^v$ there would be a multiple of $2^{v+1}$, contradicting maximality.

Put
\[
v=v_2(x).
\]
Then
\[
a_x=2^{k-v},
\]
whereas every other $a_i$ in the interval is divisible by
\[
2^{k-v+1}.
\]
Consequently, for any signs $\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}$,
\[
\sum_{i=r}^s\varepsilon_i a_i
\equiv
\pm2^{k-v}
\not\equiv0
\pmod{2^{k-v+1}}.
\]
Thus the sequence in (1), of length $2n-1$, has the required property.

We now prove that length $2n$ is impossible. Let
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_L
\]
be any sequence with
\[
1\le a_i\le n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L\ge2n.
\]
Choose signs recursively. Put $b_0=0$, and after $b_{i-1}$ has been defined, set
\[
\varepsilon_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&b_{i-1}\le0,\\
-1,&b_{i-1}\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
b_i=b_{i-1}+\varepsilon_i a_i.
\]
We claim that
\[
-n+1\le b_i\le n
\qquad(0\le i\le L).
\tag{2}
\]
This follows immediately by induction: from a nonpositive partial sum we add a number between $1$ and $n$, while from a positive partial sum we subtract such a number.

The interval in (2) contains exactly $2n$ integers, but
\[
b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_L
\]
contains at least $2n+1$ terms. Hence two are equal:
\[
b_{i-1}=b_j
\]
for some $1\le i\le j\le L$. Subtracting gives
\[
\varepsilon_i a_i+\varepsilon_{i+1}a_{i+1}
+\cdots+\varepsilon_j a_j=0.
\]
Thus no sequence of length at least $2n$ works.

Taking
\[
n=2^{2023}
\]
gives
\[
L=2n-1=2^{2024}-1.
\]
