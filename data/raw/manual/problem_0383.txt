ID: 0383

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Miquel Point, Spiral Similarity, Circle Orthogonality, Tangent-Chord Theorem

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with
\[
AC>AB,
\]
and let $O$ be its circumcentre. Let $D$ be a point on segment $BC$.

The line through $D$ perpendicular to $BC$ meets $AO$, $AC$, and $AB$ at $W$, $X$, and $Y$, respectively. The circumcircles of triangles $AXY$ and $ABC$ meet again at
\[
Z\ne A.
\]

Assume that
\[
W\ne D
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
OW=OD.
\]
Prove that $DZ$ is tangent to the circumcircle of triangle $AXY$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let
\[
E=AO\cap BC.
\]
The triangle $EDW$ is right-angled at $D$, and $O$ lies on its hypotenuse $EW$. Since
\[
OW=OD
\]
and $W\ne D$, the point $O$ is the midpoint of $EW$, hence the circumcentre of triangle $EDW$. Therefore
\[
OE=OD.
\]
As $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, the points $D$ and $E$ are reflections of one another in that perpendicular bisector.

We use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$. Since $D,X,Y$ are collinear, $A,X,Y,Z$ are cyclic, $A,B,C,Z$ are cyclic, and $B,C,D$ are collinear,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(DX,XZ)
&=\measuredangle(YX,XZ)\\
&=\measuredangle(YA,AZ)\\
&=\measuredangle(BA,AZ)\\
&=\measuredangle(BC,CZ)\\
&=\measuredangle(DC,CZ).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
C,D,X,Z
\]
are concyclic.

Let $Z'$ be the reflection of $A$ in the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. Then
\[
Z'\in(ABC)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AZ'\parallel BC.
\tag{2}
\]
The reflection maps triangle $BAE$ to triangle $CZ'D$. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DZ'C
&=\angle BAE\\
&=\angle BAO\\
&=90^\circ-\frac12\angle AOB\\
&=90^\circ-\angle ACB\\
&=\angle DXC.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
C,D,X,Z'
\]
are concyclic. Reversing the directed-angle calculation that produced (1), and using $Z'\in(ABC)$, shows that
\[
A,X,Y,Z'
\]
are also concyclic. Hence $Z'$ is the second common point of $(ABC)$ and $(AXY)$:
\[
Z'=Z.
\]
In particular,
\[
AZ\parallel BC.
\tag{3}
\]

Finally, by the cyclicity of $C,D,X,Z$ and by (3),
\[
\angle AZD
=
\angle CDZ
=
\angle CXZ
=
\angle AYZ.
\]
The tangent--chord theorem now shows that $DZ$ is tangent at $Z$ to the circle $(AXY)$.

SOLUTION 2:
The point $Z$ is the Miquel point of the four lines
\[
AC,\qquad BC,\qquad BA,\qquad DY.
\]
Indeed, it lies on $(ABC)$ and $(AXY)$. Therefore it also lies on
\[
(BDY)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(CDX).
\]

The cyclic quadrilaterals $(BDYZ)$ and $(CDXZ)$ give
\[
\angle ZBC=\angle ZYX,
\qquad
\angle ZCB=\angle ZXY.
\]
Thus
\[
\triangle ZBC\sim\triangle ZYX,
\]
so the spiral similarity centred at $Z$ sends segment $BC$ to segment $YX$. Since
\[
BC\perp YX,
\]
its rotation angle is $90^\circ$. Hence the angle between the circumcircles $(ABC)$ and $(AXY)$ at $Z$ is $90^\circ$. Thus the radius
\[
OZ
\]
of $(ABC)$ is tangent at $Z$ to $(AXY)$.

It remains to prove that $D,O,Z$ are collinear.

Let
\[
E=AO\cap BC.
\]
The triangle $EDW$ is right-angled at $D$, because $E,D\in BC$ and $DW\perp BC$. Since $O\in EW$, $W\ne D$, and
\[
OW=OD,
\]
the point $O$ is the midpoint of $EW$. Hence
\[
OE=OD,
\]
so reflection $\sigma$ in the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ sends
\[
B\leftrightarrow C,\qquad E\leftrightarrow D.
\]

Let
\[
Z'=\sigma(A).
\]
Because $\sigma$ fixes $O$ and sends line $AE=AO$ to line $Z'D$, we have
\[
D,O,Z'\ \text{collinear}.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
Z'\in(ABC).
\tag{2}
\]

We show that $Z'\in(AXY)$. Reflection sends triangle $BAE$ to triangle $CZ'D$, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DZ'C
&=\angle BAE\\
&=\angle BAO\\
&=90^\circ-\frac12\angle AOB\\
&=90^\circ-\angle ACB\\
&=\angle DXC.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
C,D,X,Z'
\]
are concyclic.

Using directed angles, the collinearities $D,X,Y$ and $B,C,D$, together with (2), now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(YX,XZ')
&=\measuredangle(DX,XZ')\\
&=\measuredangle(DC,CZ')\\
&=\measuredangle(BC,CZ')\\
&=\measuredangle(BA,AZ')\\
&=\measuredangle(YA,AZ').
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
A,X,Y,Z'
\]
are concyclic. Thus $Z'$ is the second common point of $(ABC)$ and $(AXY)$, so
\[
Z'=Z.
\]
Equation (1) gives
\[
D,O,Z\ \text{collinear}.
\]
Since $OZ$ is tangent to $(AXY)$ at $Z$, the same is true of $DZ$.
