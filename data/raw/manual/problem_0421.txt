ID: 0421

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Arithmetic Progressions, Greatest Common Divisors, Products, Rational Recurrences

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n,b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n
\]
be $2n$ positive integers such that the following $n+1$ products form a strictly increasing arithmetic progression in the displayed order:
\[
a_1a_2\cdots a_n,
\]
\[
b_1a_2\cdots a_n,
\]
\[
b_1b_2a_3\cdots a_n,
\]
\[
\ldots,
\]
\[
b_1b_2\cdots b_n.
\]

Determine the smallest possible positive common difference.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{n!}.
\]

Let $D$ be the common difference. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
D
&=(b_1-a_1)a_2a_3\cdots a_n\\
&=b_1(b_2-a_2)a_3\cdots a_n\\
&=\cdots\\
&=b_1b_2\cdots b_{n-1}(b_n-a_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Since the progression is strictly increasing,
\[
b_i>a_i
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{2}
\]

If
\[
g_i=\gcd(a_i,b_i)>1,
\]
divide both $a_i$ and $b_i$ by $g_i$. Every one of the $n+1$ products is then divided by $g_i$, so the new common difference is $D/g_i$. Repeating this reduction, it is enough to prove the lower bound under the assumptions
\[
\gcd(a_i,b_i)=1
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{3}
\]

Equating two consecutive expressions in (1),
\[
(b_i-a_i)a_{i+1}
=
b_i(b_{i+1}-a_{i+1})
\qquad(1\le i<n).
\tag{4}
\]
By (3),
\[
\gcd(b_i-a_i,b_i)=1
\]
and
\[
\gcd(a_{i+1},b_{i+1}-a_{i+1})=1.
\]
Equation (4) therefore implies
\[
a_{i+1}=b_i
\]
and
\[
b_{i+1}-a_{i+1}=b_i-a_i.
\]
Thus
\[
a_1,b_1=a_2,b_2=a_3,\ldots,b_{n-1}=a_n,b_n
\]
is an arithmetic progression with a positive integer common difference $t$.

In particular,
\[
a_i=a_1+(i-1)t\ge i.
\]
Using the first expression in (1),
\[
D=t\,a_2a_3\cdots a_n
\ge
1\cdot2\cdot3\cdots n
=
n!.
\]

Equality is attained by
\[
a_i=i,
\qquad
b_i=i+1.
\]
Indeed, the successive products then differ by exactly $n!$. Hence the minimum is $n!$.

SOLUTION 2:
For $n=1$, the common difference is $b_1-a_1\ge1=1!$, and equality is attainable. Assume henceforth that $n\ge2$.

Let
\[
P_i=b_1b_2\cdots b_i\,a_{i+1}\cdots a_n
\qquad(0\le i\le n).
\]
Thus
\[
P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_n
\]
is a strictly increasing arithmetic progression.

Divide each pair $(a_i,b_i)$ by its gcd. This divides all the $P_j$ and the common difference by the same integer, so it is enough to consider the case
\[
\gcd(a_i,b_i)=1
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{1}
\]

For $0\le i\le n-2$, the arithmetic-progression relation gives
\[
P_{i+2}=2P_{i+1}-P_i.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{b_{i+2}}{a_{i+2}}
&=
\frac{P_{i+2}}{P_{i+1}}\\
&=
2-\frac{P_i}{P_{i+1}}\\
&=
2-\frac{a_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}\\
&=
\frac{2b_{i+1}-a_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Both fractions in (2) are reduced by (1). Therefore
\[
a_{i+2}=b_{i+1},
\qquad
b_{i+2}=2b_{i+1}-a_{i+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Starting with $i=0$, relation (3) shows inductively that
\[
a_1,b_1=a_2,b_2=a_3,\ldots,a_n,b_n
\]
is an arithmetic progression with positive integer common difference $t$.

Thus $a_i\ge i$, and the common difference of the product progression is
\[
D=(b_1-a_1)a_2\cdots a_n
=t\,a_2\cdots a_n
\ge n!.
\]
The choice $a_i=i$, $b_i=i+1$ gives equality.

SOLUTION 3:
Let $D$ be the common difference. Equality of consecutive expressions for $D$ gives
\[
(b_i-a_i)a_{i+1}
=
b_i(b_{i+1}-a_{i+1})
\qquad(1\le i<n).
\tag{1}
\]
Rearranging,
\[
\frac{a_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}-a_{i+1}}
=
\frac{b_i}{b_i-a_i}
=
1+\frac{a_i}{b_i-a_i}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{a_i}{b_i-a_i}
=
\frac{a_1}{b_1-a_1}+i-1
>
i-1.
\]
Since $b_i-a_i$ is a positive integer,
\[
a_i>(i-1)(b_i-a_i)\ge i-1,
\]
and hence
\[
a_i\ge i
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{2}
\]

Now
\[
D=(b_1-a_1)a_2a_3\cdots a_n.
\]
Using (2) and $b_1-a_1\ge1$,
\[
D\ge1\cdot2\cdot3\cdots n=n!.
\]

The construction
\[
a_i=i,\qquad b_i=i+1
\]
has common difference $n!$, so the bound is sharp.
