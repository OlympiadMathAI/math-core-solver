ID: 0303

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Polynomial Interpolation, Linear Algebra, Intermediate Value Theorem, Multisets

STATEMENT:
A magician intends to perform the following trick. She announces a positive integer $n$, together with $2n$ real numbers
\[
x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_{2n}.
\]
A member of the audience secretly chooses a polynomial $P(x)$ of degree $n$ with real coefficients, computes
\[
P(x_1),P(x_2),\ldots,P(x_{2n}),
\]
and writes these $2n$ values on the blackboard in nondecreasing order. The magician then announces the secret polynomial.

Can the magician choose $n$ and the numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_{2n}$ so that she can always determine the polynomial?

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{\text{No}}.
\]

Fix any positive integer $n$ and any real numbers
\[
x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_{2n}
\]
chosen by the magician. We construct two distinct polynomials of degree $n$ that produce the same multiset of values.

Consider a polynomial
\[
P(x)=c_nx^n+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0.
\]
Impose the $n$ homogeneous linear conditions
\[
P(x_{2i-1})+P(x_{2i})=0
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{1}
\]
These are $n$ homogeneous linear equations in the $n+1$ unknown coefficients
\[
c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_n.
\]
Hence there is a nonzero solution, giving a nonzero polynomial $P$ of degree at most $n$ satisfying (1).

For every $i$, the values of $P$ at the endpoints of
\[
[x_{2i-1},x_{2i}]
\]
have opposite signs or are both zero. By the intermediate value theorem, $P$ has a root in each of these $n$ pairwise disjoint intervals. Thus $P$ has at least $n$ distinct real roots. Since $P$ is nonzero and has degree at most $n$, it follows that
\[
\deg P=n.
\]

Now define
\[
Q(x)=-P(x).
\]
The polynomials $P$ and $Q$ are distinct and both have degree $n$. From (1),
\[
P(x_{2i-1})=Q(x_{2i}),
\qquad
P(x_{2i})=Q(x_{2i-1})
\]
for every $i$. Therefore
\[
\{P(x_1),\ldots,P(x_{2n})\}
\]
and
\[
\{Q(x_1),\ldots,Q(x_{2n})\}
\]
are the same multiset. Their nondecreasing lists are identical, so the magician cannot distinguish $P$ from $Q$.

Hence no strategy of the required kind exists.
