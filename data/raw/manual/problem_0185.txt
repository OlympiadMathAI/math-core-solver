ID: 0185

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Double Counting, Regular Polygons, Parity, Isosceles Triangles

STATEMENT:
Let $n$ be a positive integer relatively prime to $6$. The vertices of a regular $n$-gon are coloured with three colours so that an odd number of vertices has each colour. Prove that there exists an isosceles triangle whose three vertices have three different colours.

SOLUTION 1:
For $j=1,2,3$, let $a_j$ denote the number of isosceles triangles whose vertices use exactly $j$ colours. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
a_3=0.
\]
Let the three colour classes have odd sizes
\[
b,\qquad c,\qquad d.
\]

Count pairs $(\Delta,E)$ in which $\Delta$ is an isosceles triangle and $E$ is a side of $\Delta$ whose endpoints have different colours.

Because no isosceles triangle uses all three colours, every triangle contributing to this count uses exactly two colours. Such a triangle has exactly two bichromatic sides. Hence the number of pairs is
\[
2a_2.
\tag{1}
\]

Now choose any two vertices $A,B$ of different colours. There are exactly three isosceles triangles having $AB$ as one of their sides:

\begin{itemize}
\item one in which $AB$ is the base; and
\item two in which $AB$ is one of the equal sides, one with apex at $A$ and one with apex at $B$.
\end{itemize}

The first triangle is unique because $n$ is odd. The three triangles are distinct because $3\nmid n$; otherwise a coincidence would produce an equilateral triangle whose vertices are vertices of the regular $n$-gon.

There are
\[
bc+cd+db
\]
unordered pairs of differently coloured vertices. Thus the same number of pairs $(\Delta,E)$ equals
\[
3(bc+cd+db).
\tag{2}
\]

The quantity in (1) is even. But $b,c,d$ are odd, so
\[
bc+cd+db
\]
is odd, and therefore the quantity in (2) is odd. This contradiction proves that
\[
a_3>0.
\]
Hence an isosceles triangle with three differently coloured vertices exists.
