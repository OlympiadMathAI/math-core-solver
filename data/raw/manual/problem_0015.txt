ID: 0015

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Covering Systems, Hall's Marriage Theorem, Arithmetic Progressions

STATEMENT:
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_s$ be arithmetic progressions of integers such that

(i) every integer belongs to at least one of them;

(ii) every progression contains an integer that belongs to none of the other progressions.

Let $n$ be the least common multiple of their steps, and write
\[
n=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k}
\]
for its prime factorization. Prove that
\[
s\ge 1+\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i(p_i-1).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We first prove a grid lemma.

Let $n_1,\ldots,n_k$ be positive integers and define the grid
\[
\mathcal{N}=\{(a_1,\ldots,a_k):0\le a_i\le n_i-1\}.
\]
A \emph{subgrid} is a set obtained by fixing a nonempty collection of coordinates. A subgrid is called orthogonal to the $i$th coordinate axis if its $i$th coordinate is fixed.

\textbf{Grid Lemma.} Suppose subgrids $L_1,\ldots,L_s$ cover $\mathcal{N}$ and satisfy:

1. every $L_j$ contains a point belonging to no other $L_m$;

2. for every coordinate axis, at least one $L_j$ is orthogonal to that axis.

Then
\[
s\ge1+\sum_{i=1}^k(n_i-1).
\tag{1}
\]

Assume otherwise that
\[
s\le s':=\sum_{i=1}^k(n_i-1).
\]
Construct a bipartite graph with left vertex set
\[
V=\{L_1,\ldots,L_s\}
\]
and right vertex set
\[
V'=\{v_{ij}:1\le i\le k,\ 1\le j\le n_i-1\}.
\]
Join $L_m$ to every $v_{ij}$ belonging to an axis to which $L_m$ is orthogonal. For $W\subseteq V$, let $F(W)$ be its neighborhood. By condition 2,
\[
F(V)=V'.
\]
Choose a set $W\subseteq V$ of maximum cardinality subject to
\[
|W|>|F(W)|;
\]
if no such set exists, let $W=\varnothing$. Put
\[
W'=F(W),
\qquad
U=V\setminus W,
\qquad
U'=V'\setminus W'.
\]
Since $|V'|\ge|V|$ and $F(V)=V'$, we have $W\ne V$, so $U\ne\varnothing$.

Each group $\{v_{ij}:1\le j\le n_i-1\}$ is either entirely contained in $W'$ or entirely outside it. After permuting coordinates, suppose $U'$ consists of the groups corresponding to axes $1,\ldots,\ell$.

Consider the induced bipartite graph on $U\cup U'$. For every $X\subseteq U$,
\[
|F(X)\cap U'|\ge|X|.
\]
Otherwise,
\[
|W\cup X|=|W|+|X|>|F(W)|+|F(X)\cap U'|=|F(W\cup X)|,
\]
contradicting the maximality of $W$. Hence Hall's theorem gives an injection assigning to each $L\in U$ one axis $g(L)\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}$ to which $L$ is orthogonal, with at most $n_i-1$ subgrids assigned to axis $i$.

Because $W\ne V$, choose $L_0\in U$. By condition 1, $L_0$ has a private point
\[
b=(b_1,\ldots,b_k)
\]
that lies in no member of $W$. For each $i\le\ell$, all subgrids $L\in U$ assigned to axis $i$ fix that coordinate to some value $c_L$. There are at most $n_i-1$ such values, so choose
\[
a_i\in\{0,\ldots,n_i-1\}
\]
different from every $c_L$ assigned to axis $i$. Define
\[
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell,b_{\ell+1},\ldots,b_k).
\]
The point $a$ lies in no $L\in U$ by its chosen coordinate. It also lies in no $L\in W$: if $a\in L\in W$ while $b\notin L$, then $L$ must fix one of the first $\ell$ coordinates, which would place a corresponding vertex of $U'$ inside $F(W)=W'$, a contradiction. Thus $a$ is uncovered, contradicting the covering assumption. The Grid Lemma is proved.

We now apply it to the arithmetic progressions. Let $d_j$ be the step of $P_j$. Assume $n>1$, since $n=1$ is trivial. For each residue $m\pmod n$ and each $i$, write the residue of $m$ modulo $p_i^{\alpha_i}$ in base $p_i$:
\[
m_i=\overline{r_{i,\alpha_i}\cdots r_{i,1}}_{p_i}.
\]
Map $m$ to the tuple of all these digits:
\[
r(m)=(r_{1,1},\ldots,r_{1,\alpha_1},r_{2,1},\ldots,r_{k,\alpha_k}).
\]
By the Chinese remainder theorem, this is a bijection from the residues modulo $n$ to the grid
\[
\underbrace{[0,p_1-1]\times\cdots\times[0,p_1-1]}_{\alpha_1\text{ factors}}
\times\cdots\times
\underbrace{[0,p_k-1]\times\cdots\times[0,p_k-1]}_{\alpha_k\text{ factors}}.
\tag{2}
\]

Write
\[
d_j=p_1^{\gamma_1}\cdots p_k^{\gamma_k}.
\]
Membership in $P_j$ fixes the residue modulo $d_j$, and therefore fixes exactly the first $\gamma_i$ base-$p_i$ digits for each $i$. Hence the image of $P_j$ under $r$ is a subgrid of (2).

These subgrids cover the grid because the progressions cover all integers. Condition (ii) gives each subgrid a private point. Finally, for every prime $p_i$, some step $d_j$ is divisible by $p_i^{\alpha_i}$ because $n$ is the least common multiple of the steps. The corresponding subgrid fixes all $\alpha_i$ coordinates associated with $p_i$, so every coordinate axis is orthogonal to some subgrid.

Applying the Grid Lemma with $\alpha_i$ copies of $n_j=p_i$ yields
\[
s\ge1+\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i(p_i-1),
\]
as required.

SOLUTION 2:
We prove a stronger statement by minimal-counterexample induction.

For a positive integer $r$, write
\[
[r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}.
\]
Condition (ii) implies that the given covering
\[
\mathcal{P}=\{P_1,\ldots,P_s\}
\]
is minimal: removing $P_j$ leaves its private integer uncovered. Let $d_j$ be the step of $P_j$, and choose a private integer $h_j\in P_j$ for each $j$. Assume $n>1$. Then every $d_j>1$; indeed, a progression of step $1$ is all of $\mathbb{Z}$, which together with condition (ii) would force the covering to consist of that single progression and hence give $n=1$.

\textbf{Claim.} Let $I\subseteq[k]$ be nonempty, and choose integers
\[
1\le\beta_i\le\alpha_i
\qquad(i\in I).
\]
Define
\[
T=\left\{j\in[s]:p_i^{\alpha_i-\beta_i+1}\mid d_j
\text{ for some }i\in I\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
|T|\ge1+\sum_{i\in I}\beta_i(p_i-1).
\tag{1}
\]
Taking $I=[k]$ and $\beta_i=\alpha_i$ gives the problem, because every $d_j>1$ is divisible by at least one $p_i$.

First suppose all steps $d_j$ are prime. If there are at least $p_i$ progressions of step $p_i$ for some $i$, then those $p_i$ residue classes are disjoint and cover all integers; minimality forces these to be the whole covering and $n=p_i$, so (1) is immediate. Otherwise, for every $i$ at most $p_i-1$ residue classes modulo $p_i$ occur. Choose a residue $q_i\pmod{p_i}$ missed by them. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies an integer $q$ with
\[
q\equiv q_i\pmod{p_i}
\]
for every $i$, and $q$ lies in no progression, a contradiction. Thus the Claim holds when all steps are prime.

Assume now that the Claim fails. Choose a counterexample for which $n$ is minimal, and subject to this, for which
\[
\sum_j d_j
\]
is minimal. Some step is composite; relabel so that $p_1\mid d_1$ and
\[
d'_1=\frac{d_1}{p_1}>1.
\]
Let $P'_1$ be the arithmetic progression of step $d'_1$ containing $P_1$.

Replace $P_1$ by $P'_1$ and retain $P_2,\ldots,P_s$. This still covers $\mathbb{Z}$. Choose a minimal subcovering
\[
\mathcal{P}'=\{P'_1,P_2,\ldots,P_{s'}\}
\]
after relabeling. The progression $P'_1$ must occur because $h_1$ belongs to none of $P_2,\ldots,P_s$. Write the period of $\mathcal{P}'$ as
\[
n'=p_1^{\alpha_1-\sigma_1}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k-\sigma_k}
=\operatorname{lcm}(d'_1,d_2,\ldots,d_{s'}).
\tag{2}
\]
For every $j>s'$, the private point $h_j$ must lie in $P'_1$; otherwise it would not be covered after the replacement.

Next, the nonempty intersections
\[
R_j=P_j\cap P'_1
\]
cover $P'_1$ and have steps
\[
r_j=\operatorname{lcm}(d_j,d'_1).
\]
After translating and scaling $P'_1$ by the factor $1/d'_1$, these intersections become progressions $Q_j$ of steps
\[
q_j=\frac{r_j}{d'_1}
\]
covering $\mathbb{Z}$. Choose a minimal subcovering $\mathcal{Q}$; it contains $Q_1$. Let
\[
n''=\operatorname{lcm}\{q_j:Q_j\in\mathcal{Q}\}
=\frac{p_1^{\gamma_1}\cdots p_k^{\gamma_k}}{d'_1}.
\tag{3}
\]
If $h_j\in P'_1$, then its image after scaling belongs only to $Q_j$, so $Q_j\in\mathcal{Q}$.

Apply the Claim to $\mathcal{P}'$. This is allowed because either its period is smaller than $n$, or its period is $n$ and the sum of its steps is smaller. Let
\[
I'=\{i\in I:\beta_i>\sigma_i\},
\qquad
\beta'_i=\beta_i-\sigma_i.
\]
The relevant divisibility threshold in $\mathcal{P}'$ is still
\[
p_i^{(\alpha_i-\sigma_i)-\beta'_i+1}
=p_i^{\alpha_i-\beta_i+1}.
\]
Hence
\[
|T\cap[s']|
\ge1+\sum_{i\in I}(\beta_i-\sigma_i)_+(p_i-1),
\tag{4}
\]
where $(x)_+=\max\{x,0\}$.

Set
\[
G=\sum_{i\in I}\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\}(p_i-1).
\tag{5}
\]
Since
\[
\beta_i=(\beta_i-\sigma_i)_++\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\},
\]
it remains to find at least $G$ members of $T$ among the indices $s'+1,\ldots,s$.

Let
\[
I''=\{i\in I:\sigma_i>0\}.
\]
For each $i\in I''$, the full power $p_i^{\alpha_i}$ does not divide $n'$. Yet, because $n$ is the least common multiple of the original steps, there is an index $j(i)$ with
\[
p_i^{\alpha_i}\mid d_{j(i)}.
\]
Such a progression cannot belong to $\mathcal{P}'$, so $j(i)>s'$. Its private point lies in $P'_1$, hence $Q_{j(i)}\in\mathcal{Q}$. It follows from (3) that
\[
\gamma_i=\alpha_i
\qquad(i\in I'').
\tag{6}
\]
Write
\[
d'_1=p_1^{\tau_1}\cdots p_k^{\tau_k}.
\]
Then
\[
n''=p_1^{\gamma_1-\tau_1}\cdots p_k^{\gamma_k-\tau_k}<n.
\]
We may therefore apply the Claim to $\mathcal{Q}$ with the index set $I''$ and exponents
\[
\beta''_i=\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\}.
\]
Define
\[
T''=\left\{j:Q_j\in\mathcal{Q},\quad
p_i^{(\gamma_i-\tau_i)-\beta''_i+1}\mid q_j
\text{ for some }i\in I''\right\}.
\]
By the Claim and (5),
\[
|T''|\ge1+G.
\tag{7}
\]
Using (6) and $q_j=r_j/d'_1$, membership in $T''$ means that, for some $i\in I''$,
\[
p_i^{\alpha_i-\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\}+1}\mid r_j.
\tag{8}
\]
The exponent in (8) is greater than $\alpha_i-\sigma_i$, which is at least the exponent $\tau_i$ of $p_i$ in $d'_1$. Therefore the divisibility in (8) must come from $d_j$, not merely from $d'_1$. Since
\[
\alpha_i-\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\}+1
\ge\alpha_i-\beta_i+1,
\]
we get $j\in T$.

Moreover, the exponent in (8) is greater than the exponent of $p_i$ in $n'$, so $P_j$ cannot belong to $\mathcal{P}'$. Thus
\[
T''\subseteq T\cap\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{s'+1,\ldots,s\}\bigr).
\]
By (7), at least $G$ of these indices lie in $\{s'+1,\ldots,s\}$. Combining this with (4) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|T|
&\ge1+\sum_{i\in I}(\beta_i-\sigma_i)_+(p_i-1)
 +\sum_{i\in I}\min\{\beta_i,\sigma_i\}(p_i-1)\\
&=1+\sum_{i\in I}\beta_i(p_i-1),
\end{aligned}
\]
contradicting the choice of the counterexample. The Claim, and therefore the problem, is proved.
