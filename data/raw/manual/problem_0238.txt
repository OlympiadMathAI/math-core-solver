ID: 0238

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Euclidean Algorithm, Continued Fractions, Quadratic Residues, Modular Inverses

STATEMENT:
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $\mathbb Z_{>0}$ be the set of positive integers. Suppose that
\[
f:\mathbb Z_{>0}\times\mathbb Z_{>0}\to\{0,1\}
\]
satisfies:

\begin{itemize}
\item $f(1,1)=0$;
\item for every relatively prime positive pair $(a,b)\ne(1,1)$,
\[
f(a,b)+f(b,a)=1;
\]
\item for every relatively prime positive pair $(a,b)$,
\[
f(a+b,b)=f(a,b).
\]
\end{itemize}

Prove that
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}f(n^2,p)\ge\sqrt{2p}-2.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of coprime positive pairs. For $(a,b)\in\mathcal A$, there are integers $u,v$ with
\[
ua+vb=1.
\]
All such pairs are
\[
(u+kb,v-ka),
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Hence there is a unique one satisfying
\[
-\frac b2<u\le\frac b2.
\]
Denote it by
\[
g(a,b)=(u,v).
\]

\textbf{Lemma.} If $g(a,b)=(u,v)$, then
\[
f(a,b)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u>0.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove this by induction on $a+b$. For $(a,b)=(1,1)$,
\[
g(1,1)=(0,1)
\]
and $f(1,1)=0$, so the assertion holds.

Suppose first that $a>b$. Then
\[
g(a-b,b)=(u,v+u),
\]
and the third property gives
\[
f(a,b)=f(a-b,b).
\]
The induction hypothesis proves (1).

Now suppose $a<b$. From
\[
vb=1-ua
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-\frac b2<u\le\frac b2
\]
we obtain
\[
-\frac a2<v<\frac{a+1}{2}.
\]
Since $v$ is an integer,
\[
-\frac a2<v\le\frac a2.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
g(b,a)=(v,u).
\]
Using the second and third properties,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(a,b)=1
&\iff f(b,a)=0\\
&\iff f(b-a,a)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the pair $(b-a,a)$,
\[
g(b-a,a)=(v,u+v).
\]
By induction,
\[
f(b-a,a)=0\iff v\le0.
\]
Finally, because $ua+vb=1$ and $b>a$,
\[
v\le0\iff u>0.
\]
This proves the lemma.

For $b=p$, the number $u$ in $g(a,p)$ is the representative of the inverse of $a$ modulo $p$ lying in
\[
\left(-\frac p2,\frac p2\right].
\]
Thus
\[
f(a,p)=1
\]
exactly when the least positive inverse of $a$ modulo $p$ is less than $p/2$.

As $n$ runs from $1$ to $p-1$, the multisets
\[
\{n^2\bmod p\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\{n^{-2}\bmod p\}
\]
coincide: both contain each nonzero quadratic residue exactly twice. Therefore
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}f(n^2,p)
=
2\,\#\left\{
q:\ 1\le q<\frac p2,\ 
q\text{ is a quadratic residue modulo }p
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every ordinary square
\[
1^2,2^2,\ldots,
\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac p2}\right\rfloor^2
\]
is a distinct quadratic residue below $p/2$. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}f(n^2,p)
&\ge
2\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac p2}\right\rfloor\\
&>
2\left(\sqrt{\frac p2}-1\right)
=\sqrt{2p}-2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the required inequality.

SOLUTION 2:
We give a continued-fraction proof of the lemma used in Solution 1.

For a coprime positive pair $(a,b)$, write the regular continued fraction
\[
\frac ab=[a_0;a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k],
\]
where
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_k>0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
a_k\ge2
\]
when $k>0$.

\textbf{Claim.}
\[
f(a,b)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k\text{ is even}.
\tag{4}
\]

We induct on $b$. If $b=1$, repeated use of
\[
f(a+b,b)=f(a,b)
\]
gives
\[
f(a,1)=f(1,1)=0,
\]
and here $k=0$.

For $b>1$, write
\[
a=qb+r,
\qquad 0<r<b.
\]
Then
\[
f(a,b)=f(r,b)=1-f(b,r).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac ab=[q;a_1,\ldots,a_k],
\qquad
\frac br=[a_1;\ldots,a_k].
\]
The continued fractions on the right have lengths of opposite parity, so the induction hypothesis proves (4).

If $k=0$, then $b=1$, $g(a,1)=(0,1)$, and the desired sign criterion is immediate. Assume now that $k>0$.

Let
\[
\frac{p_i}{q_i}=[a_0;a_1,\ldots,a_i]
\]
be the convergents. Then
\[
a=p_k,
\qquad
b=q_k,
\]
and
\[
a q_{k-1}-b p_{k-1}=(-1)^{k-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
If $k=1$, then $q_{k-1}=1\le b/2$. If $k>1$, the recurrence for the convergent denominators gives
\[
b=q_k=a_kq_{k-1}+q_{k-2}>2q_{k-1}.
\]
Therefore the normalized Bezout coefficient from Solution 1 is
\[
u=(-1)^{k-1}q_{k-1},
\]
which satisfies
\[
-\frac b2<u\le\frac b2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
u>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k\text{ is odd}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(a,b)=1.
\]
This recovers the lemma.

Applying it with $b=p$ gives the same quadratic-residue count as in Solution 1, and therefore
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}f(n^2,p)
\ge\sqrt{2p}-2.
\]
