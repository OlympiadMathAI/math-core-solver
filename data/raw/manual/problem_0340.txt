ID: 0340

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Circular Colourings, Extremal Construction, Pigeonhole Principle, Gap Arguments

STATEMENT:
Let $n\ge3$ be an integer. An integer $m\ge n+1$ is called \emph{$n$-colourful} if, given infinitely many marbles in each of $n$ colours
\[
C_1,C_2,\ldots,C_n,
\]
it is possible to place $m$ marbles around a circle so that every group of $n+1$ consecutive marbles contains at least one marble of each colour.

Prove that only finitely many positive integers are not $n$-colourful, and determine the largest one.

SOLUTION 1:
The largest integer that is not $n$-colourful is
\[
\boxed{n^2-n-1}.
\]

First take
\[
m=n(n-1)-1.
\]
In any colouring of these $m$ marbles with $n$ colours, some colour, say blue, occurs at most
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{m}{n}\right\rfloor=n-2
\]
times.

The blue marbles split the non-blue marbles into at most $n-2$ circular blocks. The number of non-blue marbles is at least
\[
m-(n-2)
=
n(n-1)-1-(n-2)
=
(n-1)^2.
\]
Since
\[
(n-1)^2=n(n-2)+1,
\]
one of those blocks contains at least $n+1$ consecutive non-blue marbles. Hence this arrangement cannot be $n$-colourful. Therefore
\[
n^2-n-1
\]
is not $n$-colourful.

We now construct a valid arrangement for every
\[
m\ge n(n-1).
\]
Write
\[
m=nk+j,
\qquad
k\ge n-1,
\qquad
0\le j\le n-1.
\]
Around the circle, place

\begin{itemize}
\item $k-j$ copies of the block
\[
1,2,\ldots,n,
\]
followed by
\item $j$ copies of the block
\[
1,1,2,\ldots,n.
\]
\end{itemize}

The total number of marbles is
\[
n(k-j)+(n+1)j=nk+j=m.
\]

For every colour $r\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$, the cyclic distance between consecutive occurrences of $r$ is either $n$ or $n+1$, according to the types of the two adjacent blocks. For colour $1$, that distance is at most $n$. Therefore every group of $n+1$ consecutive marbles contains every colour.

Thus every integer at least $n(n-1)$ is $n$-colourful, while
\[
n(n-1)-1=n^2-n-1
\]
is not. This proves the answer.
