ID: 0229

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Tangential Quadrilaterals, Incenter Geometry, Similitude Centers, Apollonius Circle, Angle Bisectors

STATEMENT:
A convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ has an incircle with center $I$. Let $I_a,I_b,I_c,I_d$ be the incenters of the triangles $DAB,ABC,BCD,CDA$, respectively.

The common external tangents of the circles
\[
(AI_bI_d)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(CI_bI_d)
\]
meet at $X$. The common external tangents of the circles
\[
(BI_aI_c)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(DI_aI_c)
\]
meet at $Y$. Prove that
\[
\angle XIY=90^\circ.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Denote the four circles by
\[
\omega_a=(AI_bI_d),
\quad
\omega_b=(BI_aI_c),
\quad
\omega_c=(CI_bI_d),
\quad
\omega_d=(DI_aI_c).
\]
Let their centers and radii be
\[
O_a,O_b,O_c,O_d
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
r_a,r_b,r_c,r_d,
\]
respectively.

\textbf{Claim 1.}
\[
I_bI_d\perp AC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
I_aI_c\perp BD.
\tag{1}
\]

Let the incircles of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACD$ touch $AC$ at $T$ and $T'$. Their tangent-length formulas give
\[
AT=\frac{AB+AC-BC}{2},
\qquad
AT'=\frac{AD+AC-CD}{2}.
\]
Because $ABCD$ is tangential,
\[
AB+CD=BC+AD,
\]
so
\[
AB-BC=AD-CD.
\]
Hence $AT=AT'$, and therefore $T=T'$. The radii $I_bT$ and $I_dT$ are both perpendicular to $AC$, proving the first relation in (1). The second follows analogously.

\textbf{Claim 2.}
\[
O_a\in AI,
\quad
O_b\in BI,
\quad
O_c\in CI,
\quad
O_d\in DI.
\tag{2}
\]

We prove the assertion for $O_a$. The incircles of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACD$ are obtained from the incircle of $ABCD$ by homotheties centered at $B$ and $D$, respectively. Thus
\[
I_b\in BI,
\qquad
I_d\in DI.
\tag{3}
\]
In the triangle $AI_dI_b$, Claim 1 says that $AT$ is an altitude. In any triangle, the altitude from a vertex and the line joining that vertex to the circumcenter are isogonal. Since $T$ lies inside $I_bI_d$,
\[
\angle I_bAT=\angle O_aAI_d.
\]
Also, $AI_b$ and $AI_d$ bisect $\angle BAC$ and $\angle CAD$, respectively. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle O_aAD
&=\angle O_aAI_d+\angle I_dAD\\
&=\angle I_bAT+\angle I_dAD\\
&=\frac12\angle BAC+\frac12\angle CAD\\
&=\frac12\angle BAD.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $O_a$ lies on the angle bisector $AI$. The angle-domain choice above places it on the segment $AI$; similarly, $O_b,O_c,O_d$ lie on the segments $BI,CI,DI$, respectively. The other three assertions follow by symmetry.

Now consider the pair $\omega_a,\omega_c$. Besides the external similitude center $X$, let $U$ be their internal similitude center. Since the two circles share the chord $I_bI_d$, their centers lie on its perpendicular bisector. By Claim 1,
\[
O_aO_c\parallel AC.
\tag{4}
\]
The similitude centers $X,U$ also lie on $O_aO_c$.

Because $O_a\in AI$, $O_c\in CI$, and (4) holds, the triangles $IO_aO_c$ and $IAC$ are similar. Equivalently, one homothety centered at $I$ sends $A,C$ to $O_a,O_c$. Using directed lengths along $IA$ and $IC$, the complementary segments have the same ratio, and hence
\[
\frac{O_aI}{O_cI}
=
\frac{O_aA}{O_cC}
=
\frac{r_a}{r_c}.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{O_aX}{O_cX}
=
\frac{O_aU}{O_cU}
=
\frac{r_a}{r_c},
\]
and, since $I_b,I_d$ lie on both circles,
\[
\frac{O_aI_b}{O_cI_b}
=
\frac{O_aI_d}{O_cI_d}
=
\frac{r_a}{r_c}.
\]
Together with (5), the five points
\[
X,U,I_b,I_d,I
\]
lie on the Apollonius circle of $O_a,O_c$ with ratio $r_a:r_c$.

The two intersections of this Apollonius circle with the line $O_aO_c$ are the internal and external division points $U,X$. Hence
\[
UX
\]
is a diameter of the Apollonius circle. Therefore $IU$ and $IX$ are the internal and external angle bisectors of
\[
\angle O_aIO_c=\angle AIC,
\]
respectively.

On the other hand, the line $UX$, which is the same line as $O_aO_c$, is the perpendicular bisector of the common chord $I_bI_d$. Hence $U$ and $X$ are the two arc midpoints determined by the chord $I_bI_d$ on the Apollonius circle. Consequently, $IX$ and $IU$ are the internal and external angle bisectors of
\[
\angle I_bII_d=\angle BID,
\]
respectively.

Applying the same argument to the pair $\omega_b,\omega_d$ shows that $IY$ is the internal angle bisector of $\angle AIC$ and the external angle bisector of $\angle BID$.

Thus $IX$ and $IY$ are the internal and external angle bisectors of the same angle $\angle BID$. They are perpendicular, and therefore
\[
\angle XIY=90^\circ.
\]
