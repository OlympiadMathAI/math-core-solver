ID: 0420

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2023

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Legendre's Formula, Prime Valuations, Factorials, LTE, Infinite Families

STATEMENT:
For positive integers $n$ and $k\ge2$, let $E_k(n)$ be the greatest nonnegative integer $r$ such that
\[
k^r\mid n!.
\]

Prove that there are infinitely many positive integers $n$ for which
\[
E_{10}(n)>E_9(n),
\]
and infinitely many positive integers $m$ for which
\[
E_{10}(m)<E_9(m).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For a prime $p$, let $v_p(N)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in $N$. Legendre's formula gives
\[
v_p(n!)=\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^j}\right\rfloor.
\]
Since
\[
v_2(n!)\ge v_5(n!),
\]
we have
\[
E_{10}(n)=v_5(n!),
\]
whereas
\[
E_9(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{v_3(n!)}2\right\rfloor.
\]

For every positive integer $\ell$, take
\[
n=5^{2\ell-1}.
\]
Then
\[
E_{10}(n)
=
1+5+\cdots+5^{2\ell-2}
=
\frac{n-1}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
n\equiv2\pmod3,
\]
so
\[
\left\lfloor\frac n3\right\rfloor=\frac{n-2}{3}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_3(n!)
&=
\left\lfloor\frac n3\right\rfloor
+
\left\lfloor\frac n{3^2}\right\rfloor+\cdots\\
&<
\frac{n-2}{3}
+
\frac n{3^2}
+
\frac n{3^3}+\cdots\\
&=
\frac n2-\frac23.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
E_9(n)
\le
\frac{v_3(n!)}2
<
\frac n4-\frac13
<
\frac{n-1}{4}
=
E_{10}(n).
\]
This gives infinitely many $n$ with $E_{10}(n)>E_9(n)$.

For the opposite inequality, take
\[
m=3^{4\ell-2}.
\]
Then
\[
v_3(m!)
=
1+3+\cdots+3^{4\ell-3}
=
\frac{m-1}{2}.
\]
Since $m\equiv1\pmod4$,
\[
E_9(m)=\frac{m-1}{4}.
\tag{2}
\]

Moreover,
\[
m\equiv4\pmod5,
\]
so
\[
\left\lfloor\frac m5\right\rfloor=\frac{m-4}{5}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{10}(m)
&=v_5(m!)\\
&<
\frac{m-4}{5}
+
\frac m{5^2}
+
\frac m{5^3}+\cdots\\
&=
\frac m4-\frac45\\
&<
\frac{m-1}{4}
=
E_9(m).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus infinitely many $m$ satisfy the reverse inequality.

SOLUTION 2:
Fix an integer $b\ge2$ and take
\[
n=5^{3^{b-1}}.
\]
By the lifting-the-exponent lemma,
\[
v_3\left(5^{3^{b-1}}+1\right)
=
v_3(5+1)+v_3\left(3^{b-1}\right)
=
b.
\]
Thus
\[
n\equiv-1\pmod{3^b}.
\tag{1}
\]

Because $n$ is a power of $5$,
\[
E_{10}(n)=v_5(n!)=\frac{n-1}{4}.
\tag{2}
\]
For $1\le j\le b$, relation (1) gives
\[
\left\lfloor\frac n{3^j}\right\rfloor
=
\frac{n-(3^j-1)}{3^j}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_3(n!)
&<
\sum_{j=1}^{b}
\frac{n-(3^j-1)}{3^j}
+
\sum_{j>b}\frac n{3^j}\\
&=
\frac n2-b+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac1{3^j}\\
&<
\frac n2-b+\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\[
E_9(n)
<
\frac n4-\frac b2+\frac14
<
\frac{n-1}{4}
=
E_{10}(n).
\]

Now take
\[
m=3^{2\cdot5^{b-1}}.
\]
Again by LTE,
\[
v_5\left(3^{2\cdot5^{b-1}}+1\right)
=
v_5(3^2+1)+v_5\left(5^{b-1}\right)
=
b,
\]
so
\[
m\equiv-1\pmod{5^b}.
\tag{3}
\]

Since $m$ is a power of $3$ and $m\equiv1\pmod4$,
\[
E_9(m)=\frac{m-1}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]
Using (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{10}(m)
&=v_5(m!)\\
&<
\sum_{j=1}^{b}
\frac{m-(5^j-1)}{5^j}
+
\sum_{j>b}\frac m{5^j}\\
&=
\frac m4-b+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac1{5^j}\\
&<
\frac m4-b+\frac14\\
&<
\frac{m-1}{4}
=
E_9(m).
\end{aligned}
\]
Letting $b$ vary gives infinitely many integers in both families.
