ID: 0319

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incircles, Homothety Centres, Monge's Theorem, Inversion, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral with no two sides parallel. Let
\[
K\in AB,\qquad L\in BC,\qquad M\in CD,\qquad N\in DA
\]
be such that $KLMN$ is a rhombus with
\[
KL\parallel AC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
LM\parallel BD.
\]
Let $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3,\omega_4$ be the incircles of triangles
\[
ANK,\qquad BKL,\qquad CLM,\qquad DMN,
\]
respectively.

Prove that the internal common tangents to $\omega_1$ and $\omega_3$, together with the internal common tangents to $\omega_2$ and $\omega_4$, are concurrent.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $I_i$ and $r_i$ be the centre and radius of $\omega_i$.

Let the internal common tangents to $\omega_1$ and $\omega_3$ meet at $S$. Thus $S$ is the centre of the homothety of negative ratio
\[
-\frac{r_3}{r_1}
\]
that maps $\omega_1$ to $\omega_3$.

Let $T_1$ and $T_3$ be the points where $\omega_1$ and $\omega_3$ touch the parallel sides $NK$ and $LM$, respectively. The homothety maps $T_1$ to $T_3$, so
\[
T_1S:ST_3=r_1:r_3.
\tag{1}
\]

Through $S$, draw
\[
S_1S_3\parallel KL,
\qquad
S_2S_4\parallel LM,
\]
where
\[
S_1\in NK,\quad S_2\in KL,\quad S_3\in LM,\quad S_4\in MN.
\]
The same homothety maps $S_1$ to $S_3$. Hence
\[
I_1S_1\parallel I_3S_3
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
S_1S:SS_3=r_1:r_3.
\tag{2}
\]

We shall prove
\[
S_2S:SS_4=r_2:r_4.
\tag{3}
\]
In a parallelogram, (3) is equivalent to
\[
KS_1:S_1N=r_2:r_4.
\tag{4}
\]
Once (3) is known, the conclusion follows: the corresponding intersection $S'$ of the internal common tangents to $\omega_2$ and $\omega_4$ satisfies both division relations (2) and (3), and there is a unique point inside $KLMN$ satisfying them. Therefore
\[
S'=S.
\]

We now prove (4).

For the original cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, let
\[
I_A,I_B,I_C,I_D
\]
be the incentres of triangles
\[
DAB,\quad ABC,\quad BCD,\quad CDA,
\]
and let their inradii be
\[
r_A,r_B,r_C,r_D.
\]

Translate triangle $CLM$ by the vector $\overrightarrow{LK}$. Its image is a triangle $C'KN$, and together with triangle $AKN$ it forms a quadrilateral
\[
AKC'N
\]
similar to $ABCD$. Consequently,
\[
r_1:r_3=r_A:r_C,
\qquad
r_2:r_4=r_B:r_D.
\tag{5}
\]

The translation maps $S_3$ to $S_1$ and $I_3$ to the incenter $I_3'$ of triangle $KC'N$. From
\[
I_1S_1\parallel I_3S_3
\]
we obtain
\[
I_1,S_1,I_3'\ \text{are collinear}.
\]
We apply the following lemma to the cyclic quadrilateral $AKC'N$.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $ABCD$ be cyclic. Let $I_A$ and $I_C$ be the incentres of triangles $DAB$ and $BCD$, and let $r_B,r_D$ be the inradii of triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$. If
\[
X=I_AI_C\cap BD,
\]
then
\[
\frac{BX}{XD}=\frac{r_B}{r_D}.
\tag{6}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Use directed angles modulo $180^\circ$. Invert about a circle centred at $X$, and denote images by primes.

Since $X,I_A,I_C$ are collinear and $X,B,D$ are collinear, inversion and the angle-bisector properties give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\measuredangle(I_C'I_A',I_A'D')
&=\measuredangle(XI_A',I_A'D')\\
&=\measuredangle(XD,DI_A)\\
&=\frac12\angle BDA\\
&=\frac12\angle BCA\\
&=\angle ACI_B.
\end{aligned}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\measuredangle(I_A'I_C',I_C'D')=\angle CAI_B.
\]
Therefore
\[
\triangle I_C'I_A'D'\sim\triangle ACI_B.
\tag{7}
\]
The analogous angle calculation gives
\[
\triangle I_C'I_A'B'\sim\triangle ACI_D.
\tag{8}
\]

It follows from (7)--(8) that the quadrilaterals
\[
I_C'B'I_A'D'
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
AI_DCI_B
\]
are similar.

Let
\[
Y=AC\cap I_BI_D.
\]
The perpendicular distances of $I_B$ and $I_D$ from $AC$ are $r_B$ and $r_D$, respectively. Hence
\[
\frac{I_BY}{YI_D}=\frac{r_B}{r_D}.
\]
Under the similarity of the two quadrilaterals, $Y$ corresponds to $X$, $I_B$ to $D'$, and $I_D$ to $B'$. Thus
\[
\frac{D'X}{B'X}=\frac{r_B}{r_D}.
\]
If the power of the inversion is $\rho$, then
\[
XD'=\frac{\rho}{XD},
\qquad
XB'=\frac{\rho}{XB}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{BX}{XD}
=
\frac{XD'}{XB'}
=
\frac{r_B}{r_D},
\]
proving the lemma. $\square$

In the quadrilateral $AKC'N$, the point $S_1$ is the intersection of the line joining the two opposite incentres with the diagonal $KN$. The lemma and (5) therefore give
\[
\frac{KS_1}{S_1N}
=
\frac{r_B}{r_D}
=
\frac{r_2}{r_4}.
\]
This is (4), and the proof is complete.

SOLUTION 2:
We use the following form of Monge's theorem.

\textbf{Lemma.}
Let $E$ and $F$ be distinct points, and let
\[
\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3,\omega_4
\]
be four circles lying in the same half-plane with respect to $EF$. For $i\ne j$, let
\[
O_{ij}^{+}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
O_{ij}^{-}
\]
denote the centres of the homotheties of positive and negative ratio, respectively, that map $\omega_i$ to $\omega_j$.

Suppose
\[
E=O_{12}^{+}=O_{34}^{+}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
F=O_{23}^{+}=O_{41}^{+}.
\]
Then
\[
O_{13}^{-}=O_{24}^{-}.
\tag{1}
\]

\textit{Proof.}
Apply Monge's theorem to the triples
\[
(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_4)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(\omega_1,\omega_3,\omega_4).
\]
It follows that both $O_{24}^{-}$ and $O_{13}^{-}$ lie on the line
\[
EO_{14}^{-}.
\]
Similarly, applying Monge's theorem to the appropriate triples containing $\omega_3$ and $\omega_4$, both points lie on
\[
FO_{34}^{-}.
\]
These two lines are distinct, so their intersection is unique. Hence
\[
O_{13}^{-}=O_{24}^{-}.
\]
$\square$

We now verify the hypotheses of the lemma.

Let
\[
E=AB\cap CD,
\qquad
F=BC\cap DA.
\]
Choose the naming so that $B$ lies on both segments $AE$ and $CF$.

The cyclicity of $ABCD$ gives
\[
\triangle ECA\sim\triangle EBD.
\]
Using this similarity, together with
\[
KN\parallel BD
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
MN\parallel AC,
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{AN}{ND}
=
\frac{AN}{AD}\cdot\frac{AD}{ND}
=
\frac{KN}{BD}\cdot\frac{AC}{NM}
=
\frac{AC}{BD}
=
\frac{AE}{ED}.
\]
By the angle-bisector theorem, $N$ lies on the internal angle bisector of $\angle AED$. The same argument shows that $L$ lies on that angle bisector. Thus
\[
E,L,N\ \text{are collinear}.
\tag{2}
\]

Similarly,
\[
F,K,M\ \text{are collinear},
\tag{3}
\]
and this line is the internal angle bisector of $\angle AFB$.

Because $KLMN$ is a rhombus, reflection in its diagonal $LN$ interchanges $K$ and $M$. By (2), the quadrilateral bounded by the lines
\[
EK,\quad KL,\quad LM,\quad ME
\]
is a kite, and therefore has an incircle; call it $\omega_0$.

The circles $\omega_0$ and $\omega_2$ have the two common external tangents $EK$ and $KL$, so
\[
O_{02}^{+}=K.
\]
Similarly,
\[
O_{03}^{+}=M.
\]
Monge's theorem applied to $\omega_0,\omega_2,\omega_3$ gives
\[
O_{23}^{+}\in KM.
\]
But $BC$ is also a common external tangent to $\omega_2$ and $\omega_3$, so
\[
O_{23}^{+}\in BC.
\]
By (3),
\[
KM\cap BC=F,
\]
and hence
\[
F=O_{23}^{+}.
\]

The same argument applied cyclically yields
\[
E=O_{12}^{+}=O_{34}^{+}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
F=O_{23}^{+}=O_{41}^{+}.
\]
The lemma now gives
\[
O_{13}^{-}=O_{24}^{-}.
\]
These are precisely the intersection points of the two pairs of internal common tangents, so all four tangents are concurrent.
