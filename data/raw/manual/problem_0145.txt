ID: 0145

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2014

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Double Factorials, p-adic Valuations, Nearest Integers, Fractional Parts

STATEMENT:
For a real number $x$, let $\|x\|$ denote the distance from $x$ to the nearest integer. Prove that for every pair $(a,b)$ of positive integers there exist an odd prime $p$ and a positive integer $k$ such that
\[
\left\|\frac{a}{p^k}\right\|
+
\left\|\frac{b}{p^k}\right\|
+
\left\|\frac{a+b}{p^k}\right\|
=1.
\tag{1}
\]

SOLUTION 1:
For every real $x$,
\[
\left\lfloor x+\frac12\right\rfloor
=x\pm\|x\|,
\tag{2}
\]
because the left-hand side is an integer nearest to $x$.

For an odd prime $p$ and a nonzero rational number $r$, let $v_p(r)$ denote its $p$-adic valuation. We first prove a counting formula for odd double factorials:
\[
v_p((2n-1)!!)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^k}+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, for each $k$, the multiples of $p^k$ among
\[
1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1
\]
are exactly the numbers $(2\ell-1)p^k$ satisfying
\[
0<(2\ell-1)p^k\le2n.
\]
The number of such $\ell$ is
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^k}+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\]
Summing this count over all $k$ counts every factor once for each power of $p$ dividing it, proving (3).

Now consider the positive rational number
\[
R=
\frac{(2a+2b-1)!!}{(2a-1)!!(2b-1)!!}
=
\prod_{j=1}^{b}\frac{2a+2j-1}{2j-1}.
\]
Every factor in the product is greater than $1$, so $R>1$. Therefore some prime $p$ satisfies
\[
v_p(R)>0.
\]
The numerator and denominator of $R$ are odd, so such a prime $p$ is odd.

By (3),
\[
v_p(R)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_k,
\]
where
\[
d_k=
\left\lfloor\frac{a+b}{p^k}+\frac12\right\rfloor
-
\left\lfloor\frac{a}{p^k}+\frac12\right\rfloor
-
\left\lfloor\frac{b}{p^k}+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\tag{4}
\]
Since the sum is positive, some $d_k$ is a positive integer, so $d_k\ge1$.

Apply (2) to the three terms in (4). Their linear parts cancel, giving
\[
d_k
=
\pm\left\|\frac{a+b}{p^k}\right\|
\pm\left\|\frac{a}{p^k}\right\|
\pm\left\|\frac{b}{p^k}\right\|.
\tag{5}
\]
Because $p^k$ is odd and the numerators are integers, none of these rational numbers is a half-integer. Hence each of the three distances is strictly less than $1/2$.

If at least one sign in (5) were negative, the right-hand side would be strictly less than $1$, because at most two positive terms would remain and each is below $1/2$. This is incompatible with the positive integer $d_k\ge1$. Therefore all three signs are positive. Their sum is less than $3/2$, so the integer $d_k$ must equal $1$. Thus
\[
\left\|\frac{a}{p^k}\right\|
+
\left\|\frac{b}{p^k}\right\|
+
\left\|\frac{a+b}{p^k}\right\|
=d_k=1,
\]
which is exactly (1).
