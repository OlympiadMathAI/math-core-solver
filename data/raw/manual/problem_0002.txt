ID: 0002

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities 

STATEMENT:
Let the real numbers $a,b,c,d$ satisfy the relations $a+b+c+d=6$ and $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=12.$ Prove that
\[36 \leq 4 \left(a^3+b^3+c^3+d^3\right) - \left(a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4 \right) \leq 48.\]

SOLUTION 1:
Let $S = \sum_{\text{cyc}} (4a^3-a^4)$. The upper bound on $S$ is proved by noting that\begin{align*} 	0 &\le \sum_{\text{cyc}} a^2(a-2)^2 	= \sum_{\text{cyc}} \left( a^4-4a^3+4a^2 \right) \\ 	&= -S + 4(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2) = 48-S. \end{align*}
For the lower bound, we need an additional estimate.

Claim: We have $a,b,c,d \in [0,3]$.
Proof. As $3(b^2+c^2+d^2) \ge (b+c+d)^2$, we get $3(12-a^2) \ge (6-a)^2 \implies 0 \le a \le 3$. $\blacksquare$

With this, we may now write\begin{align*} 	0 &\ge \sum_{\text{cyc}} (a+1)(a-1)^2(a-3) 	= \sum_{\text{cyc}} \left( a^4-4a^3+2a^2+4a-3 \right) \\ 	&= -S + 2(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2) + 4(a+b+c+d) - 12 \\ 	&= -S + 2 \cdot 12 + 4 \cdot 6 - 12 = 36 - S \end{align*}as desired.
