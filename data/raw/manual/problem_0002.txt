ID: 0002

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2010

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Inequalities

STATEMENT:
Let the real numbers $a,b,c,d$ satisfy
\[
a+b+c+d=6,\qquad a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=12.
\]
Prove that
\[
36\le 4(a^3+b^3+c^3+d^3)-(a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4)\le 48.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Set
\[
E=4(a^3+b^3+c^3+d^3)-(a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4).
\]
Expanding $(u-1)^4$ and using the two given relations, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
E
&=-\sum_{u\in\{a,b,c,d\}}(u-1)^4
  +6\sum_{u\in\{a,b,c,d\}}u^2
  -4\sum_{u\in\{a,b,c,d\}}u+4\\
&=52-\sum_{u\in\{a,b,c,d\}}(u-1)^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Put
\[
x=a-1,\qquad y=b-1,\qquad z=c-1,\qquad t=d-1.
\]
Then
\[
x^2+y^2+z^2+t^2
=(a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2)-2(a+b+c+d)+4=4.
\]
Thus it is enough to prove
\[
4\le x^4+y^4+z^4+t^4\le 16.
\]
The lower bound follows from Cauchy-Schwarz:
\[
(x^2+y^2+z^2+t^2)^2\le 4(x^4+y^4+z^4+t^4),
\]
so
\[
x^4+y^4+z^4+t^4\ge \frac{4^2}{4}=4.
\]
For the upper bound, all cross terms in the square are nonnegative, hence
\[
x^4+y^4+z^4+t^4
\le (x^2+y^2+z^2+t^2)^2=16.
\]
Consequently,
\[
36=52-16\le E\le 52-4=48.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
We first prove that
\[
0\le a,b,c,d\le 3.
\]
Indeed, by symmetry it suffices to treat $d$. Since
\[
a+b+c=6-d,\qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=12-d^2,
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
12-d^2\ge \frac{(6-d)^2}{3}.
\]
This is equivalent to
\[
2d(d-3)\le 0,
\]
and therefore $0\le d\le 3$. The same argument works for the other variables.

For every real $x$,
\[
x^2(x-2)^2\ge 0,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
4x^3-x^4\le 4x^2.
\]
Summing this inequality for $x=a,b,c,d$ yields
\[
4\sum a^3-\sum a^4\le 4\sum a^2=48.
\]

For the other direction, if $0\le x\le 3$, then
\[
(x+1)(x-1)^2(x-3)\le 0.
\]
After expansion this becomes
\[
4x^3-x^4\ge 2x^2+4x-3.
\]
Again summing for $a,b,c,d$, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
4\sum a^3-\sum a^4
&\ge 2\sum a^2+4\sum a-12\\
&=2\cdot 12+4\cdot 6-12=36.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves both required inequalities.

SOLUTION 3:
For the upper bound, apply AM-GM to each pair $u^4$ and $4u^2$:
\[
u^4+4u^2\ge 2\sqrt{u^4\cdot 4u^2}=4|u^3|\ge 4u^3.
\]
Summing over $u=a,b,c,d$ gives
\[
(a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4)+48\ge 4(a^3+b^3+c^3+d^3),
\]
which is precisely the upper bound.

For the lower bound, as in Solution 2 we have $a,b,c,d\in[0,3]$. Reorder them so that
\[
0\le a\le b\le c\le d.
\]
Then
\[
a+b\le b+c\le \frac{2}{3}(b+c+d)\le 4.
\]
Therefore $ab\le 4$ and $bc\le 4$, so
\[
4a-a^2\le 4b-b^2\le 4c-c^2.
\]
Since also $a^2\le b^2\le c^2$, Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&a^2(4a-a^2)+b^2(4b-b^2)+c^2(4c-c^2)\\
&\qquad\ge \frac{1}{3}(a^2+b^2+c^2)
\bigl(4(a+b+c)-(a^2+b^2+c^2)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
\[
a+b+c=6-d,\qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=12-d^2,
\]
and then adding $4d^3-d^4$, we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
4\sum a^3-\sum a^4
&\ge \frac{(12-d^2)(d^2-4d+12)}{3}+4d^3-d^4\\
&=36+\frac{4}{3}(3-d)(d-1)(d^2-3).
\end{aligned}
\]
Now $d^2\ge \frac{a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2}{4}=3$, while $d>1$ and $d\le 3$. Hence the final product is nonnegative, and the lower bound follows.
