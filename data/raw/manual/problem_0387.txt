ID: 0387

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2022

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Incircles, Arc Midpoints, Radical Centres, Monge's Theorem, Homothety

STATEMENT:
Let
\[
AA'BCC'B'
\]
be a convex cyclic hexagon in this order.

Assume that $AC$ is tangent to the incircle of triangle $A'B'C'$, and that $A'C'$ is tangent to the incircle of triangle $ABC$.

Let
\[
X=AB\cap A'B',
\qquad
Y=BC\cap B'C'.
\]
Prove that, if $XBYB'$ is a convex quadrilateral, then it has an incircle.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $\Omega$ be the circumcircle of the hexagon. Denote the incircles of triangles
\[
ABC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A'B'C'
\]
by $\omega$ and $\omega'$, with centres $I$ and $I'$, respectively.

Let $N\ne B$ and $N'\ne B'$ be the second intersections of
\[
BI
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B'I'
\]
with $\Omega$. Then $N$ and $N'$ are the midpoints of the appropriate arcs $AC$ and $A'C'$. Hence, if $O$ is the centre of $\Omega$,
\[
ON\perp AC,\qquad
ON'\perp A'C',\qquad
ON=ON'.
\tag{1}
\]

Both $\omega$ and $\omega'$ are tangent to the two lines
\[
AC
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
A'C'.
\]
The side choices in the convex configuration place their centres on the same angle-bisector line through
\[
P=AC\cap A'C'.
\]
Thus
\[
P,I,I'\ \text{are collinear},
\]
and $II'$ is an angle bisector of the two lines. From (1), the chord $NN'$ is parallel to the same angle-bisector direction:
\[
NN'\parallel II'.
\tag{2}
\]

Because $B,N,N',B'$ lie on $\Omega$, equation (2) gives
\[
\measuredangle(BI,II')
=
\measuredangle(BN,NN')
=
\measuredangle(BB',B'N')
=
\measuredangle(BB',B'I').
\]
Therefore
\[
B,I,I',B'\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let $M$ be the antipode of $N'$ on $\Omega$.

Consider the circle
\[
\Gamma_1
\]
with centre $N$ and radius $NA=NC$, and the circle
\[
\Gamma_2
\]
with centre $M$ and radius $MA'=MC'$.

The point $P$ has equal powers with respect to these circles:
\[
PN^2-NA^2=PA\cdot PC
\]
and
\[
PM^2-MA'^2=PA'\cdot PC',
\]
while the two products are equal to the power of $P$ with respect to $\Omega$. Hence $P$ lies on the radical axis of $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$.

Since $MN\perp NN'$ and, by (2), $IP\parallel NN'$, that radical axis is line $IP$. The standard arc-midpoint identity gives
\[
NI=NA,
\]
so $I\in\Gamma_1$. Therefore
\[
I\in\Gamma_2,
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
MI=MA'=MC'.
\tag{4}
\]

Let $Z\ne M$ be the second intersection of $MI$ with $\Omega$. The point $M$ is an arc midpoint of $A'C'$ in triangle $ZA'C'$, and (4) is the usual arc-midpoint characterization of the incenter. Thus
\[
I
\]
is the incenter of triangle $ZA'C'$. Since the circle centred at $I$ tangent to $A'C'$ is $\omega$, the circle $\omega$ is also the incircle of triangle $ZA'C'$.

Let $\omega_c$ be the incircle of triangle $C'B'Z$, and let its centre be $I_c$.

The incenter angle formulas and the cyclicity of the hexagon give
\[
\angle ZIC'
=
90^\circ+\frac12\angle ZA'C'
=
90^\circ+\frac12\angle ZB'C'
=
\angle ZI_cC'.
\]
Hence
\[
Z,I,I_c,C'\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{5}
\]

Similarly,
\[
\angle B'I'C'
=
90^\circ+\frac12\angle B'A'C'
=
90^\circ+\frac12\angle B'ZC'
=
\angle B'I_cC',
\]
so
\[
B',I',I_c,C'\ \text{are concyclic}.
\tag{6}
\]

Let $D$ be the external homothety centre of $\omega$ and $\omega_c$. Since these two circles are tangent to the common line $C'Z$,
\[
D=II_c\cap C'Z.
\]
By (5), the lines $II_c$ and $C'Z$ are radical axes of the three circles
\[
(II'I_c),\qquad
(ZII_cC'),\qquad
\Omega.
\]
Thus $D$ lies on the radical axis of
\[
\Omega
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(II'I_c).
\tag{7}
\]

Likewise, let $D'$ be the external homothety centre of $\omega'$ and $\omega_c$. Since their common tangent is $B'C'$,
\[
D'=I'I_c\cap B'C',
\]
and (6) shows that $D'$ lies on the same radical axis in (7).

Let $T$ be the external homothety centre of $\omega$ and $\omega'$. Monge's theorem applied to
\[
\omega,\qquad\omega',\qquad\omega_c
\]
gives
\[
D,D',T
\]
collinear. Hence $T$ lies on the radical axis in (7).

By definition,
\[
T\in II'.
\tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
T'=BB'\cap II'.
\]
From the cyclic quadrilateral (3),
\[
T'B\cdot T'B'=T'I\cdot T'I'.
\]
The left side is the power of $T'$ with respect to $\Omega$, and the right side is its power with respect to $(II'I_c)$. Thus $T'$ also lies on the radical axis in (7). Together with (8), this yields
\[
T'=T.
\]
Therefore
\[
T\in BB'.
\tag{9}
\]

Now construct a circle $\Omega_0$ tangent to the three lines
\[
A'B',\qquad B'C',\qquad AB
\]
on the same chosen sides as $\omega'$.

The external homothety centre of $\omega'$ and $\Omega_0$ is $B'$, because the two circles have the common tangents $A'B'$ and $B'C'$. By Monge's theorem, the external homothety centre of $\Omega_0$ and $\omega$ lies on
\[
B'T=BB'
\]
by (9).

The circles $\Omega_0$ and $\omega$ are both tangent to $AB$, so their external homothety centre also lies on $AB$. Consequently it is
\[
AB\cap BB'=B.
\]
The homothety centred at $B$ mapping $\Omega_0$ to $\omega$ preserves every line through $B$. Since $BC$ is the second tangent from $B$ to $\omega$, it follows that
\[
BC
\]
is tangent to $\Omega_0$ as well.

Thus $\Omega_0$ is tangent to all four sidelines
\[
XB\subset AB,\qquad
BY\subset BC,\qquad
YB'\subset B'C',\qquad
B'X\subset A'B'.
\]
The assumed convexity places $\Omega_0$ inside the quadrilateral. Hence $XBYB'$ has an incircle.
