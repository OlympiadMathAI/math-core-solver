ID: 0336

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Pigeonhole Principle, Base Expansions, Subset Sums, Counting

STATEMENT:
Let $A$ be a finite set of integers, and let $m\ge2$ be an integer. Suppose there are nonempty subsets
\[
B_1,B_2,\ldots,B_m
\]
of $A$ whose element sums are respectively
\[
m^1,m^2,\ldots,m^m.
\]
Prove that
\[
|A|\ge\frac m2.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
Write
\[
A=\{a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k\}
\]
and suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
k<\frac m2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
s_i=\sum_{a_j\in B_i}a_j=m^i
\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\]

Consider all expressions
\[
F(c_1,\ldots,c_m)
=
c_1s_1+c_2s_2+\cdots+c_ms_m,
\]
where
\[
c_i\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}.
\]
There are exactly
\[
m^m
\]
choices of the vector $(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$.

On the other hand, after expanding each $s_i$ as a sum of elements of $A$, every value of $F$ has the form
\[
\alpha_1a_1+\cdots+\alpha_ka_k,
\]
where
\[
0\le\alpha_j\le m(m-1).
\]
Thus the number of possible values of $F$ is at most
\[
\bigl(m(m-1)+1\bigr)^k
<
(m^2)^k
=
m^{2k}
<
m^m
\]
by (1).

The pigeonhole principle therefore gives two distinct digit vectors
\[
(c_1,\ldots,c_m)\ne(d_1,\ldots,d_m)
\]
with the same value of $F$. Since $s_i=m^i$, this says
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_im^i
=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_im^i.
\]
But all the digits $c_i,d_i$ lie in
\[
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\},
\]
so uniqueness of base-$m$ representation forces
\[
c_i=d_i
\qquad(1\le i\le m),
\]
a contradiction.

Hence
\[
|A|\ge\frac m2.
\]
