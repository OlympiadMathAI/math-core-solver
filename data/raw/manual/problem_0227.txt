ID: 0227

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2017

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Radical Center, Angle Bisectors, Symmetry, Power of a Point, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

STATEMENT:
Let $ABCC_1B_1A_1$ be a convex hexagon such that
\[
AB=BC,
\]
and suppose that the segments $AA_1$, $BB_1$, and $CC_1$ have the same perpendicular bisector. Let
\[
D=AC_1\cap A_1C.
\]
Let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$, and suppose that $\omega$ meets the circumcircle of $\triangle A_1BC_1$ again at $E\ne B$. Prove that the lines $BB_1$ and $DE$ meet at a point of $\omega$.

SOLUTION 1:
If $AA_1=CC_1$, the hexagon is symmetric about the line $BB_1$, and the circles $(ABC)$ and $(A_1BC_1)$ are tangent. This contradicts the existence of the second common point $E\ne B$. Hence
\[
AA_1\ne CC_1.
\]
After interchanging $A,A_1$ with $C,C_1$ if necessary, assume
\[
AA_1<CC_1.
\]

Let $R$ be the radical center of the following three circles:
\[
(AEBC),
\qquad
(A_1EBC_1),
\qquad
(ACC_1A_1).
\]
Their pairwise radical axes are respectively
\[
AC,
\qquad
A_1C_1,
\qquad
BE,
\]
so these three lines meet at $R$. The lines $AC$ and $A_1C_1$ are symmetric about the common perpendicular bisector of $AA_1$ and $CC_1$. Consequently, $R$ lies on this common perpendicular bisector. The diagonals $AC_1$ and $A_1C$ are also symmetric about it, so their intersection $D$ lies on it as well. Thus $RD$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle ADC$.

Let $F\ne D$ be the second intersection of $RD$ with the circumcircle of $\triangle ACD$. Taking powers of $R$ gives
\[
RB\cdot RE=RA\cdot RC=RD\cdot RF.
\tag{1}
\]
Therefore
\[
B,E,D,F
\]
are concyclic. Notice that the second product in (1) is $RD\cdot RF$, as required by the power of $R$.

Since $RDF$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle ADC$, the point $F$ is an arc midpoint of the circle $(ACD)$ and hence lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$.

Because $AB=BC$, the point $B$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. Let $M$ be the point diametrically opposite $B$ on $\omega$. Then $M$ also lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. Hence
\[
B,F,M
\]
are collinear.

Let $X\ne E$ be the second intersection of $DE$ with $\omega$. Since $BM$ is a diameter of $\omega$,
\[
\angle BXM=90^\circ.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, using the cyclic quadrilateral $BEDF$ and the collinearity of $B,F,M$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle EXM
&=180^\circ-\angle MBE\\
&=180^\circ-\angle FBE\\
&=\angle EDF.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
MX\parallel FD.
\tag{3}
\]
The line $RDF$ is the common perpendicular bisector of $BB_1$, so
\[
BB_1\perp FD.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) show that both $BX$ and $BB_1$ are the line through $B$ perpendicular to $FD$. Hence
\[
X\in BB_1.
\]
Since $X\in DE\cap\omega$, the result follows.

SOLUTION 2:
Let $M$ be the point diametrically opposite $B$ on $\omega$. Let
\[
R=AC\cap A_1C_1\cap BE.
\]
As in Solution 1, $R$ lies on the common perpendicular bisector of $AA_1,BB_1,CC_1$, and the line $DR$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle ADC$.

Since $AB=BC$, the point $B$ is an arc midpoint of the circle $\omega$. Hence the line $BER$ is the external angle bisector of $\angle CEA$, while $EM$ is its internal angle bisector.

Let the internal angle bisector of $\angle ADC$ meet $AC$ at $S$, and let the internal angle bisector $EM$ of $\angle CEA$ meet $AC$ at $S'$. Applying the internal and external angle-bisector theorems in the two triangles gives
\[
\frac{AS}{CS}
=
\frac{AD}{CD}
=
\frac{AR}{CR}
=
\frac{AE}{CE}
=
\frac{AS'}{CS'}.
\]
Therefore
\[
S=S'.
\]

The internal and external angle bisectors of an angle are perpendicular. Thus
\[
\angle RDS=90^\circ
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\angle SER=90^\circ,
\]
so
\[
R,S,D,E
\]
are concyclic.

Let
\[
X=BB_1\cap DE.
\]
Both $BB_1$ and $DS$ are perpendicular to $DR$, so
\[
\angle EXB=\angle EDS.
\tag{1}
\]
From the cyclic quadrilateral $RSDE$,
\[
\angle EDS=\angle ERS.
\tag{2}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
SR\perp BM
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
ER\perp EM,
\]
so the angle between $ER$ and $RS$ equals the angle between $EM$ and $MB$:
\[
\angle ERS=\angle EMB.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1)--(3),
\[
\angle EXB=\angle EMB.
\]
Therefore $E,X,B,M$ are concyclic. Since $E,B,M$ lie on $\omega$, we conclude that
\[
X\in\omega.
\]
Thus $BB_1$ and $DE$ meet on $\omega$.
