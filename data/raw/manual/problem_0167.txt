ID: 0167

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Recurrence Relations, Floor Function, 2-adic Valuations, Modular Arithmetic

STATEMENT:
Determine all positive integers $M$ for which the sequence $a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots$, defined by
\[
a_0=\frac{2M+1}{2}
\]
and
\[
a_{k+1}=a_k\lfloor a_k\rfloor
\qquad (k=0,1,2,\ldots),
\]
contains at least one integer term. Here, $\lfloor x\rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer not exceeding $x$.

SOLUTION 1:
The answer is
\[
\boxed{M\ge 2}.
\]

Define
\[
b_k=2a_k.
\]
Since $b_0=2M+1$ is an integer and
\[
b_{k+1}=2a_{k+1}
=2a_k\lfloor a_k\rfloor
=b_k\left\lfloor\frac{b_k}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
every $b_k$ is an integer.

Assume that no term $a_k$ is an integer. Then every $b_k$ is odd, so
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{b_k}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{b_k-1}{2},
\]
and therefore
\[
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_k(b_k-1)}2.
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that
\[
b_{k+1}-3
=\frac{b_k(b_k-1)}2-3
=\frac{(b_k-3)(b_k+2)}2.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose that $M\ge2$. Then $b_0=2M+1>3$. Equation (2) shows inductively that $b_k>3$ for every $k$. Let
\[
c_k=v_2(b_k-3),
\]
the exponent of $2$ in $b_k-3$. Because $b_k$ is odd, $b_k+2$ is odd, and (2) gives
\[
c_{k+1}=c_k-1.
\]
Thus $c_0,c_1,c_2,\ldots$ would be a strictly decreasing sequence of positive integers, which is impossible. Hence for every $M\ge2$, some $a_k$ is an integer.

It remains to inspect $M=1$. Then $a_0=3/2$, and
\[
a_{k+1}=\frac32\left\lfloor\frac32\right\rfloor=\frac32
\]
whenever $a_k=3/2$. Hence the sequence is constantly $3/2$ and contains no integer.

Therefore the required positive integers are exactly all $M\ge2$.

SOLUTION 2:
Again let $b_k=2a_k$. If no $a_k$ is an integer, then every $b_k$ is odd and
\[
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_k(b_k-1)}2.
\tag{1}
\]

We claim that
\[
b_k\equiv3\pmod{2^m}
\tag{2}
\]
for every $k\ge0$ and every $m\ge1$.

For $m=1$, this is true because every $b_k$ is odd. Suppose (2) holds for a fixed $m$ and all $k$. Write
\[
b_k=2^m d_k+3.
\]
Using (1),
\[
b_{k+1}
=(2^m d_k+3)(2^{m-1}d_k+1)
\equiv 3\cdot2^{m-1}d_k+3
\pmod{2^m}.
\]
But the induction hypothesis also gives $b_{k+1}\equiv3\pmod{2^m}$. Hence $d_k$ is even, and therefore
\[
b_k\equiv3\pmod{2^{m+1}}.
\]
This completes the induction on $m$.

For any fixed $k$, the integer $b_k-3$ is divisible by every power of $2$, so $b_k=3$. In particular,
\[
b_0=2M+1=3,
\]
which gives $M=1$.

Thus $M=1$ is the only positive integer for which the sequence can avoid integer terms. As checked above, it indeed does. Consequently the answer is again
\[
\boxed{M\ge2}.
\]
