ID: 0178

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2016

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Functional Equations, Injectivity, Iteration, Reciprocal Relations

STATEMENT:
Let $\mathbb R_{>0}$ be the set of positive real numbers. Find all functions
\[
f:\mathbb R_{>0}\to\mathbb R_{>0}
\]
such that
\[
x f(x^2)f(f(y))+f(yf(x))
=
f(xy)\bigl(f(f(x^2))+f(f(y^2))\bigr)
\tag{1}
\]
for all positive real numbers $x,y$.

SOLUTION 1:
The only solution is
\[
\boxed{f(x)=\frac1x}.
\]

Taking $x=y=1$ in (1) gives
\[
f(1)f(f(1))+f(f(1))=2f(1)f(f(1)).
\]
All values are positive, so
\[
f(1)=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Interchanging $x$ and $y$ in (1) and comparing the two equations gives
\[
x f(x^2)f(f(y))+f(yf(x))
=
y f(y^2)f(f(x))+f(xf(y)).
\tag{3}
\]
Putting $y=1$ in (3) and using (2), we obtain
\[
x f(x^2)=f(x).
\tag{4}
\]
Putting $y=1$ in the original equation and then using (4), we get
\[
f(x)+f(f(x))=f(x)\bigl(f(f(x^2))+1\bigr),
\]
so
\[
f(f(x^2))=\frac{f(f(x))}{f(x)}.
\tag{5}
\]

Applying (4) with $f(x)$ in place of $x$, and then using (5) and (4), yields
\[
f(f(x)^2)
=
\frac{f(f(x))}{f(x)}
=
f(f(x^2))
=
f\left(\frac{f(x)}x\right).
\tag{6}
\]

We now prove that $f$ is injective. Using (4) and (5), equation (1) can be written as
\[
f(x)f(f(y))+f(yf(x))
=
f(xy)\left(\frac{f(f(x))}{f(x)}+\frac{f(f(y))}{f(y)}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Taking $y=x$ in (7) and using (4), we find
\[
f(xf(x))
=
f(f(x))\left(\frac2x-f(x)\right).
\tag{8}
\]
On the other hand, equation (3), together with (4), becomes
\[
f(x)f(f(y))+f(yf(x))
=
f(y)f(f(x))+f(xf(y)).
\tag{9}
\]
Suppose $f(x)=f(y)$. Then (9) gives
\[
f(yf(y))=f(yf(x))=f(xf(y))=f(xf(x)).
\]
Using (8), and also $f(f(x))=f(f(y))$, we obtain
\[
f(f(x))\left(\frac2y-f(x)\right)
=
f(f(x))\left(\frac2x-f(x)\right).
\]
Since $f(f(x))>0$, it follows that $x=y$. Thus $f$ is injective.

By injectivity, (6) gives
\[
f(x)^2=\frac{f(x)}x.
\]
As $f(x)>0$,
\[
f(x)=\frac1x.
\]
A direct substitution verifies that this function satisfies (1).

SOLUTION 2:
Again, putting $x=y=1$ gives $f(1)=1$. Setting $x=1$ in (1), we obtain
\[
f(f(y))+f(y)=f(y)\bigl(1+f(f(y^2))\bigr),
\]
and hence
\[
f(f(y))=f(y)f(f(y^2)).
\tag{1}
\]
Setting $y=1$ in the original equation and using (1) gives
\[
x f(x^2)=f(x).
\tag{2}
\]

Now replace $y$ by $1/x$ in the original equation. Relation (2) gives
\[
f\left(\frac{f(x)}x\right)=f(f(x^2)).
\]
After cancelling this common term and using (1) with $y=1/x$, we obtain
\[
f(x)f\left(\frac1x\right)=1.
\tag{3}
\]

Replace $x,y$ in the original equation by $1/x,1/y$ and use (3), also with the positive arguments $f(x)$ and $f(y)$. The transformed equation is
\[
\frac{1}{x f(x^2)f(f(y))}
+
\frac{1}{f(yf(x))}
=
\frac1{f(xy)}
\left(
\frac1{f(f(x^2))}+
\frac1{f(f(y^2))}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Let
\[
A=x f(x^2)f(f(y)),\quad
B=f(yf(x)),\quad
D=f(f(x^2)),\quad
E=f(f(y^2)).
\]
The original equation says $A+B=f(xy)(D+E)$. Clearing denominators in (4) and using this equality gives
\[
f(xy)^2DE=AB.
\]
Finally, (1) and (2) give
\[
D=\frac{f(f(x))}{f(x)},
\qquad
E=\frac{f(f(y))}{f(y)},
\qquad
x f(x^2)=f(x).
\]
After cancellation, we obtain
\[
f(xy)^2f(f(x))
=
f(x)^2f(y)f(yf(x)).
\tag{5}
\]

Put $y=f(x)$ in (5). Using (2) with $f(x)$ in place of $x$ gives
\[
f(xf(x))^2=f(x)f(f(x)).
\tag{6}
\]
Next put $y=x$ in (5), square the resulting equality, and use (2) and (6). This yields
\[
f(f(x))=x^4f(x)^3.
\tag{7}
\]

Finally, combine (1), (2), and (7):
\[
\begin{aligned}
y^4f(y)^3
&=f(f(y))\\
&=f(y)f(f(y^2))\\
&=f(y)\,y^8f(y^2)^3\\
&=y^5f(y)^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $y,f(y)>0$, this implies
\[
f(y)=\frac1y.
\]
As checked in Solution 1, this function works.
