ID: 0333

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2021

TOPIC: Algebra

SUBTOPICS: Permutations, Floor Function, Binary Decomposition, Induction

STATEMENT:
Given a positive integer $n$, determine the smallest possible value of
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{a_1}{1}\right\rfloor+
\left\lfloor\frac{a_2}{2}\right\rfloor+
\cdots+
\left\lfloor\frac{a_n}{n}\right\rfloor,
\]
where
\[
(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n)
\]
ranges over all permutations of
\[
(1,2,\ldots,n).
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The minimum is
\[
\boxed{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1}.
\]

Choose the integer $k\ge0$ such that
\[
2^k\le n<2^{k+1}.
\]

We first construct a permutation attaining $k+1$. Use the blocks
\[
(a_1)=(1),
\]
\[
(a_2,a_3)=(3,2),
\]
\[
(a_4,a_5,a_6,a_7)=(7,4,5,6),
\]
and in general
\[
(a_{2^j},\ldots,a_{2^{j+1}-1})
=
(2^{j+1}-1,2^j,2^j+1,\ldots,2^{j+1}-2)
\]
for
\[
0\le j<k.
\]
For the last, possibly incomplete, block, set
\[
(a_{2^k},\ldots,a_n)
=
(n,2^k,2^k+1,\ldots,n-1).
\]
Each block has the form
\[
(a_p,\ldots,a_q)=(q,p,p+1,\ldots,q-1)
\]
with
\[
q<2p.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{i=p}^{q}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lfloor\frac qp\right\rfloor+
\sum_{i=p+1}^{q}\left\lfloor\frac{i-1}{i}\right\rfloor
=1.
\]
There are $k+1$ blocks, so the value $k+1$ is attainable.

We now prove the lower bound.

\textbf{Claim.}
If
\[
b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_{2^r}
\]
are distinct positive integers, then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2^r}\left\lfloor\frac{b_i}{i}\right\rfloor
\ge r+1.
\tag{1}
\]

We use induction on $r$. For $r=0$, the assertion is
\[
\lfloor b_1\rfloor\ge1.
\]

Assume (1) for $r$ and consider
\[
b_1,\ldots,b_{2^{r+1}}.
\]
If some
\[
2^r<j\le2^{r+1}
\]
satisfies $b_j\ge j$, then the first $2^r$ terms contribute at least $r+1$ by induction, while the $j$th term contributes at least $1$. Hence the full sum is at least $r+2$.

Suppose instead that
\[
b_j<j
\qquad(2^r<j\le2^{r+1}).
\tag{2}
\]
Among the $2^{r+1}$ distinct positive integers $b_i$, at least one is at least $2^{r+1}$. By (2), such a term must be $b_m$ with
\[
m\le2^r.
\]
Replace $b_m$ among the first $2^r$ terms by $b_{2^r+1}$, obtaining distinct positive integers
\[
c_1,\ldots,c_{2^r}.
\]
Since $b_{2^r+1}<2^r+1$ and $m\le2^r$,
\[
b_{2^r+1}+m\le2^{r+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\lfloor\frac{b_m}{m}\right\rfloor
&\ge
\left\lfloor\frac{2^{r+1}}{m}\right\rfloor\\
&\ge
\left\lfloor\frac{b_{2^r+1}+m}{m}\right\rfloor\\
&=
\left\lfloor\frac{c_m}{m}\right\rfloor+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The induction hypothesis applied to the $c_i$ now gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{2^{r+1}}\left\lfloor\frac{b_i}{i}\right\rfloor
\ge
\sum_{i=1}^{2^r}\left\lfloor\frac{c_i}{i}\right\rfloor+1
\ge r+2.
\]
This proves the claim.

Applying the claim to the first $2^k$ entries of any permutation gives a lower bound of $k+1$. Together with the construction, the minimum is $k+1$.

SOLUTION 2:
The construction in Solution 1 proves that the answer is at most
\[
\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1.
\]
We give another proof of the lower bound.

Let
\[
2^k\le n<2^{k+1}
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal P=\{1,2,4,\ldots,2^k\}
\]
be the powers of $2$ not exceeding $n$.

Call an index $i$ \emph{good} if
\[
a_i\ge i,
\]
and associate to it the interval
\[
[i,a_i].
\]

\textbf{Lemma 1.}
The intervals belonging to good indices cover every integer in
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.
\]

\textit{Proof.}
View the permutation as the map $i\mapsto a_i$. Fix $x$, and follow the cycle containing $x$:
\[
x,\ a_x,\ a_{a_x},\ldots.
\]
Starting at $x$, choose the first element $i$ in this cycle whose image satisfies
\[
a_i\ge x.
\]
All preceding elements of the traversal are smaller than $x$, so
\[
i\le x\le a_i.
\]
Thus $x\in[i,a_i]$. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.}
If a good interval $[i,a_i]$ contains $r$ distinct powers of $2$, then
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor\ge r.
\]

\textit{Proof.}
The ratio between the largest and smallest of those powers is at least
\[
2^{r-1}.
\]
Since both powers lie in $[i,a_i]$,
\[
\frac{a_i}{i}\ge2^{r-1}\ge r.
\]
$\square$

By Lemma 1, the good intervals cover all $k+1$ members of $\mathcal P$. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor
&=
\sum_{i\text{ good}}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor\\
&\ge
\sum_{i\text{ good}}|[i,a_i]\cap\mathcal P|\\
&\ge
|\mathcal P|\\
&=k+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
This establishes the lower bound.

SOLUTION 3:
As before, the construction in Solution 1 gives the required upper bound. For the lower bound, we use the inequality
\[
\left\lfloor\frac ab\right\rfloor
\ge
\log_2\frac{a+1}{b}
\tag{1}
\]
for all positive integers $a,b$.

Indeed, put
\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac ab\right\rfloor.
\]
Since $a<(t+1)b$ and both sides are integers,
\[
a+1\le(t+1)b.
\]
Also,
\[
2^t\ge t+1.
\]
Thus
\[
t\ge\log_2(t+1)\ge\log_2\frac{a+1}{b},
\]
proving (1).

Apply (1) to every pair $(a_i,i)$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor
&\ge
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log_2\frac{a_i+1}{i}\\
&=
\log_2
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_i+1)}
{\prod_{i=1}^{n}i}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the $a_i$ form a permutation of $1,\ldots,n$,
\[
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_i+1)}
{\prod_{i=1}^{n}i}
=
\frac{2\cdot3\cdots(n+1)}{1\cdot2\cdots n}
=n+1.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lfloor\frac{a_i}{i}\right\rfloor
\ge\log_2(n+1).
\]
The left side is an integer, so it is at least
\[
\left\lceil\log_2(n+1)\right\rceil
=
\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor+1.
\]
