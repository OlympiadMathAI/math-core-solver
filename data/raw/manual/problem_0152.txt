ID: 0152

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2015

TOPIC: Combinatorics

SUBTOPICS: Combinatorial Processes, Induction, Extremal Principle, Ordered Structures

STATEMENT:
In Lineland there are $n\ge 1$ towns arranged along a road from left to right. Each town has a left bulldozer, placed to the left of the town and facing left, and a right bulldozer, placed to the right of the town and facing right. The sizes of all $2n$ bulldozers are distinct.

Whenever a right bulldozer and a left bulldozer meet head-on, the larger one pushes the smaller one off the road. If a bulldozer reaches another bulldozer from behind, however, the first bulldozer pushes the second one off the road regardless of their sizes.

Let $A$ and $B$ be towns, with $B$ to the right of $A$. We say that $A$ can sweep $B$ away if the right bulldozer of $A$ can move to $B$, pushing off every bulldozer it meets. Similarly, $B$ can sweep $A$ away if the left bulldozer of $B$ can move to $A$, pushing off every bulldozer on its way.

Prove that there is exactly one town which cannot be swept away by any other town.

SOLUTION 1:
Let the towns, from left to right, be
\[
T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_n.
\]
We shall repeatedly use the following simple observation: if $T_i$ can sweep $T_j$ away, then it can also sweep every town lying between $T_i$ and $T_j$.

We prove the assertion by strong induction on $n$. The case $n=1$ is immediate.

Assume $n>1$. The left bulldozer of $T_1$ and the right bulldozer of $T_n$ cannot be used to sweep any other town, so they may be ignored. Among the remaining $2n-2$ bulldozers, choose the largest one. By symmetry, suppose it is the right bulldozer of $T_k$, where $k<n$.

Because this bulldozer is larger than every left bulldozer that it can meet head-on, $T_k$ can sweep away every town to its right. Conversely, no town to the right of $T_k$ can sweep $T_k$ away, because its left bulldozer would have to defeat this largest right bulldozer. Such a town therefore cannot sweep any town to the left of $T_k$ either.

Remove the towns
\[
T_{k+1},T_{k+2},\ldots,T_n.
\]
For the remaining towns $T_1,\ldots,T_k$, whether a town can be swept away by another remaining town is unchanged, and none of the removed towns can sweep any of them away. By the induction hypothesis, among $T_1,\ldots,T_k$ there is exactly one town which cannot be swept away. The preceding paragraph shows that it is also the unique such town in the original configuration.

SOLUTION 2:
Again enumerate the towns as $T_1,T_2,\ldots,T_n$ from left to right. Let $\ell_i$ and $r_i$ denote the sizes of the left and right bulldozers of $T_i$, respectively.

First note that two towns cannot sweep each other away. Indeed, if $i<j$ and $T_i$ can sweep $T_j$ away, then
\[
r_i>\ell_j.
\]
If $T_j$ could also sweep $T_i$ away, then we would have $\ell_j>r_i$, a contradiction.

Call a town swept from the right if it can be swept away by a town lying to its right. The rightmost town $T_n$ is not swept from the right, so there exists a leftmost town $T_k$ which is not swept from the right.

We first observe that no town $T_i$ with $i>k$ can sweep a town $T_j$ with $j<k$. Otherwise, in moving left from $T_i$ to $T_j$, it would also pass through and sweep $T_k$, contradicting the definition of $T_k$.

We now prove two claims.

\textbf{Claim 1.} The town $T_k$ cannot be swept from the left.

\textit{Proof.}
Take any $T_m$ with $m<k$. By the minimal choice of $k$, the town $T_m$ is swept from the right by some $T_p$, where $m<p\le k$. Hence $T_m$ cannot sweep $T_p$ away, because mutual sweeping is impossible. If $T_m$ could sweep $T_k$ away, then it would also sweep the intermediate town $T_p$, a contradiction. Thus no town to the left of $T_k$ sweeps $T_k$. $\square$

\textbf{Claim 2.} Every town $T_m$ with $m\ne k$ can be swept away.

\textit{Proof.}
If $m<k$, this follows directly from the choice of $T_k$. Now suppose $m>k$. Among
\[
T_k,T_{k+1},\ldots,T_{m-1},
\]
choose a town $T_p$ whose right bulldozer is largest. We claim that $T_p$ can sweep $T_m$ away.

If not, then for some $q$ with $p<q\le m$ we would have
\[
r_p<\ell_q.
\]
By the choice of $p$, this gives
\[
\ell_q>r_i
\qquad	ext{for every }k\le i\le m-1.
\]
Consequently the left bulldozer of $T_q$ can pass every right bulldozer between $T_q$ and $T_k$, so $T_q$ can sweep $T_k$ away. This contradicts the choice of $T_k$. Therefore $T_p$ sweeps $T_m$ away. $\square$

Claims 1 and 2 show that $T_k$ is the unique town which cannot be swept away.

SOLUTION 3:
We use two observations already implicit above:

\begin{itemize}
\item if one town can sweep another, it can sweep every intermediate town;
\item two towns cannot sweep each other.
\end{itemize}

We first prove existence. Suppose, for contradiction, that every town can be swept away. Let $t_1$ be the leftmost town, and after choosing $t_i$, choose a town $t_{i+1}$ which can sweep $t_i$ away.

We claim inductively that $t_{i+1}$ always lies to the right of $t_i$. This is clear for $i=1$. Assume it is true up to $i=k-1$, but that $t_{k+1}$ lies to the left of $t_k$. Then $t_{k+1}$ lies between two consecutive towns $t_j,t_{j+1}$ in the already constructed right-moving chain, possibly with $t_{k+1}=t_j$. Since $t_{j+1}$ can sweep $t_j$, it can also sweep $t_{k+1}$. Hence $t_{k+1}$ cannot sweep $t_{j+1}$, and therefore cannot sweep the farther town $t_k$, a contradiction. Thus the chain moves strictly to the right forever, impossible because there are finitely many towns. Hence at least one town cannot be swept away.

For uniqueness, suppose that two towns $A$ and $B$, with $A$ to the left of $B$, cannot be swept away. Consider all bulldozers lying between them, including the right bulldozer of $A$ and the left bulldozer of $B$, and choose the largest one. If it is a left bulldozer, it belongs to a town to the right of $A$ and can sweep $A$ away, since every opposing right bulldozer on the way is smaller. If it is a right bulldozer, the analogous argument shows that its town can sweep $B$ away. Either possibility is a contradiction.

Therefore exactly one town cannot be swept away.
