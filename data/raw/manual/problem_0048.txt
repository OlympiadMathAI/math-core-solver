ID: 0048

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2011

TOPIC: Geometry

SUBTOPICS: Isosceles Triangles, Angle Bisectors, Menelaus Theorem, Radical Center

STATEMENT:
Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $AB=AC$, and let $D$ be the midpoint of $AC$. The angle bisector of $\angle BAC$ meets the circle through $D,B,C$ at a point $E$ inside triangle $ABC$. The line $BD$ meets the circle through $A,E,B$ again at $F\ne B$. The lines $AF$ and $BE$ meet at $I$, and the lines $CI$ and $BD$ meet at $K$. Prove that $I$ is the incenter of triangle $KAB$.

SOLUTION 1:
Let $D'$ be the midpoint of $AB$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Since $AB=AC$, the line $AM$ is the symmetry axis of triangle $ABC$. Reflection in $AM$ sends $D$ to $D'$ and preserves the circle through $B,C,D$. Hence
\[
D'\text{ also lies on the circle }BCDE.
\]
The same symmetry shows that the arcs $D'E$ and $ED$ of this circle are equal. Therefore
\[
\angle ABI
=\angle D'BE
=\angle EBD
=\angle IBK.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus $BI$ bisects $\angle ABK$. It remains to show that $AI$ bisects $\angle BAK$.

The quadrilateral $A,B,E,F$ is cyclic. Since $B,F,D$ are collinear and $A,E,M$ are collinear,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle DFA
&=180^\circ-\angle BFA\\
&=180^\circ-\angle BEA\\
&=\angle MEB.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $EB=EC$ and $EM$ is the symmetry axis,
\[
\angle MEB=\frac12\angle CEB.
\]
Also $B,C,D,E$ are cyclic, so
\[
\angle CEB=\angle CDB.
\]
Hence
\[
\angle DFA=\frac12\angle CDB.
\tag{2}
\]
But $\angle CDB$ is the exterior angle at $D$ of triangle $ADF$, and therefore
\[
\angle CDB=\angle DAF+\angle DFA.
\]
Together with (2), this yields
\[
\angle DAF=\angle DFA,
\]
so
\[
AD=DF.
\tag{3}
\]

Apply Menelaus' theorem to triangle $ADF$ and the transversal $C,I,K$. Using directed ratios and then positive lengths in the present configuration,
\[
1=\frac{AC}{CD}\cdot\frac{DK}{KF}\cdot\frac{FI}{IA}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since $D$ is the midpoint of $AC$,
\[
\frac{AC}{CD}=2.
\]
By (1), $BI$ is the angle bisector in triangle $ABF$, so
\[
\frac{FI}{IA}=\frac{BF}{AB}.
\]
Also $AB=AC=2AD$. Therefore (4) becomes
\[
1=\frac{DK}{KF}\cdot\frac{BF}{AD},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{BF}{AD}=\frac{KF}{DK}.
\tag{5}
\]
Using (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{BD}{AD}
&=\frac{BF+FD}{AD}\\
&=\frac{BF}{AD}+1\\
&=\frac{KF}{DK}+1\\
&=\frac{DF}{DK}\\
&=\frac{AD}{DK}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus triangles $BDA$ and $ADK$ are similar, and consequently
\[
\angle ABD=\angle DAK.
\tag{6}
\]
Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle IAB
&=\angle AFD-\angle ABD\\
&=\angle DAF-\angle DAK\\
&=\angle KAI,
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used (2), (3), and (6). Hence $AI$ bisects $\angle BAK$. Together with (1), this proves that $I$ is the incenter of triangle $KAB$.

SOLUTION 2:
As in Solution 1, relation
\[
\angle ABI=\angle IBK
\tag{1}
\]
holds, so $BI$ bisects $\angle ABK$. We prove that $KI$ bisects $\angle AKB$.

Let $\omega_1$ be the circumcircle of $BCD$, with center $O_1$. Since $A,B,E,F$ are cyclic,
\[
\angle DFE=\angle BAE=\angle DAE,
\]
and
\[
\angle EAF=\angle EBF=\angle ABE=\angle AFE.
\]
It follows that
\[
\angle DAF=\angle DFA,
\]
so
\[
DF=DA=DC.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the circle $\omega_2$ through $A,F,C$ has center $D$.

Let $\omega_3$ be the circumcircle of $ABD$, with center $O_3$. The arcs $BE$ and $EC$ of $\omega_1$ are equal. Together with the congruence of triangles $ADE$ and $FDE$, this gives
\[
\angle AO_1B=2\angle BDE=\angle BDA.
\]
Therefore
\[
O_1\in\omega_3.
\]
Since $O_3$ is the center of $\omega_3$, both $O_1$ and $D$ lie on $\omega_3$, and hence
\[
O_3O_1=O_3D.
\]
Thus
\[
\angle O_3O_1D=\angle O_3DO_1.
\tag{3}
\]

The line $BD$ is the radical axis of $\omega_1$ and $\omega_3$. The point $C$ lies on both $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. Moreover,
\[
IA\cdot IF=IB\cdot IE
\]
because $A,B,E,F$ are cyclic, so $I$ has equal powers with respect to $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. Hence
\[
CI
\]
is their radical axis. Since
\[
K=BD\cap CI,
\]
the point $K$ is the radical center of $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$. Therefore
\[
AK
\]
is the radical axis of $\omega_2$ and $\omega_3$.

Radical axes are perpendicular to the corresponding lines of centers. Consequently,
\[
AK\perp DO_3,
\qquad
BK\perp O_1O_3,
\qquad
IK\perp DO_1.
\]
The equality of the base angles in (3) now implies
\[
\angle AKI=\angle IKB.
\]
Thus $KI$ bisects $\angle AKB$. Together with (1), this proves that $I$ is the incenter of triangle $KAB$.

SOLUTION 3:
Again, the symmetry argument from Solution 1 gives
\[
\angle ABI=\angle IBK,
\tag{1}
\]
so it remains to show that $AI$ bisects $\angle KAB$.

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$, and let $G\ne C$ be the second intersection of the circles through $A,F,C$ and through $B,C,D$. The lines
\[
AF,\qquad BE,\qquad CG
\]
are the three radical axes of the circles
\[
(AFC),\qquad (BCD),\qquad (ABEF).
\]
Since $I=AF\cap BE$, the radical-axis theorem gives
\[
C,G,I\text{ are collinear}.
\tag{2}
\]

The angle between $DE$ and the tangent to the circle $BCDE$ at $E$ equals
\[
\angle EBD=\angle EAF=\angle ABE=\angle AFE.
\]
The tangent at $E$ is perpendicular to the symmetry axis $AM$. Hence
\[
DE\perp AF.
\]
Also triangle $AFE$ is isosceles, so $DE$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AF$. Therefore
\[
DA=DF.
\]
Since $D$ is the midpoint of $AC$,
\[
DA=DC,
\]
and hence $D$ is the center of the circle through $A,F,C$. The point $M$ also lies on this circle because
\[
\angle AMC=90^\circ.
\]

Let $B'$ be the reflection of $B$ in the point $D$. Then the diagonals $AC$ and $BB'$ bisect each other, so $ABCB'$ is a parallelogram. Since $D$ is the center of the circle through $A,F,C,G$,
\[
DC=DG.
\]
Using (2), the cyclicity of $B,C,D,G$, and the parallelogram, we obtain
\[
\angle KCA
=\angle GCD
=\angle DBC
=\angle KB'A.
\]
Thus $A,K,C,B'$ are cyclic. Therefore
\[
\angle CAK
=\angle CB'K
=\angle ABD.
\tag{3}
\]
The equal arcs $D'E$ and $ED$ from Solution 1 give
\[
\angle ABD=2\angle EBD.
\]
Since $A,E,M$ are collinear and $A,B,E,F$ are cyclic,
\[
\angle EBD=\angle EAF=\angle MAI.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\angle CAK=2\angle MAI.
\tag{4}
\]
Finally, $AM$ bisects $\angle CAB$, and (4) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\angle IAB
&=\angle MAB-\angle MAI\\
&=\frac12\angle CAB-\frac12\angle CAK\\
&=\frac12\angle KAB.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $AI$ bisects $\angle KAB$. Together with (1), this proves that $I$ is the incenter of triangle $KAB$.
