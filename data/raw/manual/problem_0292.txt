ID: 0292

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2019

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Diophantine Equations, Inequalities, Divisibility, Unimodal Polynomials

STATEMENT:
Find all triples $(a,b,c)$ of positive integers satisfying
\[
a^3+b^3+c^3=(abc)^2.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
The equation is symmetric, so assume
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]
We prove that
\[
(a,b,c)=(3,2,1).
\]

First,
\[
3a^3\ge a^3+b^3+c^3=(abc)^2>a^3,
\]
so
\[
3a\ge b^2c^2>a.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
b^3+c^3
=a^2(b^2c^2-a)
\ge a^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
18b^3
\ge9(b^3+c^3)
\ge9a^2
\ge b^4c^4
\ge b^3c^5,
\]
where the penultimate inequality follows from (1), and the last from $b\ge c$. Hence
\[
c^5\le18,
\]
so
\[
c=1.
\]

The equation becomes
\[
a^3+b^3+1=a^2b^2.
\tag{2}
\]
We cannot have $a=b$, because then
\[
2b^3+1=b^4,
\]
which has no positive integer solution. Thus $a>b$.

From (2),
\[
2a^3>a^3+b^3+1=a^2b^2>a^3,
\]
and hence
\[
2a>b^2>a.
\tag{3}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
4(1+b^3)
=
4a^2(b^2-a)
\ge4a^2>b^4.
\]
Thus
\[
4>b^3(b-4),
\]
which gives
\[
b\le4.
\]
The case $b=1$ is impossible in (2), so $2\le b\le4$.

For these three possibilities, equation (2) becomes
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
b=2:&a^3-4a^2+9=0,\\
b=3:&a^3-9a^2+28=0,\\
b=4:&a^3-16a^2+65=0.
\end{array}
\]
The only positive integer root satisfying $a\ge b$ is
\[
a=3,\qquad b=2.
\]
Therefore the only ordered solution under $a\ge b\ge c$ is $(3,2,1)$, and all solutions are the six permutations of
\[
\boxed{(1,2,3)}.
\]

SOLUTION 2:
Assume again that
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]

We first prove that $c=1$. If $c\ge2$, then
\[
a^3+b^3+c^3\le3a^3,
\]
so
\[
b^2c^2\le3a.
\]
Since $c\ge2$,
\[
b\le\sqrt{\frac{3a}{4}}.
\]
The right side of the original equation is divisible by $a^2$, and hence
\[
a^2\le b^3+c^3\le2b^3
\le2\left(\frac{3a}{4}\right)^{3/2}.
\]
After cancelling $a^{3/2}$, this gives
\[
a\le\frac{27}{16}<2,
\]
contradicting $a\ge c\ge2$. Hence
\[
c=1.
\]

Now
\[
a^3+b^3+1=a^2b^2.
\tag{1}
\]
We have $a\ge2$. Since $a^2\mid b^3+1$,
\[
b^3+1\ge a^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
b^2
=
a+\frac{b^3+1}{a^2}
\le
2a+\frac1{a^2}.
\]
As $b^2$ is an integer,
\[
b^2\le2a.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3),
\[
(2a)^{3/2}+1\ge b^3+1\ge a^2.
\]
This inequality fails for every $a\ge9$, so
\[
2\le a\le8.
\]

Checking these seven values in (1), the only possibility with $a\ge b$ is
\[
(a,b)=(3,2).
\]
Thus the solutions are precisely the permutations of
\[
\boxed{(1,2,3)}.
\]

SOLUTION 3:
Assume
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]
Set
\[
k=\frac{b^3+c^3}{a^2}.
\]
The original equation shows that $k$ is a positive integer and
\[
a+k=b^2c^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Also,
\[
k\le2a.
\tag{2}
\]

For positive integers $u,v$,
\[
uv\ge u+v-1.
\]
Applying this to $u=b^3$ and $v=c^3$ gives
\[
(bc)^3\ge b^3+c^3-1=ka^2-1.
\]
Using (1),
\[
a+k=(bc)^2\ge(ka^2-1)^{2/3}.
\]
Cubing and rearranging,
\[
k^2a^4-a^3-5ka^2-3k^2a-(k^3-1)\le0.
\tag{3}
\]

Divide the left side of (3) by $k^2$. Since $k\ge1$ and $k\le2a$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{
k^2a^4-a^3-5ka^2-3k^2a-(k^3-1)
}{k^2}
&\ge
a^4-a^3-5a^2-3a-k\\
&\ge
a^4-a^3-5a^2-5a.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression is positive for every $a\ge4$, contradicting (3). Hence
\[
a\le3.
\]

There are only ten triples with
\[
3\ge a\ge b\ge c\ge1.
\]
Checking them gives the unique possibility
\[
(a,b,c)=(3,2,1).
\]
Therefore all solutions are the permutations of
\[
\boxed{(1,2,3)}.
\]

SOLUTION 4:
Assume
\[
a\ge b\ge c.
\]
From
\[
b^3+c^3=a^2(b^2c^2-a)
\]
we obtain
\[
b\le a\le b^2c^2-1.
\tag{1}
\]

Define
\[
F(x)=x^2(b^2c^2-x).
\]
On the interval
\[
[0,b^2c^2-1],
\]
the function $F$ increases up to $2b^2c^2/3$ and then decreases. Hence, by (1),
\[
b^3+c^3=F(a)
\ge
\min\{F(b),F(b^2c^2-1)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that
\[
b^3+c^3\ge F(b^2c^2-1)=(b^2c^2-1)^2.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
2b^3
&\ge b^3+c^3\\
&\ge(b^2c^2-1)^2\\
&>b^4c^4-2b^2c^2\\
&\ge b^4c^4-2b^3c^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
(b-2)c^4<2.
\]
Since $b\ge c$, this leaves only
\[
(b,c)=(2,2)
\quad\text{or}\quad
c=1,\ b\le3.
\]
Checking these cases in the original equation gives only
\[
(a,b,c)=(3,2,1).
\]

Otherwise, by (2),
\[
b^3+c^3\ge F(b)=b^2(b^2c^2-b).
\]
Thus
\[
2b^3\ge b^2(b^2c^2-b),
\]
so
\[
bc^2\le3.
\]
If $b\ne c$, the first inequality is strict, and hence $bc^2<3$. This forces
\[
c=1,\qquad b\le2.
\]
These cases have already been checked and again yield only $(3,2,1)$.

Thus all positive integer solutions are the permutations of
\[
\boxed{(1,2,3)}.
\]
