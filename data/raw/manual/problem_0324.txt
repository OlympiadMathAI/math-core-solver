ID: 0324

SOURCE: IMO Shortlist

YEAR: 2020

TOPIC: Number Theory

SUBTOPICS: Rational Constructions, Modular Arithmetic, Prime Avoidance, Recurrence Relations

STATEMENT:
Given a positive integer $k$, prove that there exists a prime $p$ for which one can choose distinct integers
\[
a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_{k+3}\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
\]
such that
\[
p\mid a_i a_{i+1}a_{i+2}a_{i+3}-i
\]
for every
\[
i=1,2,\ldots,k.
\]

SOLUTION 1:
We first construct distinct positive rational numbers
\[
r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_{k+3}
\]
satisfying
\[
r_i r_{i+1}r_{i+2}r_{i+3}=i
\qquad(1\le i\le k).
\tag{1}
\]

Choose three distinct primes
\[
x,y,z>k
\]
and set
\[
r_1=x,\qquad r_2=y,\qquad r_3=z,\qquad
r_4=\frac1{xyz}.
\]
For every $i\ge1$ for which the term is needed, define
\[
r_{i+4}=\frac{i+1}{i}\,r_i.
\tag{2}
\]
The first product is
\[
r_1r_2r_3r_4=1.
\]
If (1) holds for an index $i$, then by (2),
\[
r_{i+1}r_{i+2}r_{i+3}r_{i+4}
=
r_ir_{i+1}r_{i+2}r_{i+3}\cdot\frac{r_{i+4}}{r_i}
=
i\cdot\frac{i+1}{i}
=i+1.
\]
Thus (1) holds for all required indices.

We also verify that the $r_i$ are pairwise distinct. Write every $r_i$ in lowest terms. Since
\[
x,y,z>k,
\]
none of these three primes can be cancelled by any of the factors appearing in the recurrence (2). Consequently:

\begin{itemize}
\item the numerator of $r_i$ is divisible by $x$ when $i\equiv1\pmod4$;
\item it is divisible by $y$ when $i\equiv2\pmod4$;
\item it is divisible by $z$ when $i\equiv3\pmod4$;
\item it is divisible by none of $x,y,z$ when $i\equiv0\pmod4$.
\end{itemize}

Hence terms belonging to different residue classes modulo $4$ are distinct. Within each residue class, (2) shows that
\[
r_i<r_{i+4},
\]
so all the terms are distinct.

Write
\[
r_i=\frac{u_i}{v_i}
\]
in lowest terms, with $u_i,v_i$ positive. Choose a prime $p>k$ that divides none of the finitely many nonzero integers
\[
u_i,\qquad v_i,\qquad
u_iv_j-u_jv_i
\quad(1\le i<j\le k+3).
\tag{3}
\]
Such a prime exists.

For every $i$, let $a_i$ be the unique integer in
\[
\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
\]
satisfying
\[
a_iv_i\equiv u_i\pmod p.
\tag{4}
\]
The condition on $u_i$ and $v_i$ guarantees that $a_i$ is nonzero modulo $p$.

If $a_i\equiv a_j\pmod p$, then (4) gives
\[
u_iv_j\equiv u_jv_i\pmod p,
\]
contrary to (3). Thus the $a_i$ are distinct.

Finally, multiply the rational identity (1) by
\[
v_iv_{i+1}v_{i+2}v_{i+3}
\]
and reduce modulo $p$. Using (4), we obtain
\[
a_ia_{i+1}a_{i+2}a_{i+3}\equiv i\pmod p
\]
for every $1\le i\le k$. This is exactly the desired conclusion.
